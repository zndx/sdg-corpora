# Chapter 4: Regulatory Framework Alignment and Control Mapping

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the structural alignment between organizational internal controls and external regulatory mandates, specifically focusing on the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA), the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS), and the ISO/IEC 27001 information security standard. The objective is to define the ontological relationships where internal directives function as implementation mechanisms for broader compliance requirements. We examine how specific classes of directives (`cco:DirectiveICE`) are subclassed to satisfy the distinct safeguard and requirement clauses defined by these frameworks. This mapping provides the necessary evidence base for audit readiness and compliance verification, ensuring that every governance action is traceable to a specific statutory or contractual obligation.

## 4.2 HIPAA Physical Safeguards

The HIPAA Security Rule defines Physical Safeguards as physical measures to protect electronic information systems and related buildings and equipment from natural and environmental hazards and unauthorized intrusion. In the context of the ontology, a directive classified under this domain is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` that specifically enacts the `sdg:hipaaSafeguard` object property targeting a physical asset class. This relationship ensures that policy governance extends to the tangible domains of facility security and hardware management.

The implementation of physical safeguards requires a granular mapping between policy directives and the specific physical entities they protect. This is not merely a procedural formality but a structural requirement for compliance. The directive must explicitly identify the physical nature of the target—whether it is a workstation, a server facility, or portable media—to satisfy the administrative simplification provisions of the Security Rule.

### 4.2.1 Facility Access Controls
The `FacilityAccessControlDirective` (PHYS-001) represents the class of policies governing entry and exit to facilities containing Electronic Protected Health Information (ePHI). This directive safeguards the `RestrictedArea` class, encompassing server rooms, data centers, and any physical space where unauthorized access could lead to data compromise. Compliance is evidenced by access logs, badge records, and visitor manifests that demonstrate the enforcement of these directives.

### 4.2.2 Workstation Security
The `WorkstationUsePolicyDirective` (PHYS-002) addresses the security requirements for computing devices located in non-secure environments. By safeguarding `PhysicalWorkstation` entities, this class of directives mandates that screens be locked, workstations be positioned to minimize visibility, and that hardware be physically secured against theft. This mapping is critical for audits reviewing the "Workstation Use" implementation specification of the HIPAA Security Rule.

## 4.3 HIPAA Technical Safeguards

Technical Safeguards involve the technology and the policy and procedures for its use that protect electronic health information and control access to it. In the ontology model, these are represented by directives that are subclasses of `cco:DirectiveICE` and enact the `sdg:hipaaSafeguard` property regarding technical classes of data or systems. Unlike physical safeguards, which focus on the hardware and environment, technical safeguards focus on the logical access controls, encryption, and audit mechanisms that operate on the data itself.

The distinction between the directive and the safeguarded entity is critical here. The directive is the administrative rule (e.g., "Encrypt all data in transit"), while the entity is the logical object (e.g., "ElectronicPHI"). The ontology captures this by linking the class of the controlling directive to the class of the data asset.

### 4.3.1 Access Control and Audit Mechanisms
The `AccessControlMechanismDirective` (TECH-001) establishes the rules for granting or denying access to `ElectronicPHI`. This class of directives implements the Unique User Identification, Emergency Access Procedure, and Automatic Logoff specifications. By linking this directive to the data class, the organization ensures that access rights are managed centrally and are auditable.

Simultaneously, the `AuditControlMechanismDirective` (TECH-002) safeguards the `SystemActivityLog`. This directive mandates the implementation of hardware, software, and/or procedural mechanisms that record and examine activity in information systems that contain or use ePHI. This is the primary evidence source for forensic investigations and compliance monitoring.

## 4.4 HIPAA Administrative Safeguards

Administrative Safeguards are the administrative actions, and policies and procedures, to manage the selection, development, implementation, and maintenance of security measures to protect electronic health information. These are often the most complex components of the HIPAA Security Rule because they require the integration of management, workforce, and information technology processes. In the ontology, these are modeled as directives (`cco:DirectiveICE`) that safeguard administrative or procedural entities.

The mapping here captures the broad scope of the Security Management Process. The directives encompass risk analysis, risk management, sanction policies, and information system activity reviews. The target classes (`y`) represent the organizational processes or workforce elements being protected or managed.

### 4.4.1 Security Management and Workforce Training
The `SecurityManagementProcessDirective` (ADMIN-001) is the foundational class of directives that safeguards the `RiskAnalysisProcess`. This directive mandates the periodic conduct of an accurate and thorough assessment of the potential risks and vulnerabilities to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of ePHI. It is the governing policy that drives all subsequent security measures.

Furthermore, the `SecurityAwarenessTrainingDirective` (ADMIN-004) safeguards the `EmployeeTrainingRecord`. This directive ensures that all members of the workforce, including management, undergo training to ensure they are aware of the security responsibilities associated with their roles. The linkage in the ontology confirms that the directive exists specifically to protect the integrity and currency of the workforce's security knowledge.

## 4.5 PCI DSS Requirements

The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) prescribes a robust set of requirements intended to ensure that all entities that process, store, or transmit cardholder data maintain a secure environment. The ontology models these requirements as directives (`cco:DirectiveICE`) that satisfy the `sdg:pciDssRequirement` property. This relationship maps internal governance controls to the specific technical and operational requirements outlined in the PCI DSS framework (e.g., Requirement 1: Install and maintain a firewall configuration).

The mapping is essential for scoping audits. By identifying which internal directives fulfill which PCI DSS requirements, an organization can demonstrate a "clean" audit trail. The target classes (`y`) in this schema represent the specific PCI DSS requirement categories or the environments they govern (e.g., Cardholder Data Environment).

### 4.5.1 Network Security and Encryption
The `FirewallConfigurationDirective` (PCI-001) is the internal control class that maps to `Requirement1_FirewallConfiguration`. This directive establishes the standards for reviewing firewall and router rule sets at least every six months and verifying that rules are set to deny all inbound and outbound traffic not specifically needed. This is a critical control for defining the perimeter of the Cardholder Data Environment (CDE).

Similarly, the `StoredDataEncryptionDirective` (PCI-003) maps to `Requirement3_ProtectStoredData`. This directive mandates the use of cryptography consistent with industry standards (e.g., AES-256) to render cardholder data unreadable to unauthorized persons. The ontological relationship confirms that the directive is the mechanism for compliance with the encryption requirement.

## 4.6 ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A Controls

ISO/IEC 27001 is the international standard that specifies the requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an information security management system (ISMS). Annex A of the standard provides a list of information security controls objectives and controls. In the ontology, directives are subclassed as `cco:DirectiveICE` to enact the `sdg:iso27001AnnexA` property, linking organizational policies to specific Annex A control domains (e.g., A.9 Access Control, A.12 Operations Security).

This mapping allows an organization to align its ISMS with the codified best practices of ISO 27001. The directives (`x`) represent the internal implementation of the controls, while the target classes (`y`) represent the Annex A control categories or specific control codes.

### 4.6.1 Access Control and Asset Management
The `AccessControlPolicyDirective` (ISO-001) maps to the `A9_AccessControl` domain. This directive governs the logical and physical access to assets, ensuring that access rights are granted based on the business requirements and the principle of least privilege. It serves as the overarching governance for user provisioning, de-provisioning, and review processes.

The `AssetManagementDirective` (ISO-002) maps to `A8_AssetManagement`. This directive ensures that assets associated with information and information processing facilities are identified and an inventory of these assets is drawn up and maintained. This inventory is a prerequisite for effective risk management and is the primary evidence required for ISO 27001 certification audits regarding asset ownership.
