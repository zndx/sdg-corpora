# Regulatory Compliance Mapping: Directives for HIPAA, PCI DSS, and ISO 27001

This chapter delineates the formal mapping between internal governance directives and external regulatory frameworks governing information security. It provides a structured analysis of how organizational control objectives—expressed as Information Control Elements (ICE)—align with the specific safeguard requirements of the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA), the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS), and the ISO/IEC 27001 standard. The following sections define the relational schema for these mappings, offering auditable evidence of compliance through the alignment of directive classes with their respective safeguard and requirement subclasses.

## 1. HIPAA Physical Safeguards

The HIPAA Security Rule, codified at 45 CFR § 164.310, establishes Physical Safeguards to protect electronic protected health information (ePHI) and its related buildings and equipment from natural and environmental hazards, as well as unauthorized intrusion. Within the compliance ontology, a Physical Safeguard is represented as a class of directive that mandates specific controls over the physical environment. The axiom `hipaa_safeguard_physical` asserts that a DirectiveICE (X) is a subclass of entities that implement a specific physical safeguard (Y).

### 1.1 Regulatory Basis and Control Implementation
Compliance with physical safeguards requires a formal assessment of facility access controls. Covered entities must implement policies and procedures to limit physical access to its electronic information systems and the facility in which they are housed, while ensuring that properly authorized access is allowed. The implementation specification for "Contingency Operations" (§ 164.310(a)(2)(i)) mandates the establishment of procedures that allow facility access in support of restoration of lost data under the disaster recovery plan and emergency mode operations plan.

Furthermore, the "Access Control and Validation" (§ 164.310(a)(2)(ii)) specification necessitates procedures to control and validate a person's access to facilities based on their role or function, including visitor control and access to software programs. The axiom `hipaa_safeguard_physical` formally binds these operational requirements to the directive classes governing facility security.

### 1.2 Evidence of Compliance Mapping
The following table presents the relational mapping of directive classes to physical safeguard classes. Each row represents a distinct assertion that a specific governance directive (X) functions as a mechanism to enforce a physical safeguard (Y). The primary key (`id`) ensures referential integrity within the compliance database.

## 2. HIPAA Technical Safeguards

Technical Safeguards, defined under 45 CFR § 164.312, involve the technology and the policy and procedures for its use that protect electronic health information and control access to it. The axiom `hipaa_safeguard_technical` defines the relationship where a DirectiveICE (X) is a subclass of entities that enforce a specific technical safeguard (Y). Unlike physical safeguards, these controls are automated or logical mechanisms embedded within the information system architecture.

### 2.1 Access Control and Audit Mechanisms
A critical component of this domain is the implementation of "Unique User Identification" (§ 164.312(a)(1)), which requires the assignment of a unique name and/or number for identifying and tracking user identity. Additionally, "Emergency Access Procedure" (§ 164.312(a)(2)(i)) establishes the methods for obtaining necessary electronic protected health information during an emergency.

The axiom `hipaa_safeguard_technical` maps these regulatory requirements to specific technical directive classes. For instance, an `AuthenticationDirective` is formally classified as a mechanism that implements an `AuthenticationSafeguard`. This mapping is essential for automated compliance auditing, as it allows the governance framework to verify that the existence of a policy (X) correlates with the deployment of a technical control (Y).

### 2.2 Encryption and Transmission Security
The "Transmission Security" standard (§ 164.312(e)(1)) mandates technical security measures to guard against unauthorized access to electronic protected health information that is being transmitted over an electronic communications network. This includes the implementation of encryption mechanisms. The following table illustrates the mapping of directives to these technical controls.

| ID | Directive Class (X) | Technical Safeguard Class (Y) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| hipaa-tech-001 | AccessControlPolicy | UniqueUserIdentification |
| hipaa-tech-002 | EmergencyAccessProtocol | EmergencyAccessProcedure |
| hipaa-tech-003 | AuditControlDirective | AuditControlMechanism |
| hipaa-tech-004 | IntegrityControlPolicy | ElectronicDataIntegrity |
| hipaa-tech-005 | TransmissionSecurityStandard | EncryptionMechanism |

## 3. HIPAA Administrative Safeguards

Administrative Safeguards are administrative actions, policies, and procedures to manage the selection, development, implementation, and maintenance of security measures to protect electronic health information. These safeguards are outlined in 45 CFR § 164.308. The axiom `hipaa_safeguard_admin` posits that a DirectiveICE (X) is a subclass of entities that enforce an administrative safeguard (Y).

### 3.1 Security Management Process
The "Security Management Process" (§ 164.308(a)(1)) requires covered entities to implement policies and procedures to prevent, detect, contain, and correct security violations. This includes Risk Analysis and Risk Management. The directive class `RiskAssessmentProtocol` serves as the administrative vehicle for the `RiskAnalysisSafeguard`.

Additionally, the "Workforce Security" standard (§ 164.308(a)(3)) necessitates policies and procedures to ensure that all members of the workforce have appropriate access to electronic health information. This includes authorization and/or supervision. The mapping defined by `hipaa_safeguard_admin` captures the relationship between the workforce authorization directives and the safeguard classes they instantiate.

### 3.2 Training and Contingency Planning
"Security Awareness and Training" (§ 164.308(a)(5)) mandates a security awareness and training program for all members of the workforce. The directive `TrainingProgramDirective` is axiomatically linked to the `SecurityTrainingSafeguard`. The table below details these administrative mappings, providing the necessary evidence for regulatory review.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| hipaa-admin-001 | SecurityManagementPolicy | RiskAnalysisSafeguard |
| hipaa-admin-002 | RiskManagementStrategy | RiskManagementSafeguard |
| hipaa-admin-003 | WorkforceAuthorizationPolicy | WorkforceAuthorization |

## 4. PCI DSS Requirements

The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) provides a robust framework for organizations that handle cardholder data. The axiom `pci_dss_requirement` asserts that a DirectiveICE (X) is a subclass of entities that fulfill a specific PCI DSS requirement (Y). This mapping is critical for scoping assessments and validating the implementation of the 12 high-level requirements.

### 4.1 Network Security and Cardholder Data Protection
PCI DSS Requirement 1 mandates the installation and maintenance of a firewall configuration to protect cardholder data. A directive such as `FirewallConfigurationPolicy` is axiomatically bound to the `FirewallConfigurationRequirement`. Similarly, Requirement 2 requires the use of strong cryptography and security protocols to safeguard sensitive cardholder data during transmission over open, public networks.

The relational mapping for PCI DSS differs slightly from HIPAA in that the object (Y) is a specific requirement class defined by the standard. This allows the organization to trace a specific internal policy directly to a compliance requirement, facilitating gap analysis.

### 4.2 Vulnerability Management and Access Control
Requirement 5 focuses on protecting all systems against malware and regularly updating anti-virus software or programs. Requirement 6 necessitates the development and maintenance of secure systems and applications. The following table encodes the relationship between internal directives and these specific PCI DSS requirement classes.

| ID | Directive Class (X) | PCI DSS Requirement Class (Y) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| pci-req-001 | NetworkFirewallStandard | FirewallConfigurationRequirement |
| pci-req-002 | SecureTransmissionPolicy | SecureDataTransmission |
| pci-req-003 | MalwareProtectionDirective | AntiVirusSoftwareRequirement |
| pci-req-004 | SecureDevelopmentLifecycle | SecureSystemDevelopment |
| pci-req-005 | AccessControlStandard | RestrictAccessToCardholderData |
| pci-req-006 | PhysicalAccessPolicy | PhysicalAccessToCardholderData |

## 5. ISO 27001 Annex A Controls

ISO/IEC 27001 specifies the requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an information security management system (ISMS). Annex A provides a list of information security controls. The axiom `iso27001_annexa_subclass` defines that a DirectiveICE (X) is a subclass of entities that implement a specific ISO 27001 Annex A control (Y).

### 5.1 Information Security Policies and Access Control
Annex A control A.5.1.1 requires the organization to produce an information security policy. The directive `InformationSecurityPolicy` is axiomatically linked to the `InformationSecurityPolicyControl`. Furthermore, control A.9 (Access Control) mandates that access to information and other associated assets is authorized and restricted according to business requirements.

This mapping allows the organization to demonstrate that its internal directives are not merely ad-hoc policies but are formal instantiations of internationally recognized control objectives. The axiom `iso27001_annexa_subclass` serves as the bridge between the organization's governance framework and the ISO 27001 certification scope.

### 5.2 Cryptography and Operations Security
Control A.10 addresses cryptography, requiring the use of appropriate encryption to protect the confidentiality of information. Control A.12 covers operations security, including logging and monitoring. The table below maps directive classes to these Annex A control classes, ensuring alignment with the ISO 27001 control set.
