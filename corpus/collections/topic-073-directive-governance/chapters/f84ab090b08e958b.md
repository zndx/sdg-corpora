## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the structural components of the Internal Compliance Framework (ICF) as applied to data governance operations within the enterprise. It establishes the formal relationships between regulatory directives, lawful bases for processing, and the procedural mechanisms of audit and attestation. The definitions provided herein align with the OWL-based ontology utilized for semantic interoperability across the organization's governance architecture. Which aspect of compliance management is most critical? Is the definition of the directive more important than the execution of the audit? Obviously, each is important and they all interact to ensure the business operates smoothly and lawfully. But it is easier to define the rule than it is to prove adherence to it. Therefore, this text focuses on the evidence-anchored verification of compliance, mapping abstract legal requirements to concrete process instances.

## 4.2 Directive Equivalence and Target Specification

The foundational unit of governance is the Directive. In the context of the ontology, a Directive is not merely a static text but a functional entity that performs the logical operation of specifying a requirement while simultaneously applying to a specific target class. This relationship is captured by the axiom: *{X} EquivalentTo cco:DirectiveICE and (sdg:specifies some {Y}) and (sdg:appliesTo some {Z})*.

Here, X represents the Directive itself (e.g., a corporate policy regarding data retention). The slot Y represents the specific requirement or lawful basis being invoked, while Z denotes the target data domain or organizational unit affected. This tripartite structure ensures that no directive exists in a vacuum; every governance action must be explicitly linked to a requirement and a target. This equivalence class is critical for automated reasoning engines to determine if a specific data processing activity is covered by an existing governance framework.

The relational projection of this axiom creates a mapping between the directive, the requirement it specifies, and the target it governs. The primary key is the directive identifier, which serves as the anchor for all subsequent compliance activities.

## 4.3 GDPR Lawful Basis Integration

While directives provide the internal governance structure, they must be grounded in external regulatory frameworks. Specifically, for any processing of personal data to be lawful, it must satisfy the criteria set forth in the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). The axiom *{X} is something that has lawful basis {Y}* formalizes this connection.

In this structure, X is the internal governance artifact (such as the Directive defined in the previous section), and Y is the formal Lawful Basis category (e.g., Contract, Legal Obligation, Consent). This relationship is not merely associative but hierarchical; the validity of the internal directive is contingent upon the correct assignment of a valid lawful basis. The schema enforces this by linking the lawful basis entry back to the requirement specification defined in Section 4.2, creating a closed loop of verification.

The importance of this mapping cannot be overstated. Are marketing decisions more important than capital investment decisions? In the realm of data privacy, the decision to process data without a valid lawful basis carries severe regulatory penalties. Therefore, the explicit declaration of the lawful basis within the relational schema serves as a primary control point for compliance audits.

## 4.4 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

A directive, even when grounded in a lawful basis, remains a theoretical construct until it is operationalized through attestation. The axiom *{X} is something that with supporting evidence {Y}* defines the Attestation process. Here, X represents the specific act of attestation (a process occurring at a specific point in time), and Y represents the evidence or lawful basis record that supports the claim of compliance.

This distinction is vital for audit readiness. An attestation is a declaration that a process is being performed in accordance with a directive. However, under modern regulatory standards such as GDPR or ISO 27001, a bare declaration is insufficient. It must be "with supporting evidence." This evidence links the act of attestation back to the lawful basis established in Section 4.3. By enforcing a foreign key constraint to the lawful basis table, the ontology ensures that no attestation can exist without a valid legal foundation.

In practice, this means that when a Data Protection Officer (DPO) signs off on a data processing activity, the system records not just the signature, but the specific legal basis (e.g., "Contract Performance") that justifies the activity. This creates an immutable chain of custody: Directive -> Lawful Basis -> Evidence -> Attestation.

## 4.5 Audit Execution and Personnel

Governance frameworks rely on the verification of controls through audits. The axiom *{X} is something that conducted by person* establishes the agent responsible for the audit process. In this context, X is the Audit process instance, and the object property links this instance to a specific *cco:Person*.

This relationship addresses the "who" of governance. While the previous sections addressed the "what" (directives) and the "why" (lawful bases), this section addresses the accountability of the human agent. The ontology treats the Audit as a process (bfo:0000015) that is initiated and carried out by an agent. In the relational projection, the table captures the identity of the audit process. The association to the specific person is managed through the object property definition in the ontology, though the relational schema provided focuses on the process instance itself as the primary entity of interest.

The integrity of the audit function depends on the clear separation of duties and the explicit recording of the auditor. This ensures that findings can be challenged, validated, and attributed to a responsible governance actor.

## 4.6 Attestation Signatories

The final step in the governance chain is the formalization of approval. While an audit is a verification activity, an attestation often requires a formal sign-off to authorize a processing activity or validate a control state. The axiom *{X} is something that signed by person* captures this final act.

Here, X is the Attestation process. The act of signing transforms the attestation from a draft state into a binding governance record. This is conceptually distinct from the audit conducted by a person; the auditor discovers facts, whereas the signatory accepts liability for the state of compliance based on those facts. In many regulatory frameworks, the identity of the signatory is a matter of legal record; for example, under the US Sarbanes-Oxley Act or the EU GDPR, specific senior officers may be required to sign attestations regarding the effectiveness of internal controls.

The relational schema for this axiom tracks the attestation instances that have reached a signed state. It implies that the process has completed the workflow of evidence gathering and audit review and has now been formally authorized by a designated person.
