# Chapter 4: Directive Governance and Evidence Anchoring in Regulatory Ontologies

This chapter delineates the structural framework for integrating regulatory compliance directives within a formal ontology. It addresses the axiomatic relationships between governance processes, lawful bases for data processing, and the evidentiary requirements necessary for audit trails. The content is grounded in the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) and the Common Core Ontologies (CCO), providing a rigorous schema for mapping compliance obligations to verifiable data structures. The following sections detail the specific implementation of these concepts through relational schemata and controlled vocabularies, ensuring referential integrity across audit and attestation processes.

## 4.1 GDPR Lawful Basis

The concept of a "GDPR Lawful Basis" functions as a critical node in the compliance ontology, bridging the gap between abstract directives and specific regulatory permissions. Formally, the axiom asserts that a Class {X} is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` (Information Content Entity) and possesses a lawful basis {Y}. This relationship ensures that any directive or policy enacted by an organization is not merely a procedural artifact but is legally anchored to a justification recognized under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). In practical terms, this prevents the execution of data processing operations without a defined legal grounding, such as Contractual Necessity, Legal Obligation, or Consent.

The relational projection of this axiom creates a dependency structure where the lawful basis itself must reference a specific requirement specification. This circular dependency enforces a strict validation loop: a lawful basis is defined only in the context of a requirement, and a requirement is valid only if it cites a lawful basis.

## 4.2 Requirement Equivalence and Target Specification

To establish a comprehensive governance model, directives must be semantically equivalent to requirements that specify targets and scope. The axiom `requirement_equiv_specifies_target` posits that a Class {X} is equivalent to a `cco:DirectiveICE` that both specifies a requirement {Y} and applies to a target {Z}. This equivalence is fundamental for automated compliance reasoning; it allows the system to infer that if an entity is a Directive, it must necessarily possess a specification and an applicability scope.

This axiom resolves the ambiguity often found in policy documents where the "rule" and the "subject of the rule" are conflated. By separating the specification ({Y}) from the target ({Z}), the ontology supports granular querying—such as retrieving all requirements applicable to "Health Records" regardless of the specific directive containing them.

## 4.3 Audit Conducted By

The audit function represents the procedural verification of governance controls. According to the axiom `audit_conducted_by`, a Class {X} (representing an audit process) is a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process) and is conducted by a `cco:Person`. This axiom distinguishes the *event* of the audit from the *agent* performing it. In a compliance framework, this separation is vital for establishing chains of custody and responsibility. It ensures that every audit trail is associated with a responsible agent, satisfying non-repudiation requirements common in ISO 27001 and SOC 2 frameworks.

The schema for this concept is intentionally streamlined, focusing on the existence of the process and its identifier. The relationship to the person is implicit in the class definition but managed through the distinct `t_attestation_signed_by` structure in broader implementations, or via external personnel directories not depicted here. ## 4.4 Attestation Signed By

Attestation is the formal act of validating the accuracy or integrity of a state of affairs. The axiom `attestation_signed_by` defines a Class {X} as a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process) that is signed by a `cco:Person`. This concept differs from the audit in that it focuses on the *affirmation* of truthfulness rather than the *investigation* of it. In regulatory terms, attestation is the mechanism by which officers or responsible parties certify that controls are operating effectively.

This axiom is crucial for the "sign-off" phase of compliance cycles. It transforms a technical finding into a legally recognized statement of compliance. The relational structure captures the attestation process itself, which can then be linked to specific audit findings or policy reviews.

## 4.5 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

The final concept integrates the attestation process with the evidentiary backbone of the compliance framework. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` asserts that a Class {X} is a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process) and possesses supporting evidence {Y}. This axiom operationalizes the principle that "compliance claims must be evidence-based." An attestation is not merely a signature; it is a process that is substantiated by artifacts—logs, screenshots, configuration exports, or legal documents.

In this schema, the supporting evidence {Y} is explicitly linked to the lawful basis established in Section 4.1. This creates a traceable path: a Lawful Basis permits a Directive; a Directive generates a Requirement; an Audit verifies the Requirement; an Attestation confirms the Audit; and the Attestation is supported by Evidence linking back to the Lawful Basis. This closed loop provides the high assurance required for regulatory audits.
