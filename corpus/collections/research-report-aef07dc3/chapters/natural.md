## Financial Research and Sales Operations

The financial research ecosystem operates at the intersection of market analysis, client relationship management, and executive oversight. Organizations in this space produce research reports containing investment recommendations, track the performance of those recommendations against market benchmarks, and manage a portfolio of client accounts spanning family offices, institutional investors, retail clients, and hedge funds. Account managers serve as the primary liaison between the research team and clients, executing targeted sales campaigns while reporting back through structured channels to senior leadership. This chapter documents the entities, processes, and operational relationships that define this domain.

Research reports form the analytical foundation of the operation. Each report carries a unique identifier—such as `1202-0008-S` or `8189500`—and is published on a specific date, tied to a ticker symbol like `composite-ticker-63` or `adaptive-ticker-65`. The core of each report is its recommendation: BUY, SELL, or HOLD. These recommendations are evaluated against market performance, measured as `performance_vs_index`, a numeric value indicating how much the recommendation outperformed the benchmark. Report `5844272`, for instance, achieved a performance of `10.80` against the index. Reports are also flagged as `is_bold_call`, a boolean indicator reserved for particularly decisive or unconventional calls; report `8189500` and report `5844272` both carry this designation. Each report is associated with a specific client account, establishing the first link between research output and the client-facing organization.

Sales reports capture the feedback loop between field activity and organizational learning. Each report is identified by a `report_id` that corresponds to a research report, a `submission_date` marking when the report was filed, and an `observation_type` that categorizes the nature of the insight: Client Feedback, Strategic Recommendation, or Service Issue. Timestamps for `created_at` and `updated_at` provide an audit trail of report lifecycle management.

```sql
CREATE VIEW v_sales_report_executive AS
SELECT a.report_id, a.submission_date, a.observation_type, a.priority_level, b.id AS executive_id, b.executive_id AS executive_executive_id, b.executive_name AS executive_executive_name
FROM sales_reports a JOIN executives b ON a.executive_id = b.id;
```

This view connects sales reports to the executives who review them, answering: which executives are responsible for which field observations? The view provides an audit trail of executive engagement with field-level data.

The financial research and sales operations domain is defined by the continuous flow of information from research analysts through account managers to clients, and the parallel flow of field intelligence back through sales reports to executives. Research reports generate recommendations that are delivered to client accounts, managed by account executives who execute targeted sales campaigns. Sales reports capture the outcomes and observations from these interactions, feeding back into the organizational knowledge base. The junction tables and views ensure that every relationship—manager to account, manager to campaign, executive to manager, executive to report—is explicit, queryable, and auditable. This structure supports both operational execution and strategic oversight, enabling organizations to align their research output with client needs while maintaining clear lines of accountability across the management hierarchy.
