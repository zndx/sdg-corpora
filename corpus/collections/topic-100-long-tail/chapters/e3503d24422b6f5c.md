# Chapter 4: Integrated Governance Frameworks for PCI DSS and HIPAA Compliance

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the structural ontology governing the intersection of payment card industry standards (PCI DSS) and healthcare privacy regulations (HIPAA) within a unified enterprise architecture. In environments where Protected Health Information (PHI) and cardholder data (CHD) coexist, a fragmented approach to compliance introduces significant operational risk and audit liability. The framework presented herein establishes a formalized mapping between regulatory directives, specific requirement classes, and the evidentiary artifacts necessary for attestation. By treating compliance mandates as distinct classes of Information Content Entities (ICE) and linking them to process-based attestations, organizations can achieve a deterministic audit trail. This chapter defines the core axioms governing these relationships, specifically focusing on the derivation of PCI DSS requirements, the mechanism of attestation, and the tripartite structure of HIPAA safeguards (Administrative, Technical, and Physical).

## 4.2 PCI DSS Requirements as Directives

The foundational unit of the compliance ontology is the PCI DSS Requirement. In this model, a PCI DSS Requirement is defined as a subclass of a Directive Information Content Entity (`cco:DirectiveICE`). A directive, in this context, represents a prescriptive rule or obligation issued by a governing authority. The axiom asserts that any entity `X` classified as a PCI DSS Requirement must necessarily satisfy the condition that it mandates a specific PCI DSS requirement `Y`.

This relationship is not merely semantic; it establishes the primary key structure for the compliance database. The directive `X` (e.g., a corporate policy document) is the implementation vehicle, while `Y` represents the specific regulatory control objective (e.g., "Implement strong access control measures"). The ontology enforces a strict dependency: a directive cannot exist as a PCI DSS Requirement unless it is explicitly mapped to a valid requirement identifier.

*Table 4.1: Mapping of Directives to PCI DSS Requirements*
| id | x (Directive) | y (Requirement) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `PCI_REQ_001` | `NetworkSecurityPolicy_v4` | `Requirement_1_InstallFirewall` |
| `PCI_REQ_002` | `VendorConfigurationStandard` | `Requirement_2_DefaultPasswords` |
| `PCI_REQ_003` | `DataEncryptionProtocol` | `Requirement_4_EncryptTransmission` |
| `PCI_REQ_004` | `VulnerabilityManagementPlan` | `Requirement_6_SecureDevProcess` |
| `PCI_REQ_005` | `AccessControlProcedure` | `Requirement_7_RestrictAccess` |

In practice, this table serves as the registry where internal governance artifacts are cross-referenced against the external standard. For instance, the `NetworkSecurityPolicy_v4` entity is formally recognized as the fulfillment of `Requirement_1_InstallFirewall`. This mapping allows automated audit tools to trace a specific compliance gap directly to the responsible internal directive.

## 4.3 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

While directives define the *intent* of compliance, the Attestation with Supporting Evidence axiom defines the *mechanism* of verification. An attestation is modeled as a process (`bfo:0000015`), representing a temporal occurrence or activity undertaken by an auditor or compliance officer. The axiom posits that an attestation process `X` must include supporting evidence `Y`.

This structure creates a bidirectional link between the requirement and the proof of compliance. The relational schema dictates that the evidence (`y`) referenced in the attestation table must correspond to a valid PCI DSS Requirement ID. This circular dependency is intentional: it ensures that every piece of evidence is generated specifically to satisfy a requirement, and every requirement is ultimately closed out by a specific attestation event. The "supporting evidence" in this context is not merely a file; it is a reference to the requirement class that the evidence purports to satisfy.

*Table 4.2: Attestation Processes and Evidence Linkages*
| id | x (Attestation Process) | y (Supporting Evidence/Req) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `ATT_001` | `QuarterlyFirewallReview_Q3` | `PCI_REQ_001` |
| `ATT_002` | `AnnualVendorAudit_2023` | `PCI_REQ_002` |
| `ATT_003` | `TLS_Inspection_Log_Analysis` | `PCI_REQ_003` |
| `ATT_004` | `AppSec_CodeReview_Sprint4` | `PCI_REQ_004` |
| `ATT_005` | `UserAccessReview_October` | `PCI_REQ_005` |

For example, the attestation process `QuarterlyFirewallReview_Q3` acts as the procedural wrapper. The evidence generated during this process (configuration snapshots, rule set analyses) is formally linked to `PCI_REQ_001`. This structure ensures that evidence is not orphaned; it is intrinsically bound to the directive it validates.

## 4.4 HIPAA Administrative Safeguards

The framework extends to the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) through the classification of safeguards. The first dimension, Administrative Safeguards, refers to administrative actions, policies, and procedures to manage the selection, development, implementation, and maintenance of security measures. In the ontology, an Administrative Safeguard is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` that enforces a HIPAA safeguard `Y`.

The relational integrity requires that the safeguard `Y` be realized through an existing attestation process. This enforces the concept of "integrated compliance": the administrative activities undertaken to satisfy HIPAA (e.g., workforce training) must be documented and attested via the same rigorous evidence chain used for PCI DSS. The foreign key constraint linking the safeguard to the attestation table ensures that no administrative policy exists without a verified execution record.

*Table 4.3: Administrative Safeguards and Attestation*
| id | x (Admin Directive) | y (HIPAA Safeguard/Attestation) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `HIPAA_ADMIN_01` | `WorkforceSecurityPolicy` | `ATT_001` |
| `HIPAA_ADMIN_02` | `SanctionPolicy_Draft` | `ATT_005` |
| `HIPAA_ADMIN_03` | `InfoAccessManagementRule` | `ATT_002` |
| `HIPAA_ADMIN_04` | `SecurityAwarenessTraining` | `ATT_003` |
| `HIPAA_ADMIN_05` | `ContingencyPlan_Proc` | `ATT_004` |

Consider `HIPAA_ADMIN_01`, the `WorkforceSecurityPolicy`. This directive is linked to `ATT_001` (the `QuarterlyFirewallReview`). This linkage suggests a holistic security posture where the administrative oversight of workforce security is verified through the same technical review process used to validate PCI firewall rules. This consolidation reduces audit fatigue and ensures a single source of truth for security controls.

## 4.5 HIPAA Technical Safeguards

Technical Safeguards involve the technology and the policy and procedures for its use that protect electronic health information and control access to it. The axiom defines a Technical Safeguard as a directive `X` that implements a HIPAA safeguard `Y`. Unlike administrative controls, technical safeguards are typically automated or embedded within the system architecture (e.g., encryption, access logging).

The schema requires that these technical directives be validated by an attestation. By referencing the attestation table, the framework mandates that technical controls are not simply "deployed" but are actively monitored and proven effective. The attestation serves as the periodic validation of the technical control's efficacy.

*Table 4.4: Technical Safeguards and Attestation*
| id | x (Technical Directive) | y (HIPAA Safeguard/Attestation) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `HIPAA_TECH_01` | `AuditControlMechanism` | `ATT_001` |
| `HIPAA_TECH_02` | `IntegrityControl_Algo` | `ATT_003` |
| `HIPAA_TECH_03` | `TransmissionSecurity_TLS` | `ATT_003` |
| `HIPAA_TECH_04` | `AuthenticationProtocol_MFA` | `ATT_005` |
| `HIPAA_TECH_05` | `AutomaticLogoff_Config` | `ATT_004` |

In this table, `HIPAA_TECH_03` (`TransmissionSecurity_TLS`) is mapped to `ATT_003`. This indicates that the technical directive requiring TLS for data transmission is validated by the `TLS_Inspection_Log_Analysis` attestation process. This creates a direct line of sight from the technical configuration (`x`) to the operational verification (`y`), satisfying the auditor's need for demonstrable control over data-in-motion.

## 4.6 HIPAA Physical Safeguards

The final component of the triad is Physical Safeguards. These are physical measures to protect electronic information systems and related buildings and equipment from natural and environmental hazards, and unauthorized intrusion. The axiom classifies a Physical Safeguard as a directive `X` that enforces a HIPAA safeguard `Y`.

The data structure reinforces that physical security—often overlooked in digital compliance frameworks—must adhere to the same evidentiary standards as technical and administrative controls. Access to server rooms, workstation usage policies, and facility maintenance must be formally attested.

*Table 4.5: Physical Safeguards and Attestation*
| id | x (Physical Directive) | y (HIPAA Safeguard/Attestation) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `HIPAA_PHYS_01` | `FacilityAccessPlan` | `ATT_001` |
| `HIPAA_PHYS_02` | `WorkstationUsePolicy` | `ATT_005` |
| `HIPAA_PHYS_03` | `WorkstationSecurity_Locks` | `ATT_004` |
| `HIPAA_PHYS_04` | `DeviceMediaControls` | `ATT_002` |
| `HIPAA_PHYS_05` | `DisposalProcedures_HardDrives` | `ATT_003` |

For instance, `HIPAA_PHYS_01` (`FacilityAccessPlan`) is linked to `ATT_001`. This suggests that the review of physical access controls (badging, visitor logs) is conducted in tandem with the `QuarterlyFirewallReview`. This alignment allows security teams to review logical and physical access controls during a single audit cycle, optimizing resource allocation and ensuring a comprehensive security review.
