# Chapter 4: Governance Frameworks and Audit Verification Protocols

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures governing the relationship between audit processes, regulatory directives, and verification mechanisms within the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) framework. It provides a rigorous examination of the semantic constraints imposed on information entities, ensuring that compliance artifacts are traceable, verifiable, and agent-responsible. The following sections map specific ontology axioms to relational data structures, providing the necessary evidence anchors for high-assurance compliance reporting.

## 4.1 Audit Conducted By

**Ontology Axiom Reference:**
*   **Template ID:** `audit_conducted_by`
*   **Manchester Syntax:** Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:conductedBy some cco:Person
*   **Verbalization:** {X} is something that conducted by person
*   **Primary Key:** `id`
*   **Slot Types:** X: Class

The execution of an audit is formally modeled as a processual entity (bfo:0000015) that necessitates an agentive relationship with a Person (cco:Person). This relationship establishes the chain of custody and accountability for the audit lifecycle. In compliance governance, it is insufficient to merely record the occurrence of an audit; the system must axiomatically bind the process to a responsible agent. This binding ensures that every investigative or verification process is attributable to a specific human operator, thereby satisfying non-repudiation requirements inherent in audit trails.

The relational projection of this axiom creates a direct mapping between the audit instance and its classification. The table `t_audit_conducted_by` serves as the registry for these processes, where the entity `X` represents the specific audit class or instance being conducted.

**Table 4.1: Audit Process Registry**

| id | x |
| :--- | :--- |
| audit_2024_001 | InternalSecurityAudit_Q1 |
| audit_2024_002 | ExternalComplianceReview_FY24 |
| audit_2024_003 | NetworkVulnerabilityAssessment_May |

*Table 4.1 illustrates the instantiation of audit processes. Each row represents a distinct processual entity (`x`) identified by a unique primary key (`id`). The data indicates three specific audit types conducted within the fiscal period, establishing the baseline for agentive responsibility in the subsequent governance layers.*

## 4.2 PCI DSS Requirement

**Ontology Axiom Reference:**
*   **Template ID:** `pci_dss_requirement`
*   **Manchester Syntax:** Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:pciDssRequirement some {Y:Class}
*   **Verbalization:** {X} is something that pci dss requirement {Y}
*   **Primary Key:** `id`
*   **Foreign Key:** `y` REFERENCES `t_requirement_min_one_verification(id)`
*   **Slot Types:** X: Class, Y: Class

Directives within the PCI DSS framework are modeled as Information Content Entities (cco:DirectiveICE) that encapsulate specific regulatory requirements. The axiom `pci_dss_requirement` establishes a dependency between a governing directive ({X}) and a specific requirement class ({Y}). This relationship is critical for maintaining the integrity of the compliance matrix; it ensures that every directive is semantically linked to a verifiable requirement artifact.

The relational schema enforces a strict foreign key constraint, mandating that every requirement ({Y}) referenced by a directive ({X}) must possess a corresponding verification record. This structure prevents the existence of "orphan" requirements that cannot be validated against the governance framework. The following table demonstrates the mapping of directives to their requisite verification components.

**Table 4.2: PCI DSS Directive Mapping**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| dir_fw_001 | FirewallConfigurationPolicy | ver_fw_001 |
| dir_data_001 | DataEncryptionStandard | ver_enc_001 |
| dir_access_001 | AccessControlMechanism | ver_acc_001 |

*Table 4.2 encodes the directive-to-requirement mapping. Column `x` identifies the Directive Information Content Entity, while column `y` references the specific requirement class. The foreign key constraint ensures that `ver_fw_001`, `ver_enc_001`, and `ver_acc_001` are valid entries within the verification registry, thereby enforcing referential integrity across the compliance domain.*

## 4.3 Requirement Min One Verification

**Ontology Axiom Reference:**
*   **Template ID:** `requirement_min_one_verification`
*   **Manchester Syntax:** Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:verifiedBy min 1 {Y:Class}
*   **Verbalization:** {X} is a directive information content entity
*   **Primary Key:** `id`
*   **Foreign Key:** `y` REFERENCES `t_pci_dss_requirement(id)`
*   **Slot Types:** X: Class, Y: Class

To satisfy the principle of evidence-based compliance, every directive must be subject to at least one verification procedure. The axiom `requirement_min_one_verification` utilizes a minimum cardinality constraint (`min 1`) to enforce that a Directive Information Content Entity ({X}) is verified by at least one process or artifact ({Y}). This axiom acts as the validation engine of the ontology, preventing the formation of directives that are theoretically sound but operationally unverified.

Notably, the relational schema for this axiom introduces a circular dependency with the `t_pci_dss_requirement` table. This recursive structure mirrors the iterative nature of compliance auditing: a requirement defines a directive, and that directive, in turn, mandates the verification of the originating requirement. The data presented in Table 4.3 resolves this loop by referencing the identifiers established in the previous section.

**Table 4.3: Verification Mandates Registry**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ver_fw_001 | QuarterlyFirewallRuleReview | dir_fw_001 |
| ver_enc_001 | AnnualCryptographyAudit | dir_data_001 |
| ver_acc_001 | MonthlyUserAccessLogAnalysis | dir_access_001 |

*Table 4.3 details the verification procedures (`x`) mandated for each directive. The column `y` establishes the link back to the parent PCI DSS requirement ID. For instance, the `QuarterlyFirewallRuleReview` is the verification method (`x`) specifically required to satisfy the `FirewallConfigurationPolicy` (`y`), ensuring a closed loop of governance.*

## 4.4 Requirement Only Satisfied By

**Ontology Axiom Reference:**
*   **Template ID:** `requirement_only_satisfied_by`
*   **Manchester Syntax:** Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:satisfiedBy only {Y:Class}
*   **Verbalization:** {X} is something that satisfied by {Y}
*   **Primary Key:** `id`
*   **Foreign Key:** `y` REFERENCES `t_pci_dss_requirement(id)`
*   **Slot Types:** X: Class, Y: Class

The axiom `requirement_only_satisfied_by` introduces a universal restriction on the satisfaction conditions of a directive. Unlike the existential verification discussed in Section 4.3, this axiom asserts that a Directive Information Content Entity ({X}) is satisfied *only* by specific classes of artifacts or processes ({Y}). This creates a closed-world assumption regarding compliance satisfaction; if a method or artifact is not a member of class {Y}, it cannot validly claim to satisfy {X}.

This constraint is vital for maintaining the rigor of audit evidence. It precludes the use of unauthorized or substandard verification methods to satisfy regulatory mandates. For example, a requirement for "penetration testing" might be restricted to be satisfied *only* by a "qualified independent assessor," excluding internal self-assessments. The relational implementation enforces this by linking the satisfaction condition directly to the canonical requirement ID.

**Table 4.4: Exclusive Satisfaction Conditions**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| sat_cond_01 | IndependentQualifiedAssessor | dir_fw_001 |
| sat_cond_02 | FIPS140-2ValidatedModule | dir_data_001 |
| sat_cond_03 | MultiFactorAuthenticationToken | dir_access_001 |

*Table 4.4 defines the exclusive classes (`x`) permitted to satisfy the respective directives (`y`). The data dictates that the `FirewallConfigurationPolicy` can only be satisfied by an `IndependentQualifiedAssessor`, thereby enforcing strict qualification criteria on the compliance process.*

## 4.5 Attestation At Period

**Ontology Axiom Reference:**
*   **Template ID:** `attestation_at_period`
*   **Manchester Syntax:** Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:atAttestationPeriod some {Y:Class}
*   **Verbalization:** {X} is something that at attestation period {Y}
*   **Primary Key:** `id`
*   **Foreign Key:** `y` REFERENCES `t_pci_dss_requirement(id)`
*   **Slot Types:** X: Class, Y: Class

The final concept in this governance framework addresses the temporal dimension of compliance. The axiom `attestation_at_period` binds a processual entity ({X}), specifically an attestation, to a defined temporal interval ({Y}). Attestation is the formal declaration that the requirements have been met, and this axiom ensures that such declarations are temporally scoped.

In the context of PCI DSS, requirements are often recurring (e.g., quarterly reviews, annual scans). This ontology axiom captures the necessity of aligning the attestation process with the specific compliance period defined by the requirement. The foreign key constraint linking the period to the requirement ID ensures that attestations are not floating events but are anchored to the specific directive cycle they validate.

**Table 4.5: Attestation Period Mapping**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| att_2024_q1 | Q1_Attestation_Process | dir_fw_001 |
| att_2024_yr | FY24_Attestation_Process | dir_data_001 |
| att_2024_m05 | May_Attestation_Process | dir_access_001 |

*Table 4.5 aligns specific attestation processes (`x`) with their target directives (`y`). The `Q1_Attestation_Process` is explicitly linked to the `FirewallConfigurationPolicy`, reflecting the quarterly cadence inherent in that specific control requirement. This temporal binding ensures that compliance status is always evaluated within the correct reporting window.*
