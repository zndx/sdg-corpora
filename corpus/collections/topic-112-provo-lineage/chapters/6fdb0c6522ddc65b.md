# Chapter 7: Evidentiary Reasoning and Belief Structures in Governance Frameworks

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures required to model evidentiary reasoning within automated governance and compliance frameworks. It moves beyond simple binary assertions to describe complex belief structures, specifically focusing on the representation of mass functions over frames of discernment, the process of attestation with supporting evidence, and the logical status of claims regarding refutation or non-disconfirmation. The content is grounded in the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) and the Common Core Ontologies (CCO), providing a rigorous schema for representing uncertainty and verification in high-assurance environments.

## 1. Mass Function Subclass and Frames of Discernment

In the context of advanced audit methodologies and compliance verification, the representation of uncertainty is as critical as the representation of fact. The axiom `mass_function_subclass` provides the necessary structure to define a specific class of Information Content Entity (ICE) that operates as a belief function. Formally, this class is defined as a subclass of `cco:DescriptiveICE`, which serves as the informational artifact, and is characterized by the property `sdg:overFrameOfDiscernment`.

A "frame of discernment," in this formalization, refers to the set of mutually exclusive and exhaustive hypotheses or states of the world that are under consideration. The mass function (or basic probability assignment) does not assign probability to individual hypotheses directly, but rather to subsets of the frame, representing the degree of belief allocated to exactly that set of possibilities. In a governance setting, this allows for the modeling of partial evidence where a specific compliance state cannot be isolated, but a range of possible states can be weighted.

The relational projection of this axiom establishes a dependency where the frame of discernment (Slot Y) must be an instance of an attestation process. This recursive linkage implies that the belief structure is always defined relative to a specific verification activity. The following table illustrates instances of mass functions defined over various attestation contexts, demonstrating how belief distributions are anchored to specific procedural frames.

### 1.1 Application in Risk Assessment

The utility of the `mass_function_subclass` is most evident in scenarios requiring probabilistic risk assessment. For instance, consider `mfs_01` (SystemAccessRiskProfile). This entity does not merely assert that a risk exists; it encapsulates a distribution of belief regarding the nature of access violations—whether they stem from credential theft, insider threat, or misconfiguration. By mapping this mass function over `att_01` (a specific access control audit), the framework ensures that the belief structure is contextually grounded in the actual evidentiary process. This prevents the abstraction of risk from the audit trail, maintaining the integrity of the compliance chain.

## 2. Attestation with Supporting Evidence

The `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` axiom formalizes the process of verification as a distinct temporal and causal entity. Under the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO), this class is a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process), emphasizing that attestation is an activity occurring over time, rather than a static state. The defining characteristic of this process is the possession of `sdg:withSupportingEvidence`.

In professional technical documentation, an attestation is invalid without evidentiary backing. This axiom enforces that rigor by requiring a link to a supporting evidence class. The relational schema dictates that the supporting evidence (Slot Y) must be a valid mass function. This creates a rigorous evidentiary loop: an attestation process is supported by a belief structure (mass function), and that belief structure is defined over the frame of the attestation itself.

This circularity is not a logical flaw but a representation of the hermeneutic circle of audit: the interpretation of evidence (the mass function) defines the scope of the audit (the frame), and the audit process generates the evidence that supports the attestation. The table below presents specific attestation processes and the specific mass functions that serve as their evidentiary basis.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| att_01 | QuarterlyAccessControlAudit | mfs_01 |
| att_02 | EndOfYearFinancialReconciliation | mfs_02 |
| att_03 | DatabaseConsistencyVerification | mfs_03 |
| att_04 | ThirdPartySupplyChainReview | mfs_04 |
| att_05 | PenetrationTestingEngagement | mfs_05 |

*Table 2.1 maps the attestation processes (Column `x`) to their respective supporting evidence (Column `y`). The foreign key constraint ensures that only defined mass functions can serve as evidence, enforcing the ontology's requirement for quantified belief structures rather than unqualified assertions.*

### 2.1 Governance Implications

The strict typing of the supporting evidence slot as a mass function elevates the standard of compliance. It mandates that an attestation cannot be supported merely by anecdotal claims or unstructured data; it must be supported by a quantified distribution of belief. For example, `att_02` (EndOfYearFinancialReconciliation) is supported by `mfs_02` (FinancialDiscrepancyDistribution). This implies that the attestation of financial health is not a binary "pass/fail" but is substantiated by a detailed analysis of the probability and magnitude of discrepancies. This level of granularity is essential for high-stakes regulatory reporting where the confidence interval of the findings is as material as the findings themselves.

## 3. Claims Refuted by Evidence

A critical component of any governance framework is the ability to formally invalidate false or non-compliant assertions. The `claim_refuted_by_evidence` axiom captures this logic. It defines a class of Descriptive ICE (a claim or assertion) that is explicitly negated by the presence of specific evidence. The relationship `sdg:refutedByEvidence` establishes a causal link between the proposition and the data that invalidates it.

In the relational schema, the evidence responsible for the refutation (Slot Y) is identified as an instance of an attestation process. This aligns with the real-world logic that refutation is rarely performed by raw data alone; rather, it is the outcome of a structured process (the attestation) that analyzes data to produce a finding of refutation. For a claim to be formally refuted within the system, it must be linked to an attestation process that has conclusively demonstrated the claim's invalidity.

The following table catalogs claims that have been systematically refuted by specific governance processes. This serves as a negative registry, essential for maintaining the integrity of the knowledge base by explicitly tracking disproven hypotheses.

| id | x | y |

| ref_01 | AssertionOfFullAdminAccess | att_01 |
| ref_02 | ClaimOfBalancedLedgerQ1 | att_02 |
| ref_03 | StatementOfZeroDataLoss | att_03 |
| ref_04 | DeclarationOfVendorSoleSourcing | att_04 |

*Table 3.1 identifies specific claims (Column `x`) that have been refuted. The refutation is effected by the attestation process listed in Column `y`. The foreign key constraint ensures that refutation is only recorded when a valid, defined process has been executed to invalidate the claim.*

### 3.2 The Logic of Negation

The formalization of refutation allows for automated reasoning over the compliance status of an entity. If `ref_02` (ClaimOfBalancedLedgerQ1) is linked to `att_02` (EndOfYearFinancialReconciliation), and `att_02` has a status of "Complete" with findings of discrepancy, the system can automatically infer that the claim is false. This prevents the persistence of "zombie claims"—assertions that have been disproven but not formally removed from the record. In audit contexts, this capability is vital for remediation tracking, ensuring that refuted issues are not only noted but are actively driving corrective action workflows.

## 4. Claims Not Disconfirmed

While refutation deals with proving a claim false, the axiom `claim_not_disconfirmed` addresses the epistemic status of claims that have withstood scrutiny without being necessarily proven true. This class represents assertions that are `cco:DescriptiveICE` and are explicitly defined by the logical negation of another class (`not {Y:Class}`).

This distinction is vital in audit and compliance, which often operate under the principle of "absence of evidence is not evidence of absence." A claim that is "not disconfirmed" has survived the testing procedures; no evidence has been found to refute it, yet it may not meet the higher burden of proof required for positive validation. This state is often the default status for ongoing monitoring scenarios where full verification is resource-prohibitive.

The relational structure for this axiom is straightforward, consisting of the claim ID, the claim itself (X), and the class or property it is asserted not to be (Y). Unlike previous tables, there is no foreign key dependency in this specific schema fragment, allowing for the classification of claims based on their negative properties.

**Table 4.1: Claims Not Disconfirmed by Scrutiny**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| nd_01 | HypothesisOfExternalIntrusion | DisprovenInternalThreat |
| nd_02 | AssumptionOfDataEncryptionAtRest | CleartextStorageViolation |
| nd_03 | BeliefInRegulatoryAlignment | CriticalNonCompliance |
| nd_04 | ExpectationOfServiceAvailability | SystemOutageEvent |
| nd_05 | ProjectionOfBudgetAdherence | MaterialVariance |

*Table 4.1 lists claims (Column `x`) that have not been disconfirmed relative to a specific negative condition (Column `y`). For instance, `nd_01` represents the hypothesis that an intrusion was external; the fact that it is "not disconfirmed" means the investigation has ruled out (or failed to find evidence of) it being an `DisprovenInternalThreat`.*

### 4.1 Managing Uncertainty in Ongoing Governance

The `claim_not_disconfirmed` category provides a mechanism for managing residual risk. In the example of `nd_03` (BeliefInRegulatoryAlignment), the organization asserts that it is aligned with regulations. The system records this as "not disconfirmed" against the class `CriticalNonCompliance`. This does not guarantee perfect compliance, but it certifies that the entity has not been identified as critically non-compliant. For governance officers, this classification triggers a different monitoring regime than a "validated" claim or a "refuted" claim—typically involving periodic sampling rather than continuous deep-dive audits. It formalizes the concept of "clean bill of health" in the face of incomplete information.
