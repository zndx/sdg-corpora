# Chapter 4: Formal Ontology of Evidence and Belief Structures in Governance Frameworks

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures required to model evidentiary reasoning, belief assignment, and refutation within automated governance and compliance systems. The primary objective is to establish a rigorous, machine-readable framework for representing how information content entities (ICE) relate to frames of discernment and how processes of attestation interact with supporting evidence. The concepts presented herein are grounded in the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) and the Common Core Ontologies (CCO), providing a semantic backbone for audit trails, compliance reporting, and regulatory verification. By defining specific axioms for mass functions, attestation, and refutation, this chapter enables the precise mapping of qualitative governance activities into quantitative relational schemas.

## 4.2 Mass Functions over Frames of Discernment

### 4.2.1 Conceptual Definition

In the context of governance and uncertainty management, a "Frame of Discernment" represents the set of all mutually exclusive and exhaustive hypotheses relevant to a specific domain of inquiry. A Mass Function, formally derived from Dempster-Shafer theory within this ontology, is a Descriptive Information Content Entity (ICE) that assigns a degree of belief (basic probability assignment) to subsets of this frame.

The axiom **mass_function_subclass** posits that a specific class of belief structure (X) is a subclass of `cco:DescriptiveICE` and operates over a specific Frame of Discernment (Y). This relationship is critical for audit scenarios where conclusions must be drawn from incomplete or aggregated data. Unlike strict Bayesian probability, a mass function allows for the explicit allocation of belief to the union of hypotheses (uncertainty), rather than forcing distribution across atomic states. This distinction is vital in high-stakes compliance environments where the absence of evidence is not necessarily evidence of absence.

### 4.2.2 Relational Implementation

The relational projection of this axiom creates a dependency where the definition of a belief structure is inextricably linked to the existence of a frame of discernment. In the schema below, the primary key `id` uniquely identifies the mass function instance. The column `x` represents the specific belief structure class, while `y` acts as a foreign key referencing the attestation process that validates or defines the frame. This circular dependency implies that a frame of discernment is often substantiated or operationalized only through the act of attestation.

### 4.2.3 Application in Governance

Consider a regulatory audit of a financial institution's risk models. The auditor must evaluate the "OperationalRiskBelief" (mf_001). This belief structure does not exist in a vacuum; it is defined over a "Frame of Discernment" consisting of all possible operational loss events. The validity of this frame is contingent upon the "Attestation" process (att_001) which aggregates loss data and internal reports. By linking the mass function to the attestation, the ontology ensures that the belief model is traceable to its evidentiary roots.

## 4.3 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

### 4.3.1 Conceptual Definition

Attestation is the formal process of examining, verifying, and validating information against established criteria. Within the BFO hierarchy, this is modeled as a `bfo:Process`—an occurrent entity that unfolds in time. The axiom **attestation_with_supporting_evidence** asserts that any instance of an attestation process (X) must possess the object property `withSupportingEvidence` linking it to some evidentiary entity (Y).

This axiom serves as the bridge between procedural governance (the act of auditing) and informational artifacts (the data collected). It mandates that no attestation can be considered valid or complete within the system without a demonstrable connection to supporting evidence. This prevents "orphan" audit findings and ensures that every compliance judgment is anchored in verifiable data.

### 4.3.2 Relational Implementation

The schema for `t_attestation_with_supporting_evidence` enforces the directionality of the evidence relationship. The `x` column denotes the specific attestation process class (e.g., "QuarterlyAudit"), while `y` references the supporting evidence. Notably, the schema includes a foreign key constraint referencing the `t_mass_function_subclass` table. This structure reflects the complex reality where the supporting evidence for an attestation often consists of the mass functions or belief structures generated by prior analytical steps.

### 4.3.3 Audit Trail Implications

The reciprocal relationship between Tables 4.1 and 4.2 creates a robust audit trail. For instance, the `QuarterlyComplianceAudit` (att_001) utilizes `OperationalRiskBelief` (mf_001) as its supporting evidence. Simultaneously, `OperationalRiskBelief` is defined over the frame of discernment substantiated by that very audit. This circularity is not a logical error but a representation of the iterative nature of assurance: evidence informs the process, and the process refines the evidence. In a regulatory submission, this structure allows an examiner to drill down from a high-level attestation to the specific mathematical belief structures underpinning it.

## 4.4 Claims Refuted by Evidence

### 4.4.1 Conceptual Definition

A central tenet of scientific and regulatory rigor is falsifiability. The axiom **claim_refuted_by_evidence** models the specific state where a Descriptive ICE (a claim or assertion X) is demonstrated to be false by a specific body of evidence (Y). This is distinct from a claim being merely unsupported; a refuted claim is actively disproven by the available data.

In the ontology, this relationship is critical for maintaining the integrity of the knowledge base. As new evidence enters the system via attestation processes, existing claims must be re-evaluated. If a claim contradicts the new evidence, it is tagged as `refutedByEvidence`. This mechanism supports the dynamic updating of compliance statuses, ensuring that governance frameworks adapt to new findings rather than retaining obsolete or incorrect assertions.

### 4.4.2 Relational Implementation

The table `t_claim_refuted_by_evidence` tracks these negative validations. The `x` column contains the identifier of the claim being refuted, while `y` contains the identifier of the refuting evidence. The foreign key constraint ensures that the refuting evidence (`y`) must be a valid attestation record. This enforces the rule that refutation is not a subjective opinion but a formal outcome of a defined attestation process.

*This table embodies the axiom `claim_refuted_by_evidence`. It lists assertions (X) that have been actively disproven by specific attestation outcomes (Y). This structure is essential for exception reporting and remediation tracking in governance systems.*

### 4.4.4 Governance Workflow

When an automated system identifies a row in Table 4.3, it triggers a remediation workflow. For example, if `AssertionOfZeroRisk` (ref_001) is refuted by the `QuarterlyComplianceAudit` (att_001), the system must automatically update the risk register, notify responsible officers, and potentially adjust the mass functions used for capital calculation. The explicit recording of refutation prevents the "zombie" persistence of disproven claims within the corporate memory.

## 4.5 Claims Not Disconfirmed

### 4.5.1 Conceptual Definition

The axiom **claim_not_disconfirmed** addresses the logical complement of refutation. It states that a specific Descriptive ICE (X) is *not* a member of the class of disconfirmed claims (Y). In formal logic, this is a negation, but in governance practice, it represents the "survivorship" of a claim through the scrutiny of the audit process.

This concept is distinct from positive confirmation. A claim that is "not disconfirmed" has withstood the current battery of tests and evidence evaluations without being proven false. However, it retains the status of a hypothesis that remains open to future testing. This category is essential for managing "provisional approvals" or "compliance with caveats," where an organization proceeds with an activity because no evidence has been found to prohibit it, even if full positive proof of safety is unavailable.

### 4.5.2 Relational Implementation

The table `t_claim_not_disconfirmed` serves as a registry of active, surviving claims. The `x` column identifies the claim, and the `y` column identifies the class of disconfirmation from which it is exempt. Unlike previous tables, this schema does not impose a foreign key constraint on `y`, treating it as a class reference rather than an instance link. This allows the system to categorize claims broadly as "not belonging to the set of refuted claims."

### 4.5.3 Operational Significance

In a dynamic compliance environment, the "not disconfirmed" status is the operational baseline for most business activities. For instance, a `ModelValidationPass` (nd_001) indicates that the risk model has not been classified as a `DisconfirmedModel`. This status permits the continued use of the model for regulatory reporting. The ontology allows this status to be fluid; should new evidence emerge that populates `t_claim_refuted_by_evidence`, the corresponding entry here would be removed or updated, triggering an immediate operational halt.
