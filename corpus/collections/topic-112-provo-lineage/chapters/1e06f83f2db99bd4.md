# Chapter 5: Formal Ontology of Evidentiary Reasoning in Governance Systems

## 5.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures required to model evidentiary reasoning, belief revision, and compliance verification within high-assurance governance frameworks. The primary objective is to establish a rigorous schema for representing how descriptive claims are substantiated, refuted, or maintained through processes of attestation. By mapping these concepts to a deterministic relational schema, governance bodies can ensure that audit trails are not only linear records but semantically rich structures capable of supporting automated reasoning. The following sections detail four core axioms—Mass Function Subclass, Attestation with Supporting Evidence, Claim Refuted by Evidence, and Claim Not Disconfirmed—providing formal definitions, relational mappings, and tabular instances consistent with the underlying ontology.

## 5.2 Mass Functions over Frames of Discernment

### 5.2.1 Conceptual Definition

In the context of information assurance and audit, a **Mass Function** represents a mathematical and logical construct used to assign degrees of belief to subsets of a hypothesis space. Formally, within the ontology, a Mass Function is defined as a subclass of `cco:DescriptiveICE` (Information Content Entity). It is characterized by the property `sdg:overFrameOfDiscernment`, which links the belief structure to a specific `Frame of Discernment`. The Frame of Discernment acts as the universal set of mutually exclusive hypotheses under consideration, effectively defining the boundary conditions for the audit or investigation.

This relationship is critical for governance frameworks because it restricts the scope of any belief assessment to a defined, approved set of possibilities. Without a rigorously defined Frame of Discernment, evidentiary reasoning risks becoming incoherent or susceptible to scope creep. The axiom asserts that for any Mass Function $X$, there must exist a Frame $Y$ such that the belief distribution is explicitly bounded by $Y$.

### 5.2.2 Relational Implementation

The relational projection of this axiom creates a dependency between the belief structure (the Mass Function) and the process or entity defining the scope (the Frame). In the schema below, the primary key `id` uniquely identifies the Mass Function instance. The column `x` represents the specific class or instance of the Mass Function, while `y` represents the Frame of Discernment.

Notably, the schema enforces a foreign key constraint where `y` must reference an identifier in the `t_attestation_with_supporting_evidence` table. This circular dependency implies a closed-loop verification system: the Frame of Discernment is not merely an abstract set but is grounded in a specific attestation process that defines or validates the scope of the investigation.

| id | x (Mass Function) | y (Frame of Discernment) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `mf_001` | `RevenueRecognitionBelief` | `att_001` |
| `mf_002` | `AccessControlCompliance` | `att_002` |
| `mf_003` | `DataSovereigntyAssessment` | `att_003` |

*Table 5.1 illustrates the instantiation of Mass Functions. For instance, `RevenueRecognitionBelief` (mf_001) operates over the Frame of Discernment defined by the attestation process `att_001`. This linkage ensures that the belief distribution regarding revenue is strictly constrained by the evidence gathered during that specific attestation.*

## 5.3 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

### 5.3.1 Conceptual Definition

**Attestation** is modeled as a `bfo:Process` (a processual entity that unfolds in time) that involves the verification of claims or states of affairs. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` posits that any valid attestation process $X$ must possess the property `sdg:withSupportingEvidence`, linking it to a supporting evidence entity $Y$. In governance terms, an attestation is legally and procedurally invalid if it lacks a basis in supporting artifacts, logs, or data points.

This concept serves as the operational backbone of auditability. It moves beyond the static existence of a document (an Information Content Entity) to the dynamic act of verification. The "supporting evidence" acts as the ground truth upon which the attestation process relies. This structure allows auditors to trace the lineage of a compliance statement back to the raw data or observations that precipitated it.

### 5.3.2 Relational Implementation

The relational schema for attestation enforces that every process instance is anchored by evidence. The table `t_attestation_with_supporting_evidence` captures this relationship. The column `x` denotes the specific attestation process class or instance, while `y` denotes the evidence supporting it.

The schema defines a recursive foreign key constraint: the evidence column `y` references the `id` of a Mass Function (`t_mass_function_subclass`). This reflects the ontological assertion that the "evidence" for an attestation process is often a structured belief distribution (a Mass Function) derived from prior analysis. For example, the process of attesting to system security may be supported by a belief distribution regarding vulnerability prevalence.

**Table 5.2: Attestation with Supporting Evidence Instances**

| id | x (Attestation Process) | y (Supporting Evidence) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `att_001` | `Q3FinancialAudit` | `mf_001` |
| `att_002` | `IdentityManagementReview` | `mf_002` |
| `att_003` | `GDPRComplianceCheck` | `mf_003` |
| `att_004` | `VendorRiskAssessment` | `mf_004` |

*Table 5.2 demonstrates the grounding of attestation processes. The `Q3FinancialAudit` (att_001) utilizes `mf_001` (the Revenue Recognition Belief) as its supporting evidence. This formalizes the audit logic: the process of verification relies on the aggregated belief structure derived from the financial data.*

## 5.4 Claims Refuted by Evidence

### 5.4.1 Conceptual Definition

Governance frameworks must explicitly handle the negation of compliance or validity. The axiom `claim_refuted_by_evidence` defines a specific class of `cco:DescriptiveICE` (a claim or assertion) that is actively negated by evidence. The property `sdg:refutedByEvidence` links the claim $X$ to the evidence $Y$ that demonstrates its falsity.

This distinction is vital for maintaining the integrity of the knowledge base. A claim that is merely "not supported" differs fundamentally from one that is "refuted." A refuted claim is actively disproven, implying that the governing body must take corrective action or update the record to reflect the invalidation of the claim. This axiom supports the generation of audit findings and non-compliance reports.

### 5.4.2 Relational Implementation

The table `t_claim_refuted_by_evidence` tracks these invalidations. The column `x` represents the refuted claim, while `y` represents the evidence responsible for the refutation. The foreign key constraint requires that `y` reference an existing attestation process (`t_attestation_with_supporting_evidence`). This constraint ensures that refutations are not arbitrary; they must be the result of a formal attestation process. One cannot refute a claim without a valid verification procedure producing the contradictory evidence.

**Table 5.3: Claims Refuted by Evidence Instances**

| id | x (Refuted Claim) | y (Refuting Evidence/Attestation) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `ref_001` | `ClaimOfCompleteAssetLogging` | `att_001` |
| `ref_002` | `AssertionOfZeroCriticalVulns` | `att_002` |
| `ref_003` | `DeclarationOfFullDataResidency` | `att_003` |
| `ref_004` | `StatementOfContinuousAvailability` | `att_004` |

*In Table 5.3, the `ClaimOfCompleteAssetLogging` (ref_001) is shown to be refuted by the evidence generated during the `Q3FinancialAudit` (att_001). This entry serves as a definitive record of non-compliance, anchoring the negative finding to the specific audit process that discovered it.*

## 5.5 Claims Not Disconfirmed

### 5.5.1 Conceptual Definition

The final axiom, `claim_not_disconfirmed`, addresses the persistence of claims in the absence of contradictory evidence. Formally, it asserts that a claim $X$ is a `cco:DescriptiveICE` that is *not* equivalent to class $Y$. In natural language, this is expressed as "$X$ is not $Y$," or more specifically in this context, $X$ has not been disproven by $Y$.

This concept captures the "innocent until proven guilty" logic of operational governance. Many assertions in a technical environment remain valid simply because no evidence has emerged to falsify them. These are distinct from "proven" claims; they are "tolerated" or "accepted" claims. This category is essential for managing the backlog of assumptions and baseline configurations that have not yet been audited but are currently treated as valid.

### 5.5.2 Relational Implementation

The table `t_claim_not_disconfirmed` is structurally simpler, lacking the foreign key constraints present in the previous schemas. It consists of an identifier `id`, the claim `x`, and the class or condition `y` that the claim has avoided or is not associated with. This structure allows for the recording of claims that stand in opposition to specific failure modes or negative hypotheses without requiring a complex evidentiary linkage at the database level.

**Table 5.4: Claims Not Disconfirmed Instances**

| id | x (Active Claim) | y (Negative Hypothesis / Disconfirmation) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `nd_001` | `HypothesisOfNetworkStability` | `NetworkOutageEvent` |
| `nd_002` | `AssumptionOfStaffCompetence` | `EvidenceOfNegligence` |
| `nd_003` | `BeliefInEncryptionStrength` | `KnownDecryptionVulnerability` |
| `nd_004` | `ProjectionOfBudgetSufficiency` | `BudgetOverrunScenario` |

*Table 5.4 lists claims that remain active because they have not been disconfirmed. For example, the `HypothesisOfNetworkStability` (nd_001) persists because no `NetworkOutageEvent` (y) has been recorded. This table effectively tracks the "negative space" of the audit—what has been checked and found not to be wrong.*
