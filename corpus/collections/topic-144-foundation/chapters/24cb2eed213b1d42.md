# Governance Frameworks for Information Security: ISO 27001 Implementation and Audit Protocols

This chapter delineates the structural components necessary for establishing a rigorous information security governance framework, specifically aligned with the ISO/IEC 27001 standard. It provides a granular analysis of the ontological relationships between audit processes, directive governance, and compliance requirements. The objective is to define the precise entities and their interdependencies—ranging from the designation of auditors to the temporal validity of attestations—thereby ensuring that internal control environments are both auditable and evidence-based.

## 1. Audit Conducted By

The foundational unit of any governance framework is the audit event itself, formally defined as a process occurring within a specific temporal and organizational context. In accordance with the axiom `audit_conducted_by`, an audit is not merely an abstract observation but a realized process (`bfo:0000015`) that necessitates agency. This agency is provided by a `cco:Person`, typically an internal auditor or an external consultant, who assumes the responsibility for executing the audit plan. The distinction between the process and the agent is critical; the process exists as the audit engagement, while the person provides the authority and execution capability.

In formal governance systems, the integrity of the audit relies on the explicit linkage between the event and the conductor. This linkage prevents ambiguity regarding accountability and ensures that the audit trail is anchored to a verified identity within the organizational hierarchy. The following table illustrates instances where specific audit processes are formally linked to the individuals responsible for their execution.

### 1.1 Role of the Auditor
The auditor, represented in this ontology as a `cco:Person`, must possess the requisite competence and authority to evaluate the subject matter. The governance framework must validate that the individual assigned to `x` is credentialed appropriately. For instance, the `InternalPenetrationTestQ3` requires a conductor with technical cybersecurity certifications, whereas the `HRComplianceReview` requires expertise in labor law and personnel policy. The ontology does not merely record a name; it enforces a structural dependency where the process cannot exist without a valid agent.

## 2. ISO 27001 Annex A Subclass

To achieve compliance with the ISO/IEC 27001 standard, organizations must map their internal directives to the formal controls outlined in Annex A. The axiom `iso27001_annexa_subclass` establishes that a specific internal directive (`cco:DirectiveICE`) is a subclass of a broader governance category and must explicitly reference an Annex A control (`{Y:Class}`). This relationship is the mechanism by which abstract corporate policy is grounded in internationally recognized best practices.

This mapping is not merely cosmetic; it serves as the bridge between the organization's operational reality and the auditor's checklist. When a directive is defined as a subclass of an Annex A control, it inherits the compliance obligations of that control. For example, an internal "Access Control Policy" must be mapped to "Annex A.9 Access Control" to be considered valid within the scope of an Information Security Management System (ISMS). The relational structure below demonstrates how internal directives are rigorously tied to specific requirement identifiers.

### 2.1 Mapping Granularity
The granularity of the mapping defined in Table 2 is essential for audit readiness. A single internal directive (`x`) may address multiple compliance obligations, but in this schema, it is formally linked to a specific requirement identifier (`y`). This allows the governance framework to trace a finding in an audit report back to a specific paragraph in the internal policy and subsequently to a specific clause in ISO 27001. The foreign key constraint ensures that a directive cannot claim compliance with a requirement that has not been formally defined and specified within the system.

## 3. Requirement Equivalent Specifies Target

The axiom `requirement_equiv_specifies_target` provides the most granular definition of a compliance obligation. It posits that a Requirement (`{X:Class}`) is equivalent to a Directive (`cco:DirectiveICE`) that *specifies* a control mechanism (`{Y:Class}`) and *applies to* a specific target asset or domain (`{Z:Class}`). This tripartite structure—Directive, Specification, and Target—forms the atomic unit of governance.

In practice, this means that a requirement is never vague; it is always a directive that mandates a specific control (the "specifies" relationship) for a specific object (the "applies to" relationship). For instance, a requirement is not simply "encrypt data"; it is "The Data Encryption Directive (X) specifies AES-256 encryption (Y) and applies to the Customer Relationship Management Database (Z)." This level of precision eliminates ambiguity during compliance audits.

### 3.1 The Target Domain
The inclusion of the target (`z`) is what transforms a policy into an operational control. Without the target, the directive is merely a statement of intent. By anchoring the requirement to a specific target—such as `ActiveDirectory` or `FileServers`—the organization assigns ownership and accountability. The foreign key relationship to `t_iso27001_annexa_subclass` via column `y` ensures that the specification being applied is itself a valid, ISO-mapped directive. This recursive validation ensures that the requirement chain is unbroken from the international standard down to the specific IT asset.

## 4. Attestation with Supporting Evidence

A requirement, no matter how well-defined, is ineffective without verification. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` governs the verification process. It defines an Attestation (`{X:Class}`) as a process (`bfo:0000015`) that must include `sdg:withSupportingEvidence` (`{Y:Class}`). This formalizes the principle that compliance claims must be evidence-based. The evidence acts as the proof point that the directive specified in the requirement has been executed.

In the context of an audit, the attestation is the formal assertion (e.g., a manager's sign-off or an automated scan result) that a control is functioning. The supporting evidence is the artifact that substantiates this assertion, such as a screenshot, a log file, or a configuration dump. The ontology mandates that the attestation process is structurally dependent on this evidence; an attestation cannot exist in the system without a linked evidence object.

### 4.2 Evidence Integrity
The integrity of the compliance framework relies on the immutability of the link between the attestation (`x`) and the evidence (`y`). By referencing the `t_iso27001_annexa_subclass` table, the system ensures that the evidence provided is relevant to the specific control in question. For example, the `VendorRiskAssessment` attestation is linked to the `SupplierSecurityProtocol` directive. This prevents the submission of generic or irrelevant evidence (such as a financial report) to satisfy a technical security requirement. The structure enforces a strict evidentiary standard.

## 5. Attestation at Period

Compliance is not a static state but a dynamic condition that varies over time. The axiom `attestation_at_period` introduces the temporal dimension to the attestation process. It defines an Attestation (`{X:Class}`) as a process that occurs `sdg:atAttestationPeriod` (`{Y:Class}`). This axiom is critical for defining the validity window of a compliance assertion and for scheduling recurring audits.

In governance terms, this allows the organization to define "compliance periods" (e.g., Q3 2023, FY2024, or "Retrospective Review"). An attestation is only valid within the context of its defined period. This temporal indexing is essential for trend analysis, for detecting lapses in compliance, and for meeting the continuous monitoring requirements of modern security frameworks. It ensures that the evidence presented is current and applicable to the timeframe under review.
