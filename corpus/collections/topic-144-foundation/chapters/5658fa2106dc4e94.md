# Chapter 4: Governance Frameworks and ISO/IEC 27001 Attestation Protocols

This chapter delineates the structural components of a robust governance framework, specifically focusing on the integration of ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A controls within organizational directives and the subsequent audit lifecycle. It establishes the ontological relationships between audit processes, control objectives, and evidence collection, providing a standardized schema for compliance reporting. The content herein is designed to assist compliance officers, internal auditors, and governance bodies in the precise articulation of security postures and the verification of control effectiveness through formal attestation.

## 4.1 Audit Conducted By

The foundational unit of any governance assessment is the audit process itself, formally defined as a `bfo:Process` entity. In accordance with the axiom `audit_conducted_by`, an audit is not an abstract event but a specific activity mandated to be conducted by a responsible agent, identified as a `cco:Person`. This relationship ensures accountability and traceability within the governance hierarchy. The auditor acts as the primary node of execution, responsible for initiating, planning, and reporting on the audit process. This distinction is critical for separating the procedural timeline of the audit from the individuals or systems subject to its scope.

In practice, this means that every audit instance recorded in the governance ledger must be explicitly linked to a qualified individual. This linkage prevents orphaned audit records and ensures that authority is delegated appropriately. For instance, a "Quarterly Access Control Review" cannot exist as a process instance without a designated auditor, such as a Senior Information Security Officer, assigned to execute it.

## 4.2 ISO 27001 Annex A Subclass

To align organizational directives with international standards, governance frameworks must explicitly map internal policies to the ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A controls. The axiom `iso27001_annexa_subclass` posits that a specific organizational directive (`cco:DirectiveICE`) is a subclass of a broader control category defined in the Annex A. This relationship is expressed as `{X} is something that iso27001 annex a {Y}`, where X represents the internal directive and Y represents the formal Annex A control class.

This mapping serves as the bridge between abstract compliance requirements and tangible corporate policy. For example, an internal "Password Management Policy" is not merely a standalone document; it is a manifestation of the Annex A control "A.9.4 - Password management." By formalizing this relationship in the ontology, the organization establishes a clear line of sight from regulatory text to operational implementation. It ensures that every internal directive has a valid legal or regulatory pedigree, thereby strengthening the organization's defense during regulatory inquiries or external certification audits.
