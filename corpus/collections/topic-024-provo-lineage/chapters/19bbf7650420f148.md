# Chapter 4: Ontological Mapping of Asset Valuation and Descriptive Claims in Compliance Frameworks

This chapter establishes the formal ontological framework required to map physical artifacts to their financial representations and subsequent compliance claims within a regulated digital environment. We define the structural relationships between tangible assets, their pricing mechanisms, and the descriptive information content entities (ICEs) used to record observations and anomalies. The following sections detail the specific axioms governing these relationships, supported by relational data schemas that enforce referential integrity and auditability. It is imperative that practitioners adhere to these definitions to ensure consistency across reporting mechanisms and to facilitate automated validation of compliance data.

## 1. Descriptive Information Content Entities

The foundational unit of information within our compliance architecture is the Descriptive Information Content Entity (DescriptiveICE). As defined by the upper-level ontology, a DescriptiveICE is not the artifact itself but rather the information bearer that describes, denotes, or signifies a state of affairs. In the context of financial auditing and governance, these entities serve as the immutable records of observations, assertions, or basic data points that exist independently of the observer.

Formally, we adopt the following axiom:

**Axiom 2 (outlier_claim_basic):**
*   **Manchester Syntax:** `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DescriptiveICE`
*   **Verbalization:** {X} is a descriptive information content entity.

This axiom posits that any entity classified as a basic claim or observation is fundamentally a DescriptiveICE. In practice, this allows the system to treat valuation entries, log entries, and basic assertions as members of the same information superclass. This is critical for data lineage; we must be able to trace the origin of a financial figure back to a specific, atomic descriptive entity.

To illustrate the population of this concept, consider the following data set. These entries represent atomic valuation records—specific descriptive entities that capture the magnitude of a price point without yet associating it with a specific physical product. The primary key (`id`) ensures that each descriptive entity is unique and addressable within the system.

The relational integrity of the audit trail depends on the existence of these records prior to the assignment of prices to artifacts.

## 2. Artifacts and Price Specification

Having established the informational substrate, we must now define the relationship between physical artifacts and their economic attributes. An artifact, in this ontology, refers to a distinct, persisting object that may be the subject of a transaction or inventory control. The assignment of a price to an artifact is not merely a data property but a defined object property linking the artifact to a specific DescriptiveICE (the price).

The governing axiom for this relationship is:

**Axiom 1 (schemaorg_product_price):**
*   **Manchester Syntax:** `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:Artifact, sdg:hasPrice some {Y:Class}`
*   **Verbalization:** {X} is something that has price {Y}

This axiom asserts that for an entity to be considered a priced product in this system, it must be a subclass of `cco:Artifact` and must possess a `sdg:hasPrice` relationship to at least one instance of a price class. The structure is deterministic: the Artifact (`X`) is the domain, and the Price (`Y`) is the range. Crucially, the relational schema enforces that the Price (`Y`) must correspond to an existing DescriptiveICE, as defined in the previous section.

The following table illustrates this mapping. It lists specific control units (Artifacts) and links them to the valuation entries (Prices) defined in Table 1.1. This creates a rigid chain of custody for financial data: the Artifact points to the Price, and the Price points to the underlying DescriptiveICE.

The use of a foreign key constraint (`y`) ensures that a price cannot be assigned to an artifact unless that price has been formally defined as a descriptive information content entity, thereby preventing orphaned or unverified financial data.

## 3. Complex Claims and Attribute Sets

The final layer of this ontological framework addresses the complexity of compliance reporting. Often, an observation or claim regarding an artifact is not atomic but relies on a set of attributes or context. For instance, an "outlier claim"—a flag indicating a deviation from expected norms—does not exist in a vacuum; it is defined by the attributes of the product it investigates.

We define this more complex structure using the following axiom:

**Axiom 3 (outlier_claim_min_one_attribute):**
*   **Manchester Syntax:** `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DescriptiveICE, sdg:hasAttributeSet min 1 {Y:Class}`
*   **Verbalization:** {X} is a descriptive information content entity that has at least one attribute set {Y}

Here, {X} represents the complex claim itself (which is also a DescriptiveICE), and {Y} represents the attribute set. The constraint `min 1` indicates that a valid claim must be associated with at least one attribute set. In our relational model, the attribute set is instantiated by referencing the Product/Price records. This effectively means that a claim about an artifact "has as an attribute" the definition and price of that artifact.

This recursive dependency—where a Claim references an Attribute Set, which is a Product/Price record, which in turn references a basic Price entity—creates a dense, highly interconnected graph suitable for deep forensic analysis.

This structure satisfies the requirement that a descriptive claim (the anomaly) must possess a minimum of one attribute set (the product context) to be valid within the system.

The integration of these three axioms provides a robust mechanism for governance. It separates the definition of the value (DescriptiveICE) from the object of value (Artifact) and layers the analytical observation (Outlier Claim) on top, ensuring that every assertion in the system is evidence-anchored and referentially intact.
