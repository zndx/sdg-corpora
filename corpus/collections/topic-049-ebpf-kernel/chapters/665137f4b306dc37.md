# Chapter 7: Audit Evidence and Anomaly Detection in Information Systems

## 7.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the structural requirements for identifying, validating, and documenting outlier claims within automated audit frameworks. It establishes the ontological relationships between data artifacts, audit processes, and the descriptive information content entities that constitute evidence. The principles herein are designed to ensure traceability and evidentiary support for all anomaly detection outputs, aligning with the rigorous standards of digital forensic analysis and regulatory compliance. By defining the precise constraints governing datasets under audit and the traces that support descriptive claims, this framework provides a foundation for high-assurance reporting in complex information environments.

## 7.2 Dataset Under Audit

### 7.2.1 Conceptual Framework

In the context of information governance, an artifact is defined as a discrete, persistent information bearer that is subject to examination. The axiom `dataset_under_audit` formalizes the relationship between such an artifact and the process to which it is subjected. Specifically, the axiom posits that a dataset (Class X) is a subclass of `cco:Artifact` and is involved in a realization relationship with an audit process (`bfo:0000015`). This relationship, denoted as `sdg:underAudit`, signifies that the artifact is not merely static data but is the object of a temporal, investigative process.

The implication of this axiom is that any dataset designated for audit must be explicitly linked to an active or historical audit process. This linkage ensures that the scope of the audit is bounded and that the artifacts are recognized as legal or functional entities capable of bearing evidentiary weight. Without this explicit classification, data repositories may exist outside the governance framework, rendering them inadmissible or irrelevant for compliance reporting.

### 7.2.2 Relational Implementation

The implementation of this concept within a relational database schema requires a dedicated table to track the artifacts currently or historically under audit. The table `t_dataset_under_audit` serves this purpose. Its primary key is the unique identifier of the dataset instance, while the column `x` captures the classification or specific name of the artifact class being audited.

The following table illustrates instances of artifacts that have been subjected to audit processes pursuant to the defined axiom. The data represents a snapshot of the audit inventory, reflecting the "Amounts are expressed in..." style of rigorous reporting where the entity definitions are as critical as the data itself.

### 7.2.3 Audit Process Constraints

The audit process (`bfo:0000015`) acts as the independent variable in this relationship. It is the process that brings the artifact into the regulatory scope. The axiom ensures that for a dataset to be considered "under audit," it must be the object of a process that has a defined start and, implicitly, an end state or continuation. This prevents the ambiguity of "perpetual audit" states without active process engagement. In practice, this means that the audit management system must maintain a valid process reference for every entry in `t_dataset_under_audit`.

## 7.3 Trace Supports Claim

### 7.3.1 Evidentiary Foundations

The validity of any audit finding relies on the existence of a trace—a process-generated artifact that serves as evidence. The axiom `trace_supports_claim` defines a specific class of processes (Class X) that are subclasses of `bfo:0000015` (Process) and possess the object property `sdg:supportsClaim`. This property asserts that the process output acts as a foundation for a `cco:DescriptiveICE` (Descriptive Information Content Entity).

In verbal terms, this axiom captures the essence of evidentiary support: a process (such as a log generation event, a calculation step, or a data transformation) is the mechanism by which a claim is substantiated. The "trace" is the manifestation of this process. It is not merely data; it is data that is explicitly generated or utilized to support a proposition. This distinction is vital in audit trails, where the provenance of a claim is as important as the claim itself.

### 7.3.2 Trace Identification and Classification

To operationalize this axiom, the system must maintain a registry of traces that are explicitly designated as claim supporters. The table `t_trace_supports_claim` provides this registry. Each row represents a specific instance of a process (the trace) that is sanctioned to support a descriptive claim.

The structure of this table is deceptively simple, containing only the identifier and the class of the process. However, the semantic weight is significant. By populating this table, the organization asserts that these specific process instances have been vetted and validated as reliable sources of evidence. This aligns with the "Standard Precautions" approach referenced in clinical governance, where specific protocols are established to handle all potential carriers of risk—in this case, all potential carriers of information must be traceable to a valid process.

*Note: The 'x' column denotes the specific class of the process instance acting as a trace. These processes are the sole sanctioned sources for supporting descriptive claims within the framework.*

### 7.3.3 The Role of Process in Claim Support

The process defined here (`bfo:0000015`) is the active agent. It is the execution of a workflow or a system event that results in the creation of the trace. The axiom `sdg:supportsClaim some cco:DescriptiveICE` implies a directional relationship: the process (via its output) points toward the claim. In a forensic context, this reverses the burden of proof; one does not simply assert a claim and look for data. Rather, one identifies a valid process (a trace) and derives the claim from it. This ensures that all claims are grounded in executable, verifiable system activities.

## 7.4 Outlier Claim Basic

### 7.4.1 Defining the Descriptive Claim

The central unit of analysis in anomaly detection is the claim itself. The axiom `outlier_claim_basic` establishes the fundamental nature of this unit. It asserts that a claim (Class X) is a subclass of `cco:DescriptiveICE`. A Descriptive Information Content Entity is an entity that is about something—a proposition, a statement, or an assertion regarding the state of the world or the system.

This axiom serves as the baseline definition for any assertion of anomaly. It strips away the complexity of supporting evidence and focuses on the informational content. Whether the claim is "Transaction value exceeds limit" or "User login time is anomalous," it is fundamentally a descriptive entity. This classification is necessary to distinguish the claim from the data it describes (the artifact) and the process that generated it (the trace).

### 7.4.2 Registry of Basic Claims

For the purposes of audit transparency, all basic claims generated by the system must be cataloged. The table `t_outlier_claim_basic` functions as this catalog. It records the unique identifier of each claim instance and its classification.

The maintenance of this table is a requirement for compliance. It ensures that every assertion made by the monitoring system is recorded. In the event of a dispute or a regulatory review, this table provides the manifest of all "Descriptive Information Content Entities" that the system has produced. It is the "condensed consolidated balance sheet" of assertions—a summary of what the system has declared to be true about the data under its supervision.

| CLM-001 | HighValueTransaction_Anomaly |

*Note: Column 'x' represents the specific class or type of the descriptive claim. These entries constitute the basic informational output of the anomaly detection framework.*

### 7.4.3 Characteristics of Descriptive Entities

As `cco:DescriptiveICE` instances, these claims are generically dependent continuants. They depend on the bearer (the system or the report) for their existence. They do not exist in the physical world but exist as information. The axiom reinforces the idea that to manage the audit environment effectively, one must manage these information entities with the same rigor as physical assets. They must be stored, versioned, and accessed according to strict protocols.

## 7.5 Outlier Claim Min One Attribute

### 7.5.1 Complex Claims and Attribute Requirements

While basic claims define the *what* of an anomaly, complex claims define the *why* and *how*. The axiom `outlier_claim_min_one_attribute` introduces a constraint that elevates a claim from a simple statement to a substantiated finding. It asserts that a claim (Class X) is a `cco:DescriptiveICE` and must possess an attribute set (`sdg:hasAttributeSet`) containing a minimum of one attribute (Class Y).

Crucially, the schema definition for this axiom establishes that the attribute (Y) acts as a foreign key referencing `t_trace_supports_claim`. This creates a mandatory dependency: a complex outlier claim *must* be supported by at least one valid trace. This embodies the "evidence-anchored" requirement of the framework. A claim cannot exist in this complex state without pointing back to a process that supports it.

### 7.5.2 Relational Integrity and Evidence Linkage

The table `t_outlier_claim_min_one_attribute` is the structural realization of this evidence linkage. It links the claim (id, x) to the supporting trace (y). The foreign key constraint ensures that only traces previously registered in `t_trace_supports_claim` can be used to support a claim. This enforces data integrity at the database level, preventing the association of claims with unverified or non-existent process evidence.

This structure mirrors the rigor of financial auditing, where every line item in a statement must be traceable to a specific ledger entry or source document. The following table demonstrates this linkage, showing how specific anomaly claims are anchored to specific process traces.

### 7.5.3 Governance Implications

The requirement for "min one attribute" is a control mechanism. It prevents the generation of "orphan" claims—assertions that float free of any evidentiary basis. In a regulatory context, such orphan claims are liabilities. They represent potential false positives or, worse, unsubstantiated accusations. By enforcing the axiom `outlier_claim_min_one_attribute`, the governance framework ensures that every reported anomaly is accompanied by the "fingerprints" of the system process that detected it.

This linkage facilitates the audit trail review process. An auditor can query `t_outlier_claim_min_one_attribute`, retrieve the ID of the claim, and immediately join to `t_trace_supports_claim` to identify the specific process event (e.g., "AuthenticationSuccess_Event") that triggered the anomaly. This traceability is the hallmark of a mature, compliant information system.
