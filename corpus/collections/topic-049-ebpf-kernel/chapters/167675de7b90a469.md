# Chapter 7: Formal Ontology for Audit Evidence and Anomaly Classification

## 7.1 Scope and Applicability

This chapter establishes the ontological framework required for the systematic classification of audit evidence, anomaly detection, and dataset governance. The definitions provided herein are aligned with the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) and the Common Core Ontologies (CCO), ensuring interoperability between regulatory reporting mechanisms and internal audit algorithms. The primary objective is to define the structural relationships between descriptive information content entities (ICEs), the traces that substantiate them, and the artifacts subject to audit procedures. These constructs are essential for maintaining the integrity of financial reporting and compliance with Section 13 or 15(d) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, particularly where automated anomaly detection is employed to identify material misstatements.

## 7.2 Basic Descriptive Information Content Entities

### 7.2.1 Definition and Axiomatic Structure

The fundamental unit of information within an audit engagement is the Descriptive Information Content Entity (DescriptiveICE). Per Axiom 3 (`outlier_claim_basic`), any entity classified as a basic outlier claim is, by definition, a subclass of `cco:DescriptiveICE`. This axiom asserts that information content entities which serve as claims—specifically those identifying deviations from expected patterns—are ontologically distinct from the physical documents or digital records in which they are encoded.

In the context of a financial audit, a "claim" represents a declarative statement regarding the value, state, or existence of an asset or liability. When such a claim identifies a value that falls outside a defined control threshold (e.g., a variance exceeding 5% from the projected budget), it is categorized as an outlier claim. The ontological constraint here is minimal but absolute: the entity must be a generically dependent continuant that conveys meaning.

## 7.3 Audit Traces and Claim Support

### 7.3.1 The Evidentiary Link

A claim, regardless of its precision, holds no probative value without supporting evidence. Axiom 2 (`trace_supports_claim`) formalizes this requirement by defining a class of entities that are "Independent Continuants" (specifically `bfo:0000015`) which possess the property `sdg:supportsClaim` directed at a `cco:DescriptiveICE`.

In practical terms, a "trace" is a distinct entity—such as a system log entry, a digital signature, or a processed transaction record—that exists independently of the claim it supports. The axiom mandates that for a claim to be valid within the audit ontology, there must exist at least one trace that establishes a `supportsClaim` relationship. This structure prevents the generation of "orphan" anomalies within automated auditing systems, ensuring that every flagged outlier is anchored to a specific, verifiable data artifact.

## 7.4 Attributed Outlier Claims

### 7.4.1 Complex Constraints and Attribute Sets

While basic claims identify the existence of an anomaly, Axiom 1 (`outlier_claim_min_one_attribute`) introduces a higher level of semantic rigor. This axiom states that a specific subclass of outlier claims is not only a `cco:DescriptiveICE` but must also possess an `sdg:hasAttributeSet` relationship with a minimum cardinality of 1 (`min 1`) to another class `{Y}`.

This structure enforces the "Attribution Principle" in audit analytics: an outlier claim is only actionable if it is described by a set of specific attributes or metrics. Furthermore, the relational schema dictates that the attribute set (`y`) must reference a valid trace (Foreign Key to `t_trace_supports_claim`). This creates a rigid dependency chain: the Claim is defined by the Attribute, and the Attribute is grounded in the Trace.

The following table demonstrates the application of Axiom 1. Here, the outlier claims (e.g., `UnauthorizedAccessClaim`) are explicitly linked to specific attribute sets (e.g., `GeolocationCoordinate`), which in turn correspond to the trace evidence logged in Table 7.2.

## 7.5 Datasets Under Audit

### 7.5.1 Artifact Governance

The scope of an audit is defined by the artifacts under examination. Axiom 4 (`dataset_under_audit`) classifies these artifacts as `cco:Artifact` entities that are currently undergoing a `bfo:0000015` (Process) defined as `sdg:underAudit`. This axiom distinguishes between static data repositories and the active process of auditing them.

An "Artifact" in this context may be a consolidated general ledger, a customer relationship management (CRM) database export, or a collection of unstructured document images. The ontological classification of an entity as "under audit" triggers specific retention, access control, and immutability requirements pursuant to governance frameworks. It signals that the data object is within the temporal bounds of an audit engagement and is subject to the evidentiary standards defined in Sections 7.2 through 7.4.
