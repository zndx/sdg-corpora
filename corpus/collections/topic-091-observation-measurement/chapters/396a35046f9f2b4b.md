# Regulatory Control Mapping and Compliance Directives

This chapter delineates the formal mapping between organizational governance artifacts and external regulatory frameworks. It provides a rigorous examination of the ontology axioms that bind internal control directives to specific compliance requirements within the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) and the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA). The following sections define the structural constraints of these relationships, supported by tabular evidence derived from the relational schema, to ensure precise alignment between declared policies and mandated safeguards.

## 1. PCI DSS Requirement Mapping

The implementation of PCI DSS controls within an enterprise information security environment necessitates a formal linkage between internal directives and the specific requirements set forth by the PCI Security Standards Council. The axiom `pci_dss_requirement` establishes that a given class `X` (an internal directive) is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` and possesses the property `sdg:pciDssRequirement` linking it to a specific requirement class `Y`. This relationship asserts that the directive `X` is not merely a general policy but a specific informational control element designed to satisfy the obligation defined in `Y`.

In practical terms, this mapping allows compliance officers to query the governance ontology and retrieve all internal directives that pertain to a specific PCI DSS article. The existential restriction (`some {Y:Class}`) ensures that for a directive to be classified under this axiom, it must be associated with at least one valid PCI DSS requirement identifier. This prevents the classification of generic security policies as PCI-specific controls without explicit evidence of their scope.

## 2. HIPAA Physical Safeguards

Under the HIPAA Security Rule, physical safeguards are defined as physical measures to protect electronic health information (ePHI) and related buildings and equipment from natural and environmental hazards, as well as unauthorized intrusion. The axiom `hipaa_safeguard_physical` formalizes this by asserting that a class `X` (a directive) is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` and implements the property `sdg:hipaaSafeguard` targeting a physical safeguard specification `Y`.

This distinction is critical for governance frameworks because it segregates physical security controls from technical or administrative measures. By utilizing this axiom, the ontology ensures that directives concerning facility access, workstation usage, and device media controls are semantically grouped. The verbalization "{X} is something that hipaa safeguard {Y}" implies that the directive `X` acts as the enforcement mechanism for the safeguard specification `Y`.

## 3. HIPAA Technical Safeguards

Technical safeguards under HIPAA involve the technology and the policy and procedures for its use that protect electronic health information and control access to it. The axiom `hipaa_safeguard_technical` defines the relationship where a class `X` (a directive) is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` and is linked via `sdg:hipaaSafeguard` to a technical safeguard class `Y`. This axiom covers the automated mechanisms used to protect data, including access control, audit controls, and integrity controls.

The application of this axiom allows for the precise categorization of technical configurations and software implementations. Unlike physical safeguards, which rely on human and physical barriers, technical safeguards are embedded within the information system architecture. The axiom ensures that directives such as encryption protocols or authentication mechanisms are correctly identified as technical implementations of the HIPAA Security Rule.

## 4. HIPAA Administrative Safeguards

Administrative safeguards are administrative actions, policies, and procedures to manage the selection, development, implementation, and maintenance of security measures to protect electronic health information. These safeguards also manage the conduct of the workforce in relation to the protection of ePHI. The axiom `hipaa_safeguard_admin` asserts that a class `X` (a directive) is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` and fulfills the property `sdg:hipaaSafeguard` for an administrative safeguard class `Y`.

This axiom captures the "human" and "process" layer of the compliance framework. It encompasses security management processes, assigned security responsibilities, workforce training, and contingency planning. By mapping administrative directives to these safeguards, the ontology provides a comprehensive view of the governance structure, ensuring that policy-level decisions are auditable and traceable.
