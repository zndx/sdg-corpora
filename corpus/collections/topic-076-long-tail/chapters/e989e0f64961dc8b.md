# Chapter 7: Structural Integrity of Audit Processes and Evidence Verification

## 7.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures underpinning audit processes within directive governance frameworks. It establishes the necessary dependencies between processual entities, temporal intervals, agentive roles, and evidentiary artifacts. The definitions provided herein are grounded in the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) and are intended to ensure rigorous compliance with regulatory standards for data integrity and audit trail maintenance. The following sections detail the specific axioms governing audit periods, verification evidence, agentive responsibility, attestation, and the generation of findings.

## 7.2 Temporal Delimitation of Audit Processes

### 7.2.1 Axiom Definition: Audit for Period

The foundational axiom for any governance process is the establishment of its temporal boundaries. Formally, this is expressed as: *Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:forAuditPeriod some {Y:Class}*. In natural language, this asserts that **{X} is something that for audit period {Y}**.

This constraint ensures that no audit process exists in a temporal vacuum; it must be instantiated with a specific reference to a defined duration. This temporal anchoring is critical for the validity of compliance reports and for the alignment of audit activities with fiscal or regulatory reporting cycles. The process entity (X) is necessarily a `bfo:Process` (BFO:0000015), implying it occurs in time and has a start and end point, while the audit period (Y) provides the contextual scope.

## 7.3 Agentive Responsibility in Execution

### 7.3.1 Axiom Definition: Audit Conducted By

The execution of an audit process requires an agentive source, specifically a responsible party capable of performing the action. The governing axiom is: *Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:conductedBy some cco:Person*. This verbalizes to: **{X} is something that conducted by person**.

This axiom binds the processual entity to the `cco:Person` class, ensuring that automated or purely algorithmic executions are excluded from the definition of a formal audit unless human oversight is explicitly modeled. It establishes the chain of custody and accountability essential for regulatory adherence. The "conducted by" relationship is not merely associative but constitutive of the audit's validity as a governance action.

To maintain traceability, the system must register all process instances that satisfy the agentive constraint. The table below records the processes that have been formally assigned to and conducted by authorized personnel.

This structure facilitates rapid querying of all auditable actions attributable to human agents within the system.

## 7.4 Evidentiary Output and Verification

### 7.4.1 Axiom Definition: Verification Produces Evidence

Verification is defined not by the intent to verify, but by the production of material or digital artifacts. The axiom is formalized as: *Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:producesEvidence min 1 {Y:Class}*. This asserts that **{X} is a process** that produces evidence.

The use of the cardinality constraint `min 1` is significant; it dictates that a verification process is ontologically invalid if it results in zero evidence artifacts. This prevents "phantom" audits—processes that are logged as completed but yield no supporting documentation. The evidence class ({Y}) can range from digitized log files and scanned receipts to generated summary reports.

The linkage between the process and the evidence allows for traceability from the high-level governance action down to the atomic data unit.

## 7.5 Formal Attestation and Authorization

### 7.5.1 Axiom Definition: Attestation Minimum One Signer

Attestation represents the formal closure of a verification process, transitioning it from a "draft" or "in-progress" state to a "certified" state. The relevant axiom is: *Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:signedBy min 1 cco:Person*. This verbalizes to: **{X} is a process** signed by a person.

Similar to the verification axiom, the `min 1` cardinality constraint ensures that an attestation cannot be self-executing or anonymous. It requires the explicit intervention of a `cco:Person` to apply a digital or physical signature. This agentive endorsement is what grants the audit process its legal and regulatory weight.

### 7.5.2 Attestation Registry

The registry below tracks those processes that have successfully completed the attestation phase. It serves as a definitive list of audited items that have been formally signed off.

**Table 7.4: Registry of Signed Attestations (t_attestation_min_one_signer)**

The schema enforces that for a process to appear in this table, it must satisfy the condition of having at least one authorized signatory associated with it in the broader knowledge graph.

## 7.6 Findings Generation and Classification

### 7.6.1 Axiom Definition: Audit with Findings

The ultimate semantic output of an audit process is the generation of findings—observations of non-compliance, risks, or discrepancies. The axiom is defined as: *Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:hasFindings some {Y:Class}*. This asserts that **{X} is something that has findings {Y}**.

This axiom structures the relationship between the audit process and the specific findings it uncovers. A finding ({Y}) is treated as a distinct entity, allowing it to be categorized, prioritized, and tracked independently of the audit process that created it. This separation is crucial for workflow management, as findings often require remediation workflows that extend beyond the lifespan of the audit itself.
