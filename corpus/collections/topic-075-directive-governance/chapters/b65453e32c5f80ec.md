# Chapter 7: Governance Directives and Attestation Protocols

This chapter establishes the formal axioms governing the creation, enforcement, and validation of directive information content entities within the enterprise governance framework. It delineates the structural dependencies between policy classes, their jurisdictional boundaries, and the requisite attestation processes that ensure regulatory compliance. The content herein is intended for systems architects, compliance officers, and data governors responsible for implementing the ontology-based governance model.

## 1. Policy Equivalence Relative to Jurisdiction and Scope

In the context of enterprise information architecture, a Policy is not merely a document but a logical entity defined strictly by its intersection with a legal domain and a functional area. The axiom `policy_equiv_jurisdiction_and_scope` posits that a Policy Class is equivalent to a Directive Information Content Entity (ICE) only when it possesses a specific jurisdiction and governs a specific scope. This tripartite equivalence ensures that governance artifacts are never ambiguous; a directive is valid only if its authority (jurisdiction) and its subject matter (scope) are explicitly declared and axiomatically linked.

To operationalize this, we must instantiate policies that map specific directive classes to their respective jurisdictional and scope classes. The following table embodies this axiom, where the primary key (`id`) uniquely identifies the policy instance, and columns `x`, `y`, and `z` represent the Policy Class, Jurisdiction Class, and Scope Class, respectively. There are no foreign key constraints in this specific schema, as it defines the ontological equivalence of the classes themselves.

| id | x | y | z |
|---|---|---|---|
| POL-001 | DataRetentionPolicy | EU_GDPR_Jurisdiction | CustomerPIIScope |
| POL-002 | AccessControlPolicy | US_NIST_800_53_Jurisdiction | FederalSystemScope |
| POL-003 | EncryptionStandard | Global_ISO27001_Jurisdiction | TransitDataScope |
| POL-004 | AuditLoggingPolicy | UK_GDPR_Jurisdiction | FinancialTransactionScope |
| POL-005 | RemoteWorkProtocol | CaliforniaCCPA_Jurisdiction | EmployeeDataScope |

The instantiation of `POL-001`, for example, demonstrates that the `DataRetentionPolicy` class is valid only insofar as it operates within the `EU_GDPR_Jurisdiction` and governs the `CustomerPIIScope`. Without these constraints, the directive fails to meet the formal definition of a Policy Class within the ontology. This structure prevents the deployment of generic, unscoped directives that lack legal enforceability or technical relevance.

## 2. Attestation as a Signed Process

Attestation represents the critical bridge between static directives and active compliance. According to the axiom `attestation_signed_by`, an Attestation is a specific type of process (BFO:0000015) that is characterized by the act of being signed by a person. This definition elevates attestation from a mere status update to a distinct processual event involving human agency. It implies that the validity of the attestation is inextricably bound to the identity and authority of the signatory.

The table below illustrates this axiom by listing specific attestation classes. The primary key (`id`) serves as the unique identifier for the attestation process instance, while `x` denotes the Attestation Class. The schema relies on the ontological definition that `x` is a subclass of `bfo:Process` and possesses the property `signedBy` referencing `cco:Person`.

| id | x |
|---|---|
| ATT-2023-01 | QuarterlyComplianceAttestation |
| ATT-2023-02 | AnnualSecurityAuditAttestation |
| ATT-2023-03 | IncidentResponseReviewAttestation |
| ATT-2023-04 | ThirdPartyRiskAttestation |
| ATT-2023-05 | DataBreachVerificationAttestation |

In this schema, `ATT-2023-02` represents an instance of the `AnnualSecurityAuditAttestation` class. By definition, this class is a process that must be signed by a person. The relational structure captures the class of the attestation, anchoring it to the broader ontology of processes, ensuring that workflow engines treat it as an active event rather than a passive data object.

## 3. Minimum Enforcement Constraints for Policies

A directive without an enforcement mechanism is advisory, not governance. The axiom `policy_min_one_enforcer` mandates that any valid Policy Class (a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE`) must be enforced by a minimum of one Enforcer Class. This constraint introduces cardinality requirements into the governance model, ensuring that for every policy defined, there exists at least one agent or system role responsible for its execution and monitoring.

The following table embodies this enforcement constraint. The primary key (`id`) identifies the specific policy-enforcement relationship instance. Column `x` references the Policy Class, and column `y` references the Enforcer Class. The `NOT NULL` constraint on `y` enforces the axiom's requirement that at least one enforcer must be present.

| id | x | y |
|---|---|---|
| ENF-001 | DataRetentionPolicy | DataGovernanceOfficer |
| ENF-002 | AccessControlPolicy | IdentityManagementSystem |
| ENF-003 | EncryptionStandard | CybersecurityTeam |
| ENF-004 | AuditLoggingPolicy | SIEMAdmin |
| ENF-005 | RemoteWorkProtocol | HRComplianceManager |

For instance, row `ENF-002` asserts that the `AccessControlPolicy` is enforced by the `IdentityManagementSystem`. This relationship is mandatory; should the `y` column be null, the policy instance would violate the ontology's integrity rules. This structure allows auditors to query the governance layer to instantly identify the responsible parties for any specific directive, facilitating accountability maps and automated compliance checks.

## 4. Requirement Specification and Target Application

While policies provide the broad governance architecture, Requirements represent the granular, actionable controls derived from them. The axiom `requirement_equiv_specifies_target` defines a Requirement Class as a Directive ICE that specifies a particular control (the `specifies` property) and applies to a specific target entity (the `appliesTo` property). This equivalence axiom ensures that requirements are never abstract; they must explicitly state what is being controlled and the object or system to which the control applies.

The table below captures these granular directives. The primary key (`id`) uniquely identifies the requirement instance. Column `x` holds the Requirement Class, while `y` and `z` hold the Specified Class (the control) and the Target Class (the entity), respectively. Both `y` and `z` are mandatory (`NOT NULL`), reflecting the axiom's "EquivalentTo" logic which requires both properties to define the class.

| id | x | y | z |
|---|---|---|---|
| REQ-101 | MultiFactorAuthRequirement | TimeBasedOTP | RemoteAccessGateway |
| REQ-102 | DataAtRestEncryptionRequirement | AES256Algorithm | CustomerDatabase |
| REQ-103 | NetworkSegmentationRequirement | VLAN隔离 | PaymentCardEnvironment |
| REQ-104 | LogRetentionRequirement | WORMStorage | SecurityEventLogs |
| REQ-105 | CodeReviewRequirement | StaticAnalysisTool | ProductionRepository |

Consider `REQ-102`: the `DataAtRestEncryptionRequirement` is defined by its specification of the `AES256Algorithm` and its application to the `CustomerDatabase`. This precise mapping allows for automated verification scripts to check the configuration of the `CustomerDatabase` against the `AES256Algorithm` specification, closing the loop between governance definition and technical implementation.

## 5. Cardinality of Signers in Attestation Processes

To ensure the non-repudiation and integrity of the attestation process, the axiom `attestation_min_one_signer` reinforces the cardinality constraints introduced in Section 2. It formally states that an Attestation Class (a subclass of `bfo:Process`) must have a minimum of one signer (`cco:Person`). This axiom prevents the creation of empty attestation workflows within the system, ensuring that every process instance is associated with human accountability.

The table below lists attestation process instances that satisfy this cardinality rule. The primary key (`id`) identifies the specific attestation event, and column `x` identifies the Attestation Class. The schema enforces that `x` is a class involving the `signedBy` property with a minimum cardinality of 1.

| id | x |
|---|---|
| SIG-AUTH-01 | ExecutivePolicySignOff |
| SIG-AUTH-02 | ExternalAuditorValidation |
| SIG-AUTH-03 | SystemAdminCertification |
| SIG-AUTH-04 | PrivacyOfficerReview |
| SIG-AUTH-05 | RiskManagerAcknowledgment |

In this view, `SIG-AUTH-04` represents an instance of `PrivacyOfficerReview`. The underlying axiom guarantees that this process class cannot be instantiated unless at least one `cco:Person` fills the role of the signer. This structural constraint is vital for maintaining the chain of custody in regulatory filings, as it mathematically precludes the existence of unverified or unsigned compliance artifacts within the governed system.
