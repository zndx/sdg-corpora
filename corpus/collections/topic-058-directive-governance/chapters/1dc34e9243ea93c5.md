# Chapter 4: Governance Frameworks and the Ontology of Compliance Evidence

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the structural relationships between regulatory directives, control implementations, and the evidentiary artifacts required to establish compliance within a formal governance framework. The focus is on the precise mapping of Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) requirements and Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) physical safeguards to their corresponding control mechanisms and verification processes. By defining these entities through a formal ontology, organizations can automate the audit trail, ensuring that every attestation is anchored to verifiable evidence and that every directive is traceable to specific implementation controls.

## 4.2 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

In the context of governance, an attestation is not merely a declaration of compliance but a formal process (bfo:0000015) that substantiates adherence to a standard through reference to specific artifacts. The ontology defines the class **Attestation with Supporting Evidence** as a process entity that validates the state of compliance by linking a declarative statement to a specific requirement. This relationship ensures that the act of attestation is never orphaned; it must always be "with supporting evidence."

The critical distinction here is the directionality of the dependency. The attestation relies on the existence of the requirement it seeks to validate. In a relational schema, this is enforced by a foreign key constraint linking the attestation record to the specific PCI DSS requirement identifier. Without a valid requirement target, the attestation is structurally invalid.

**Table 4.1: Mapping of Attestations to PCI DSS Requirements**

| ID | Attestation Entity (X) | PCI DSS Requirement (Y) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ATT_2023_01 | VisitorLogAttestation | PCI_09.2 |
| ATT_2023_02 | DestructionCertificate | PCI_09.9 |
| ATT_2023_03 | FloorWalkAttestation | PCI_09.1 |
| ATT_2023_04 | FirewallConfigReview | PCI_01.1 |
| ATT_2023_05 | WirelessAuditAttestation | PCI_02.1 |

*Table 4.1 illustrates the `t_attestation_with_supporting_evidence` structure. The primary key is the Attestation ID, while the column `y` represents the foreign key constraint enforcing the link to the PCI requirement table.*

### 4.2.1 The Role of Substantiation
When an auditor signs an attestation, they are effectively asserting that the evidence linked to the requirement ID (e.g., `PCI_09.2`) has been reviewed and found satisfactory. The ontology captures this by treating the attestation as a process that consumes the requirement context. For instance, the `VisitorLogAttestation` (ATT_2023_01) is only valid because it references the specific requirement regarding visitor control (PCI_09.2). This prevents generic compliance statements and forces granular accountability.

## 4.3 PCI DSS Requirement

The **PCI DSS Requirement** functions as the central node in this compliance ontology. It is defined as a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE`, representing an information control element that mandates specific actions or states. The axiom asserts that a PCI DSS Requirement is an entity that "has PCI DSS requirement" relationships, but structurally, it serves as the bridge between high-level policy and low-level control implementation.

In the relational model, the requirement table exhibits a complex dependency. It references a `Control with Evidence Requirement`. This creates a bidirectional mapping where the requirement defines the control, and the control, in turn, provides the evidence necessary to satisfy the requirement. This circular dependency is intentional, reflecting the real-world iterative nature of compliance management where requirements drive control design, and control efficacy validates the requirement.

**Table 4.2: PCI DSS Requirements and Associated Controls**

| ID | Requirement Entity (X) | Control with Evidence (Y) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| PCI_09.2 | AccessControlPolicy | CTRL_PHYS_01 |
| PCI_09.9 | MediaDisposalPolicy | CTRL_PHYS_02 |
| PCI_09.1 | CleanDeskPolicy | CTRL_PHYS_03 |
| PCI_01.1 | FirewallConfiguration | CTRL_NET_01 |
| PCI_02.1 | WirelessUsagePolicy | CTRL_NET_02 |

*Table 4.2 depicts the `t_pci_dss_requirement` schema. The `x` column denotes the specific PCI directive (e.g., Requirement 9.2), while `y` is the foreign key pointing to the control table, establishing the implementation mandate.*

### 4.3.1 Directive-to-Control Mapping
The mapping in Table 4.2 demonstrates how abstract policies are grounded in operational reality. The `AccessControlPolicy` (PCI_09.2) is not merely a document; it is inextricably linked to `CTRL_PHYS_01`, the specific control mechanism (e.g., a badge reader system) that enforces the policy. This structure ensures that for every directive requirement, there is a corresponding control object capable of generating the necessary audit trail.

## 4.4 Control with Evidence Requirement

A **Control with Evidence Requirement** is a directive (`cco:DirectiveICE`) that is defined by its capacity to generate proof of execution. The axiom verbalizes this as "{X} is something that requires evidence {Y}". In this ontology, the control entity is the active agent of compliance. It is not sufficient for a control to exist; it must produce output that can be inspected.

The schema for this entity references the PCI DSS requirement table, closing the loop established in the previous section. This reciprocal relationship ensures that controls are not designed in a vacuum. A control is defined as "requiring evidence" of a specific PCI requirement. This means the evidence generated by the control must be directly applicable to the assessment of the requirement it is linked to.

**Table 4.3: Control Entities and Evidence Provenance**

| ID | Control Entity (X) | PCI DSS Requirement (Y) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| CTRL_PHYS_01 | VisitorLogControl | PCI_09.2 |
| CTRL_PHYS_02 | HardDriveDestruction | PCI_09.9 |
| CTRL_PHYS_03 | ScreenLockMechanism | PCI_09.1 |
| CTRL_NET_01 | NextGenFirewall | PCI_01.1 |
| CTRL_NET_02 | WIPS_Sensor | PCI_02.1 |

*Table 4.3 represents the `t_control_with_evidence_requirement`. The `y` column links back to the PCI requirement, indicating that the `VisitorLogControl` (CTRL_PHYS_01) is the mechanism by which `PCI_09.2` is satisfied.*

### 4.4.2 Evidence Generation Mechanisms
The definition of a control in this framework emphasizes output. The `HardDriveDestruction` control (CTRL_PHYS_02) is valid only insofar as it produces a certificate of destruction or a log of the sanitization process. By linking this control back to `PCI_09.9` (Media Disposal), the ontology enforces that the evidence produced by the destruction process must meet the specific criteria outlined in that PCI requirement.

## 4.5 HIPAA Safeguard Physical

While PCI DSS provides the directive framework, specific regulatory regimes such as HIPAA impose distinct safeguard categories. The **HIPAA Safeguard Physical** entity is defined as a directive (`cco:DirectiveICE`) that implements a HIPAA safeguard. This axiom maps the specific physical security measures required by HIPAA to the generalized control definitions established in the previous section.

The relational structure here connects the HIPAA safeguard to the `Control with Evidence Requirement`. This allows the organization to maintain a unified control library while satisfying multiple regulatory frameworks. A single physical control (e.g., a badge reader) can serve as the implementation for both a PCI requirement and a HIPAA safeguard.

**Table 4.4: HIPAA Physical Safeguards Mapped to Controls**

| ID | HIPAA Safeguard Entity (X) | Control with Evidence (Y) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| HIPAA_PHYS_01 | BadgeReaderSystem | CTRL_PHYS_01 |
| HIPAA_PHYS_02 | ShredderDevice | CTRL_PHYS_02 |
| HIPAA_PHYS_03 | PrivacyScreenFilter | CTRL_PHYS_03 |
| HIPAA_PHYS_04 | SecurityGuardForce | CTRL_PHYS_04 |
| HIPAA_PHYS_05 | KeyCardLock | CTRL_PHYS_05 |

*Table 4.4 illustrates the `t_hipaa_safeguard_physical` schema. The foreign key `y` links the HIPAA-specific safeguard to the generic control ID, demonstrating how a `BadgeReaderSystem` satisfies the `VisitorLogControl` requirement.*

### 4.5.1 Regulatory Convergence
The data in Table 4.4 highlights the convergence of compliance standards. The `BadgeReaderSystem` (HIPAA_PHYS_01) is the physical instantiation of the `VisitorLogControl` (CTRL_PHYS_01). This mapping allows compliance officers to perform a single verification of the control's evidence to satisfy both the HIPAA Physical Safeguard standard (§164.310(a)(2)(iii)) and the PCI DSS Requirement 9.2 regarding visitor authorization.

## 4.6 Verification Produces Evidence

The final concept in this ontology is the **Verification** process. Defined as a process (bfo:0000015) that "produces evidence," this entity represents the actual audit or test activity. It is distinct from the control itself; the control operates continuously, but the verification is the discrete act of checking that control.

This axiom links the verification process directly to the PCI DSS requirement. This linkage is crucial for audit reporting because it connects the *method* of verification (e.g., a visual inspection or a log review) to the *standard* being verified. The output of this process is the evidence artifact that is subsequently consumed by the attestation process described in Section 4.2.

**Table 4.5: Verification Processes and Evidence Output**

| ID | Verification Entity (X) | PCI DSS Requirement (Y) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| VER_09_2 | BadgeAuditReview | PCI_09.2 |
| VER_09_9 | WitnessedDestruction | PCI_09.9 |
| VER_09_1 | VisualInspection | PCI_09.1 |
| VER_01_1 | RuleSetAnalysis | PCI_01.1 |
| VER_02_1 | SpectrumAnalysis | PCI_02.1 |

*Table 4.5 details the `t_verification_produces_evidence` structure. The `y` column confirms that the `BadgeAuditReview` (VER_09_2) is the specific verification activity generating evidence for `PCI_09.2`.*

### 4.6.1 The Audit Trail Lifecycle
The verification process is the point of origin for the compliance data. When an auditor performs a `WitnessedDestruction` (VER_09_9) of a hard drive, that process produces the evidence (e.g., a destruction log). That log is required by the `HardDriveDestruction` control (CTRL_PHYS_02), which satisfies `PCI_09.9`. Finally, the `DestructionCertificate` attestation (ATT_2023_02) references this requirement. This chain—from verification to control to requirement to attestation—forms the complete, immutable backbone of the governance framework.
