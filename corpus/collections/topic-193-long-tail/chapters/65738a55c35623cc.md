### 4.0 Preamble and Scope

This chapter establishes the formal ontological structures required to define, audit, and govern directive information content entities within a regulated digital environment. It addresses the necessity of mapping abstract governance concepts to concrete relational schemas, ensuring that compliance frameworks are not merely descriptive but are computationally enforceable. The definitions provided herein align with the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) upper-level ontology, specifically utilizing the Information Content Entity (ICE) branch to delineate the boundaries between directives, controls, and supporting evidence. The following sections detail five core axioms that constitute the backbone of the governance model, ranging from basic directive classification to complex, recursive enforcement chains.

---

### 4.1 Basic Directive Entities

**Axiom Template:** `policy_basic`
**Ontology Reference:** `cco:DirectiveICE`

The fundamental unit of governance is the Directive Information Content Entity (`cco:DirectiveICE`). As defined in Axiom 3, the class structure asserts that any entity designated as a governance policy must be a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE`. This classification serves as the primary entry point for all governance objects within the system. Unlike descriptive claims, which state facts about the world, directives prescribe specific courses of action or states of affairs. The axiom `policy_basic` is a declarative rule that ensures type consistency: any instance of class {X} is inherently a directive.

In practical terms, this axiom prevents the misclassification of descriptive data (such as logs or observations) as prescriptive policies. It establishes the `t_policy_basic` registry, which acts as the master index for all governance directives within the organization. The relational structure is intentionally sparse, containing only the unique identifier and the class designation, reflecting the foundational nature of this entity.

### 4.2 Ownership and Accountability Structures

**Axiom Template:** `control_min_one_owner`
**Ontology Reference:** `sdg:hasOwner`

While a directive defines *what* must be done, a control defines *how* it is enforced. Axiom 4 introduces the constraint that any directive functioning as a control must have at least one designated owner. The verbalization "{X} is a directive information content entity" is extended by the object property `sdg:hasOwner`, which has a range of `cco:Person`. This axiom enforces the principle of non-repudiation and accountability in governance frameworks. A control without an owner is considered orphaned and, by definition, unenforceable in a compliant audit environment.

The `t_control_min_one_owner` schema captures this relationship. It is critical to note that while the schema definition provided in the appendix only explicitly lists the entity ID and class, the axiom implies a mandatory existential quantification over the set of persons. In the relational implementation below, the ownership is implicit in the operational context, though the table serves to validate that the class {X} meets the criteria of a "Control" subclass.

### 4.3 Scope of Governance: Artifact Restriction

**Axiom Template:** `policy_only_governs_artifacts`
**Ontology Reference:** `sdg:governsScope`

Governance directives must be bounded in their application to prevent scope creep and ensure operational clarity. Axiom 5, `policy_only_governs_artifacts`, imposes a universal restriction on the property `sdg:governsScope`. It asserts that for any directive {X}, the scope of governance is restricted exclusively to instances of `cco:Artifact`. This means that a policy may govern physical objects, digital files, or hardware components (Artifacts), but it may not directly govern abstract temporal regions or independent continuants without an artifact medium.

This distinction is vital in audit trails where the "subject" of a policy action must be tangible or digitally manipulable. The `t_policy_only_governs_artifacts` table identifies those policies whose scope is strictly artifact-centric. This ensures that when a policy violation is recorded, the associated object is verifiably an artifact, satisfying the "only" (allValuesFrom) restriction of the axiom.

### 4.4 Enforcement Mechanisms and Dependency Chains

**Axiom Template:** `policy_min_one_enforcer`
**Ontology Reference:** `sdg:enforcedBy`

The transition from policy to practice is mediated by enforcement mechanisms. Axiom 1, `policy_min_one_enforcer`, posits that a directive information content entity {X} is enforced by a minimum of one entity {Y}. This establishes a dependency chain where the validity of the policy is contingent upon the existence of its enforcer. In this ontology, the enforcer {Y} is modeled as a distinct class, creating a structural link between the high-level governance intent and the operational enforcement layer.

The relational schema `t_policy_min_one_enforcer` implements this via a foreign key constraint linking the enforcer {Y} to a claim of evidence. This structure implies that enforcement is not merely an action but is substantiated by a specific, recorded entity. The table below illustrates this mapping, where the policy ID is linked to a specific enforcer record. It is imperative that the `y` column in this table references a valid identifier in the evidence claim registry, ensuring referential integrity across the governance framework.

### 4.5 Evidence Support for Descriptive Assertions

**Axiom Template:** `claim_min_one_evidence`
**Ontology Reference:** `sdg:supportedByEvidence`

The final axiom in this sequence closes the loop by defining the evidentiary basis for enforcement. Axiom 2, `claim_min_one_evidence`, classifies {X} as a `cco:DescriptiveICE`—a statement that describes a state of affairs—which must be supported by a minimum of one evidence item {Y}. In the context of this schema, the evidence {Y} is identified as the policy directive itself. This creates a rigorous logical structure: a Claim (e.g., "The system is compliant") is supported by Evidence (the Policy), and that Policy is enforced by the Claim.

This circular dependency (Policy -> Enforced By -> Claim -> Supported By -> Policy) creates a self-reinforcing compliance loop. The `t_claim_min_one_evidence` table records these descriptive assertions. The foreign key constraint ensures that every claim of compliance or operational status is anchored to a specific governing policy. This structure is essential for audit reporting, as it traces the lineage of any assertion back to the governing directive.

### 4.6 Audit and Verification Summary

The integration of these five axioms provides a robust framework for automated compliance auditing. By mapping the ontological restrictions to relational tables, the system can programmatically verify that:
1.  All directives are properly classified (`t_policy_basic`).
2.  All controls have assigned ownership (`t_control_min_one_owner`).
3.  Governance scopes are restricted to artifacts (`t_policy_only_governs_artifacts`).
4.  Enforcement mechanisms are linked to valid claims (`t_policy_min_one_enforcer`).
5.  Descriptive claims are supported by directive evidence (`t_claim_min_one_evidence`).

Any violation of these constraints—such as a null owner, a non-artifact scope, or a broken enforcement link—constitutes a material weakness in the governance framework.
