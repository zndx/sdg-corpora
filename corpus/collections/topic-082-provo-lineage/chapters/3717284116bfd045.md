# Chapter 4: Verification Protocols and Evidence Generation in Governance Frameworks

This chapter delineates the structural requirements for verification processes within high-assurance governance frameworks. It establishes the formal relationship between procedural execution and the generation of evidentiary artifacts, ensuring that all compliance activities are auditable and traceable. The focus is restricted to the ontological definition of verification as a process subclass and the mandatory production of evidence. Readers should refer to the accompanying data tables for specific class mappings and operational constraints.

## 1. Verification Processes as Occurrents

In the context of governance and compliance, a verification process is not merely a static definition but a temporal entity—a span of time during which specific operations occur. Ontologically, this is formalized by asserting that the verification class is a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process). A process, in the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO), is an occurrent that has temporal parts and unfolds in time. Unlike continuants (such as documents or hardware), a process exists only from its initiation to its termination.

### 1.2 The Imperative of Production

The defining characteristic of a verification process within this framework is its capacity to produce output. It is insufficient for a process to simply execute; it must generate a tangible artifact that serves as a proxy for the execution itself. This is governed by the axiom `sdg:producesEvidence min 1 {Y:Class}`. The "min 1" cardinality constraint is critical: a process instance that completes without generating at least one evidence instance is considered invalid, null, or non-compliant.

This requirement anchors the abstract concept of "verification" to the physical or digital reality of "auditability." Without the production of evidence, the process cannot be verified by an external auditor or an automated governance agent.

## 2. Evidence Artifacts and Class Mapping

The output of the verification process (`Y`) is the Evidence Class. Evidence is defined here as any persistable record that can be queried to prove that the process (`X`) occurred with specific parameters. The relationship between the process and the evidence is deterministic; the nature of the process dictates the class of evidence required.

### 2.1 Evidence Stakeholders and Custodians

The generation of evidence is not an end in itself; it serves the needs of various stakeholders within the governance ecosystem. The following list identifies the primary actors responsible for the definition, consumption, and custody of the evidence artifacts defined in Section 3.
