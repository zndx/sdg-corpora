# Chapter 4: Verification Protocols and Evidence Generation in Industrial Compliance

This chapter delineates the procedural requirements for verification protocols within high-pressure fluid management systems, specifically focusing on the governance relationship between operational processes and the evidence they generate. The scope encompasses the definition of verification entities as processes, the mandatory production of evidence artifacts, and the structural constraints ensuring auditability. Technical specifications for media compatibility, environmental thresholds, and data persistence are provided to ensure compliance with ISO 9001 quality management standards and industrial safety directives.

## 1. Verification Processes as Governance Entities

In the context of industrial control systems, a verification process is formally defined as a specific class of occurrent entity (bfo:Process) that validates the state or functionality of a system component. Unlike continuous operations, a verification process is discrete, bounded by a start event and a termination event, and is executed with the intent to confirm adherence to predefined tolerances.

### 1.1 Process Classification and Parameters

Verification processes are categorized by their interaction with system media and the physical constraints of the operating environment. These processes are not abstract software routines but physical interventions that must be logged to satisfy regulatory frameworks. The execution of a verification process is subject to strict environmental and media parameters to ensure the validity of the resulting evidence.

Media: Hydraulic Fluid – Light Oil – Gas – Air
Pressure range: 0.5 to 140 Bar max
Media temperature: -15°C to +145°C max
Ambient temperature: -15°C to +50°C
Media viscosity: 50 centistokes max
Mounting: Coil upright / Solenoid direct
Opening / Closing time: 200 mSec Max

### 1.2 Process Execution

The execution of a verification process involves the actuation of control valves or sensors to capture state data. For instance, a "Pressure Safety Valve Test" constitutes a process wherein the valve is actuated to measure the cracking pressure. The process is valid only if it occurs within the specified ambient and media temperature ranges. Deviations outside the -15°C to +50°C ambient window may result in viscosity anomalies that invalidate the process output, rendering any generated evidence non-compliant.

## 2. Evidence Artifacts and Data Integrity

The output of a verification process is the evidence artifact. In compliance governance, evidence is defined as a persistent data entity that records the outcome of a process. This evidence must be immutable, timestamped, and stored within a secure repository to facilitate audit trails.

### 2.1 Evidence Types

Evidence artifacts vary in format depending on the nature of the verification process. They may include binary time-series data from sensor arrays, digitized certificates from calibration equipment, or event logs from emergency shutdown systems. The critical requirement is that the evidence must be directly attributable to the specific process instance that generated it.

### 2.2 Storage and Retention

Evidence artifacts must adhere to strict retention policies. For high-pressure systems, evidence retention periods typically span the operational lifecycle of the component plus a statutory period (e.g., 10 years post-decommissioning). Storage nodes must be redundant and write-once, read-many (WORM) compliant to prevent tampering.

## 3. The Verification-Evidence Dependency

The foundational axiom of this governance framework dictates that every verification process is structurally bound to the production of evidence. Formally, this is expressed as the constraint that a verification process produces a minimum of one evidence artifact. This dependency ensures that no verification activity occurs without a corresponding auditable record.

### 3.1 Axiomatic Constraints

The relationship between the process and the evidence is not merely causal but constitutive. If a process is executed and fails to produce an evidence artifact—due to sensor failure, data corruption, or logging interruption—the process is considered incomplete and non-compliant. ## 4. Implementation Standards and Technical Specifications

To ensure the reliable generation of evidence artifacts, verification processes must be implemented using hardware and software that meet specific technical grades. The following section details the specifications for evidence capture systems and the environmental constraints necessary for valid data collection.

Evidence capture systems must be capable of recording high-frequency data without latency that could obscure transient events. The specifications below outline the minimum requirements for data logging units associated with the verification processes listed in Section 3.

### 4.2 Environmental and Media Constraints

The integrity of the evidence artifact is contingent upon the environmental conditions prevailing during the verification process. The following constraints apply to all processes defined in Table 3.1:

*   **Media Compatibility:** Processes involving light oil or hydraulic fluid require seals compatible with Nitrile (NBR) or Viton, depending on temperature exposure.
*   **Viscosity Limits:** Media viscosity must not exceed 50 centistokes at the operating temperature. Higher viscosity may delay valve actuation beyond the 200 mSec maximum opening time, resulting in timestamp skew in the evidence artifact.
*   **Electrical Standards:** Coils and solenoids must adhere to IP65 ingress protection standards when deployed in environments where ambient humidity exceeds 90% or where media spray is a risk.

### 4.3 Non-Compliance Scenarios

In scenarios where the evidence artifact `y` cannot be generated—for example, if the Storage Node `Node_Archive_01` is offline—the verification process `x` must be aborted. The system must flag the process as "Attempted - No Evidence" in the audit log. This ensures that the axiom `producesEvidence min 1` is preserved; the process record exists, but its status explicitly reflects the failure to satisfy the evidence constraint, thereby maintaining the integrity of the compliance data set.
