# Chapter 4: Mapping Regulatory Directives to Compliance Frameworks

## Scope and Preamble

This chapter provides a comprehensive technical analysis of the alignment between internal information control elements and external regulatory mandates. It details the formalization of compliance directives through the lens of the `DirectiveICE` class, establishing precise mappings to the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS), the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) Security Rule, and the ISO/IEC 27001 information security management standard. The objective is to define the structural relationships between organizational governance artifacts and the specific requirements or safeguards they satisfy, ensuring traceability and auditability within a compliance management system.

## 4.1 PCI DSS Requirement Mapping

The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) prescribes a robust set of requirements intended to ensure the secure handling of cardholder data. Within the context of the ontology, a `DirectiveICE` represents an internal policy, procedure, or technical control implemented by an entity. The axiom `pci_dss_requirement` formalizes the relationship where a specific class of directive is a subclass of `DirectiveICE` and satisfies a specific PCI DSS requirement class. This mapping is critical for establishing a line of evidence during a Level 1 on-site review or a Report on Compliance (ROC). It ensures that every administrative or technical action taken by the organization is directly traceable to a mandate within the PCI DSS framework (e.g., Requirement 3.1 regarding retention policy or Requirement 8.2 regarding authentication).

The relational schema for this axiom defines a primary key `id` for the mapping record, with `x` representing the internal directive class and `y` representing the external PCI DSS requirement class it fulfills. This structure allows for a one-to-many relationship where a single directive may address multiple requirements, though the table structure presented here captures distinct mapping assertions.

### 4.1.1 Implementation Evidence
When implementing `DataRetentionDirective` (map_pci_001), the organization must demonstrate that the directive's operational parameters strictly adhere to `PCI_DSS_Req_3_1`, which mandates that retention policies include a limit for data storage and a disposal process. The mapping asserts that the directive is not merely a general policy but a specific subclass of information control designed to meet this exact requirement. Similarly, the `FirewallConfigurationDirective` must provide evidence of rule sets reviewed every six months, satisfying the necessity for `PCI_DSS_Req_1_1`.

## 4.2 HIPAA Technical Safeguards

The Security Rule under HIPAA necessitates the implementation of technical safeguards to protect electronic Protected Health Information (ePHI). The axiom `hipaa_safeguard_technical` addresses the technology and the policy and procedures for its use that protect electronic health information and control access to it. This section focuses on the automated mechanisms, distinct from physical or administrative measures, used to enforce confidentiality, integrity, and availability. The ontology classifies these directives as subclasses of `DirectiveICE` that specifically realize `hipaaSafeguard` types falling under the "Technical" specification category (45 CFR § 164.312).

The `TransmissionEncryptionProtocol` (map_hipaa_tech_01) serves as a concrete implementation of the `HIPAA_Technical_TransmissionSecurity` safeguard. In an audit context, the existence of this directive class within the ontology provides the semantic proof that the organization has addressed the requirement to guard against unauthorized access to ePHI being transmitted over an electronic communications network. Furthermore, the `UniqueUserSessionLog` (map_hipaa_tech_02) maps to the Audit Controls safeguard, necessitating hardware, software, and/or procedural mechanisms that record and examine activity in information systems that contain or use ePHI.

## 4.3 HIPAA Physical Safeguards

Physical safeguards are measures to protect physical electronic information systems and related buildings and equipment from natural and environmental hazards, as well as unauthorized intrusion. The axiom `hipaa_safeguard_physical` categorizes directives that govern the tangible aspects of security. These directives, while still subclasses of `DirectiveICE`, are distinct in that they prescribe behaviors regarding facility access, workstation usage, and device security. The ontology maps these directives to the specific physical safeguard definitions outlined in 45 CFR § 164.310.

The schema for this axiom utilizes `id` as the primary key to track the relationship between the directive class `x` and the physical safeguard class `y`. This structure is essential for facility managers and security officers who must demonstrate that physical access controls—such as badge readers or security guards—are formally governed by directives mapped to regulatory requirements.

### 4.3.1 Facility and Workstation Security
The `FacilityAccessControlPolicy` (map_hipaa_phys_01) is a critical directive class that maps to `HIPAA_Physical_FacilityAccessControls`. This directive must include procedures for controlling and validating a person's access to facilities based on their role and function, including visitor control and access to emergency power systems. Similarly, the `WorkstationPlacementDirective` (map_hipaa_phys_02) ensures that workstations are positioned to minimize unauthorized viewing of ePHI, satisfying the `HIPAA_Physical_WorkstationSecurity` safeguard. The mapping provides the necessary evidence that physical layout considerations are not merely aesthetic choices but compliance-driven directives.

## 4.4 HIPAA Administrative Safeguards

Administrative safeguards are administrative actions, policies, and procedures to manage the selection, development, implementation, and maintenance of security measures to protect electronic protected health information. These actions also manage the conduct of the covered entity's workforce in relation to the protection of that information. The axiom `hipaa_safeguard_admin` captures the governance layer of the compliance framework. Directives in this category (Class X) are typically high-level policies that dictate the management of security risks.

In the relational schema, `id` identifies the specific mapping between the administrative directive class `x` and the administrative safeguard class `y`. This mapping is foundational for the Security Management Process standard, requiring covered entities to implement policies and procedures to prevent, detect, contain, and correct security violations.

By mapping this directive to the safeguard, the organization formalizes its commitment to periodic risk assessments. Additionally, the `WorkforceSanctionPolicy` (map_hipaa_admin_02) maps to the `HIPAA_Admin_WorkforceSecurity` safeguard, ensuring that there are appropriate sanctions against workforce members who fail to comply with the security policies and procedures of the entity.

## 4.5 ISO 27001 Annex A Controls

ISO/IEC 27001:2013 specifies the requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an information security management system (ISMS). Annex A provides a list of information security controls (or objectives) that are considered to be best practice. The axiom `iso27001_annexa_subclass` maps internal directives to these specific Annex A controls. Unlike HIPAA, which is prescriptive, ISO 27001 is risk-based, allowing organizations to select applicable controls from Annex A. Therefore, the mapping defined by this axiom is evidence of the Statement of Applicability (SoA).

The relational schema for this axiom uses `id` as the primary key to track the relationship between the directive class `x` and the ISO 27001 Annex A control class `y`. This structure supports the audit process by demonstrating that specific control objectives (e.g., A.9.1.1 Access Control Policy) have been instantiated within the organization's directive framework.

The `AccessControlPolicyDirective` (map_iso_001) maps to `ISO_AnnexA_A_9_1_1`, establishing the requirement for an access control policy that is documented, reviewed, and communicated. This directive serves as the governing document for all subsequent access control measures. The `UserAccessReviewProcedure` (map_iso_002) maps to `ISO_AnnexA_A_9_2_6`, requiring the organization to review access rights at regular intervals. Through these mappings, the ontology provides a structured representation of the organization's ISMS, linking the abstract control objectives of Annex A to concrete, enforceable directives within the enterprise.
