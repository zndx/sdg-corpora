# Chapter 4: Regulatory Compliance Mapping and Directive Ontologies

## 4.1 Scope and Framework Applicability

This chapter establishes the formal ontological mappings required to align internal governance artifacts—specifically Information Control Elements (ICE)—with external regulatory frameworks. In the context of enterprise compliance management, a directive serves as the primary vehicle for translating abstract legal requirements into actionable technical controls. The following sections delineate the precise subclass relationships between internal directives and the specific control objectives defined by the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS), the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA), and the ISO/IEC 27001 information security standard. Each section provides a formal verbalization of the governing axiom, a technical exposition of the concept, and a data table illustrating the instantiation of these relationships within a compliance database.

## 4.2 PCI DSS Requirement Directives

The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) prescribes a robust set of requirements intended to ensure the secure handling of cardholder data. From an ontological perspective, the alignment between an internal governance framework and PCI DSS is modeled by the axiom that a specific class of directives is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE`, which necessitates the satisfaction of a PCI DSS requirement.

**Axiom 1 (template_id: pci_dss_requirement):**
*   **Manchester Syntax:** `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:pciDssRequirement some {Y:Class}`
*   **Verbalization:** {X} is something that pci dss requirement {Y}

In this schema, {X} represents the internal policy or procedural directive (e.g., a firewall configuration rule), while {Y} represents the specific PCI DSS requirement identifier (e.g., "Requirement 1.1.1"). This relationship asserts that the existence and enforcement of directive {X} are logically sufficient to partially satisfy the external compliance obligation {Y}. The mapping is granular; a single directive may address multiple requirements, or a complex requirement may necessitate a suite of directives.

The following table illustrates the instantiation of this axiom, mapping specific internal directives to their corresponding PCI DSS requirements. The primary key is the directive identifier, which serves as the unique reference for the compliance artifact.

## 4.3 HIPAA Technical Safeguards

The Security Rule established under HIPAA requires covered entities to implement technical safeguards to protect electronic Protected Health Information (ePHI). Technical safeguards refer to the technology and the policy and procedures for its use that protect electronic health information and control access to it. The ontological definition for technical safeguards asserts that a specific class of directives is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE`, which implements a HIPAA technical safeguard.

**Axiom 2 (template_id: hipaa_safeguard_technical):**
*   **Manchester Syntax:** `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:hipaaSafeguard some {Y:Class}`
*   **Verbalization:** {X} is something that hipaa safeguard {Y}

Here, {X} denotes the technical directive, such as an encryption protocol or an access control mechanism, and {Y} denotes the specific HIPAA technical safeguard specification (e.g., "164.312(a)(2)(i)" - Unique User Identification). This axiom is critical for audit trails, as it provides the evidence that automated controls are in place to satisfy the regulatory code. Unlike administrative measures, technical safeguards are typically executable and automatable, reducing the reliance on human adherence to procedure.

**Table 4.2: HIPAA Technical Safeguard Implementation**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| dir_hip_tech_01 | UniqueUserAuthenticationProcedure | 164.312(d) |
| dir_hip_tech_02 | EmergencyAccessProcedure | 164.312(a)(2)(ii) |
| dir_hip_tech_03 | AutomaticLogoffMechanism | 164.312(a)(2)(iii) |
| dir_hip_tech_04 | EncryptionAndDecryptionProtocol | 164.312(a)(2)(iv) |
| dir_hip_tech_05 | AuditControlMechanism | 164.312(b) |

*In Table 4.2, the `EncryptionAndDecryptionProtocol` (x) is the directive governing the implementation of cryptographic logic, directly satisfying the HIPAA safeguard addressing transmission security (y). The table structure ensures that every technical directive is traceable to a specific section of the regulatory code.*

## 4.4 HIPAA Physical Safeguards

Physical safeguards are measures to protect physical electronic information systems and related buildings and equipment from natural and environmental hazards, as well as unauthorized intrusion. The ontological mapping for physical safeguards follows a structure identical to technical safeguards but focuses on facility security and hardware management.

**Axiom 3 (template_id: hipaa_safeguard_physical):**
*   **Manchester Syntax:** `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:hipaaSafeguard some {Y:Class}`
*   **Verbalization:** {X} is something that hipaa safeguard {Y}

In this context, {X} represents directives regarding physical access (e.g., badge readers, security guards) or workstation usage, while {Y} represents the physical safeguard clauses within the HIPAA Security Rule (e.g., "164.310(a)(1)" - Facility Access Controls). This distinction is vital for compliance officers conducting site inspections; it separates the logical access controls (Technical) from the tangible environmental controls (Physical).

**Table 4.3: HIPAA Physical Safeguard Implementation**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| dir_hip_phys_01 | FacilityAccessControlPolicy | 164.310(a)(1) |
| dir_hip_phys_02 | WorkstationSecurityProtocol | 164.310(d) |
| dir_hip_phys_03 | DeviceAndMediaDisposalProcedure | 164.310(d)(2)(i) |
| dir_hip_phys_04 | SelfServiceKioskRestriction | 164.310(b) |
| dir_hip_phys_05 | MaintenanceRecordRetention | 164.310(d)(1) |

*Table 4.3 encodes the physical security posture. For example, the `WorkstationSecurityProtocol` (x) mandates the placement of screens and locking of terminals, fulfilling the `Workstation Security` requirements (y) defined in 164.310(d).*

## 4.5 HIPAA Administrative Safeguards

Administrative safeguards are administrative actions, policies, and procedures to manage the selection, development, implementation, and maintenance of security measures to protect electronic protected health information. These safeguards also manage the conduct of the covered entity’s workforce in relation to the protection of ePHI.

**Axiom 4 (template_id: hipaa_safeguard_admin):**
*   **Manchester Syntax:** `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:hipaaSafeguard some {Y:Class}`
*   **Verbalization:** {X} is something that hipaa safeguard {Y}

For administrative safeguards, {X} typically refers to high-level policy documents, training programs, or risk assessment methodologies. {Y} refers to the administrative requirements of the HIPAA Security Rule (e.g., "164.308(a)(1)" - Risk Analysis). This category often represents the most complex aspect of compliance, as it requires ongoing human oversight and procedural discipline rather than static configuration.

| dir_hip_admin_01 | RiskAnalysisProcess | 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A) |

| dir_hip_admin_03 | SanctionPolicy | 164.308(a)(3)(ii)(B) |
| dir_hip_admin_04 | InformationAccessManagementPolicy | 164.308(a)(4)(ii)(i) |
| dir_hip_admin_05 | SecurityIncidentResponsePlan | 164.308(a)(6)(i) |

*As shown in Table 4.4, the `SanctionPolicy` (x) provides the governance framework for penalizing non-compliance, thereby satisfying the HIPAA requirement for workforce sanctions (y). This mapping ensures that administrative procedures are formally documented and enforceable.*

## 4.6 ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A Controls

ISO/IEC 27001 is the international standard for Information Security Management Systems (ISMS). Annex A of the standard provides a list of information security controls objectives and controls. The ontological relationship defined here maps internal directives to these specific Annex A controls.

**Axiom 5 (template_id: iso27001_annexa_subclass):**
*   **Manchester Syntax:** `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:iso27001AnnexA some {Y:Class}`
*   **Verbalization:** {X} is something that iso27001 annex a {Y}

In this axiom, {X} is the internal directive, and {Y} is the specific ISO 27001 Annex A control (e.g., "A.9.2.1" - User access management). This mapping is essential for organizations seeking ISO certification, as it demonstrates that their internal control library addresses the comprehensive set of controls outlined in Annex A. Unlike the prescriptive nature of HIPAA, ISO 27001 allows for flexibility in implementation, making the precise mapping of {X} to {Y} a critical exercise in gap analysis.
