# Chapter 4: Spatial and Fiscal Identification of Physical Artifacts

## 4.1 Scope and Regulatory Framework

This chapter establishes the mandatory protocols for the spatial and fiscal identification of physical artifacts within the Global Infrastructure Registry (GIR). It delineates the precise axiomatic relationships between tangible assets and their descriptive attributes, specifically postal addresses, geospatial coordinates, and tax identifiers. The guidelines provided herein ensure that every registered artifact maintains a verifiable chain of custody and location data, compliant with international auditing standards. The following sections detail the implementation of these axioms, supported by relational data structures that enforce referential integrity and data consistency across the registry.

## 4.2 Postal Address Integration

### 4.2.1 Regulatory Basis and Definition

The identification of a physical artifact is predicated on its association with a standardized postal address. This linkage is not merely operational but is a foundational requirement for legal jurisdiction, service delivery, and regulatory compliance. Pursuant to the `schemaorg_place_address` axiom, every physical artifact (Class X) must be subclassified as an `cco:Artifact` and possess a `sdg:hasPostalAddress` relationship to a valid postal address entity (Class Y). This relationship ensures that the artifact is anchored to a recognized civic location, facilitating unambiguous communication and logistical planning.

### 4.2.2 Implementation Guidelines

In practice, the assignment of a postal address to an artifact involves the validation of the address string against national postal databases. The artifact record must retain a direct, immutable link to this address. It is observed that failures in maintaining this linkage often result in service disruptions during audit cycles. Therefore, the registry enforces a strict foreign-key constraint where the artifact identifier references a specific address entry.

## 4.3 Geospatial Coordinate Systems

### 4.3.1 The Shift to Digital Precision

While postal addresses provide civic context, they lack the precision required for modern geospatial analysis, emergency response, and automated logistics. Consequently, the framework mandates the assignment of precise geospatial coordinates to all registered artifacts. The `schemaorg_place_geocoord` axiom stipulates that an Artifact (Class X) must have a `sdg:hasGeoCoordinates` relationship to a specific coordinate set (Class Y). This requirement facilitates the integration of physical assets into Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and allows for real-time tracking and spatial auditing.

### 4.3.2 Coordinate Reference Standards

All coordinate values must adhere to the World Geodetic System 1984 (WGS 84) standard to ensure global interoperability. The use of local or legacy datums is strictly prohibited unless a rigorous transformation matrix is applied and documented in the artifact's metadata. The coordinate entity serves as a composite object that encapsulates the spatial identity of the artifact, distinct from its descriptive address.

## 4.4 Latitude Specification

### 4.4.1 Technical Definition and Constraints

Latitude defines the north-south position of a point on the Earth's surface. Within the registry, the `schemaorg_place_latitude` axiom requires that an Artifact (Class X) has a `sdg:hasLatitude` relationship to a specific latitude value (Class Y). This value must be expressed in decimal degrees, with a minimum precision of six decimal places (approximately 0.11 meter accuracy). This level of precision is critical for high-stakes environments such as power distribution and hazardous material storage.

### 4.4.2 Validation and Audit Protocols

Auditors routinely verify latitude data to ensure that artifacts are not erroneously recorded in restricted zones or maritime boundaries. The data entry system must enforce range checks, rejecting values outside the interval [-90, 90]. Furthermore, the latitude value is treated as a distinct class entity within the ontology, allowing for the attachment of metadata regarding the measurement method and timestamp.

### 4.4.3 Relational Data Representation

The `t_schemaorg_place_latitude` table captures this specific attribute relationship. The `id` serves as the primary key for the assertion. The `x` column links to the Artifact, and the `y` column contains the latitude value. This schema enforces the axiom's requirement that the latitude is a fundamental, non-nullable property of the artifact's spatial definition.

## 4.5 Fiscal Identification (Tax ID)

### 4.5.1 Compliance and Fiscal Responsibility

The integration of fiscal identifiers into the physical registry is a mandate derived from cross-border tax compliance regulations. The `schemaorg_org_taxid` axiom asserts that an Artifact (Class X) must possess a `sdg:hasTaxID` relationship to a valid tax identifier (Class Y). This linkage associates the physical asset with the legal entity responsible for its taxation, ensuring that property taxes, customs duties, and operational levies can be accurately assessed and collected.

### 4.5.2 Jurisdictional Variations

Tax IDs vary significantly by jurisdiction; they may include Employer Identification Numbers (EIN), VAT numbers, or GST identifiers. The registry treats the Tax ID as a class of data that must be validated against the issuing authority's algorithmic checks (e.g., mod-10 checks). It is imperative that the Tax ID is current and active; inactive or revoked IDs will trigger an immediate compliance alert in the system.

## 4.6 Longitude Specification

### 4.6.1 Technical Definition and Constraints

Longitude specifies the east-west position of a point on the Earth's surface, referenced from the Prime Meridian. The `schemaorg_place_longitude` axiom dictates that an Artifact (Class X) must have a `sdg:hasLongitude` relationship to a specific longitude value (Class Y). Like latitude, this value must be recorded in decimal degrees with high precision. Positive values indicate locations east of the Prime Meridian, while negative values indicate locations west.

### 4.6.2 Synchronization with Latitude

Longitude data must be collected and updated in strict synchronization with latitude data to prevent spatial drift. The registry maintains a temporal log of coordinate changes to track the movement of mobile artifacts or the correction of survey errors. Discrepancies between the recorded longitude and the physical location of the artifact are considered critical non-conformities.

### 4.6.3 Relational Data Representation

The final table, `t_schemaorg_place_longitude`, represents the `schemaorg_place_longitude` axiom. The `id` column is the primary key for the relationship instance. The `x` column references the Artifact, and the `y` column contains the longitude value. This completes the spatial definition of the artifact, ensuring that the location is fully defined by the pair of coordinate classes.
