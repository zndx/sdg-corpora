# Chapter 7: Physical Asset Verification and Site Governance Protocols

This chapter establishes the ontological framework for the identification, verification, and governance of physical artifacts within the supply chain and regulatory compliance environments. It delineates the mandatory mapping of postal addresses, geospatial coordinates, and tax identifiers to specific artifact classes, ensuring that all physical entities are auditable against the Corporate Compliance Ontology (cco) and Sustainable Development Goals (sdg) interface standards. The protocols detailed herein are binding upon all entities engaged in the storage, transit, and reporting of physical assets.

## 1. Postal Address Assignment and Verification

The assignment of a postal address to a physical artifact is a fundamental requirement for establishing legal jurisdiction and logistical reachability. Under the current governance framework, any entity classified as a `cco:Artifact` must possess a distinct and verifiable postal address. This relationship is not merely associative but structural; the artifact is ontologically defined by its capacity to possess an address.

### 1.1 Regulatory Context

The verification of postal addresses serves as the primary control mechanism for ensuring that physical assets are not "orphaned" within the digital ledger. Recent updates to the compliance handbook have emphasized the need for granular address data, moving beyond simple city-level designations to full street-level specificity.

### 1.2 Data Schema and Implementation

The axiom governing this relationship asserts that `{X} is something that has postal address {Y}`. In this formulation, `X` represents the artifact (e.g., a specific warehouse, a sensor unit, or a transit vehicle), and `Y` represents the postal address object itself. The relational projection of this axiom requires a unique identifier for each relationship, linking the artifact ID to the address string.

The following table illustrates the implementation of this axiom for a subset of high-priority assets. The primary key is the `id` column, ensuring that each address assignment is distinct and traceable.

## 2. Geospatial Coordinate Integration

While postal addresses provide a human-readable reference, the integration of geospatial coordinates provides the precision necessary for automated systems, drone delivery logistics, and satellite monitoring. The axiom `{X} is something that has geo coordinates {Y}` establishes that every artifact must be linked to a specific coordinate set.

### 2.1 Technical Specifications

The geospatial sensors illustrated below are generally used for surface coordinate verification. The series GS and GT spade GPS modules are sandwiched between two thin shims of either stainless steel or two pieces of fiberglass tape. They can be attached using a worm drive hose clamp or by placing under heater bands. These spades can be formed and secured to the outside of various size artifacts to ensure constant geospatial tracking without interfering with the artifact's primary function.

The integration of these sensors allows for the real-time updating of the `sdg:hasGeoCoordinates` property. This property is not static; it must be refreshed at intervals defined by the artifact's mobility class. Static assets (e.g., warehouses) require quarterly verification, while mobile assets (e.g., transit vehicles) require continuous streaming updates.

## 3. Latitude Determination and Scalar Values

The decomposition of geospatial coordinates into scalar components—specifically latitude—is essential for granular filtering and regulatory reporting. The axiom `{X} is something that has latitude {Y}` mandates that the latitude value be treated as a distinct property of the artifact, enabling queries based on latitudinal bands (e.g., "show all assets north of 45°N").

### 3.1 Scalar Precision Requirements

Latitude values must be recorded with a minimum of six decimal places to satisfy the precision requirements of the Global Asset Tracking System (GATS). This level of precision corresponds to approximately 0.11 meters of accuracy on the ground, sufficient for distinguishing between adjacent loading docks or storage units.

## 4. Longitude Determination and Scalar Values

Complementary to latitude, the longitude property provides the second axis of geospatial identity. The axiom `{X} is something that has longitude {Y}` functions identically to the latitude axiom in terms of structure but requires distinct validation logic to ensure correct longitudinal placement relative to the Prime Meridian.

### 4.1 Hemispherical Validation

A critical compliance check involves the validation of the sign (+/-) of the longitude scalar. Positive values must correspond to East longitude, while negative values must correspond to West longitude. Automated validation scripts are employed to flag any artifacts where the longitude value contradicts the known regional placement of the artifact's postal address.

## 5. Organizational Tax Identification Linkage

The final axiom in this chapter bridges the physical artifact with the legal entity that owns or operates it. The axiom `{X} is something that has tax id {Y}` asserts that artifacts, particularly those classified as `cco:Artifact`, must be linked to a valid Tax ID. This linkage is crucial for fiscal compliance, asset depreciation calculations, and inter-jurisdictional tax reporting.

### 5.1 Legal Entity Mapping

While the artifact is the physical subject of the ontology, the Tax ID slot anchors it to a legal persona. In many jurisdictions, the failure to accurately link high-value assets to a Tax ID can result in severe penalties, including the seizure of the asset or the revocation of operating licenses.

### 5.2 Registry Compliance

The Tax ID values stored in the `y` slot must be valid according to the issuing authority's algorithm (e.g., the Luhn algorithm for certain VAT numbers or specific checksums for EINs). The system performs a real-time validation upon entry.
