# Chapter 7: Geospatial Attribution and Fiscal Identification in Asset Governance

This chapter establishes the protocols for defining physical and organizational artifacts within the governance framework, specifically focusing on the precise mapping of spatial and fiscal attributes. It details the axiomatic relationships between entities and their associated postal addresses, geodetic coordinates, and tax identifiers, ensuring data integrity across distributed ledgers. The following sections provide a rigorous breakdown of the ontology schema required for compliance reporting, emphasizing the necessity of unambiguous location and identification data for audit trails.

## 1. Postal Address Attribution

In the context of asset governance, the ability to pinpoint a physical artifact to a valid, standardized postal address is a foundational requirement for regulatory compliance and logistical execution. The axiom governing this relationship posits that an Artifact (X) must possess a Postal Address (Y). This linkage is not merely descriptive but functional; it serves as the primary mechanism for jurisdiction determination and service delivery verification. Within the compliance framework, the `cco:Artifact` class encompasses physical locations, facilities, and tangible assets that require distinct addressing to resolve ownership or operational responsibility.

### 1.1 Definition and Scope
The property `sdg:hasPostalAddress` establishes a necessary link between the asset and its location descriptor. This relationship ensures that every registered artifact is associated with a string or object representing a valid address format, conforming to local postal standards. For auditors, this data point is critical when verifying the physical existence of assets declared in financial statements or operational inventories. Without this binding, an artifact exists in the ledger as an orphaned entity, lacking the physical grounding required for legal enforcement or tax assessment.

## 2. Geospatial Coordinate Assignment

While postal addresses provide human-readable location data, they are often insufficient for high-precision auditing, automated logistics, or environmental monitoring. Consequently, the governance framework mandates that every Artifact (X) be associated with a specific set of GeoCoordinates (Y). This axiom, defined by the property `sdg:hasGeoCoordinates`, shifts the locus of definition from semantic address strings to numerical, mathematically precise spatial data. This transition is essential for integrating physical asset data with Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and satellite monitoring tools used in regulatory oversight.

### 2.1 Definition and Scope
The assignment of geo-coordinates allows for the calculation of precise distances, the determination of inclusion within specific zones (e.g., tax zones, environmental protection areas), and the verification of asset location against reported coordinates. The `cco:Artifact` subclass here implies that the object has a physical footprint or fixed location that can be sensor-verified. In audit scenarios, discrepancies between the declared coordinates and the sensor-detected location often trigger compliance alerts regarding asset misappropriation or unauthorized relocation.

### 2.2 Axiomatic Implementation
The relational representation of this concept links the Artifact directly to a GeoCoordinates object. This object acts as a container for the specific latitude and longitude values, enforcing a clean separation between the entity and its spatial properties. ## 3. Latitude Determination

For the geo-coordinates to be computationally useful, they must be decomposed into their scalar components: latitude and longitude. This section addresses the specific axiom regarding Latitude (Y), where an Artifact (X) has a Latitude value. Latitude represents the north-south position of a point on the Earth's surface and is a critical parameter for determining the hemisphere and climatic zone of an asset. In compliance frameworks, latitude data is frequently used to enforce regional regulations, such as export controls or environmental mandates that vary by parallel.

### 3.1 Definition and Scope
The property `sdg:hasLatitude` binds a floating-point value or a specific class representing that value to the Artifact. This granularity is necessary when the governance framework requires automated validation of an asset's location relative to specific latitudinal boundaries. For instance, certain hazardous materials may be restricted from storage above specific latitudes due to permafrost risks or other geological factors. Therefore, the latitude is not merely a coordinate component but a regulatory trigger.

These values are typically expressed in decimal degrees conforming to the WGS 84 standard. The relational schema treats the latitude value (`y`) as a class or literal instance directly linked to the artifact (`x`), ensuring that the spatial attribute is an intrinsic property of the entity record.

The table `t_schemaorg_place_latitude` embodies the axiom that an Artifact (X) has a Latitude (Y). The primary key `id` uniquely identifies the record; `x` is the Artifact, and `y` is the specific Latitude value, enabling high-precision spatial filtering.

## 4. Fiscal Identification via Tax ID

Moving from spatial to fiscal attributes, the governance framework requires that organizational artifacts be explicitly linked to their tax identification numbers. The axiom states that an Artifact (X), in this case representing an Organization or a taxable entity, has a Tax ID (Y). This linkage is the cornerstone of fiscal compliance, enabling the automated cross-referencing of asset holdings with tax obligations. It ensures that the entity managing or owning the artifact is financially transparent and accountable to the relevant revenue authorities.

### 4.1 Definition and Scope
The `sdg:hasTaxID` property serves as a unique identifier for the organization within the fiscal ecosystem. In audit reports, this field is mandatory for all entities engaging in taxable transactions. It allows the system to validate that the artifact is owned or operated by a legitimate, registered corporate body. The absence of a valid Tax ID in the registry typically results in an immediate compliance flag, preventing the entity from executing financial transactions until the discrepancy is rectified.

### 4.2 Axiomatic Implementation
The relational table for this axiom maps the organizational artifact to its specific tax identifier. This value is often validated against external government databases to ensure authenticity. ## 5. Longitude Determination

Complementing the latitude data, the final axiom in this chapter addresses the Longitude (Y) of an Artifact (X). Longitude defines the east-west position of the asset and is essential for determining time zone alignment and longitudinal regulatory zones. In conjunction with latitude, the longitude provides the complete geodetic coordinate pair necessary for unambiguous global positioning. This data is particularly vital for assets in transit or those spanning large geographic areas, where longitudinal changes can affect operational timing and regulatory jurisdiction.

### 5.1 Definition and Scope
The property `sdg:hasLongitude` captures the specific angular distance of the artifact east or west of the Prime Meridian. In the context of technical documentation and audit trails, longitude data is often used to correlate asset location with maritime boundaries, transnational shipping lanes, or specific longitudinal regulatory sectors defined by trade agreements. Accurate longitude recording is mandatory for assets operating in international waters or cross-border terrestrial zones.
