# Chapter 4: Audit Process Documentation and Findings Correlation

This chapter delineates the structural and semantic dependencies between audit processes and their resultant findings within the Enterprise Governance Ontology (EGO). It provides the necessary framework for classifying audit events as occurrents (processes) that necessarily generate dependent continuants (findings). The guidelines herein ensure that all audit documentation adheres to the rigorous standards required for regulatory compliance and internal governance, facilitating precise data mapping between the audit event and the specific observations or non-compliances discovered during the execution of said event.

## 1. The Audit Process as an Occurrent Entity

### 1.1 Ontological Definition and Classification

In accordance with the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) framework utilized by this organization, an Audit Process is formally defined not as a static object, but as an occurrent entity. Specifically, the Audit Process is a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process). This distinction is critical for temporal reasoning and data integrity; an audit is an entity that unfolds in time, has a start point and an end point, and occupies a specific temporal region. Unlike a material entity which exists continuously, the Audit Process exists only as long as the audit activities are being performed.

The formal axiom governing this classification is expressed as:
*Class: {Audit Process} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:hasFindings some {Finding}.*

This verbalization asserts that {Audit Process} is something that has findings {Finding}. This implies that an instance of an Audit Process cannot exist in a vacuum; by definition, its execution must result in the generation of at least one Finding, even if that finding is a confirmation of compliance (a "positive" finding).

### 1.2 Operational Context and Authorization

The operational execution of an Audit Process follows strict protocols analogous to established wildlife and environmental survey methodologies, requiring formal authorization and clear delineation of scope. Every Audit Process instance must be initiated via a formal charter or grant of authority, similar to the Pittman-Robertson Federal Aid in Wildlife Restoration grants.

For example, consider the "FY2024 Q3 Information Security Compliance Audit." This process is not merely a document but a temporally bounded investigation. It is authorized under Internal Governance Policy 402-A and executed by the designated Office of Internal Audit. The process encompasses the planning phase, fieldwork, data collection, and the final reporting phase. The temporal boundaries of this process are strictly recorded in the audit management system, ensuring that the `bfo:0000015` (Process) characteristics are preserved in the digital record.

### 1.3 Process Instances and Scope

The scope of an Audit Process defines the boundaries of the investigation. Whether the audit is focused on financial controls, operational efficiency, or regulatory adherence, the process remains the parent entity for all subsequent data. The "Audit Process" acts as the container for the evidence gathered.

To illustrate, a "Vendor Risk Assessment" (Class X) is a specific type of Audit Process. It involves the systematic review of third-party compliance documents. The process begins when the assessment is scheduled and concludes when the final risk rating is assigned. Throughout this duration, the process acts as the "subject" in the subject-predicate-object relationship required by the ontology.

## 2. Audit Findings as Dependent Continuants

### 2.1 Nature and Derivation of Findings

While the Audit Process is the occurrent that unfolds in time, the Audit Finding is a dependent continuant—a specific type of information entity that derives its existence from the process. A Finding (Class Y) represents the output of the audit. It is the realization of the audit's objective to identify gaps, risks, or confirmations of adherence.

The relationship between the Audit Process and the Finding is mediated by the object property `sdg:hasFindings`. This property is functional in nature; it links the specific instance of the process to the specific instances of the findings it generated. It is important to distinguish the Finding from the "Issue" itself. The Issue is the real-world problem (e.g., a broken lock), whereas the Finding is the formal record of that issue within the audit context.

### 2.2 Evidence and Verification

Every Finding (Y) must be anchored in evidence. This mirrors the evidence-anchored prose found in high-quality technical reports. A Finding is not merely an opinion; it is a documented assertion supported by data points, interviews, or physical observations.

For instance, if the Audit Process is "Annual Safety Inspection," a resulting Finding might be "Emergency Exit Obstruction." This Finding is valid only because the Audit Process generated it, and it is substantiated by the auditor's logbook and photographic evidence. The Finding does not exist prior to the process, nor does it exist independently of the audit record once the process is complete.

### 2.3 Distribution and Classification

Findings are often categorized by severity or type to facilitate reporting. Similar to the distribution of course results in academic reports, findings are aggregated to provide a histogram of compliance health. However, at the granular level required for the ontology, each finding must be treated as a distinct entity linked to its parent process.

## 3. Relational Mapping and Data Integrity

### 3.1 Schema Implementation

To enforce the axiom {Audit Process} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:hasFindings some {Finding}, the organization utilizes a relational schema that mandates the existence of a finding for every recorded audit process. This schema prevents the creation of "orphan" audit processes that claim to have occurred but produced no output.

The primary table for this relationship is `t_audit_with_findings`. This table embodies the axiom by structurally requiring a foreign key reference to a Finding (`y`) for every Audit Process (`x`). The primary key (`id`) ensures that every specific relationship instance is unique and traceable.

*Table Structure Explanation:*
The `t_audit_with_findings` table serves as the realization of the `sdg:hasFindings` object property.
*   **id (VARCHAR)**: The unique identifier for the specific relationship instance (Primary Key).
*   **x (VARCHAR)**: The identifier for the Audit Process Class or Instance (the domain).
*   **y (VARCHAR)**: The identifier for the Finding Class or Instance (the range). This column is set to `NOT NULL` to enforce the axiom that a process *must* have findings.
