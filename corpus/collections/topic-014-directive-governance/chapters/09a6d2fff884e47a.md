# Chapter 7: Ontological Structures for Metric Governance and Anomaly Detection

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures required to govern the generation, observation, and aggregation of metrics within a regulated digital environment. It establishes a rigorous framework for defining "Descriptive Information Content Entities" (DescriptiveICE), ensuring that all anomaly claims are traceable to their originating processes and associated alerting mechanisms. The definitions provided herein align with the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) and are designed to support high-fidelity audit trails, ensuring that every data point subject to compliance review is anchored by a verifiable provenance chain.

## 7.1 Basic Outlier Claims

In the context of continuous monitoring and control frameworks, the fundamental unit of observation is the Outlier Claim. Under the axiom `outlier_claim_basic`, this entity is classified strictly as a `cco:DescriptiveICE`. This classification asserts that the claim is not a physical object but a informational entity that describes a state of affairs—specifically, a deviation from a normative baseline.

For an auditor or a governance system, the basic outlier claim represents the atomic assertion that a specific metric has exceeded a defined threshold. It is devoid of complex relational attributes at this level of abstraction, serving merely as a container for the descriptive data indicating the presence of an anomaly. This distinction is critical for maintaining a clean separation between the raw observation of an event and the subsequent processing or alerting logic that may be applied to it.

## 7.2 Attributed Outlier Claims

While a basic outlier claim provides a descriptor, compliance frameworks often require that observations carry sufficient metadata to support automated remediation or human review. The `outlier_claim_min_one_attribute` axiom refines the basic definition by stipulating that the Outlier Claim (Class X) must possess a minimum of one attribute (Class Y). In this specific ontology, the attribute slot is structurally mapped to the alerting mechanism triggered by the claim.

This structure creates a bidirectional link between the observation and the system's response. The claim is no longer a passive data point; it is an active entity that possesses an attribute referencing the specific alert state it has engendered. This ensures that every outlier claim recorded in the system is inextricably linked to a corresponding alert event, satisfying the traceability requirements of modern audit standards.

## 7.3 Metric Emission Processes

To understand the origin of outlier claims, one must examine the processes that generate the underlying metrics. The `metric_emission_subclass` axiom defines a relationship wherein a specific Process (Class X), which is a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (a processual entity), emits a Metric (Class Y). In this schema, the emitted metric is realized as the Outlier Claim defined in previous sections.

This axiom grounds the informational entities in the physical or computational reality of the system. It asserts that the `DescriptiveICE` (the claim) did not manifest spontaneously but is the output of a distinct, observable process—such as a log aggregation daemon, a network sniffer, or a financial calculation engine. For governance purposes, this allows compliance officers to trace an anomaly back to the specific subsystem or process responsible for its generation, facilitating root cause analysis.

## 7.4 Alert Triggering Mechanisms

The operational response to an emitted metric is governed by the `alert_triggered_by` axiom. This axiom defines an Alert (Class X), also a processual entity (`bfo:0000015`), which is triggered by a specific Metric (Class Y). This formalizes the causal chain: the Process emits the Metric (Claim), and that Metric triggers the Alert.

It is imperative to note the circular integrity enforced by the schema between this section and Section 7.2. The Alert table references the Outlier Claim as its trigger, while the Outlier Claim table references the Alert as its attribute. This recursive structure ensures that the state of the system is self-consistent: an alert exists only if triggered by a valid claim, and a claim possesses the alert attribute only if that alert has been instantiated. This prevents "orphan" alerts or claims that lack corresponding system responses, a common finding in regulatory deficiencies.

## 7.5 Data Aggregation Entities

Finally, the ontology provides for the aggregation of lower-level data points into summary forms for reporting and strategic oversight. The `aggregation_basic` axiom defines an Aggregation (Class X) as a `cco:DescriptiveICE`. Unlike the outlier claims, which describe specific, atomic events, aggregations describe collective states or statistical summaries derived from multiple data points.

Aggregations are essential for the upper layers of the governance framework, such as dashboarding and executive reporting. They provide the "rolled-up" view of system health and compliance posture. However, ontologically, they remain distinct from the raw event data; they are descriptive entities that synthesize information rather than reporting on discrete process emissions. Distinguishing these classes prevents category errors in reporting, ensuring that summary statistics are not misinterpreted as granular event logs during forensic audits.
