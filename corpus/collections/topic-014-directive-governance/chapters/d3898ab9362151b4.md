# Chapter 4: Formal Ontologies for Operational Risk Monitoring and Metric Emission

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures required to govern the detection, reporting, and aggregation of operational anomalies within high-frequency trading environments. The definitions provided herein are grounded in the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) and the Common Core Ontologies (CCO), ensuring that all information content entities (ICE) adhere to rigorous semantic standards. The primary objective is to establish a deterministic framework for mapping outlier claims to their triggering metrics and the resultant alert processes. This framework facilitates the generation of audit-compliant data trails, ensuring that every deviation from expected system behavior is traceable to a specific process and descriptive attribute set. The following sections detail the specific axioms governing these relationships, accompanied by relational schema projections that enforce referential integrity across the monitoring infrastructure.

## 4.2 Outlier Claims with Attribute Sets

### 4.2.1 Conceptual Definition

In the context of continuous system monitoring, an outlier claim is defined as a specific subclass of a Descriptive Information Content Entity (DescriptiveICE). The axiom `outlier_claim_min_one_attribute` posits that for any entity classified as an outlier claim, there exists a mandatory constraint requiring the possession of at least one attribute set. This axiom is critical for ensuring that anomaly reports are not merely empty flags but are substantiated by a quantifiable or qualitative set of characteristics. Formally, if an entity $X$ is an instance of the class `OutlierClaim`, it must satisfy the condition `sdg:hasAttributeSet min 1 {Y}`, where $\{Y\}$ represents the attribute class.

This structure ensures that every outlier claim recorded in the governance ledger is semantically rich and capable of supporting forensic analysis. For instance, an outlier claim regarding a latency spike must include attributes such as the timestamp of the event, the magnitude of the deviation, and the affected instrument identifier. Without this mandatory attribute set, the claim lacks the evidentiary density required for regulatory reporting and automated remediation.

### 4.2.2 Relational Projection and Data

The relational schema derived from this axiom enforces that every instance of an outlier claim is linked to a specific attribute set. The primary key `id` serves as the unique identifier for the claim, while the foreign key `y` references the identifier of the attribute set, ensuring that no claim can exist without a corresponding attribute definition.

## 4.3 Alert Triggering Mechanisms

### 4.3.1 Conceptual Definition

The generation of an alert is modeled as a temporal process, specifically a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process). The axiom `alert_triggered_by` formalizes the causal relationship between the alert process and the metric that precipitated it. According to the Manchester syntax, `{X} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:triggeredByMetric some {Y}`. This verbalizes to: $X$ is a process that is triggered by metric $Y$.

This distinction is vital for audit trails because it separates the *event* (the alert firing) from the *cause* (the metric threshold breach). In compliance frameworks, it is insufficient to know that an alert occurred; the governing ontology requires the explicit identification of the metric value that crossed the defined threshold. This allows auditors to reconstruct the state of the system at the exact moment of the trigger, verifying whether the alert was valid, false, or the result of a manipulated data feed.

### 4.3.2 Relational Projection and Data

The schema for `t_alert_triggered_by` captures this causal link. The column `x` represents the alert process instance, while `y` represents the metric instance that triggered it. The foreign key constraint `REFERENCES t_outlier_claim_min_one_attribute(id)` establishes a cyclical dependency in the data model, implying that the metric triggering the alert is, in this specific ontology, formally categorized as an outlier claim with an attribute set. This recursive binding ensures that the alert is inextricably linked to the descriptive entity that defined the anomaly.

## 4.4 Basic Outlier Claims

### 4.4.1 Conceptual Definition

While complex outlier claims require attribute sets, the ontology also accommodates basic outlier claims through the axiom `outlier_claim_basic`. This axiom classifies entity $X$ simply as a subclass of `cco:DescriptiveICE`. These entities represent atomic observations or flags that do not require an internal complex structure of attributes for their immediate processing. They serve as the fundamental units of data within the monitoring system, representing raw signals prior to enrichment or correlation with other data points.

In practice, basic outlier claims are often generated by high-velocity sensors where the overhead of maintaining attribute sets is prohibitive. They act as precursors to more complex claims; a basic claim may be aggregated or enriched post-ingestion to become a full `outlier_claim_min_one_attribute`. However, at the moment of creation, they satisfy the basic requirement of being a descriptive information content entity.

### 4.4.2 Relational Projection and Data

The relational schema `t_outlier_claim_basic` is structurally simple, consisting only of a primary key `id` and the class identifier `x`. This table functions as a registry for all atomic anomaly flags generated by the system. The absence of foreign key constraints in this table reflects the standalone nature of these basic claims; they exist as independent observations within the database.

## 4.5 Metric Emission Processes

### 4.5.1 Conceptual Definition

The origin of metrics is formally defined by the axiom `metric_emission_subclass`. This axiom asserts that a process $X$ (subclass of `bfo:0000015`) emits a metric $Y$. The verbalization, "$X$ is something that emits metric $Y$," establishes the provenance of the data. In a regulated environment, understanding the source of a metric is as important as the metric itself. This axiom allows the governance framework to trace every metric back to the specific process, algorithm, or sensor that generated it.

This distinction is particularly relevant when diagnosing systemic issues. If multiple outlier claims are traced back to metrics emitted by a single process, it suggests a failure in that specific emitter rather than a distributed market anomaly. Consequently, this axiom supports root cause analysis and system health monitoring, providing a structural map of data flow from the emitter to the descriptive entity.

### 4.5.2 Relational Projection and Data

The schema `t_metric_emission_subclass` links the emitting process (`x`) to the emitted metric (`y`). The foreign key constraint `REFERENCES t_outlier_claim_min_one_attribute(id)` dictates that the metric being emitted is formally treated as an outlier claim with attributes. This reinforces the concept that in this system, "metrics" are not merely raw numerical values but are structured information content entities describing anomalies.

## 4.6 Basic Aggregations

### 4.6.1 Conceptual Definition

The final concept addressed in this chapter is the basic aggregation, defined by the axiom `aggregation_basic`. An aggregation is a descriptive information content entity (`cco:DescriptiveICE`) that summarizes or combines other data points. While the specific aggregation logic (e.g., sum, average, percentile) is not detailed in this axiom, the classification ensures that all summaries are treated as first-class informational entities within the ontology.

Aggregations are essential for high-level reporting and dashboarding. They reduce the noise of high-frequency outlier claims into manageable indicators of system health. By formally defining aggregations as DescriptiveICE, the ontology ensures that these summaries are subject to the same governance, access control, and audit requirements as the raw data they comprise. This prevents ambiguity where summaries might otherwise be treated as unstructured metadata.

### 4.6.2 Relational Projection and Data

The table `t_aggregation_basic` serves as a repository for these summary entities. Like the basic outlier claims, this table relies on a simple structure with `id` and `x` columns, reflecting its role as a container for descriptive entities that do not require the complex relational bindings of the alert or emission schemas.
