# Chapter 4: Anomaly Detection and Metric Emission in Compliance Frameworks

This chapter delineates the structural and functional requirements for integrating outlier detection mechanisms within enterprise compliance frameworks. It addresses the formalization of descriptive information content entities (ICE) as they pertain to audit trails, the procedural generation of alerts via metric triggers, and the systemic emission of metrics from underlying processes. The discussion is grounded in the necessity for high-fidelity data provenance and rigorous referential integrity, ensuring that all assertions regarding non-compliance or anomalous activity are substantiated by deterministic, auditable data structures.

## 4.1 Basic Outlier Claims

In the context of regulatory auditing, an outlier claim serves as the foundational unit of observation. Axiomatically defined as a subclass of the Common Core Ontology (CCO) Descriptive Information Content Entity, the basic outlier claim represents a declarative statement about a data point that deviates from established baselines. It is distinct from the process of detection; rather, it is the informational artifact resulting from such observation.

Under the `outlier_claim_basic` template, the entity is identified solely by its class designation. This represents the simplest form of anomaly registration, where the existence of the claim itself is the primary datum without immediate attachment to complex attribute sets or triggering processes. In practice, these claims often serve as the initial entries in a risk register, pending further enrichment.

## 4.2 Attributed Outlier Claims

While basic claims establish the presence of an anomaly, compliance protocols demand that such claims be substantiated by specific attributes. The `outlier_claim_min_one_attribute` axiom refines the basic definition by imposing a cardinality constraint: a valid descriptive ICE of this type must possess a minimum of one attribute set.

Structurally, this necessitates a dependency relationship where the claim (X) is linked to a specific attribute set (Y). In the relational schema, this is enforced by a foreign key constraint linking the attribute set to an entry in the alert subsystem. This design ensures that an outlier claim is not merely a label but is anchored to a procedural artifact that defines its characteristics. For instance, a claim regarding a "High Value Transaction" must be associated with an attribute set that defines the specific thresholds or metadata constituting the "high value" status.

## 4.3 Alert Triggering Mechanisms

The transition from static observation to active compliance enforcement is mediated by the alert triggering mechanism. Defined by the `alert_triggered_by` axiom, this concept classifies a process (X) that is initiated or "triggered" by a specific metric (Y). This aligns with the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) definition of a process, representing a temporal entity that unfolds in response to data conditions.

The schema establishes a reciprocal relationship with the attributed outlier claims. The metric that triggers an alert is, in this data model, realized as an attributed outlier claim. This circular dependency (referential integrity) is intentional; it creates a closed loop of verification where an alert process must be justified by a specific metric (the claim), and that claim must be validated by the alert context. This prevents the generation of "orphan" alerts that lack a defined metric basis.

## 4.4 Metric Emission Subclasses

To ensure the continuous monitoring of compliance states, systems must emit metrics that feed into the alerting subsystems. The `metric_emission_subclass` axiom describes a process (X) that emits a metric (Y). This is distinct from the alerting process; emission refers to the generation of the raw signal or data point, whereas alerting refers to the reactive workflow initiated by that signal.

In the relational model, the metric emitted (Y) is stored as an attributed outlier claim. This linkage implies that the output of an emission process is immediately formalized as a structured claim with attributes, ready for assessment. For example, a "Transaction Stream Processor" (the emitting process) outputs a metric which is formally recorded as a "Suspicious Wire Transfer" claim. This direct mapping from emission to formal claim accelerates the audit timeline by removing intermediate transformation steps.

## 4.5 Basic Aggregations

Finally, the compliance framework requires the ability to synthesize individual claims into higher-level summaries. The `aggregation_basic` axiom defines a descriptive information content entity (X) that represents a collective or summary view. Unlike outlier claims, which focus on specific deviations, aggregations provide the macro-level context necessary for governance reporting (e.g., total risk exposure, volume of anomalies).

This entity relies on the `cco:DescriptiveICE` anchor but does not require the complex attribute linking of the outlier claims. It serves as the reporting layer, consuming the IDs and statuses of the claims and processes described in previous sections to produce executive-level dashboards and regulatory filings.
