# Chapter 7: Ontological Formalization of Audit Processes and Evidence Artifacts

This chapter delineates the structural and semantic requirements for defining audit processes, datasets, and attestations within a governance framework. It establishes the formal axioms necessary to ensure that audit trails are both verifiable and semantically rigorous, adhering to the principles of directive governance and long-tail data management. The content herein provides the necessary definitions, relational mappings, and evidentiary standards required for high-compliance environments.

## 7.1 Audit Periods and Temporal Scope

The definition of an audit process is inextricably bound to its temporal dimension. In formal ontology terms, an audit is not merely a static event but a process (`bfo:0000015`) that unfolds over a specific interval. The axiom governing this relationship posits that the audit process (`X`) is a subclass of processes that are defined for a specific audit period (`Y`). This temporal binding ensures that all artifacts and actions are contextualized within a fixed window, thereby preventing ambiguity regarding the temporal validity of the evidence.

To implement this axiomatically, the relational schema utilizes a foreign key constraint that anchors the audit period directly to the dataset under examination. This structure enforces the rule that a period cannot exist in isolation; it must be associated with a specific artifact instance.

Column `y` represents the audit period class, which is structurally enforced to reference a valid dataset identifier (`t_dataset_under_audit`), ensuring the period is grounded in reality.

In practice, this mapping allows governance bodies to query all audit processes associated with a specific dataset or, conversely, to identify the specific temporal window applicable to a given audit instance. It prevents the "orphan period" problem where an audit scope is defined without a corresponding subject.

## 7.2 Datasets Under Audit

The object of the audit process is the dataset. Formally defined as an Artifact (`cco:Artifact`), the dataset under audit represents the static or semi-static collection of data points subject to verification. The axiom `dataset_under_audit` asserts that the entity (`X`) is a subclass of artifacts that are currently undergoing an audit process (`bfo:0000015`). This classification is critical for maintaining the integrity of the audit scope; only entities explicitly classified as under audit are subject to the rigorous constraints of the governance framework.

This entity serves as the central node in the relational graph, referenced by both the audit period and the supporting evidence. It acts as the "long tail" anchor, ensuring that even disparate or unstructured data collections are brought into the formal compliance fold.

By populating this registry, the organization establishes a definitive boundary for the audit. Any data manipulation or query occurring outside the bounds of these registered artifacts is not considered part of the official audit record, thereby preserving the chain of custody.

## 7.3 Audit Conducted By

An audit process, while defined by its period and subject, requires an agent to execute it. The axiom `audit_conducted_by` introduces the human element, stipulating that the audit process (`X`) is a subclass of processes conducted by a specific person (`cco:Person`). This axiom is foundational for accountability; it ensures that every process instance is traceable to an individual agent with the authority and responsibility to perform the audit.

In the context of compliance frameworks, this mapping satisfies the requirement for non-repudiation. It is insufficient to state that an audit occurred; the system must record *who* initiated and managed the process. This distinction is vital for regulatory reviews where the competence and authorization of the auditor are frequently scrutinized.

This table operationalizes the axiom `audit_conducted_by`. The `id` corresponds to the unique audit process identifier, and column `x` contains the identifier of the `cco:Person` responsible for the audit.

The data above demonstrates the direct assignment of personnel to specific audit IDs. Note that the granularity of the `x` slot allows for the inclusion of full identifiers or internal employee codes, provided they resolve to a valid `cco:Person` entity within the broader ontology.

## 7.4 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

The culmination of the audit process is the attestation. However, an attestation is not a mere assertion; it must be substantiated by evidence. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` formalizes this by asserting that the attestation process (`X`) is a subclass of processes that include supporting evidence (`Y`). This evidence must be an artifact—specifically, a dataset that has been previously registered and subjected to audit procedures.

This relationship creates a dependency graph where the validity of the attestation is contingent upon the existence and integrity of the supporting dataset. The relational schema enforces this via a foreign key constraint, mandating that any evidence cited in an attestation must correspond to a valid entry in the `t_dataset_under_audit` table.

## 7.5 Attestation Signed By

The final step in the governance chain is the authorization of the attestation. While the audit may be conducted by one party, the formal sign-off often requires the authorization of a designated officer or a separate responsible party. The axiom `attestation_signed_by` asserts that the attestation process (`X`) is a subclass of processes signed by a person (`cco:Person`). This signature represents the formal acceptance of liability regarding the accuracy of the attestation and its underlying evidence.

This axiom serves as the bridge between the technical audit process and legal or corporate governance. It transforms a technical finding into a formal corporate record. The relational structure captures this by associating the attestation ID with the unique identifier of the signing individual.
