# Chapter 7: Formalization of Audit Lifecycle and Attestation Protocols

This chapter delineates the structural and procedural requirements for managing the audit lifecycle within a regulated governance framework. It establishes the formal ontology governing the relationship between audited artifacts, the audit processes themselves, and the subsequent attestations that validate compliance. The content herein is prescriptive, defining the exact data schema and logical constraints required to ensure traceability from the initial dataset submission through to the final sign-off by authorized personnel.

## 1.0 Definition of Artifacts Under Audit

The foundational unit of any audit procedure is the artifact or dataset subject to examination. In accordance with governance standards, an entity is classified as a `cco:Artifact` only when it is formally designated as being under audit. This designation triggers the creation of a record within the governance system, establishing the immutable identity of the data object under review. The process of designation ensures that all subsequent actions—interrogation, validation, and attestation—are anchored to a specific, identified entity.

The axiom governing this relationship asserts that the artifact (X) is a subclass of `cco:Artifact` and possesses the property of being under an audit process (`bfo:0000015`). This classification is not merely descriptive but functional; it restricts the application of audit protocols to formally registered entities only.

## 2.0 Audit Period Specification

An audit cannot exist in a temporal vacuum; it must be defined by a specific scope or period to which it applies. The concept of an "audit period" is formally modeled as a process (`bfo:0000015`) that encompasses the specific duration or scope of the examination. This temporal bounding is critical for establishing the relevance of the evidence gathered and for ensuring that the audit's findings are applicable to the timeframe in question.

The axiom `audit_for_period` defines a relationship where the audit process (X) is specifically designated for a particular audit period (Y). In the relational implementation, this period is inextricably linked to the dataset being audited. This linkage enforces the constraint that an audit period is not an abstract concept but is directly bound to the specific artifact registry defined in the previous section.

This foreign key constraint (`y` REFERENCES `t_dataset_under_audit`) ensures that every audit period is valid only if it is associated with an existing, registered dataset.

## 3.0 Assignment of Audit Conductors

Governance frameworks require that no automated or manual audit process occurs without a responsible agent. The assignment of a conductor (an auditor or an automated auditing agent) to the audit process provides the necessary chain of custody and accountability. This relationship is modeled by the axiom `audit_conducted_by`, which asserts that the audit process (X) is conducted by a `cco:Person`.

This section mandates the explicit recording of the conductor for every audit process initiated. Whether the conductor is an internal compliance officer or an external third-party auditor, their association with the audit process must be recorded at the inception of the audit lifecycle.

## 4.0 Attestation and Supporting Evidence

Following the execution of an audit, the formal attestation phase commences. An attestation is a declaration of truth or verification, typically regarding the compliance of the dataset with specific controls. However, an attestation is not valid in isolation; it must be supported by evidence. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` formalizes this by stating that the attestation process (X) must include supporting evidence (Y).

This evidence is invariably derived from the dataset under audit. Consequently, the relational schema enforces a dependency where the evidence reference (`y`) must point to a valid dataset ID in the `t_dataset_under_audit` table. This ensures that attestations are grounded in empirical data derived from the specific artifacts reviewed during the audit period.

**Table 4: Attestation Evidence Linkage**

The `y` column is a foreign key referencing the `t_dataset_under_audit` table, effectively linking the attestation to the specific evidence source.

*Note: The supporting evidence referenced in column `y` must exist in the `t_dataset_under_audit` table. Attestations lacking this linkage are considered non-compliant and invalid.*

## 5.0 Attestation Signatory Authorization

The final step in the audit lifecycle is the formal signing of the attestation. This action confers legal and procedural authority to the document. The axiom `attestation_signed_by` dictates that the attestation process (X) must be signed by a `cco:Person`. This requirement ensures that a natural or legal person accepts responsibility for the validity of the attestation and the accuracy of the supporting evidence.

This section outlines the requirements for capturing the signatory event. It is distinct from the conductor of the audit; the signatory is often a higher-authority officer, such as a Chief Compliance Officer or Director, who reviews the audit findings and evidence before authorizing the final attestation.
