# Chapter 4: Axiomatic Governance of Directive Conflicts and Evidence Verification

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures governing the interaction between prescriptive directives, descriptive conflict states, and verification processes within high-assurance governance frameworks. The primary objective is to establish a rigorous model for how Information Content Entities (ICE) classified as directives mitigate, supersede, or require evidence regarding specific conflict masses derived from uncertainty analysis. The text adheres to the axioms defined in the governance ontology, specifically mapping the relationships between `cco:DirectiveICE`, `cco:DescriptiveICE`, and processual entities defined by the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO). By formalizing these relationships, auditors and compliance officers can trace the lineage of control efficacy from the initial definition of a conflict mass through to the verification evidence that substantiates mitigation.

## 4.2 Control Subclasses and Mitigation of Conflict Masses

In the context of governance frameworks, a control is not merely a procedural step but a specialized Information Content Entity (ICE) that functions as a `cco:DirectiveICE`. According to Axiom 1, a Control Subclass is defined by its specific capacity to mitigate a particular class of conflict or uncertainty. The relationship `sdg:mitigates` establishes a directed dependency where the efficacy of the directive (X) is measured against the reduction of the conflict mass (Y).

The formalization of this relationship is critical for audit trails. It asserts that for a directive to be valid within the system, it must be ontologically bound to a conflict mass it is designed to resolve. This binding prevents the existence of "orphaned" controls that do not address a quantified risk or uncertainty. The mitigation relationship is not merely semantic but functional; it implies that the execution of the directive X results in the state change of entity Y.

To illustrate this, consider the implementation of access control directives within a secure facility. The directives are designed to mitigate specific conflict masses arising from uncertainty in user authorization.

## 4.3 Descriptive ICE and the Assignment of Conflict Mass

While directives prescribe action, the current state of the system is captured by `cco:DescriptiveICE`. Axiom 2 posits that a Descriptive ICE possesses a property `sdg:hasConflictMass`. In Dempster-Shafer theory, which underpins the logic of conflict masses, the "mass" represents a belief allocation to a specific proposition, strictly bounded by the uncertainty of the evidence.

Within this ontology, the assignment of a conflict mass to a descriptive entity is not a scalar value assignment but a structural relationship. The entity Y (the conflict mass) is itself a classifiable object that is ontologically linked to the control mechanism intended to resolve it. This recursive structure—where the conflict mass references the control—ensures that the description of the system state is always framed in terms of the governance mechanisms applicable to it.

This relationship allows for a granular assessment of risk. A descriptive entity, such as a system log or a sensor report, is not merely a collection of data points; it is an entity that *has* a conflict mass. This mass is the specific uncertainty profile that the associated control must address.

The "mass" is the gap between the directive's prescription and the log's reality.

## 4.4 Policy Supersedence and Conflict Resolution

Governance frameworks are dynamic; directives and policies must evolve to address emerging threats or to correct previous inadequacies. Axiom 3 defines the `sdg:supersedes` relationship, wherein a new Policy Class (X) replaces or overrides a previous Conflict Mass (Y).

This relationship is distinct from mitigation. While mitigation addresses the uncertainty or threat inherent in a conflict mass, supersedence addresses the governance structure itself. When a policy supersedes a conflict mass, it effectively declares that the previous definition of the conflict (or the previous method of measuring it) is obsolete. The new policy establishes a new regime of evidence and control.

For instance, if a regulatory body updates the definition of "interception risk," the new policy supersedes the old conflict mass associated with interception. The old conflict mass is no longer a valid target for mitigation; it is subsumed or replaced by the new policy's scope.

The entry `pol_sup_01` illustrates the `RevisedAccessProtocol_2024` superseding the conflict mass associated with `SensorLogEntry_Alpha`. This action effectively retires the previous conflict definition, mandating that all future descriptive entities align with the new protocol's conflict parameters.

## 4.5 Evidence Requirements for Control Implementation

A directive is theoretical until its implementation is verified. Axiom 4 introduces the `sdg:requiresEvidence` relationship. A Control Class (X) must require a specific form of Evidence (Y), which in this schema is modeled as a Conflict Mass entity.

This requirement creates a closed loop of accountability. The control cannot be considered "implemented" merely by its existence; it requires the generation of evidence that corresponds to the conflict mass it mitigates. This evidence is typically the output of a verification process. By linking the evidence requirement to the conflict mass, the ontology ensures that the evidence is relevant and sufficient to measure the specific uncertainty the control was designed to address.

For example, an `AuditLogDirective` is useless without the actual logs (evidence) that demonstrate its operation. The axiom formalizes this by stating the directive *requires* the conflict mass entity (which represents the evidence state).

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ctrl_ev_01 | PhysicalAccessDirective | SensorLogEntry_Alpha |
| ctrl_ev_02 | DataEncryptionDirective | NetworkTrafficCapture_Beta |
| ctrl_ev_03 | AuditLogDirective | RegistrySnapshot_Gamma |

In `ctrl_ev_01`, the `PhysicalAccessDirective` requires `SensorLogEntry_Alpha`. This means the compliance status of the directive is dependent on the presence and integrity of this specific descriptive entity (the conflict mass/evidence).

## 4.6 Verification Processes and Evidence Production

The final axiom in this sequence bridges the gap between process and information. Axiom 5 classifies a Verification Process (X), a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process), as an entity that `sdg:producesEvidence` (Y). Here, Y is the Conflict Mass entity.

This relationship grounds the abstract concept of "verification" in the production of concrete data artifacts. Verification is not a binary state of "verified/unverified"; it is a process that yields the specific descriptive entities (conflict masses) required by the controls. The ontology treats the output of the verification process as the instantiation of the conflict mass entity.

This structural definition is crucial for automated compliance systems. It defines the inputs (processes) and outputs (evidence/conflict masses) necessary to satisfy the evidence requirements of the directives defined in Axiom 4.
