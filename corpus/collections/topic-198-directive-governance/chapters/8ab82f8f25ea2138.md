# Chapter 4: Structural Governance of Directives and Attestations

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter establishes the formal ontological structures governing the creation, enforcement, and verification of directives within a regulated information environment. The provisions herein define the necessary and sufficient conditions for an information content entity to function as a valid governance instrument. It is determined that compliance with these structural axioms is mandatory for all entities subject to the regulatory framework described herein. The definitions provided do not impose additional operational burdens beyond those required by the underlying logic of the governance model but serve to clarify the relationships between directive entities, enforcing agents, and scoped artifacts. Consequently, this chapter details the classification of directives, the assignment of enforcement authorities, the restriction of scope to artifacts, the protocols for verification, and the requirements for attestation processes.

## 4.2 The Directive Information Content Entity

### 4.2.1 Definition and Classification

In accordance with the foundational axioms of the governance framework, a Directive Information Content Entity (DirectiveICE) constitutes the primary unit of regulatory logic. Formally, a DirectiveICE is defined as any information content entity that prescribes, governs, or directs behavior or processing within the system. This classification serves as the superclass for all specific policy manifestations, including procedural guidelines, strategic mandates, and technical requirements.

The ontological axiom `policy_basic` asserts that any entity classified as a DirectiveICE must be explicitly categorized as such within the registry. This classification is not merely semantic but functional; it designates the entity as a valid target for enforcement and verification mechanisms. Without this explicit classification, an information artifact exists merely as data and lacks the normative force required to compel organizational action.

## 4.3 Enforcement Mechanisms and Cardinality

### 4.3.1 Mandatory Enforcement Assignment

A directive, in isolation, is inert; it requires an active agent to ensure its provisions are actualized. The axiom `policy_min_one_enforcer` formalizes this requirement by stipulating that any valid DirectiveICE must be enforced by a minimum of one enforcing entity. This constraint enforces a cardinality of "min 1" on the `enforcedBy` property, ensuring that there is no orphaned policy within the governance framework.

The enforcing entity (Class Y) may be a human role, an automated system, or an organizational unit. However, the axiom mandates that the relationship must be explicit. The assignment of an enforcer transforms the directive from a passive document into an active governance instrument. This structure prevents the ambiguity often found in decentralized organizations where policy ownership is unclear.

## 4.4 Scope Restriction to Artifacts

### 4.4.1 The Artifact-Centric Governance Model

To prevent regulatory overreach and ensure precise applicability, the governance framework implements the axiom `policy_only_governs_artifacts`. This axiom restricts the scope of any DirectiveICE such that it may only govern entities classified as Artifacts. An Artifact, in this context, is defined as a distinct, persisting information entity or physical object produced or acted upon by a process.

This restriction serves a critical governance function: it delineates the boundary of authority. A DirectiveICE governs the *handling*, *storage*, or *transmission* of Artifacts (such as logs, records, or configuration files), rather than asserting direct control over persons or abstract concepts. By constraining the `governsScope` property to the class `cco:Artifact`, the framework ensures that directives are actionable and verifiable within the domain of managed resources.

## 4.5 Verification of Requirements

### 4.5.1 The Verification Imperative

While enforcement ensures the active application of a directive, verification provides the evidence of compliance. The axiom `requirement_min_one_verification` addresses the subset of directives classified as Requirements. It stipulates that every Requirement must be verified by a minimum of one Verification entity.

Verification is distinct from enforcement. While enforcement involves the application of rules, verification involves the assessment of adherence to those rules. The `verifiedBy` property links the Requirement to a specific verification method, such as an automated audit script, a manual inspection checklist, or a third-party certification process. The "min 1" cardinality ensures that no requirement is introduced into the framework without a corresponding mechanism to prove its fulfillment.

## 4.6 Attestation Processes and Signatory Authority

### 4.6.1 The Attestation Process

Attestation represents the formal acknowledgment of the execution or validity of a process. The axiom `attestation_min_one_signer` defines the structural properties of this activity. It classifies the Attestation as a subtype of a Process (specifically, inheriting from `bfo:0000015`, which denotes a processual entity).

Crucially, this axiom mandates that any Attestation process must be signed by a minimum of one Person (`cco:Person`). This requirement anchors the digital or physical process to a legally responsible natural person. The signature serves as the definitive link between the abstract process of attestation and the human accountability underlying it. This prevents the delegation of attestation to purely automated agents without human oversight, ensuring that a specific individual asserts the validity of the attested state.
