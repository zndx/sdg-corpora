# Chapter 4: Ontological Structures for Evidence-Based Governance

## Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures required to establish a rigorous evidence-based governance framework, specifically within the context of regulatory compliance and auditability. The content herein defines the axiomatic relationships between governance directives, processes, and evidence artifacts, providing a standardized schema for mapping organizational controls to legal requirements such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). By adhering to the definitions and relational schemas provided, compliance officers and system architects can ensure that their governance models are both semantically precise and operationally verifiable.

---

## 4.1 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

In the context of formal governance, an attestation represents a specific type of process (bfo:Process) wherein an entity formally certifies the validity of a state of affairs. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` posits that for any attestation to be valid, it must be substantiated by supporting evidence. This relationship is critical in audit scenarios where mere assertion is insufficient; the assertion must be anchored to a demonstrable lawful basis.

The structure of this axiom requires that the attestation (Class X) is a process that utilizes or references a specific lawful basis (Class Y). This is not a loose association but a dependency constraint: the attestation cannot exist as a compliant record without the referenced evidence. In practice, this means that when an organization signs off on a data processing activity, the signature event (the attestation) must be digitally or logically linked to the specific legal justification (the supporting evidence) that permits that activity.

**Table 4.1: Relational Schema for Attestation with Supporting Evidence**

The following table illustrates instances of attestation processes mapped to their requisite lawful bases. The primary key (`id`) represents the unique instance of the attestation event, while the foreign key (`y`) ensures referential integrity to the lawful basis registry.

| id | x (Attestation Process) | y (Supporting Evidence/Lawful Basis) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ATTEST_001 | Annual_Security_Audit_Certification | LB_CONTRACT_01 |
| ATTEST_002 | Data_Transfer_Impact_Assessment_Signoff | LB_LEGITIMATE_01 |
| ATTEST_003 | Vendor_Compliance_Attestation | LB_PUBLIC_01 |
| ATTEST_004 | Internal_Policy_Acknowledgement | LB_CONTRACT_02 |
| ATTEST_005 | Incident_Response_Validation | LB_VITAL_01 |

In the schema above, the `x` column denotes the specific process instance (e.g., the execution of an annual audit), while `y` denotes the identifier of the lawful basis. This structure ensures that any query for `ATTEST_001` automatically retrieves the governing legal context `LB_CONTRACT_01`, enforcing the axiom that the attestation is "with supporting evidence."

## 4.2 GDPR Lawful Basis

The concept of a Lawful Basis under the GDPR is central to the compliance framework. The axiom `gdpr_lawful_basis` formalizes this by asserting that a Directive or Information Collection Entity (cco:DirectiveICE) must possess a lawful basis to be valid. This axiom serves as the bridge between high-level organizational policies and the specific legal justifications required by regulation.

This relationship is defined as a subclass relationship where the Directive (Class X) is characterized by the possession of a Lawful Basis (Class Y). The relational projection of this axiom creates a dependency where the lawful basis itself may reference specific controls, creating a chain of custody from legal text to operational implementation. It is imperative to understand that in this ontology, the "Lawful Basis" is treated as a distinct entity that must be explicitly linked to the directive it governs.

**Table 4.2: Relational Schema for GDPR Lawful Basis**

The table below demonstrates how organizational directives are mapped to specific lawful basis entities. Note that the `y` column references a control entity, establishing the link between the legal basis and the control mechanism that enforces it.

| id | x (Directive) | y (Control/Lawful Basis) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| LB_CONTRACT_01 | Vendor_Management_Directive | CTRL_CONTRACT_01 |
| LB_LEGITIMATE_01 | Marketing_Automation_Directive | CTRL_LEGITIMATE_01 |
| LB_PUBLIC_01 | Transparency_Policy_Directive | CTRL_PUBLIC_01 |
| LB_CONTRACT_02 | Employee_Data_Processing_Policy | CTRL_CONTRACT_02 |
| LB_VITAL_01 | Health_Safety_Monitoring_Directive | CTRL_VITAL_01 |

Here, the `id` serves as the unique identifier for the lawful basis record itself. The foreign key constraint on `y` (referencing `t_control_with_evidence_requirement`) ensures that every declared lawful basis is backed by a specific control mechanism. This enforces the regulatory principle that a legal basis cannot merely be stated; it must be enacted through a control.

## 4.3 Verification Produces Evidence

Verification is a distinct process from attestation. While attestation is the act of certification, verification is the act of testing or validation that generates the necessary artifacts. The axiom `verification_produces_evidence` defines a process (Class X) that results in the creation or production of evidence (Class Y).

This axiom is foundational for audit trails. It dictates that evidence is not an abstract concept but a product of a specific verification process. In a technical environment, this might be an automated script that checks configuration settings and outputs a log file, or a manual review that produces a signed checklist. The ontology treats the verification process as the causal ancestor of the evidence artifact.

**Table 4.3: Relational Schema for Verification Produces Evidence**

The following table maps verification processes to the evidence artifacts they generate. These artifacts subsequently serve as the supporting evidence for attestations and lawful bases.

| id | x (Verification Process) | y (Evidence Produced) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| VERIFY_001 | Automated_Log_Audit_Script | LB_LEGITIMATE_01 |
| VERIFY_002 | Manual_Access_Review_Procedure | LB_CONTRACT_01 |
| VERIFY_003 | Network_Vulnerability_Scan | LB_VITAL_01 |
| VERIFY_004 | Data_Inventory_Crawl | LB_PUBLIC_01 |
| VERIFY_005 | Consent_Tracking_Database_Query | LB_LEGITIMATE_01 |

In this schema, the `y` column references the `t_gdpr_lawful_basis` table. This indicates that the output of the verification process serves to instantiate or validate the lawful basis. For example, the `Automated_Log_Audit_Script` (x) produces the evidence that substantiates `LB_LEGITIMATE_01` (y). This linkage ensures that the evidence is not orphaned but is directly tied to the legal justification it supports.

## 4.4 GDPR Article Subclass

To ensure granular compliance, organizational directives must often be mapped to specific articles of the GDPR. The axiom `gdpr_article_subclass` establishes that a Directive (Class X) is a subclass or implementation of a specific GDPR Article (Class Y). This allows the governance framework to query all directives related to a specific legal article (e.g., Article 32 regarding security of processing) efficiently.

This relationship is critical for regulatory reporting. When an auditor requests evidence of compliance with Article 32, the system can traverse this axiom to identify all relevant directives (Class X) that fall under this article (Class Y). The structure enforces a strict hierarchy where the directive is an instance of the broader legal requirement.

**Table 4.4: Relational Schema for GDPR Article Subclass**

The table below illustrates the mapping of internal directives to specific GDPR articles, which are modeled as controls requiring evidence.

| id | x (Directive) | y (GDPR Article/Control) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| SUB_ART28 | Data_Processor_Agreement_Template | CTRL_CONTRACT_01 |
| SUB_ART6 | Marketing_Consent_Mechanism | CTRL_LEGITIMATE_01 |
| SUB_ART13 | Privacy_Notice_Publishing_Workflow | CTRL_PUBLIC_01 |
| SUB_ART32 | Encryption_Key_Rotation_Policy | CTRL_VITAL_01 |
| SUB_ART17 | Right_to_Erasure_Request_Tool | CTRL_CONTRACT_02 |

The `y` column in this table references `t_control_with_evidence_requirement`. This signifies that the GDPR Article itself is treated as a control requirement within the ontology. For instance, the `Data_Processor_Agreement_Template` (x) is an implementation of the requirements found in Article 28, which is represented here by the control entity `CTRL_CONTRACT_01`.

## 4.5 Control with Evidence Requirement

The final axiom, `control_with_evidence_requirement`, closes the loop by defining the nature of the control itself. It asserts that a Directive or Control (Class X) requires evidence (Class Y) to be considered active or compliant. This is the operational side of the governance framework; it defines the "proof of work" for every control implemented.

Without this axiom, controls could exist in a theoretical state without verification. By mandating that a control requires evidence, the framework enforces a continuous cycle of implementation, verification, and evidence generation. This axiom often interacts with the `gdpr_lawful_basis` axiom to create a circular dependency where a control defines a basis, and the basis validates the control.

**Table 4.5: Relational Schema for Control with Evidence Requirement**

This table details the specific evidence requirements for various organizational controls. The foreign key to `t_gdpr_lawful_basis` reinforces that the evidence required is the very definition of the lawful basis for that control.

| id | x (Control) | y (Required Evidence/Lawful Basis) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| CTRL_CONTRACT_01 | Contractual_Obligation_Control | LB_CONTRACT_01 |
| CTRL_LEGITIMATE_01 | Legitimate_Interest_Assessment_Control | LB_LEGITIMATE_01 |
| CTRL_PUBLIC_01 | Public_Interest_Transparency_Control | LB_PUBLIC_01 |
| CTRL_CONTRACT_02 | Employment_Contract_Compliance_Control | LB_CONTRACT_02 |
| CTRL_VITAL_01 | Vital_Interest_Safeguard_Control | LB_VITAL_01 |

In this relational view, the `id` represents the control, and `y` represents the evidence (lawful basis) that must be produced. For example, `CTRL_CONTRACT_01` (the control managing contractual obligations) requires `LB_CONTRACT_01` as evidence. This creates a rigorous validation chain: the Control produces the Evidence, which serves as the Lawful Basis, which supports the Attestation.
