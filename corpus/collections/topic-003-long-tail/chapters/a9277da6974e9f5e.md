# Chapter 4: Governance Frameworks and Evidence-Based Compliance

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the structural requirements for establishing a compliant governance framework under the Directive ICE (Information and Content Entity) protocols. It details the ontological relationships between lawful bases, evidence requirements, and audit mechanisms. The objective is to provide a precise, data-driven methodology for documenting compliance activities, ensuring that every processing activity is anchored to a valid legal mandate and supported by verifiable audit trails. The following sections define the core axioms governing these relationships, supported by relational schema implementations that facilitate rigorous data integrity and cross-referencing.

## 4.2 GDPR Lawful Basis

In the context of the Directive ICE framework, any processing activity involving personal data must be predicated on a valid legal justification. This is not merely a procedural formality but a structural requirement where the processing entity (Class X) is formally subclassed as a Directive ICE entity that possesses a specific lawful basis (Class Y). The relationship is mandatory; without a defined lawful basis, the processing activity is ontologically invalid and non-compliant.

The axiom `gdpr_lawful_basis` asserts that {X} is something that has lawful basis {Y}. In practice, this means that a specific data processing operation—such as "Customer Data Processing"—must be explicitly linked to a legal ground, such as "Contractual Necessity" or "Legitimate Interest." This linkage serves as the primary node in the compliance graph, to which all subsequent evidence and attestations must refer.

The following table embodies this axiom. The primary key is the unique identifier for the lawful basis record (`id`). The column `x` represents the processing class or directive, while `y` represents the lawful basis itself. Critically, the foreign key constraint on `y` mandates that the lawful basis must reference an existing control with an evidence requirement (`t_control_with_evidence_requirement`), ensuring that the legal basis is operationalized through a specific governance control.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| LB_CONTRACT_01 | CustomerDataProcessing | CTRL_RETENTION_01 |

## 4.3 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

Governance frameworks rely heavily on the concept of attestation—the formal act of signing off on the validity of a control or process. However, an attestation is only as robust as the evidence supporting it. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` defines that {X} is something that with supporting evidence {Y}. Here, {X} represents the attestation process or artifact (a subclass of `bfo:0000015`, a Process), and {Y} represents the evidentiary backbone.

In a mature compliance environment, an attestation cannot exist in a vacuum. It must be intrinsically linked to the lawful basis record established in Section 4.2. For instance, when a Data Protection Officer (DPO) signs an annual compliance attestation, the system must record a relationship between that attestation event and the specific lawful basis record (e.g., `LB_CONTRACT_01`) that validates the processing being attested. This creates a verifiable chain of custody: the Attestation points to the Evidence, which points to the Lawful Basis.

The relational schema for this concept requires that the supporting evidence (`y`) be a valid entry in the `t_gdpr_lawful_basis` table. This constraint prevents "orphaned" attestations that claim compliance without referencing a defined legal ground.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ATT_DPO_2023_Q1 | DPO_Annual_Attestation | LB_CONTRACT_01 |

## 4.4 Audit for Period

Temporal scoping is a critical component of any audit framework. An audit is not an infinite state; it is a process bound by a specific timeframe. The axiom `audit_for_period` formalizes this by stating that {X} is something that for audit period {Y}. In this schema, the audit process {X} is linked to a specific period {Y}, which is modeled as a reference to a lawful basis record.

This linkage implies that the audit period is not merely a date range but is defined by the validity period of the underlying lawful basis or the control implementation. For example, an audit of "Quarterly Compliance" (X) for the period "Q1 2023" (Y) is valid only insofar as it relates to the active lawful basis records during that time. By enforcing a foreign key relationship to the `t_gdpr_lawful_basis` table, the framework ensures that audits are only conducted against active, legally grounded processing activities. This prevents retrospective auditing of processing activities that lacked a legal basis during the target period.

The table below illustrates this mapping. The `id` is the unique audit identifier, `x` is the audit process name, and `y` is the reference to the lawful basis record that defines the scope and validity of that audit period.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| AUD_Q1_2023 | Q1_Compliance_Audit | LB_CONTRACT_01 |
| AUD_Q2_2023 | Q2_Marketing_Audit | LB_LEGITIMATE_01 |
| AUD_Q3_2023 | Q3_Safety_Audit | LB_VITAL_01 |

## 4.5 Control with Evidence Requirement

The operational arm of the governance framework is the Control. A control is a directive or automated mechanism designed to enforce compliance. The axiom `control_with_evidence_requirement` posits that {X} is something that requires evidence {Y}. This establishes a bidirectional or recursive dependency within the ontology: a Control (X) requires Evidence (Y), and that Evidence is often the manifestation of the Lawful Basis.

In this schema, the Control (e.g., a "Data Retention Directive") is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE`. It requires evidence to prove its operation. The foreign key structure here is significant: the evidence required (`y`) must reference an entry in `t_gdpr_lawful_basis`. This creates a rigorous loop: the Lawful Basis (from Section 4.1) references a Control (as its implementation mechanism), and that Control, in turn, requires the Lawful Basis record as evidence of its necessity. This circular dependency ensures that controls are not implemented in isolation but are strictly tethered to the legal grounds that justify them.

For instance, a "Retention Control" is implemented because of a "Contractual Lawful Basis." To prove the Retention Control is working, one must present the "Contractual Lawful Basis" record as the required evidence artifact.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| CTRL_RETENTION_01 | DataRetentionDirective | LB_CONTRACT_01 |
| CTRL_CONSENT_01 | ConsentManagementDirective | LB_LEGITIMATE_01 |
| CTRL_AUDIT_LOG_01 | AuditLoggingDirective | LB_LEGAL_01 |
| CTRL_ACCESS_01 | AccessControlDirective | LB_VITAL_01 |

## 4.6 Audit Subclass

While general audits provide temporal oversight, specific audit subclasses are designed to target particular types of controls or directives. The axiom `audit_subclass` defines that {X} is something that audits control directive information content entity. This is a specialization of the general audit process, focusing specifically on `cco:DirectiveICE` entities.

This concept allows the governance framework to differentiate between broad process audits and specific control audits. For example, a "System Audit" might check general uptime, but an "Audit Subclass" instance would specifically target the "Access Control Directive" to verify its configuration and efficacy. This distinction is crucial for granular compliance reporting, allowing stakeholders to query not just *if* an audit happened, but *which specific controls* were subjected to scrutiny.

The relational structure for this axiom is straightforward, consisting only of the audit subclass ID (`id`) and the class name (`x`). It does not require a foreign key to the lawful basis table directly, as it represents the *type* of audit activity rather than a specific instance of an audit record linked to a period.
