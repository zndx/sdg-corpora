# Chapter 4: Ontological Mapping of Compliance Directives and Audit Evidence

This chapter delineates the formal mapping between OWL-based ontological structures and relational schema implementations within the context of GDPR compliance frameworks. It addresses the logical derivation of audit trails, evidence requirements, and lawful basis assertions, providing a rigorous foundation for data architects and compliance officers. The definitions provided herein are binding for the construction of compliant data architectures and serve as the definitive reference for interpreting the semantic constraints of the underlying ontology.

## 1. GDPR Lawful Basis (Axiom 1)

### 1.1 Conceptual Definition
In the context of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the lawful basis acts as the foundational legal grounding for any processing activity involving personal data. The axiom `gdpr_lawful_basis` establishes a relationship between a Directive Information Content Entity (`cco:DirectiveICE`) and a specific legal justification. Formally, the axiom asserts that a Directive (`X`) has a Lawful Basis (`Y`). This relationship is not merely associative but constitutive; the validity of the directive is contingent upon the existence and applicability of the lawful basis.

For instance, a "Data Processing Directive" intended to process employee payroll records must derive its legitimacy from a lawful basis such as "Contractual Necessity." Without this linkage, the directive is considered non-compliant and void ab initio. The ontology captures this by defining `X` as a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` and asserting the object property `sdg:hasLawfulBasis` to some instance of `Y`.

### 1.2 Relational Implementation
The relational projection of this axiom is realized in the table `t_gdpr_lawful_basis`. This structure enforces the integrity of the compliance mapping by ensuring that every directive is explicitly tied to a valid basis. The primary key (`id`) serves as the unique identifier for the specific assertion of compliance.

The schema enforces a foreign key constraint where the lawful basis (`y`) must reference an existing identifier in the `t_control_with_evidence_requirement` table. This circular dependency highlights a critical governance principle: a lawful basis is not an abstract concept but is operationalized through specific controls that require evidence. Thus, the table `t_gdpr_lawful_basis` acts as a bridge between the high-level legal mandate and the operational control environment.

## 2. Attestation with Supporting Evidence (Axiom 2)

### 2.1 Conceptual Definition
Attestation represents the formal verification process by which an entity asserts the validity of a compliance state. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` defines a Process (`bfo:0000015`)—specifically an attestation process—that is substantiated by supporting evidence. The verbalization "{X} is something that with supporting evidence {Y}" indicates that the process `X` cannot exist in a valid state without the attachment of evidence `Y`.

In practice, this means that an auditor or compliance officer performing an attestation must reference specific artifacts. For example, an "Annual Security Attestation" must be supported by evidence such as "Penetration Test Reports" or "Access Control Logs." The ontology classifies the attestation as a distinct process subclass, relying on the `sdg:withSupportingEvidence` property to link the procedural assertion to the informational content entity that serves as proof.

### 2.2 Relational Implementation
The table `t_attestation_with_supporting_evidence` encodes this relationship. The primary key (`id`) uniquely identifies the attestation event. The column `x` refers to the specific attestation process instance, while `y` references the supporting evidence.

Critically, the schema mandates that the supporting evidence (`y`) must reference a valid entry in the `t_gdpr_lawful_basis` table. This constraint enforces the hierarchy of compliance: evidence is not merely a document; it must be traceable back to a specific lawful basis assertion. This ensures that the audit trail is anchored in the legal framework defined in Axiom 1, preventing the accumulation of irrelevant or orphaned evidence artifacts.

## 3. Audit for Period (Axiom 3)

### 3.1 Conceptual Definition
Temporal scoping is a fundamental requirement in governance frameworks. The axiom `audit_for_period` associates a specific Audit Process (`X`) with a defined Audit Period (`Y`). This relationship ensures that compliance activities are bounded in time, allowing for precise historical reporting and adherence to regulatory review cycles.

The verbalization "{X} is something that for audit period {Y}" implies that the audit process is instantiated for the specific purpose of evaluating compliance during `Y`. For example, a "Fiscal Year 2023 Audit" is distinct from a "Q1 2024 Audit." The ontology utilizes the `sdg:forAuditPeriod` property to link the process to the temporal entity, ensuring that audit findings are contextually accurate and temporally relevant.

### 3.2 Relational Implementation
The relational schema `t_audit_for_period` captures this temporal binding. The primary key (`id`) identifies the specific audit instance. Column `x` denotes the audit process, while column `y` denotes the audit period.

The foreign key constraint linking `y` to the `t_gdpr_lawful_basis` table introduces a semantic dependency between time and law. This implies that the audit period is not merely a date range but is defined or activated in relation to a specific lawful basis. For instance, the validity of an audit period for "Contractual Processing" is contingent upon the active status of the "Contractual" lawful basis. This design prevents retrospective auditing of directives that were not legally valid during the specified period.

## 4. Control with Evidence Requirement (Axiom 4)

### 4.1 Conceptual Definition
Controls are the operational mechanisms implemented to enforce compliance policies. The axiom `control_with_evidence_requirement` posits that a Directive (`X`)—specifically a control directive—requires Evidence (`Y`). This axiom formalizes the "evidentiary burden" placed on control operators. It is insufficient to merely implement a control; one must also generate and retain evidence that the control is functioning as intended.

For example, a "Password Rotation Control" requires evidence in the form of "System Logs" showing successful rotation events. The ontology defines this through the property `sdg:requiresEvidence`, linking the control directive to the informational entity that constitutes proof of execution. This linkage is essential for demonstrating "due diligence" in the event of a regulatory investigation.

### 4.2 Relational Implementation
The table `t_control_with_evidence_requirement` serves as the registry for these requirements. The primary key (`id`) uniquely identifies the control requirement. Column `x` references the control directive, while column `y` references the required evidence type.

The schema includes a foreign key constraint where `y` references `t_gdpr_lawful_basis`. This creates a bidirectional dependency with Axiom 1. While Axiom 1 states that a Directive has a Lawful Basis, Axiom 4 implies that the Evidence required by the Control is categorized or validated by that same Lawful Basis. This circularity enforces a tight coupling: the evidence required to prove the control's efficacy must be relevant to the legal basis underpinning the directive. An evidence type that does not align with the lawful basis (e.g., using "Consent Logs" to prove a "Legal Obligation") would violate this schema constraint.

## 5. Audit Subclass (Axiom 5)

### 5.1 Conceptual Definition
The final axiom, `audit_subclass`, defines the specific nature of an Audit Process. It asserts that an Audit (`X`) is a subclass of a Process (`bfo:0000015`) that specifically audits a Control Directive (`cco:DirectiveICE`). This axiom distinguishes an audit from generic administrative processes by defining its specific target: the governance controls.

Verbalized as "{X} is something that audits control directive information content entity," this axiom establishes the directional relationship between the auditor and the auditee. It ensures that the audit process is semantically bound to the review of directives, rather than general operational activities. This distinction is crucial for automated compliance systems, as it triggers specific audit protocols and reporting formats tailored to directive evaluation.

### 5.2 Relational Implementation
The table `t_audit_subclass` provides a catalog of audit process instances. Unlike the previous tables, this schema does not enforce a foreign key constraint to the lawful basis tables within its own definition, focusing instead on the classification of the audit entity itself.

The primary key (`id`) identifies the audit instance, and column `x` denotes the specific audit process subclass. While the schema definition provided does not include explicit FK columns for the audited control, the ontology implies that the entity `X` operates upon the controls defined in Axiom 4. In a full implementation, application logic would join this table to the control tables via process identifiers to establish the complete audit lineage.
