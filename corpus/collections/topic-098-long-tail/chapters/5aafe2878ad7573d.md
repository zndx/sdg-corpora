# Chapter 4: Integrated Compliance Directives and Evidence Management

## 4.1 Scope and Framework Applicability

This chapter delineates the structural and functional relationships governing the integration of Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) safeguards with Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) requirements within a unified governance framework. The provisions detailed herein establish the requisite ontological mappings for administrative, physical, and technical directives, ensuring that compliance artifacts are rigorously anchored to evidentiary attestations. The framework is designed to support audit readiness by maintaining a deterministic link between regulatory directives (the "what") and the procedural evidence (the "how") that substantiates adherence to said controls. Organizations operating within the scope of protected health information (PHI) and cardholder data environments (CDE) must implement the relational structures defined below to demonstrate due diligence to supervisory authorities.

## 4.2 Administrative Safeguards and Directive Governance

Administrative safeguards constitute the foundational policies and procedures designed to manage the selection, development, implementation, and maintenance of security measures. These safeguards are formally modeled as directives that inherently possess the characteristic of a HIPAA safeguard. In the ontology, an Administrative Safeguard is defined as a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` (Information and Content Entity), which necessitates a relationship to a specific attestation object. This relationship ensures that every administrative policy is not merely a static document but an active control linked to a verification process.

The relational schema for administrative safeguards enforces a foreign key constraint where the safeguard (`X`) must point to a valid attestation record (`Y`). This structure prevents the existence of "orphan" policies—administrative rules that are defined but never formally attested to or reviewed.

**Table 4.1: Administrative Safeguard Directives and Linked Attestations**

The following table illustrates the instantiation of administrative safeguards. The primary key (`id`) represents the unique identifier for the directive, while `x` denotes the specific class of the administrative safeguard, and `y` provides the foreign key reference to the attestation record that validates the safeguard's implementation.

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ADM-2023-01 | SecurityManagementProcess | ATT-SMP-01 |
| ADM-2023-02 | WorkforceSecurityPolicy | ATT-WSP-04 |
| ADM-2023-03 | InformationAccessManagement | ATT-IAM-02 |
| ADM-2023-04 | ContingencyPlanProtocol | ATT-CPP-09 |
| ADM-2023-05 | EvaluationAndComplianceAudit | ATT-ECA-12 |

In this configuration, the directive `SecurityManagementProcess` (ADM-2023-01) is explicitly bound to the attestation `ATT-SMP-01`. This binding signifies that the existence of the process is contingent upon and evidenced by the corresponding attestation artifact. The governance framework utilizes this mapping to query all administrative directives currently under review or requiring re-attestation based on the status of the linked `y` records.

## 4.3 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

The attestation entity serves as the critical verification node within the compliance architecture. Defined formally as a subclass of `bfo:Process` (a processual entity), an attestation represents the act of validating a control or directive. Crucially, the ontology mandates that every attestation must possess supporting evidence. This requirement is operationalized through a circular dependency within the schema: an attestation (`X`) must reference an administrative safeguard (`Y`) as its evidentiary basis.

This recursive structure between administrative safeguards and attestations creates a closed-loop verification system. The administrative safeguard defines the rule, the attestation verifies the rule's execution, and the attestation points back to the safeguard to provide the legal or procedural basis for the verification. This ensures that attestations are not abstract assertions but are grounded in specific administrative authorities.

**Table 4.2: Attestation Processes and Evidentiary Support**

The table below details the attestation records. Column `x` identifies the class of the attestation process, while column `y` acts as a foreign key referencing the `id` of the administrative safeguard that serves as the supporting evidence.

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ATT-SMP-01 | RiskAnalysisAttestation | ADM-2023-01 |
| ATT-WSP-04 | SanctionPolicyAttestation | ADM-2023-02 |
| ATT-IAM-02 | AccessAuthorizationAttestation | ADM-2023-03 |
| ATT-CPP-09 | DataBackupPlanAttestation | ADM-2023-04 |
| ATT-ECA-12 | PeriodicSecurityReview | ADM-2023-05 |

For instance, the `RiskAnalysisAttestation` (ATT-SMP-01) utilizes `ADM-2023-01` (SecurityManagementProcess) as its supporting evidence. This indicates that the act of attesting to the risk analysis is authorized and substantiated by the overarching Security Management Process directive. This mapping allows auditors to trace the lineage of a compliance verification from the specific act of attestation back to the governing administrative policy.

## 4.4 Physical Safeguards

Physical safeguards are directives designed to protect electronic information systems and related buildings and equipment from natural and environmental hazards, as well as unauthorized intrusion. Within the ontology, a Physical Safeguard is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` that enforces a HIPAA safeguard relationship with an attestation entity.

Unlike administrative safeguards, which may reference a broad range of policy documents, physical safeguards are strictly operational controls regarding facility access and workstation usage. The relational schema requires that every physical safeguard directive be linked to a specific attestation record, confirming that the physical control (e.g., a lock, a badge reader, a security guard) has been inspected or verified.

**Table 4.3: Physical Safeguard Directives and Verification Attestations**

The following table encodes the physical safeguards implemented within the facility perimeter. The `x` column specifies the class of the physical control, and the `y` column references the attestation ID that verifies the control's efficacy.

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| PHY-SEC-01 | FacilityAccessControl | ATT-FAC-01 |
| PHY-SEC-02 | WorkstationSecurityProtocol | ATT-WKS-03 |
| PHY-SEC-03 | DeviceAndMediaControl | ATT-DMC-05 |
| PHY-SEC-04 | DisposalProcedureStandard | ATT-DPS-07 |
| PHY-SEC-05 | PhysicalAccessLogValidation | ATT-PAL-11 |

Here, the `FacilityAccessControl` directive (PHY-SEC-01) is mapped to attestation `ATT-FAC-01`. This linkage implies that the policy controlling entry to the facility is formally verified by the evidence contained within attestation `ATT-FAC-01`, such as a recent access log audit or a physical security inspection report. By enforcing this relationship, the organization ensures that physical security policies are not merely theoretical but are subject to regular, evidence-based verification.

## 4.5 Technical Safeguards

Technical safeguards involve the technology and the policy and procedures for its use that protect electronic health information and control access to it. These directives are automated controls, such as encryption, authentication mechanisms, and audit controls. In the ontology, a Technical Safeguard is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` that must be associated with an attestation via the `hipaaSafeguard` object property.

The schema for technical safeguards mirrors that of physical safeguards, necessitating a foreign key reference to an attestation table. This requirement bridges the gap between technical configurations (often managed by IT departments) and compliance verification (often managed by risk or compliance officers). It ensures that changes to technical architectures are reflected in the compliance documentation through updated attestations.

**Table 4.4: Technical Safeguard Directives and Audit Attestations**

This table presents the technical safeguards currently active within the information system. Column `x` denotes the technical control class, while column `y` links to the attestation record evidencing the control's implementation and integrity.

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| TEC-SEC-01 | AccessControlMechanism | ATT-ACM-02 |
| TEC-SEC-02 | AuditControlMechanism | ATT-AUD-04 |
| TEC-SEC-03 | IntegrityControlMechanism | ATT-INT-06 |
| TEC-SEC-04 | TransmissionSecurityProtocol | ATT-TSP-08 |
| TEC-SEC-05 | EncryptionConfiguration | ATT-ENC-10 |

For example, the `AccessControlMechanism` (TEC-SEC-01), which may define user authentication protocols, is linked to `ATT-ACM-02`. This attestation would contain the evidence—such as a penetration test result or an access review report—confirming that the mechanism is functioning correctly and meets the standards set forth in the directive. This relational mapping is essential for maintaining the "continuous compliance" posture required by modern regulatory frameworks.

## 4.6 PCI DSS Requirements Integration

While HIPAA provides the baseline for protected health information, organizations processing payment cards must also adhere to the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS). The ontology accommodates this cross-domain requirement through a specific axiom for PCI DSS Requirements. Here, the directive (`X`) is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` that satisfies a PCI DSS requirement (`Y`).

The schema dictates that these PCI requirements must also reference an attestation record. This design allows the organization to maintain a unified evidence repository. A single attestation (e.g., a quarterly scan report) might satisfy the evidence requirements for multiple directives across both HIPAA and PCI DSS domains, although the table structure represents a specific link between one PCI requirement and one attestation instance.

**Table 4.5: PCI DSS Requirements and Compliance Attestations**

The table below maps specific PCI DSS requirement directives to their corresponding attestation records. Column `x` identifies the PCI requirement class, and column `y` provides the reference to the attestation evidence.

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| PCI-REQ-01 | FirewallConfigurationPolicy | ATT-FW-01 |
| PCI-REQ-02 | VendorDefaultPasswordChange | ATT-VDP-02 |
| PCI-REQ-03 | CardholderDataEncryption | ATT-CDE-03 |
| PCI-REQ-04 | TransmissionEncryptionProtocol | ATT-TEP-04 |
| PCI-REQ-05 | AntiVirusSoftwareMechanism | ATT-AVS-05 |
| PCI-REQ-06 | SecureSystemDevelopment | ATT-SSD-06 |
| PCI-REQ-07 | AccessControlBusinessNeed | ATT-ACB-07 |

In this schema, the `FirewallConfigurationPolicy` (PCI-REQ-01) is substantiated by attestation `ATT-FW-01`. This linkage demonstrates that the organization's compliance with PCI Requirement 1 is not self-declared but is anchored to a specific, auditable evidence packet. By integrating these requirements into the same relational structure as the HIPAA safeguards, the governance framework enables a holistic view of the organization's security posture, reducing the redundancy of separate audits and ensuring that evidence artifacts are leveraged efficiently across regulatory domains.
