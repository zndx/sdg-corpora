# Chapter 4: Compliance Control Ontology and Evidence Mapping

This chapter delineates the structural framework for mapping regulatory directives to verifiable compliance artifacts within the enterprise governance architecture. It establishes the requisite ontological relationships between HIPAA Security Rule safeguards, PCI DSS requirements, and the evidentiary attestations that substantiate their implementation. The content herein provides a formal specification of the `cco:DirectiveICE` class hierarchy and its intersection with `bfo:Process` entities, ensuring that audit trails are both machine-readable and human-interpretable pursuant to internal controls.

## 4.1 HIPAA Administrative Safeguards

The HIPAA Administrative Safeguards constitute the foundational administrative actions, policies, and procedures employed to manage the selection, development, implementation, and maintenance of security measures. These safeguards are formally modeled within the ontology as a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` (Information Control Element), which serves as the governing construct for all compliance directives.

In accordance with Axiom 1 (`hipaa_safeguard_admin`), an Administrative Safeguard is defined as a directive that specifically safeguards an Attestation process. This relationship establishes a protective dependency where the administrative policy (X) acts as the governing control for the evidence-gathering process (Y). Unlike static documentation, these directives are dynamic; they prescribe the management lifecycle of security controls and ensure that the subsequent attestation of compliance is performed under a structured governance framework.

For instance, the *Security Management Process* directive is not merely a policy statement but a control mechanism that safeguards the integrity of the *Risk Analysis* attestation. By formalizing this relationship, the ontology ensures that every administrative action is intrinsically linked to the verification process it supports. The table below illustrates instances of Administrative Safeguards mapped to their corresponding Attestation IDs, demonstrating the foreign key constraint where the safeguarded entity (Y) must exist as a valid attestation record.

**Table 4.1: Administrative Safeguard Mapping to Attestation Entities**

| ID | Directive Class (X) | Safeguarded Attestation (Y) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ADM-01 | SecurityManagementProcess | ATT-01 |
| ADM-02 | WorkforceSecurityPolicy | ATT-02 |
| ADM-03 | InformationAccessManagement | ATT-03 |
| ADM-04 | SecurityAwarenessTraining | ATT-04 |
| ADM-05 | IncidentResponseProcedure | ATT-05 |

The directives listed in Table 4.1 function as the primary controls for the attestation processes identified in column Y. It is imperative that the `y` value in this table references a valid primary key in the attestation register, ensuring referential integrity across the compliance database.

## 4.2 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

Attestation represents the formal verification mechanism by which an entity confirms that the requisite controls have been implemented and are operating effectively. Modeled as a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process), the Attestation entity captures the temporal and procedural aspect of compliance validation. Per Axiom 2 (`attestation_with_supporting_evidence`), this process is characterized by the possession of supporting evidence, which in this ontological structure is derived from an Administrative Safeguard.

This axiom creates a cyclical yet validating dependency: the Administrative Safeguard safeguards the Attestation, while the Attestation process utilizes the Administrative Safeguard as its supporting evidence. This structure mirrors real-world audit logic where a policy document (the Safeguard) serves as the proof (Evidence) that a review process (the Attestation) was conducted according to standard.

The *Risk Analysis Process* (ATT-01), for example, is a distinct activity performed by the compliance officer. The supporting evidence for this activity is the existence and implementation of the *Security Management Process* directive (ADM-01). This linkage ensures that attestations are not abstract claims but are anchored to specific governing directives.

**Table 4.2: Attestation Processes and Supporting Administrative Evidence**

| ID | Attestation Process (X) | Supporting Evidence (Y) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ATT-01 | RiskAnalysisProcess | ADM-01 |
| ATT-02 | SanctionPolicyReview | ADM-02 |
| ATT-03 | AccessControlAudit | ADM-01 |
| ATT-04 | WorkforceTrainingLog | ADM-04 |
| ATT-05 | IncidentResponseTest | ADM-05 |

As shown in Table 4.2, the `y` column is strictly constrained to reference identifiers from the Administrative Safeguard table (`t_hipaa_safeguard_admin`). This enforces a rigid hierarchy where operational attestations must be backed by administrative authorities. The data indicates that multiple attestation processes (e.g., ATT-01 and ATT-03) may rely on a single administrative directive (ADM-01), reflecting the efficient application of broad governance policies across multiple verification activities.

## 4.3 HIPAA Physical Safeguards

Physical Safeguards are defined as the physical measures, policies, and procedures to protect electronic information systems and related buildings and equipment from natural and environmental hazards and unauthorized intrusion. Within the technical taxonomy, Axiom 3 (`hipaa_safeguard_physical`) classifies these entities as `cco:DirectiveICE` instances that safeguard specific Attestation processes.

The distinction between Administrative and Physical safeguards lies in the locus of control: while Administrative safeguards govern the management of security, Physical safeguards govern the tangible environment. However, the ontological structure remains consistent: the Physical Directive (X) safeguards the Attestation (Y). This implies that the physical control measure (e.g., a facility access policy) is the protective agent for the verification process (e.g., the maintenance of an access log).

Consider the *Facility Access Control* directive. Its purpose is to safeguard the *PhysicalAccessLog* attestation. Without the directive, the log-keeping process lacks a governing control framework, rendering the evidence potentially inadmissible during a compliance audit. The table below details the instantiation of Physical Safeguards and their relationship to the attestation layer.

**Table 4.3: Physical Safeguard Directives and Protected Attestations**

| ID | Directive Class (X) | Safeguarded Attestation (Y) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| PHY-01 | FacilityAccessControl | ATT-06 |
| PHY-02 | WorkstationSecurity | ATT-07 |
| PHY-03 | DeviceAndMediaControl | ATT-08 |
| PHY-04 | ContingencyOperations | ATT-09 |

The foreign key constraint in this schema requires that all entries in the `y` column (ATT-06 through ATT-09) correspond to valid entries in the attestation registry. This mapping ensures that every physical security measure implemented within the facility is directly traceable to a specific compliance verification activity, closing the loop between physical security operations and audit reporting.

## 4.4 HIPAA Technical Safeguards

Technical Safeguards encompass the technology and the policy and procedures for its use that protect electronic health information and control access to it. Axiom 4 (`hipaa_safeguard_technical`) formalizes these as `cco:DirectiveICE` elements, creating a relationship where the technical mechanism safeguards the Attestation process.

In practice, this means that automated controls such as encryption, access authentication, and audit logging are not merely IT configurations but are treated as formal directives. The *Unique User Identification* directive, for instance, safeguards the *SystemAccessAudit* attestation. The directive ensures that the audit trail is attributable to a specific identity, thereby securing the validity of the attestation data itself.

The implementation of technical safeguards must be rigorously documented to satisfy the `sdg:hipaaSafeguard` object property. The following table provides a representative dataset of Technical Safeguards mapped to their respective attestation targets.

**Table 4.4: Technical Safeguard Directives and Protected Attestations**

| ID | Directive Class (X) | Safeguarded Attestation (Y) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| TEC-01 | UniqueUserIdentification | ATT-10 |
| TEC-02 | EmergencyAccessProcedure | ATT-11 |
| TEC-03 | AutomaticLogoff | ATT-12 |
| TEC-04 | EncryptionAndDecryption | ATT-13 |
| TEC-05 | IntegrityControl | ATT-14 |

The integrity of the data in Table 4.4 is maintained by the referential integrity constraint linking the `y` column to the attestation table. This structure facilitates automated compliance queries, allowing auditors to instantly retrieve all technical directives responsible for safeguarding a specific piece of evidence, such as ATT-13 (DataTransmissionLog).

## 4.5 PCI DSS Requirements

The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) requirements represent a distinct but intersecting regulatory domain. Axiom 5 (`pci_dss_requirement`) maps these requirements to the same underlying evidence structure utilized by HIPAA, classifying them as `cco:DirectiveICE` entities that satisfy a `pciDssRequirement` relationship to an Attestation.

This integration allows for a unified governance framework where a single Attestation process may satisfy multiple regulatory obligations. For example, an attestation regarding firewall configuration (ATT-15) may be safeguarded by both a HIPAA Technical Safeguard (Access Control) and a PCI DSS Requirement (Requirement 1: Install and maintain a firewall configuration).

The table below illustrates the mapping of PCI DSS requirements to attestation processes. Note that the `y` column continues to reference the global attestation namespace, demonstrating the cross-compatibility of the evidence model.

**Table 4.5: PCI DSS Requirements and Associated Attestations**

| ID | Directive Class (X) | Requirement Attestation (Y) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| PCI-01 | FirewallConfiguration | ATT-15 |
| PCI-02 | DefaultPasswordChange | ATT-16 |
| PCI-03 | CardholderDataEncryption | ATT-17 |
| PCI-04 | AntiVirusSoftwareUpdate | ATT-18 |
| PCI-05 | SecureSystemDevelopment | ATT-19 |

By populating the `t_pci_dss_requirement` table with these mappings, the organization establishes a clear line of sight from high-level regulatory mandates (PCI DSS Requirements) down to the granular evidence artifacts (Attestations). This structure is essential for unified compliance reporting, allowing the organization to demonstrate adherence to multiple standards (HIPAA and PCI DSS) through a single, coherent set of verification processes.
