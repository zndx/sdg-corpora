# Chapter 7: Ontological Mapping of Regulatory Directives and Evidentiary Controls

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures required to map regulatory directives to technical controls within a compliant information system. It establishes the necessary relationships between lawful bases, jurisdictions, and evidentiary requirements, providing a rigorous framework for the implementation of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and associated governance mandates. The following sections detail the specific axioms governing these relationships, supported by relational data tables that instantiate the logical constraints defined in the underlying schema.

## 7.1 Lawful Basis for Directives

The foundation of any compliant data processing architecture lies in the explicit identification of a lawful basis for operation. Within the context of the Common Core Ontologies (CCO) and the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO), a directive is not merely a suggestion but a prescriptive information content entity that must be anchored to a legal justification. The axiom `gdpr_lawful_basis` asserts that a specific class of directive (`X`) is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` and possesses a lawful basis (`Y`). This relationship is not merely associative but constitutive; without the referenced lawful basis, the directive lacks the ontological status required for valid execution within a regulated environment.

In practice, this means that system architects must ensure that every processing directive—whether an automated data ingestion script or a manual user intervention—is traceable to a specific legal statute or contractual provision. The relational schema for this axiom enforces a strict dependency, where the lawful basis (`y`) must reference a valid policy identifier, ensuring that no directive operates in a legal vacuum.

## 7.2 Jurisdictional Scope of Policies

While lawful basis provides the legal *authority* for a directive, the policy with jurisdiction defines the *territorial and organizational reach* of that authority. The axiom `policy_with_jurisdiction` formalizes the assertion that a directive (`X`) holds a specific jurisdiction (`Y`). This distinction is critical for multinational systems where a single technical control may be subject to conflicting regulatory requirements across different geographies. The ontology treats jurisdiction as a distinct class of entity that must be explicitly linked to the governing directive.

The implementation of this axiom requires a granular approach to policy definition. A "Policy" in this context is a `cco:DirectiveICE` that encapsulates the rules of engagement for a specific region or legal domain. The relational schema enforces a circular dependency with the lawful basis table, reflecting the real-world interdependence of legal authority and jurisdictional applicability. Specifically, the jurisdiction (`y`) must reference a valid lawful basis, indicating that the scope of a policy is constrained by the legal grounds upon which it stands.

## 7.3 Trace Support for Descriptive Claims

In the event of an audit or litigation, the system must demonstrate not only that controls exist but that they functioned correctly at a specific point in time. The axiom `trace_supports_claim` addresses this by defining a class (`X`) that is a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (a process) and supports a claim defined by a `cco:DescriptiveICE`. This concept bridges the gap between dynamic system processes and static descriptive assertions, such as "All data was encrypted at rest on November 1, 2023."

This ontological structure serves as the backbone for the audit trail. It posits that a process (the trace) is the evidentiary vehicle for a descriptive claim. Unlike the previous axioms, this structure does not rely on a complex foreign key web within this specific schema fragment, focusing instead on the intrinsic capability of a process instance to validate a claim. In a technical implementation, this maps to log files, hash chains, or digital signatures that cryptographically verify the state of the system.

## 7.4 Evidence Requirements for Controls

Governance frameworks often fail not due to a lack of controls, but due to a lack of verifiable evidence regarding those controls. The axiom `control_with_evidence_requirement` mandates that a directive (`X`)—specifically a control directive—must require evidence of a specific type (`Y`). This formalizes the "compliance by design" principle, where the definition of a control is inextricably linked to the proof of its execution.

This axiom utilizes the `cco:DirectiveICE` class to represent the control mechanism. The critical structural component is the requirement for evidence (`y`), which must reference a lawful basis. This linkage ensures that the evidence required is not arbitrary but is grounded in the legal necessity established in Section 7.1. For instance, a control governing data deletion requires evidence that satisfies the "right to be forgotten" lawful basis. The schema enforces this by requiring the evidence column to contain a valid identifier from the lawful basis table.

## 7.5 Policy Equivalence: Jurisdiction and Scope

The final axiom, `policy_equiv_jurisdiction_and_scope`, introduces a higher level of semantic complexity by defining an equivalence class. It asserts that a policy (`X`) is equivalent to the intersection of a directive (`cco:DirectiveICE`), a specific jurisdiction (`Y`), and a governed scope (`Z`). This allows the ontology to define policies not just by their existence, but by their precise applicability within a multi-dimensional space of law and operational domain.

This construct is essential for automated compliance reasoning. It allows a system to query, "Which policies apply to Jurisdiction Y and Scope Z?" and receive a precise policy identifier in return. The relational implementation requires that the jurisdiction component (`y`) again reference the lawful basis, maintaining the chain of custody back to the legal foundation, while the scope (`z`) is defined as a non-nullable attribute of the policy itself.
