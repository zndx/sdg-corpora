# Chapter 7: Governance Frameworks for Audit Processes and Artifacts

This chapter establishes the ontological framework governing the definition, execution, and output of audit processes within a regulated environment. It provides a formal specification of the relationships between audit events, temporal scopes, responsible agents, and the resulting artifacts. The definitions herein are aligned with the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) and the Common Core Ontologies (CCO) to ensure semantic interoperability between compliance systems and regulatory reporting mechanisms. The following sections detail the structural axioms required to model the lifecycle of an audit, from the identification of the dataset under review to the finalization of findings.

## 7.1 The Dataset Under Audit

The foundational entity in any audit engagement is the object of scrutiny: the dataset. In compliance governance, a dataset is not merely a collection of data points but is formally categorized as an *Information Content Entity* (ICE) that functions as an artifact. The axiom defining the *Dataset Under Audit* posits that such an entity is a subclass of `cco:Artifact` which participates in the `sdg:underAudit` relationship with a process.

This distinction is critical for regulatory frameworks such as the County Road Administration Board standards or financial reporting requirements (e.g., GASB Statement No. 34). By defining the dataset as an artifact, the framework asserts that the data has a distinct existence, independent of the audit process, yet is the specific target of evaluative procedures. The status of being "under audit" is a relational property that attaches to the artifact for the duration of the audit process.

## 7.2 Audit Period Specification

An audit process is temporally bounded; it does not exist in an indefinite state but is defined by a specific period of coverage. The axiom *Audit For Period* establishes that an audit process (a subclass of `bfo:0000015`, or *Process*) is necessarily associated with a specific audit period.

In this ontological model, the "audit period" is treated as a distinct class of entity. To maintain referential integrity and strict data typing, the audit period is modeled as a reference to a dataset artifact. This approach ensures that the definition of the period—whether it be a fiscal year, a quarter, or a specific bi-weekly interval—is itself a documented, auditable artifact. This prevents ambiguity regarding the scope of the audit (e.g., "Fiscal Year 2020") and anchors the temporal claim to a specific definition record within the `t_dataset_under_audit` table.

## 7.3 Conduct of Audit by Responsible Agents

Governance frameworks require clear accountability. The axiom *Audit Conducted By* formalizes the relationship between the audit process and the agent performing the work. Specifically, it asserts that the audit process is conducted by a `cco:Person`.

This axiom aligns with the "Standards of Good Practice" found in administrative codes (e.g., WAC 136-12-060), which often designate specific roles, such as a County Engineer or an Independent Auditor, responsible for certification. By modeling this as a direct object property between the process and a person, the ontology facilitates the automatic generation of responsibility matrices and audit trail logs. It excludes the possibility of an audit being conducted by a non-agent entity (such as a software script) without a designated responsible human overseer, satisfying regulatory requirements for human verification.

## 7.4 Audit Subclass and Control Directives

Not all audits are structurally identical; they vary based on the object of their evaluation. The axiom *Audit Subclass* refines the definition of an audit process by asserting that it audits a specific `cco:DirectiveICE` (Directive Information Content Entity).

This concept is pivotal for compliance frameworks where the audit target is a policy, a regulation (such as WAC 136-12), or an internal control directive. For example, a "Safety Compliance Audit" is distinct from a "Financial Statement Audit" because the former targets a safety directive, while the latter targets accounting standards. This axiom allows the system to categorize audits based on the specific directive they are enforcing, thereby enabling automated filtering of reports based on regulatory domains (e.g., environmental, financial, administrative).

## 7.5 Audit Findings as Artifacts

The culmination of the audit process is the generation of findings. The axiom *Audit With Findings* posits that an audit process has findings, which are modeled as a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process) resulting in or possessing a finding entity.

Consistent with the treatment of the audit period, the "Findings" are modeled as a reference to a dataset artifact (`t_dataset_under_audit`). This structural choice enforces the principle that audit findings must be documented, persisted, and managed as formal artifacts within the system. They cannot be transient oral communications; they must be recorded as datasets (e.g., "Findings Report 2020") to be valid. This linkage supports the generation of the "Independent Auditors' Report" by providing a direct data path from the audit process instance to the finalized report artifact.
