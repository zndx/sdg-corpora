# Chapter 4: Ontological Formalization of Audit Processes and Artifacts

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter establishes the formal ontological framework required to map audit governance structures to relational data environments. It addresses the systematic definition of audit processes, the artifacts subjected to scrutiny, and the agents responsible for verification. The content herein is intended for systems architects and compliance officers who must implement rigorous evidence-anchored reporting mechanisms consistent with the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) and the Common Core Ontologies (CCO). The definitions provided ensure that audit trails are not merely narrative descriptions but are structurally sound, queryable data entities capable of supporting regulatory inspection and automated governance logic.

## 4.2 Dataset Under Audit

The foundational unit of any audit engagement is the specific object or collection of data to be examined. Within the ontology, this is formalized by the axiom defining the "Dataset Under Audit." This entity is not merely a file; it is a distinct `cco:Artifact` that is temporally locked within the scope of an audit process (`bfo:0000015`). The classification of an entity as a dataset under audit triggers specific immutability requirements and chain-of-custody protocols. It signifies that the artifact has been removed from the operational production environment—conceptually or physically—and placed into a controlled state where it may be subjected to verification procedures without risk of alteration.

In practical terms, the declaration of a dataset under audit serves as the primary key for the engagement. Without this defined artifact, the audit process lacks an object and therefore lacks validity. The relational schema for this concept isolates the artifact, ensuring that all subsequent process steps refer back to this single source of truth.

## 4.3 Audit for Period

An audit process is temporally bounded; it does not exist in an infinite state but is rather a `bfo:Process` that occurs over a specific duration. The axiom "Audit for Period" links the audit process (`X`) to a defined audit period (`Y`). In the context of compliance and governance, this linkage is critical for establishing the relevance of evidence. A dataset from a previous fiscal year may be relevant for a trend analysis, but it is not within the *scope* of the current audit period unless explicitly included.

The relational projection of this axiom requires that the period (`Y`) be mapped to a specific dataset identifier. This enforces a strict data integrity constraint: an audit period cannot be defined in the abstract; it must be anchored to the specific artifact (the dataset) that represents that temporal window. Consequently, the period is effectively defined by the snapshot of data selected for review. This structure prevents ambiguity regarding which version of a temporally evolving dataset is being scrutinized.

## 4.4 Audit Conducted By

Governance frameworks require the attribution of agency. The axiom "Audit Conducted By" formalizes the relationship between the audit process (`X`) and a `cco:Person`. This is not merely a record of participation; it is a structural assertion of responsibility. In the ontology, the audit process is a dependent entity that requires an agent (the person) to execute or oversee it. This relationship is the basis for establishing auditor independence, verifying credentials, and managing conflicts of interest.

When this axiom is instantiated in a relational database, it creates a direct link between the process identifier and the person entity. This allows the system to generate traceability reports that answer "who audited what and when." It is a critical component for forensic readiness, ensuring that every verification step can be traced back to a human agent with the authority and clearance to perform the review.

## 4.5 Audit Subclass

Not all audit processes are homogeneous; they vary based on the object of their control and the regulatory framework they serve. The axiom "Audit Subclass" defines that an audit process (`X`) specifically audits a `cco:DirectiveICE` (Information Content Entity). This distinguishes a financial audit from a security audit or a privacy audit based on the specific directive or control framework being enforced.

In this schema, the audit process is classified by its relationship to a directive. For example, an audit process may be subclassified as a "SOC2 Type 2 Audit" or an "ISO 27001 Surveillance Audit." The relational representation captures this by associating the process ID with the specific directive it serves. This allows the governance system to filter and report on audits based on the specific control objectives they verify, rather than treating all audits as identical procedural events.

## 4.6 Audit with Findings

The ultimate output of an audit process is the generation of findings. The axiom "Audit with Findings" posits that an audit process (`X`) has findings (`Y`). In a rigorous ontological model, findings are not text annotations; they are artifacts in their own right, often derived from the dataset under audit. The findings represent the delta between the expected state (defined by the directive) and the observed state (the dataset).

The relational schema for this axiom links the audit process to the dataset that contains the findings. This creates a closed loop: the audit process consumes a dataset (the evidence), is conducted by a person, adheres to a directive, and produces a new dataset (the findings). By treating findings as a dataset (`y` referencing `t_dataset_under_audit`), the system ensures that audit outputs are subject to the same versioning, retention, and integrity controls as the input data.
