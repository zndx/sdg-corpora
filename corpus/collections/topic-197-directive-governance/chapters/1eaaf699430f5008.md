# Chapter 4: Formal Ontology of Compliance Artifacts and Measurement Provenance

This chapter delineates the structural framework for mapping compliance artifacts—specifically those derived from the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS)—into a formal ontological schema. The objective is to provide a rigorous, machine-readable representation of security directives, descriptive claims, and measurement observations. By grounding these entities in the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) and the Common Core Ontologies (CCO), auditors and governance systems may achieve semantic interoperability between unstructured regulatory text and structured evidence repositories. The following sections introduce five foundational axioms, their relational projections, and the evidentiary context required for high-fidelity compliance reporting.

## 4.1 Descriptive Information Content Entities (DescriptiveICE)

The fundamental unit of semantic compliance data is the Descriptive Information Content Entity (DescriptiveICE). Within the context of the audit ontology, a DescriptiveICE is defined as an information content entity that is about some entity or phenomenon. In the specific case of the `outlier_claim_basic` axiom, we concern ourselves with classes of entities that represent descriptive assertions deviating from a baseline norm. These are not merely data points but declarative statements regarding the state of a system, often identifying anomalies or specific configurations that require scrutiny.

The axiom `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DescriptiveICE` asserts that any class X designated as an outlier claim is fundamentally a descriptive artifact. This classification is critical for audit trails, as it segregates normative statements (what should be) from observational statements (what is). In practice, these classes represent specific categories of findings or log entries that an automated scanning tool or a human auditor might isolate.

The relational projection of this axiom creates a primary registry for these claim classes. The structure is minimal, reflecting the foundational nature of the concept.

## 4.2 Directive ICE and PCI DSS Requirements

While descriptive entities define the state of the world, Directive Information Content Entities (DirectiveICE) prescribe action or adherence to a standard. The `pci_dss_requirement` axiom establishes a critical bridge between the abstract directive and the specific requirement text within the PCI DSS framework. The axiom `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:pciDssRequirement some {Y:Class}` posits that a directive class X is characterized by the satisfaction of a specific requirement class Y.

In this schema, the directive represents the governance artifact (e.g., a corporate policy or a control implementation), while the requirement Y represents the specific PCI DSS mandate being addressed. The relationship is existential: for the directive X to be valid or instantiated, it must necessitate or reference the requirement Y. This structure allows compliance engines to map internal control identifiers directly to external regulatory obligations without ambiguity.

The relational schema enforces a foreign key constraint linking the requirement (`y`) back to the foundational descriptive claims (`t_outlier_claim_basic`). This dependency ensures that every regulatory requirement referenced by a directive is anchored to a defined, descriptive concept of non-compliance or a specific control state.

## 4.3 Attribute Sets and Claim Granularity

To achieve a sufficient level of audit granularity, descriptive claims must be decomposable into specific attributes. The `outlier_claim_min_one_attribute` axiom refines the basic descriptive entity by introducing the `sdg:hasAttributeSet` property. The axiom `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DescriptiveICE, sdg:hasAttributeSet min 1 {Y:Class}` mandates that a descriptive entity X possesses at least one attribute Y.

This structure facilitates the detailed breakdown of a compliance event. For instance, if the basic claim is an "UnencryptedTransmissionEvent," the attribute set might include "CipherSuite," "TLSVersion," or "PayloadSize." By enforcing a `min 1` cardinality, the ontology ensures that no descriptive claim is entered into the audit record without at least one defining characteristic. This is essential for root cause analysis and automated remediation workflows, as the attributes provide the parameters necessary for filtering and correlation logic.

The relational projection links these attribute-rich claims back to the PCI requirements. The foreign key constraint (`y` references `t_pci_dss_requirement`) ensures that the detailed attributes are always attached to a valid, requirement-bound directive.

## 4.4 Measurement Entities

Compliance verification relies heavily on the quantitative assessment of controls. The `measurement_basic` axiom formalizes this by defining classes of entities that represent specific measurements. The axiom `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DescriptiveICE` treats a measurement as a specific type of descriptive information content entity—one that is generated by a measuring process and bears a value.

In the context of this framework, a measurement class X might represent "EncryptionKeyLength," "FileIntegrityCheckTimestamp," or "UserSessionDuration." Unlike the outlier claims, which may be categorical (e.g., "Unencrypted"), measurements provide the raw numerical or temporal data that supports the compliance conclusion. The distinction is vital for the "evidence-anchored" prose of audit reports, where assertions must be backed by verifiable metrics.

The relational schema for measurements is a simple registry, reflecting that these are the atomic data points collected by monitoring agents. They serve as the leaf nodes in the compliance tree, providing the empirical basis for higher-level claims.

## 4.5 Aggregation Entities

Finally, to support high-level reporting and dashboarding, individual measurements must be synthesized into aggregate forms. The `aggregation_basic` axiom defines classes of entities that represent these summaries. Following the axiom `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DescriptiveICE`, an aggregation is treated as a descriptive entity that is about a collection of other entities (typically measurements).

Aggregations are necessary for executive summaries and trend analysis. For example, while a "KeyLengthMetric" provides a single value, an "AverageKeyLengthAggregation" describes the state of encryption across an entire subnet. Similarly, a "TotalFailedLoginCount" aggregates individual "LoginSourceIPObservation" events. The ontology treats these aggregations as first-class information entities, allowing them to be annotated with metadata (such as time windows or scope) just like raw measurements.

The relational structure for aggregations mirrors that of basic measurements, maintaining a flat registry of aggregation types. This design allows the reporting layer to query all available aggregation methods dynamically without needing to traverse complex hierarchies at runtime.
