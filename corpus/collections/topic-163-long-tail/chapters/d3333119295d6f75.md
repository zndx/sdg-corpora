# Chapter 7: Governance Provisions and Evidence Verification Mechanisms

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures governing compliance processes within the enterprise data environment. It establishes the requisite protocols for attestation, the determination of lawful bases under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), and the procedural verification of evidence. Furthermore, it defines the roles of responsible persons in signing and conducting audits. The provisions herein are binding upon all data processing entities subject to the DirectiveICE framework and must be implemented with strict adherence to the relational schemas defined in the technical appendices.

## 1. Attestation with Supporting Evidence

### 1.1 Definition and Scope
An attestation is formally defined as a process (bfo:0000015) that substantiates the validity of a specific state of affairs through the explicit citation of supporting evidence. In the context of information governance, an attestation is not merely a declaration but a procedural artifact that must be intrinsically linked to evidentiary proof. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` posits that for an attestation {X} to be valid, it must possess the property `sdg:withSupportingEvidence`, which relates the attestation to a specific evidence class {Y}. This relationship ensures that every compliance claim is anchored to a verifiable datum, thereby mitigating the risk of baseless assertions.

### 1.2 Evidence Chain of Custody
The supporting evidence referenced in an attestation must maintain a strict chain of custody. The evidence class {Y} may comprise logs, contractual agreements, or system outputs that demonstrate the factual basis of the attestation. The integrity of the attestation process relies on the immutability of this linkage. Governance frameworks mandate that the relationship between {X} and {Y} be recorded in a manner that prevents decoupling; if the evidence is invalidated or retracted, the attestation must automatically be flagged for review.

### 1.3 Relational Implementation
The following table embodies the axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence`. The primary key `id` uniquely identifies the attestation instance. The column `x` denotes the specific attestation process class, while the column `y` acts as a foreign key referencing the lawful basis that supports this attestation. This structure enforces the dependency of the attestation on the existence of a valid lawful basis record.

## 2. GDPR Lawful Basis

### 2.1 DirectiveICE Compliance
Under the DirectiveICE framework, any processing of personal data must be grounded in a specific lawful basis as prescribed by the GDPR. The axiom `gdpr_lawful_basis` asserts that a DirectiveICE class {X} must possess the property `sdg:hasLawfulBasis`, linking it to a basis class {Y}. This provision is critical for establishing the legality of processing operations. The lawful basis serves as the legal justification for the processing activity and must be determined prior to the commencement of any operation.

### 2.2 Basis Determination and Mapping
The determination of a lawful basis is a complex legal and technical undertaking. It requires a mapping between the processing activity and one of the six bases identified in Article 6 of the GDPR. The ontology treats this as a directed relationship where the Directive (the processing instruction) is the subject and the lawful basis is the object. This mapping must be documented in the system of record to facilitate automated compliance checking and reporting.

### 2.3 Relational Implementation
The table below represents the axiom `gdpr_lawful_basis`. The `id` column serves as the primary key for the lawful basis record. Column `x` references the DirectiveICE or specific processing class requiring the basis. Column `y` is a foreign key that references the attestation record which validates this basis. This circular dependency with the attestation table ensures that lawful bases are not merely theoretical but are actively supported by attestation processes.

## 3. Attestation Signed By

### 3.1 Authorization and Accountability
To ensure non-repudiation and personal accountability, every attestation process must be formally signed by an authorized natural person. The axiom `attestation_signed_by` defines that an attestation {X} (a subclass of bfo:0000015) must be associated with a `cco:Person` via the property `sdg:signedBy`. This requirement binds the procedural artifact to a legal entity, ensuring that an individual can be held accountable for the veracity of the attestation.

### 3.2 Signatory Protocols
The signatory must be an individual with the requisite authority level commensurate with the scope of the attestation. In automated environments, the signing process may utilize digital certificates or cryptographic keys, but the underlying entity referenced must remain a `cco:Person` (e.g., a designated officer), not a system account. The governance framework mandates that the signature include a timestamp and the unique identifier of the signatory to satisfy audit trail requirements.

### 3.3 Relational Implementation
The table `t_attestation_signed_by` records the execution of the signing process. The `id` is the unique identifier for the signing event. The column `x` references the attestation process that was signed. While the schema does not explicitly include a column for the person's ID in this projection, the axiom implies the existence of the person object; in this relational view, the focus is on the existence of the signing event linked to the process.

## 4. Verification Produces Evidence

### 4.1 Verification Process Parameters
Verification is the systematic process of checking the accuracy, integrity, or compliance of a state against a defined standard. The axiom `verification_produces_evidence` states that a verification process {X} yields evidence {Y}. This evidence is the output of the verification activity and serves as the factual record of the check. To ensure the reliability of the verification, the following technical parameters must be observed:

*   **Media:** Structured Data – Unstructured Logs – Binary Blobs
*   **Latency:** < 200 mSec Max
*   **Integrity Check:** SHA-256
*   **Retention Period:** 7 years minimum
*   **Mounting:** Read-Only snapshot

### 4.2 Evidence Generation
The evidence produced by verification is distinct from the raw data being verified. It is a meta-layer that asserts a property (e.g., "verified," "compliant," "intact") about the data at a specific point in time. This evidence is subsequently utilized to support attestations or lawful bases. The ontology dictates that this evidence is a first-class class {Y}, allowing it to be queried, audited, and referenced independently of the verification process that created it.

### 4.3 Relational Implementation
The table `t_verification_produces_evidence` captures the output of verification processes. The `id` is the primary key. Column `x` identifies the verification process instance. Column `y` is a foreign key referencing the lawful basis record that this evidence supports. This linkage demonstrates that the evidence generated by verification is instrumental in establishing or maintaining the lawful basis for processing.

## 5. Audit Conducted By

### 5.1 Audit Governance
An audit is a formal, systematic inspection of an entity's processes, records, and controls. The axiom `audit_conducted_by` establishes that an audit {X} (a subclass of bfo:0000015) must be performed by a `cco:Person` via the property `sdg:conductedBy`. This ensures that audits are conducted by qualified individuals capable of exercising professional judgment. The audit process is distinct from automated verification; it involves a holistic review of governance, compliance, and operational effectiveness.

### 5.2 Proceedings and Record-Keeping
Audit proceedings must be documented with the same rigor as regulatory minutes. The following structure is prescribed for the commencement of audit activities:

**AUDIT LOG ENTRY:**
*   **Date:** October 24, 2023
*   **Time:** 09:00 AM
*   **Location:** Secure Conference Room B / Virtual Bridge 4
*   **Call to Order:** The Audit Committee Chair called the meeting to order.
*   **Members Present:** J. Smith (Lead Auditor), A. Doe (Compliance Officer), R. Roe (Legal Counsel).
*   **Agenda Item 1:** Review of Q3 Processing Activities.
*   **Agenda Item 2:** Verification of Lawful Basis documentation.

### 5.3 Relational Implementation
The table `t_audit_conducted_by` logs the assignment and execution of audit processes. The `id` serves as the unique identifier for the audit instance. Column `x` references the specific audit process being conducted. This table serves as a registry of audit events, ensuring that every audit is traceable to a defined process scope.
