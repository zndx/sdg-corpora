# Chapter 4: Regulatory Compliance Mapping and Control Directives

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter establishes the formal ontology for mapping regulatory directives to specific control implementations within a hybrid governance framework. The content herein defines the structural relationships between the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) Security Rule safeguards, the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) requirements, and the generalized Information Control Elements (ICE) that constitute the organization's defensive posture. The objective is to provide a deterministic, auditable lineage from high-level regulatory obligations down to specific technical, physical, and administrative controls. All mappings presented herein are derived from the foundational axiom set defined in the Compliance Ontology (CO) version 2.4, ensuring that every directive is traceable to a specific threat mitigation class.

## 4.2 Physical Safeguards and Implementation Directives

### 4.2.1 Axiomatic Definition

Under the HIPAA Security Rule, Physical Safeguards are defined as physical measures to protect electronic information systems and related buildings and equipment from natural and environmental hazards and unauthorized intrusion. In the formal ontology, this relationship is captured by Axiom 1 (`hipaa_safeguard_physical`). The axiom posits that a Class (X), representing a specific Physical Safeguard directive, is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` and necessitates the existence of a HIPAA safeguard relationship with a target Class (Y).

Verbalization: {X} is something that hipaa safeguard {Y}.

This axiom establishes the dependency between the directive (the rule requiring an action) and the safeguard (the mechanism of protection). It ensures that physical controls are not merely abstract concepts but are instantiated as specific directives within the governance framework.

### 4.2.2 Implementation and Data Structure

The implementation of physical safeguards requires a rigorous mapping of directives to the specific controls they enforce. The relational schema derived from this axiom treats the Physical Safeguard as the primary entity, which must reference a valid Control ID that addresses a specific threat. This structure enforces the constraint that a physical safeguard cannot exist in isolation; it must be a manifestation of a broader control strategy.

The following table illustrates the instantiation of Physical Safeguard directives. The primary key (`id`) represents the unique identifier for the physical directive, while the foreign key (`y`) references the specific control implementation that addresses the risk. This linkage ensures that for every physical measure—such as facility access controls or workstation usage—there is a corresponding, validated control mechanism.

**Table 4.1: Physical Safeguard Directives Mapping**

| id | x (Directive Class) | y (Control Reference) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| phys_facility_access | FacilityAccessControl | ctrl_unauth_access |
| phys_workstation_security | WorkstationSecurity | ctrl_device_theft |
| phys_media_disposal | MediaDisposalControl | ctrl_data_remnant |

*Table 4.1 embodies Axiom 1 (`hipaa_safeguard_physical`). The primary key is `id`, representing the unique directive instance. The column `y` acts as a foreign key referencing the control table (`t_control_only_addresses_threat_class`), establishing that the physical directive safeguards the entity defined in the referenced control.*

## 4.3 Risk Addressing Controls and Threat Classes

### 4.3.1 Axiomatic Definition

The core of the compliance framework lies in the definition of controls that exclusively address specific threat classes. Axiom 2 (`control_only_addresses_threat_class`) defines the structural constraint where a Class (X), representing a Control Directive, is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` and possesses the property of addressing a Risk (Y) exclusively. This "only" constraint is critical for auditability; it implies that the control is scoped strictly to the defined threat class, preventing scope creep and ensuring precise risk mitigation.

Verbalization: {X} is something that addresses risk {Y}.

This axiom serves as the central hub in the relational model. It defines the "Control" entity that is referenced by the various safeguard types (Physical, Technical, Administrative) and PCI DSS requirements. The bidirectional nature of the schema—where controls reference physical safeguards and vice-versa—creates a closed loop of verification. A control addressing a risk must be grounded in a physical reality (the safeguard), and that physical safeguard must be justified by the control it enables.

### 4.3.2 Control-to-Threat Lineage

The data structure for this axiom requires that every control addressing a risk must be associated with a Physical Safeguard ID. This recursive dependency validates the physical enforcement of logical controls. For instance, a control designed to address "Unauthorized Ingress" must be linked to a physical directive, such as "Facility Access Control," ensuring that the logical risk mitigation has a tangible enforcement mechanism.

**Table 4.2: Control Directives and Threat Classes**

| id | x (Control Class) | y (Physical Safeguard Reference) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ctrl_unauth_access | UnauthorizedIngressControl | phys_facility_access |
| ctrl_device_theft | AssetTheftMitigation | phys_workstation_security |
| ctrl_data_remnant | DataLeakagePrevention | phys_media_disposal |
| ctrl_transmission_intercept | NetworkEavesdroppingControl | phys_facility_access |

*Table 4.2 embodies Axiom 2 (`control_only_addresses_threat_class`). The primary key is `id`, identifying the specific control. The column `y` is a foreign key referencing `t_hipaa_safeguard_physical(id)`, mandating that every risk-addressing control must be anchored by a physical safeguard directive, thereby closing the loop between logical risk mitigation and physical enforcement.*

## 4.4 Technical Safeguards

### 4.4.1 Axiomatic Definition

Technical Safeguards involve the technology and the policy and procedures for its use that protect electronic health information and control access to it. Axiom 3 (`hipaa_safeguard_technical`) formalizes this by defining a Class (X) as a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` that enforces a HIPAA safeguard relationship with a target Class (Y). Unlike physical safeguards, technical safeguards are concerned with the logical mechanisms of access control, audit trails, and integrity controls.

Verbalization: {X} is something that hipaa safeguard {Y}.

The distinction in the ontology between technical and physical safeguards is maintained through separate class hierarchies, though both ultimately reference the same central control table. This separation allows for granular reporting—e.g., generating a technical compliance matrix independent of facility access logs—while maintaining a unified view of risk at the control layer.

### 4.4.2 Technical Control Implementation

In the relational schema, the Technical Safeguard table references the central Control table. This allows a single control (e.g., "Network Eavesdropping Control") to be the target of multiple safeguard types. For example, the risk of eavesdropping might be mitigated by a Technical Safeguard (Encryption) and a Physical Safeguard (Shielded Cabling). The schema supports this many-to-many relationship by linking distinct safeguard rows to the same control ID.

**Table 4.3: Technical Safeguard Directives**

| id | x (Directive Class) | y (Control Reference) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| tech_access_control | AccessControlMechanism | ctrl_unauth_access |
| tech_audit_control | AuditTrailMechanism | ctrl_unauth_access |
| tech_integrity_control | DataIntegrityMechanism | ctrl_data_remnant |
| tech_transmission_encryption | EncryptionInTransit | ctrl_transmission_intercept |

*Table 4.3 embodies Axiom 3 (`hipaa_safeguard_technical`). The primary key is `id`. The column `y` is a foreign key referencing `t_control_only_addresses_threat_class(id)`. This mapping demonstrates how technical directives, such as encryption or access control, implement the broader risk-addressing controls defined in Axiom 2.*

## 4.5 Administrative Safeguards

### 4.5.1 Axiomatic Definition

Administrative Safeguards are the administrative actions, policies, and procedures to manage the selection, development, implementation, and maintenance of security measures to protect electronic health information. Axiom 4 (`hipaa_safeguard_admin`) defines these as classes of directives (`cco:DirectiveICE`) that safeguard specific entities. These are often the most complex safeguards to implement, as they rely on human behavior and organizational culture rather than hardware or software configuration.

Verbalization: {X} is something that hipaa safeguard {Y}.

The administrative layer provides the governance oversight for the technical and physical layers. In the ontology, administrative safeguards link to the same control definitions as technical safeguards, reinforcing the principle that controls (risk mitigations) require multi-layered enforcement strategies (Defense in Depth).

### 4.5.2 Policy and Governance Mapping

The data table for administrative safeguards maps specific policy directives—such as "Security Awareness Training" or "Contingency Planning"—to the controls they support. For instance, a "Security Awareness Training" directive supports the "Unauthorized Ingress Control" by reducing the likelihood of social engineering attacks that bypass physical or technical barriers.

**Table 4.4: Administrative Safeguard Directives**

| id | x (Directive Class) | y (Control Reference) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| admin_security_training | SecurityAwarenessTraining | ctrl_unauth_access |
| admin_contingency_plan | ContingencyPlanProtocol | ctrl_device_theft |
| admin_sanction_policy | SanctionPolicyEnforcement | ctrl_unauth_access |
| admin_info_access_policy | InformationAccessPolicy | ctrl_data_remnant |

*Table 4.4 embodies Axiom 4 (`hipaa_safeguard_admin`). The primary key is `id`. The column `y` is a foreign key referencing `t_control_only_addresses_threat_class(id)`. This structure allows the organization to trace specific administrative policies (e.g., sanction policies) back to the specific risks they are intended to mitigate (e.g., unauthorized access).*

## 4.6 PCI DSS Requirements Integration

### 4.6.1 Axiomatic Definition

For organizations operating in hybrid environments where healthcare data intersects with payment card processing, the PCI DSS framework must be integrated into the governance ontology. Axiom 5 (`pci_dss_requirement`) defines a Class (X) as a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` that necessitates a PCI DSS requirement relationship with a target Class (Y). This axiom allows the organization to map specific PCI requirements (e.g., "Change vendor defaults") to the internal control framework.

Verbalization: {X} is something that pci dss requirement {Y}.

By mapping PCI requirements to the same `t_control_only_addresses_threat_class` table used for HIPAA safeguards, the organization achieves a unified control framework. This allows for a single source of truth for risk management, where a single control (e.g., "Asset Theft Mitigation") satisfies both a HIPAA Physical Safeguard (Workstation Security) and a PCI DSS Requirement (Restrict physical access to cardholder data).

### 4.6.2 Cross-Framework Compliance

The relational schema for PCI DSS requirements links external compliance obligations to internal control definitions. This mapping is essential for unified audits. It demonstrates to external assessors that the organization's internal control architecture (`cco:DirectiveICE`) is sufficient to meet the specific requirements of multiple regulatory regimes simultaneously.

**Table 4.5: PCI DSS Requirement Mapping**

| pci_req_9_2 | MediaControl | phys_media_disposal |
| pci_req_10_2 | AuditTrailAccess | ctrl_unauth_access |

*Table 4.5 embodies Axiom 5 (`pci_dss_requirement`). The primary key is `id`. The column `y` is a foreign key referencing `t_control_only_addresses_threat_class(id)`. This table maps specific PCI DSS requirements, such as restricting physical access (Req 9), to the internal control definitions, thereby harmonizing the HIPAA and PCI DSS compliance landscapes.*
