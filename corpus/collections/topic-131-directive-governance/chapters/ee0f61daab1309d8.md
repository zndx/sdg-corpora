# Chapter 4: Kernel Audit Frameworks and Syscall Classification

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the structural requirements for kernel-level syscall auditing within secure computing environments, specifically focusing on the intersection of eBPF observability and information governance frameworks. It establishes the ontological foundation for classifying system calls as Designative Information Content Entities (ICE) subject to process-based auditing, ensuring that kernel interactions adhere to strict compliance mandates. The guidelines provided herein are essential for system architects and compliance officers tasked with implementing granular audit trails in high-assurance systems. By formalizing the relationship between syscall classes and auditing processes, this framework facilitates the generation of tamper-evident logs required for regulatory verification.

## 4.2 Ontological Foundations of Syscall Auditing

### 4.2.1 The Axiom of Auditability

In the context of kernel observability, the classification of system calls extends beyond mere functional enumeration to include their role as information carriers within a governance architecture. The core axiom governing this relationship posits that a specific class of system calls, denoted as `{X}`, is a subclass of `cco:DesignativeICE` and is audited by a process entity defined as `bfo:0000015`.

Formally, the relationship is expressed as:
`Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DesignativeICE, sdg:auditedBy some bfo:0000015`

This axiom asserts two critical properties. First, the syscall class `{X}` functions as a *Designative Information Content Entity*, meaning it serves as a symbolic or linguistic designation within the system's operational semantics. Second, the existence of this entity is intrinsically linked to an auditing process (`bfo:0000015`). This linkage ensures that any instantiation of `{X}` is not merely a computational event but a recorded artifact subject to oversight. In practical terms, this means that when a syscall matching class `{X}` is invoked, the kernel must trigger an audit event captured by the designated process.

### 4.2.2 Implementation via eBPF

The enforcement of the `auditedBy` relationship is typically achieved through Extended Berkeley Packet Filter (eBPF) programs attached to the kernel's tracepoints or kprobes. The eBPF program acts as the enforcement mechanism, intercepting the execution of the syscall class `{X}` and marshaling the relevant context (registers, return values, timestamps) to a user-space auditing process (`bfo:0000015`). This architecture ensures that the audit trail is generated in real-time with minimal overhead, maintaining the integrity of the `DesignativeICE` without compromising system performance.

## 4.4 Governance and Compliance Mapping

### 4.4.1 Regulatory Alignment

The classification of syscalls as `DesignativeICE` entities facilitates direct mapping to regulatory controls. Compliance frameworks such as NIST 800-53 or GDPR Article 32 require evidence of access control and system monitoring. The `auditedBy` relationship provides the necessary evidence chain, linking the specific system event (the syscall) to the recording mechanism (the process).

To ensure comprehensive coverage, the audit framework must distinguish between high-risk and low-risk operations. High-risk classes, such as `ProcessControlSignal` (which includes `kill` and `ptrace`), often require immediate alerting capabilities, whereas `FileDescriptorOperation` may be aggregated for periodic review.

## 4.5 Verification and Integrity

The integrity of the audit process relies on the immutable linkage between the syscall event and the log entry. As the `DesignativeICE` is captured by the `bfo:0000015` process, it must be cryptographically signed. This ensures that the `auditedBy` relationship can be verified post-facto, satisfying the requirements for forensic investigation and regulatory auditing. Any discrepancy between the defined class in `t_syscall_audited_by` and the runtime behavior constitutes a compliance violation and must trigger an automated incident response.
