# Chapter 4: Governance Ontology and Compliance Attestation

## 4.1 Scope and Framework

This chapter delineates the formal ontology governing the intersection of regulatory compliance frameworks and internal governance controls. Specifically, it addresses the structural integration of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the System and Organization Controls (SOC 2) criteria within a unified information architecture. The text establishes the axiomatic relationships between directive classes, attestation processes, and evidentiary requirements, providing a deterministic mapping for compliance officers and auditors. The following sections define the core entities—Directive Information Content Entities (ICE), Processes, and Persons—and describe how these classes interact to satisfy legal and operational obligations. Through the relational projection of these axioms, the chapter demonstrates how specific regulatory articles are instantiated as auditable controls supported by verifiable evidence.

## 4.2 Attestation and Signatory Authority

In the context of governance frameworks, the validity of any control or directive is contingent upon the formal attestation by a responsible natural person. This concept is captured by the axiom defining the attestation process as a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process), which necessitates a `sdg:signedBy` relationship with a `cco:Person`.

The entity class `AttestationSignedBy` represents the formalization of this requirement. It posits that for a governance process to be valid, it must be explicitly signed by an authorized individual. This is not merely a procedural step but a structural requirement of the ontology, ensuring that accountability is traceable to a specific human agent rather than an automated system or abstract role. The attestation serves as the bridge between the static definition of a control and its operational enactment.

Table 4.1 illustrates instances of attestation processes where the signatory requirement is enforced. The primary key (`id`) serves as the unique reference for the attestation class, while the `x` column denotes the specific process class being attested.

**Table 4.1: Attestation Processes and Signatory Requirements**

| id | x |
|---|---|
| Article15 | DataSubjectAccessAttestation |
| Article33 | BreachNotificationAttestation |
| CC6.1 | LogicalAccessAttestation |
| CC6.6 | DeleteDataAttestation |
| CC7.2 | IncidentResponseAttestation |

The data in Table 4.1 demonstrates that specific regulatory identifiers (such as GDPR Articles or SOC 2 Criteria codes) are utilized as the primary keys for these attestation classes. This design choice facilitates direct referencing by higher-level directives, ensuring that every governance action is anchored to a specific, signed authorization.

## 4.3 GDPR Directive Integration

The General Data Protection Regulation imposes specific legal obligations that must be mapped onto the organization's governance architecture. This is formalized by the axiom stating that a Directive Information Content Entity (`cco:DirectiveICE`) is a subclass that satisfies a specific GDPR article.

The `GDPRArticleSubclass` axiom defines the relationship where a directive class (`X`) is characterized by its adherence to a specific GDPR article (`Y`). In this schema, the `y` slot is populated by the identifier of the attestation class defined in the previous section. This foreign key relationship enforces the rule that a GDPR directive cannot exist in isolation; it must be intrinsically linked to an attestation process that has been signed by a responsible person.

For instance, the "Right to Access" (Article 15) is not merely a legal text but a directive that activates a specific attestation process. Table 4.2 presents the mapping of directive classes to their corresponding GDPR articles, utilizing the attestation identifiers established in Table 4.1.

**Table 4.2: GDPR Directive Subclass Mappings**

| id | x | y |
|---|---|---|
| dir_01 | RightToAccessDirective | Article15 |
| dir_02 | BreachNotificationDirective | Article33 |
| dir_03 | DataMinimizationDirective | CC6.6 |
| dir_04 | SecureDisposalDirective | CC6.6 |
| dir_05 | PrivacyImpactDirective | Article35 |

In this relational structure, `dir_01` (RightToAccessDirective) is functionally dependent on `Article15`. This ensures that any operationalization of the Right to Access directive automatically invokes the `DataSubjectAccessAttestation` process, thereby satisfying the signatory requirement defined in Section 4.2.

## 4.4 SOC 2 Trust Criteria Alignment

Parallel to the GDPR framework, the SOC 2 trust services criteria require organizations to implement controls related to security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy. The `SOC2TrustCriterion` axiom establishes that a Directive ICE is a subclass satisfying a specific SOC 2 criterion.

Similar to the GDPR integration, the SOC 2 criteria are mapped to the attestation framework. The `y` slot in this context references the same `t_attestation_signed_by` table, reinforcing the principle that trust criteria are operationalized through signed attestations. This unified approach allows the organization to maintain a single registry of attestations that serve multiple compliance frameworks simultaneously.

Table 4.3 depicts the alignment of SOC 2 directives with specific attestation identifiers. Notably, some identifiers (such as `CC6.1` and `CC6.6`) originate from the SOC 2 Common Criteria (CC), yet they function as the primary keys for attestation classes, demonstrating the interoperability of the ontology.

**Table 4.3: SOC 2 Trust Criteria and Attestation Linkages**

| id | x | y |
|---|---|---|
| soc_01 | AccessControlPolicyDirective | CC6.1 |
| soc_02 | IncidentResponseProcedureDirective | CC7.2 |
| soc_03 | DataDisposalStandardDirective | CC6.6 |
| soc_04 | ChangeManagementDirective | CC8.1 |
| soc_05 | SystemMonitoringDirective | CC6.1 |

The mapping in Table 4.3 indicates that the `AccessControlPolicyDirective` is substantiated by the `LogicalAccessAttestation` (ID `CC6.1`). This structure ensures that the abstract criteria of SOC 2 are grounded in concrete, signed processes, providing the necessary evidence for auditors evaluating the organization's control environment.

## 4.5 Evidence and Supporting Documentation

A fundamental requirement of any robust compliance regime is the maintenance of supporting evidence. The `AttestationWithSupportingEvidence` axiom asserts that a process (specifically an attestation process) must include `sdg:withSupportingEvidence` linking it to a specific record or artifact.

In this ontology, the evidence is not an unstructured file but is formally linked to the directive subclass it supports. The relational schema dictates that the evidence (`y`) references a `GDPRArticleSubclass`. This creates a dependency chain: the Evidence supports the Directive, and the Directive references the Attestation. This hierarchy ensures that every piece of evidence is traceable to a specific legal or regulatory requirement, and that requirement is, in turn, validated by a signatory.

Table 4.4 provides instances of attestation processes that are supported by specific evidence artifacts. The `x` column represents the process of maintaining evidence, while the `y` column references the directive ID (e.g., `dir_01`) from Table 4.2.

**Table 4.4: Attestation Processes with Supporting Evidence**

| id | x | y |
|---|---|---|
| ev_01 | AccessLogRetentionProcess | dir_01 |
| ev_02 | BreachTimelineDocumentationProcess | dir_02 |
| ev_03 | DataWipeCertificateProcess | dir_04 |
| ev_04 | AccessReviewLogProcess | soc_01 |
| ev_05 | ChangeTicketAuditTrailProcess | soc_04 |

For example, `ev_01` (AccessLogRetentionProcess) serves as the supporting evidence for `dir_01` (RightToAccessDirective). This linkage confirms that the organization maintains the necessary logs to prove that data subject access requests are handled in accordance with the attestation signed under Article 15.

## 4.6 Audit Control Mechanisms

The final component of the governance ontology is the audit function. The `AuditSubclass` axiom defines a class of processes (`X`) that are responsible for auditing `cco:DirectiveICE`. This represents the meta-governance layer—the controls that control the controls.

Audit processes are distinct from the directives they audit; they are independent verification mechanisms designed to assess the efficacy of the directives and the validity of the associated attestations. While the audit process itself is a `bfo:0000015` (Process), its specific purpose is the examination of Directive ICEs.

Table 4.5 enumerates the specific audit subclasses implemented within the framework. These processes are responsible for systematically reviewing the directives defined in the previous sections to ensure ongoing compliance.

**Table 4.5: Audit Subclass Definitions**

| id | x |
|---|---|
| aud_01 | AnnualGDPRComplianceAudit |
| aud_02 | QuarterlySOC2Review |
| aud_03 | ContinuousAccessControlAudit |
| aud_04 | DataBreachResponseDrillAudit |
| aud_05 | ThirdPartyRiskAudit |

The `AnnualGDPRComplianceAudit` (`aud_01`) is the process that scrutinizes all `GDPRArticleSubclass` instances, verifying that the attestations are current and the supporting evidence (as defined in Table 4.4) is adequate. This closed-loop system—from legal axiom to audit verification—constitutes a comprehensive, evidence-anchored governance framework.
