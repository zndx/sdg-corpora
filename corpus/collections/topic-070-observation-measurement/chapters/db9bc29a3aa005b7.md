# Chapter 4: Formalization of Regulatory Directives and Attestation Mechanisms

This chapter delineates the ontological mapping of regulatory compliance frameworks, specifically focusing on the integration of GDPR Articles and SOC2 Trust Criteria within a unified governance model. It examines the structural dependencies between attestation processes, evidence chains, and audit protocols as defined by the core axioms of the compliance ontology. The analysis provides a deterministic schema for representing these relationships in a relational database environment, ensuring referential integrity across compliance artifacts. Readers will gain a technical understanding of how to instantiate these classes to support automated compliance verification and reporting.

## 1. Attestation Signed By

The foundational unit of any governance framework is the attestation, a process wherein a responsible party formally acknowledges their adherence to specific controls or directives. Ontologically, this is represented by the axiom `attestation_signed_by`, which asserts that a specific class of process (`X`) is a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (a Process) and is characterized by the property `sdg:signedBy` referencing a `cco:Person`. This axiom establishes the agency and accountability required for valid compliance activities; without a signatory, the attestation lacks legal and procedural standing.

In practice, this structure requires the distinct identification of the attestation process itself, separate from the person signing it. The relational projection of this axiom isolates the process entity (`x`) while utilizing a surrogate key (`id`) to serve as the anchor for downstream dependencies. This design allows multiple directives or evidence records to reference a single signing event, ensuring data normalization and preventing redundant storage of signatory details.

## 2. GDPR Article Subclass

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) imposes specific legal obligations that must be mapped onto technical controls. The axiom `gdpr_article_subclass` formalizes this by defining a class (`X`) as a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` (an Information Content Entity) which satisfies `sdg:gdprArticle` of type `Y`. This structure allows the governance framework to categorize various internal policies or directives as direct implementations of specific GDPR articles (e.g., Article 17 regarding the right to erasure, or Article 32 regarding data security).

Crucially, the relational schema for this axiom enforces a dependency on the attestation process. The column `y` is defined as a foreign key referencing `t_attestation_signed_by(id)`. This constraint ensures that a GDPR directive cannot exist in a vacuum; it must be operationally linked to a specific attestation event (`y`) where a person has formally signed off on that directive. This linkage enforces the principle that regulatory compliance is not merely the existence of a policy, but the execution and acknowledgment of that policy.

The table below illustrates this mapping. Each row represents a specific directive instance (`x`) that corresponds to a GDPR article, anchored to a specific attestation record (`y`).

| GDPR-ART-25-01 | DataProtectionByDesignPolicy | ATT-2023-002 |

| GDPR-ART-28-01 | ProcessorContractualControls | ATT-2023-001 |

## 3. SOC2 Trust Criterion

Parallel to the GDPR framework, the Service Organization Control 2 (SOC2) reporting framework requires the mapping of organizational activities to specific Trust Services Criteria. The axiom `soc2_trust_criterion` defines a class (`X`) as a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` that fulfills `sdg:soc2TrustCriterion` of type `Y`. This enables the organization to align its internal control environment with the criteria defined by the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants (AICPA), such as CC6.1 (Logical and Physical Access Controls) or CC7.2 (System Monitoring).

The relational structure mirrors that of the GDPR subclass, utilizing the same foreign key dependency. The column `y` references `t_attestation_signed_by(id)`, signifying that the SOC2 criteria are only considered satisfied when a specific attestation process has occurred. This design facilitates a unified compliance view where a single attestation event (e.g., a quarterly control review) may satisfy the requirements for both a GDPR directive and a SOC2 criterion, demonstrating the convergence of regulatory frameworks.

## 4. Attestation With Supporting Evidence

For a compliance framework to be auditable, assertions of compliance must be backed by verifiable evidence. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` addresses this by defining a class (`X`) as a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process) that possesses `sdg:withSupportingEvidence` of type `Y`. This axiom models the "audit trail" or the evidence package that substantiates the claims made during an attestation process.

The relational schema establishes a direct link between the evidence and the specific regulatory directive it supports. The column `y` is a foreign key referencing `t_gdpr_article_subclass(id)`. This structure implies that evidence is not merely attached to a person or a generic process, but is specifically validating the implementation of a defined directive (such as a GDPR Article). This granularity is essential for audit queries, allowing an auditor to immediately retrieve all supporting evidence for a specific legal requirement without traversing unrelated records.

## 5. Audit Subclass

The final concept in this governance ontology is the audit itself—the independent examination of these controls and directives. The axiom `audit_subclass` defines a class (`X`) as a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process) that `sdg:auditsControl` a `cco:DirectiveICE`. This axiom distinguishes the act of auditing from the act of attesting; while attestation is an internal acknowledgment, auditing is an external or independent verification process targeting the directives themselves.

In the relational model, this table serves as a registry of audit activities. While the schema provided does not include explicit foreign keys to the directive tables, the `x` column represents the specific audit process class (e.g., "ExternalSOC2Audit" or "InternalISGAudit"). In a full implementation, the relationship between the audit process and the directives it reviews would be resolved through the `auditsControl` object property, but the table provided here captures the definition of the audit processes available within the system.
