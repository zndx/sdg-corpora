# Chapter 4: Governance Frameworks and the Ontology of Compliance Artifacts

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures underpinning modern governance, risk management, and compliance (GRC) frameworks, specifically focusing on the intersection of regulatory directives (GDPR) and audit standards (SOC2). The content herein establishes a rigorous mapping between abstract compliance classes and their instantiated relational schemas, providing the necessary architecture for automated audit trails and evidence verification. Readers are assumed to possess a working understanding of the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) and the Common Core Ontologies (CCO), as these standards are utilized to anchor the definitions of process, information entities, and personnel roles. The following sections decompose five critical axioms governing the attestation lifecycle, from personnel delegation to the accumulation of supporting evidence, ensuring that all data structures are deterministic and auditable.

## 4.2 Attestation Signed By

The foundational unit of any governance framework is the attribution of responsibility. In the context of the ontology, the concept of "signing" is not merely a graphical mark but a distinct process (`bfo:0000015`) that binds a specific class of artifact to a `cco:Person`. This relationship is axiomatized as "Attestation Signed By," which asserts that for a given class X (an attestation process or document), there exists a necessary relation `sdg:signedBy` targeting a person.

This axiom serves as the primary control mechanism for non-repudiation. By defining the attestation as a subclass of a BFO Process, we acknowledge that signing occurs over a temporal interval and involves an agent. The relational projection of this axiom creates a registry where every attestation instance is irrevocably linked to a unique human identifier. This structure prevents the "orphaned policy" problem, where directives exist without accountable ownership.

### 4.2.1 Implementation and Personnel Registry

In practice, this requires a registry of authorized signatories. When a directive is issued or a control is validated, the system must query this registry to validate that the signatory holds the appropriate authority. ## 4.3 GDPR Article Subclass

Moving from the actor to the artifact, we must define how internal governance documents map to external legal obligations. The axiom "GDPR Article Subclass" formalizes the relationship between an internal directive (`cco:DirectiveICE`) and a specific article of the General Data Protection Regulation. The axiom asserts that Class X (a directive) is a subclass that satisfies or implements `sdg:gdprArticle` Y.

This mapping is critical for automated gap analysis. By explicitly typing an internal policy as a subclass of a GDPR Article requirement, the organization creates a machine-readable link between its operational procedures and the legal text. For instance, a "Data Breach Notification Policy" is not merely a document; it is an information content entity that realizes the obligations of GDPR Article 33.

### 4.3.1 Relational Mapping to Signatories

The relational schema for this axiom introduces a foreign key constraint linking the GDPR article instance to the attestation table (`t_attestation_signed_by`). This enforces a governance rule: a specific interpretation or implementation of a GDPR article must be anchored to a signatory. This ensures that every legal artifact within the system has an accountable owner who has formally attested to its validity and implementation status.

## 4.4 SOC2 Trust Criterion

While GDPR addresses privacy and data protection, the SOC2 (System and Organization Controls 2) framework addresses security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy. The "SOC2 Trust Criterion" axiom parallels the GDPR structure but applies to the Trust Services Criteria (TSC). Here, Class X (a DirectiveICE) is defined as satisfying `sdg:soc2TrustCriterion` Y.

This axiom allows the organization to maintain a unified control library where a single directive may satisfy multiple regulatory frameworks. For example, an "Access Control Directive" might simultaneously satisfy GDPR Article 32 (security of processing) and SOC2 CC6.1 (Logical Access Controls). The ontology treats these as distinct relational assertions, allowing for granular reporting.

### 4.4.1 Cross-Framework Consistency

The schema for `t_soc2_trust_criterion` similarly enforces a foreign key relationship to the attestation table. This reflects the operational reality that SOC2 controls must be tested and signed off by qualified personnel (often internal audit or external CPA firms). The structure ensures that a trust criterion is not merely a theoretical label but an auditable entity tied to a specific attestation event.

## 4.5 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

A claim of compliance is substantiated only through evidence. The axiom "Attestation with Supporting Evidence" defines a process (`bfo:0000015`) that includes the relation `sdg:withSupportingEvidence`. This axiom posits that Class X (an attestation or audit process) possesses evidence Y.

In a mature governance framework, this structure separates the *assertion* of compliance from the *proof* of compliance. The evidence (Y) might be a log file, a screenshot, a configuration dump, or a signed review document. By linking the attestation process to the evidence class, auditors can traverse directly from the high-level compliance statement to the granular data artifacts that support it.

### 4.5.1 Evidence Chain and GDPR Linkage

The relational schema for this axiom is particularly notable for its foreign key constraint: it references `t_gdpr_article_subclass`. This design choice implies that evidence is often collected and categorized in the context of specific regulatory requirements. For instance, evidence of "user access reviews" is collected specifically to support the GDPR Article 32 implementation. ## 4.6 Audit Subclass

The final concept encompasses the verification mechanism itself. The "Audit Subclass" axiom defines Class X as a process (`bfo:0000015`) that `sdg:auditsControl` a `cco:DirectiveICE`. This axiom formalizes the audit function as a distinct process that acts upon the governance directives defined in previous sections.

This distinction is vital for separation of duties. The entity that *defines* the directive (the policy owner) is distinct from the entity that *audits* the directive (the auditor). By modeling the audit as a specific subclass of process that targets the DirectiveICE, the ontology supports meta-analysis—auditing the audits, and reporting on the frequency and coverage of control assessments.

### 4.6.1 Audit Independence

The relational schema for `t_audit_subclass` is straightforward, identifying the audit process instances. While it does not enforce a foreign key to the signatory table in this specific schema projection, in a broader implementation, the audit process would typically invoke the `attestation_signed_by` axiom to identify the auditor. This table serves as the index of audit events, the "who, when, and what" of the control verification lifecycle.
