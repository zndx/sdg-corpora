# Chapter 4: Governance Frameworks and Attestation Logic

This chapter delineates the structural and procedural relationships governing compliance directives, attestation processes, and audit mechanisms within a formalized ontology. It provides a rigorous examination of how Information Content Entities (ICEs) are subclassified under specific regulatory frameworks—namely the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the System and Organization Controls (SOC) 2—and how these directives are operationally bound to verification processes. The content herein establishes the axiomatic links between legal requirements, personnel attestations, supporting evidence, and audit subprocesses, offering a deterministic schema for mapping compliance obligations to executable governance activities.

## 4.1 Attestation Processes and Personnel Signatories

The foundational unit of governance verification is the attestation process, formally defined as a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process). Within the ontology, the axiom `attestation_signed_by` posits that a specific class of attestation is characterized by the property `sdg:signedBy`, which necessitates the involvement of a `cco:Person`. This relationship establishes the irreducible link between a procedural act and the responsible agent, ensuring that governance actions are not merely abstract events but are anchored to individual accountability.

In practical terms, an attestation represents a formal declaration by a designated person regarding the veracity of a specific state of affairs or the execution of a control. The ontology treats this not as a loose association but as a structural dependency: the validity of the attestation process is contingent upon the identification of the signatory. This construct facilitates the traceability required in high-compliance environments, such as financial audits or data protection impact assessments, where the "who" is as critical as the "what."

To illustrate this mapping, consider the following data instances which represent specific attestation processes and the classes they instantiate. Each row identifies a unique attestation event (`id`) and the class of the attestation process (`x`).

| :--- | :--- |
| `att_proc_001` | `DataRetentionAttestation` |

The table above reflects the schema `t_attestation_signed_by`, where the primary key (`id`) serves as the reference point for downstream governance objects. For instance, `DataRetentionAttestation` is a process class that, by definition, requires a signatory (a `cco:Person`) to validate that data retention schedules comply with policy. This entity will subsequently act as the foreign key target for directives that require such substantiation, thereby creating a chain of custody from the person to the policy.

## 4.2 GDPR Directives and Legal Subclassing

The regulatory landscape governing data protection is modeled through the axiom `gdpr_article_subclass`. This axiom defines a class {X} as a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` (Directive Information Content Entity) which possesses the property `sdg:gdprArticle` referencing a specific class {Y}. In the relational projection of this ontology, the entity {Y}—the specific GDPR article—is operationally realized through the attestation process defined in Section 4.1.

This structure implies that a "Directive" is not merely a static text but an active information entity that is substantiated by a specific attestation instance. The verbalization "{X} is something that gdpr article {Y}" captures the essence of compliance: the directive {X} (e.g., a policy regarding data erasure) is the implementation mechanism for the legal requirement {Y} (e.g., GDPR Article 17). The foreign key constraint linking {Y} to the attestation table enforces the rule that a legal requirement cannot exist within the system without a corresponding, signed attestation process validating its implementation.

The following table enumerates instances where specific internal directives are mapped to GDPR articles. The column `x` represents the internal directive class, while `y` references the attestation process ID that validates the adherence to the specific legal article.

**Table 4.2: GDPR Article Subclass Instances**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `dir_gdpr_01` | `RightToErasureDirective` | `att_proc_001` |
| `dir_gdpr_02` | `DataPortabilityDirective` | `att_proc_004` |
| `dir_gdpr_03` | `IntegrityAndConfidentialityDirective` | `att_proc_003` |
| `dir_gdpr_04` | `DataProtectionByDesignDirective` | `att_proc_002` |

In this schema, `id` serves as the unique identifier for the directive subclass itself. The `x` column defines the nature of the directive, such as the `RightToErasureDirective`, which operationalizes the "right to be forgotten." The `y` column, `att_proc_001`, links this directive to the `DataRetentionAttestation` process established in Table 4.1. This linkage indicates that the organization's compliance with the Right to Erasure is formally attested to via the retention attestation process. This rigorous mapping ensures that for every claim of GDPR compliance, there exists a traceable, signed process record.

## 4.3 SOC 2 Trust Criteria Integration

Parallel to the GDPR framework, the ontology accommodates the Trust Services Criteria (TSC) utilized in SOC 2 examinations through the axiom `soc2_trust_criterion`. This axiom defines a class {X} as a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` that satisfies the property `sdg:soc2TrustCriterion` referencing class {Y}. Similar to the GDPR structure, the SOC 2 criterion {Y} is linked to an attestation process, ensuring that the criteria are not merely aspirational but are verified through specific governance activities.

The SOC 2 framework requires that systems be available, secure, and confidential, among other criteria. The ontology models these requirements by mapping internal control directives to specific SOC 2 criteria points. The relational schema enforces that the criterion {Y} must reference a valid attestation ID (`t_attestation_signed_by`), thereby bridging the gap between the abstract criteria defined by the AICPA and the concrete operational realities of the organization.

The following table presents the alignment of internal directives with SOC 2 trust criteria.

**Table 4.3: SOC 2 Trust Criterion Subclass Instances**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `dir_soc2_01` | `LogicalAccessControlDirective` | `att_proc_002` |
| `dir_soc2_02` | `ChangeManagementDirective` | `att_proc_005` |
| `dir_soc2_03` | `SystemAvailabilityDirective` | `att_proc_003` |
| `dir_soc2_04` | `DataDisposalDirective` | `att_proc_001` |

Here, the `LogicalAccessControlDirective` (`x`) is the internal manifestation of the SOC 2 criteria regarding access control. It is linked via `y` (`att_proc_002`) to the `AccessControlAttestation` process. This confirms that the organization's claims regarding logical access are substantiated by the specific attestation process defined in Section 4.1. The use of a shared attestation table across both GDPR and SOC 2 frameworks (as seen by the reuse of `att_proc_001` and `att_proc_003` in different contexts) demonstrates the efficiency of the ontology in supporting a unified compliance model where a single control activity may satisfy multiple regulatory obligations.

## 4.4 Evidence Chain and Substantiation

For a governance framework to be defensible, assertions must be supported by objective evidence. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` addresses this by defining a class {X} (a subclass of `bfo:0000015`, or Process) that possesses the property `sdg:withSupportingEvidence` referencing class {Y}. In this context, {X} represents the attestation process itself, while {Y} represents the evidence—specifically, the directive or subclass instance that the evidence supports.

Crucially, the relational schema dictates that the evidence {Y} must reference an existing `t_gdpr_article_subclass` record. This creates a hierarchical dependency: the Audit/Evidence process points to the Directive, which in turn points to the Attestation. This structure prevents "orphaned" evidence; every piece of supporting evidence must be explicitly tied to a specific directive (e.g., a GDPR Article subclass), ensuring that the evidence is relevant and material to the compliance claim.

This axiom effectively models the "audit trail." An attestation is a claim; the supporting evidence is the proof. By linking the evidence to the `gdpr_article_subclass`, the ontology ensures that the proof is directly validating the specific legal interpretation of the directive.

**Table 4.4: Attestation with Supporting Evidence Instances**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `ev_link_01` | `QuarterlyComplianceReview` | `dir_gdpr_01` |
| `ev_link_02` | `AnnualAccessAudit` | `dir_soc2_01` |
| `ev_link_03` | `CryptographyAudit` | `dir_gdpr_03` |
| `ev_link_04` | `VendorRiskAssessment` | `dir_gdpr_02` |

In Table 4.4, `x` represents the broader audit or review process (e.g., `QuarterlyComplianceReview`), while `y` references the specific directive ID from Table 4.2 (e.g., `dir_gdpr_01`, the `RightToErasureDirective`). This entry indicates that the Quarterly Compliance Review serves as the supporting evidence process for the Right to Erasure Directive. The foreign key constraint ensures that one cannot claim evidence for a directive that has not been formally defined and linked to an attestation process in the previous steps.

## 4.5 Audit Subclass and Control Verification

The final concept in this governance ontology is the `audit_subclass`. This axiom defines a class {X} as a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process) that `sdg:auditsControl` a `cco:DirectiveICE`. This represents the overarching audit function that examines the directives established earlier in the chapter. Unlike the attestation process, which is a declaration of validity by a person, the audit process is an investigative procedure that tests the controls encapsulated in the directives.

The ontology treats the audit as a distinct process type that targets the Information Content Entities (the directives). This separation allows for the distinction between *attesting* to a process (signing off that it was done) and *auditing* a process (independently verifying the control). The schema for this axiom is straightforward, consisting of the audit ID and the class of the audit process, as the target of the audit (the `cco:DirectiveICE`) is implied by the broader framework context or handled through distinct relationship tables in a full implementation. However, within this specific schema projection, the focus is on the classification of the audit process itself.

**Table 4.5: Audit Subclass Instances**

| id | x |
| :--- | :--- |
| `audit_001` | `InternalControlAudit` |
| `audit_002` | `ExternalSOC2Examination` |
| `audit_003` | `DataProtectionImpactAudit` |
| `audit_004` | `NetworkSecurityAudit` |

Table 4.5 catalogs the specific audit processes utilized by the organization. For example, `ExternalSOC2Examination` (`audit_002`) is a process class that audits the controls defined in the SOC 2 directives (such as `dir_soc2_01`). While the table structure here is simple, the existence of these audit classes is predicated on the existence of the directives they audit. The `audit_003` entry, `DataProtectionImpactAudit`, would logically target the GDPR directives defined in Section 4.2, completing the loop from legal requirement, to directive, to attestation, to evidence, and finally to independent audit.
