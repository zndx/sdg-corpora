# Chapter 4: Formal Ontology Mapping in Compliance Governance

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the formalization of governance, risk, and compliance (GRC) constructs through the application of upper-level ontologies. The objective is to establish a rigorous, machine-readable framework that maps regulatory mandates—specifically the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and System and Organization Controls (SOC 2)—to internal control environments and audit processes. By anchoring these concepts in the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) and the Common Core Ontologies (CCO), organizations can achieve semantic interoperability between compliance artifacts and operational evidentiary trails. The following sections detail the axiomatic structure of five core concepts: attestation, regulatory directive integration, trust criteria alignment, evidentiary support, and audit subprocesses. Each concept is accompanied by a relational schema projection to demonstrate how these abstract axioms are instantiated within a physical data governance layer.

## 4.2 Attestation and Personnel Responsibility

### 4.2.1 Conceptual Definition

At the foundational level of any governance framework lies the concept of attestation. Formally, an attestation is a process (BFO:0000015) that validates the execution or existence of a state of affairs. However, for an attestation to hold legal or regulatory weight, it must be attributable to a responsible agent. In the context of this ontology, we define the class of attestations as those processes that are `signedBy` a `cco:Person`. This axiom ensures that no automated or system-generated log entry is misconstrued as a human-verified attestation without an explicit link to a natural person.

### 4.2.2 Implementation and Governance

The implementation of this axiom requires that the organization maintain a strict registry of all attestation events. Each event must be cryptographically signed or otherwise verifiably associated with a unique identifier representing the individual. This is not merely a record-keeping exercise; it is a structural requirement for establishing non-repudiation. In audit scenarios, the inability to map a control validation to a specific `cco:Person` invalidates the control's effectiveness rating.

The relational projection of this axiom creates a primary repository for these signatures. The primary key serves as the immutable reference for the attestation event, while the `x` slot denotes the specific class of the attestation (e.g., *AnnualReviewAttestation*, *AccessGrantAttestation*).

## 4.3 GDPR Directive Integration

### 4.3.1 Conceptual Definition

The integration of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) into the ontology requires mapping specific regulatory articles to internal directive information content entities (`cco:DirectiveICE`). The axiom `{X} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:gdprArticle some {Y}` formalizes the relationship between an internal governance directive (X) and a specific GDPR article (Y). This mapping is critical for gap analysis; it allows the organization to query its internal control library and instantly retrieve the specific regulatory legal basis for each control.

### 4.3.2 Relational Constraints and Dependencies

A critical constraint in this schema is the foreign key relationship requiring the GDPR article reference (`y`) to correspond to an existing attestation (`t_attestation_signed_by`). This enforces a "compliance-by-design" principle where the invocation of a GDPR article within the governance framework is inextricably linked to a human-signed attestation. In practice, this means that an organization cannot merely declare adherence to Article 32 (Security of Processing); it must instantiate that adherence through a specific attestation event (e.g., a signed security review).

## 4.4 SOC 2 Trust Criteria Alignment

### 4.4.1 Conceptual Definition

Parallel to the GDPR framework, many organizations must adhere to the Trust Services Criteria (TSC) established for SOC 2 reports. The axiom `{X} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:soc2TrustCriterion some {Y}` maps internal directives to specific SOC 2 criteria points (e.g., CC6.1 regarding logical and physical access controls). This semantic mapping facilitates the automated generation of SOC 2 readiness reports by aligning internal policy documents directly with the AICPA criteria.

### 4.4.2 Cross-Reference with Attestation

Similar to the GDPR integration, the SOC 2 mapping relies on the attestation layer. The foreign key constraint (`y` references `t_attestation_signed_by`) dictates that for a SOC 2 trust criterion to be considered "implemented" within this ontology, it must be backed by a signed attestation. This prevents the "tick-the-box" compliance phenomenon where controls are documented on paper but lack operational verification. ## 4.5 Evidentiary Support for Attestations

### 4.5.1 Conceptual Definition

A robust compliance framework distinguishes between the *act* of attestation and the *evidence* supporting that act. The axiom `{X} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:withSupportingEvidence some {Y}` defines a subclass of attestation processes that are explicitly linked to supporting evidence classes. This evidence may take the form of system logs, screenshots, configuration exports, or third-party audit reports. This linkage is the cornerstone of the "audit trail" concept.

### 4.5.2 Evidence Hierarchy

The relational schema for this axiom establishes a dependency where the evidence (`y`) must reference a GDPR article subclass (`t_gdpr_article_subclass`). This implies a specific hierarchy of evidence: the attestation is supported by evidence, and that evidence is specifically an instance of a GDPR directive implementation. For example, an attestation regarding "Encryption" (Table 4.1) is supported by evidence which is formally recognized as the "Encryption Standard Directive" (Table 4.2). This recursive referencing ensures that evidence is not merely a loose file but is semantically typed and categorized within the regulatory framework.

## 4.6 Audit Control Mechanisms

### 4.6.1 Conceptual Definition

The final concept in this chapter is the audit subprocess itself. While attestations represent point-in-time validations, audits represent the systematic review of these controls over time. The axiom `{X} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:auditsControl some cco:DirectiveICE` defines the audit class as a process that specifically targets `cco:DirectiveICE` entities. This formalizes the scope of an audit: it is not a generic investigation, but a targeted examination of the directives defined in the previous sections.

### 4.6.2 Audit Execution and Reporting

In the relational model, the `t_audit_subclass` table serves as the registry for audit events. These events are the high-level wrappers that consume the attestations, evidence, and directive mappings defined elsewhere. By defining the audit as a distinct class of process, the ontology allows for the meta-analysis of audit frequency, scope, and coverage. For instance, one can query the ontology to determine which `cco:DirectiveICE` entities have *not* been the object of an `sdg:auditsControl` process within a given fiscal year, thereby identifying coverage gaps in the governance program.
