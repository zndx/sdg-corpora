# Chapter 7: Governance Frameworks and Compliance Attestation Protocols

This chapter delineates the structural and procedural relationships governing regulatory compliance within enterprise information systems. It provides a rigorous examination of the ontological mappings between regulatory directives, attestation processes, and audit mechanisms. The scope encompasses the integration of General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) mandates, System and Organization Controls (SOC) 2 trust criteria, and the evidentiary standards required to substantiate compliance postures. Readers will acquire a technical understanding of how directive classes are instantiated, linked to signatories, and subjected to audit verification.

## 1. Attestation Signed By

In the context of governance frameworks, an attestation represents a formal declaration or verification of truth, typically functioning as a distinct process node within a broader compliance workflow. The axiom defining this relationship establishes that the attestation entity is a subclass of `bfo:Process`, specifically characterized by the property of being signed by a person (`cco:Person`). This relationship is foundational; it anchors abstract regulatory requirements to specific natural persons who bear accountability for the execution or verification of a control.

The necessity of this mapping is to ensure non-repudiation and traceability. When a process is defined as an attestation, it ceases to be a purely automated or procedural event and becomes a legally significant act. The `sdg:signedBy` property creates a direct lineage from the governance artifact to the human agent. In relational implementations, this requires a dedicated schema where the attestation instance serves as the primary key, while the signatory attribute is either embedded or normalized through a separate entity relation.

The following table illustrates instances of attestation processes where the class `X` represents the specific governance event being signed.

**Table 1.1: Attestation Instances and Signatory Mapping**

| id | x |
| :--- | :--- |
| ATT-2023-001 | DataProcessingAgreement |
| ATT-2023-002 | PrivacyImpactAssessment |
| ATT-2023-003 | IncidentResponsePlan |
| ATT-2023-004 | SubprocessorReview |
| ATT-2023-005 | AccessControlCertification |

These instances represent the specific "X" classes defined in the ontology. Each row corresponds to a unique event where a `cco:Person` has executed a signature, thereby transforming the document or process from a draft state to an attested state. The `id` serves as the persistent identifier for the attestation event, which will be referenced by subsequent governance objects to establish dependency chains.

## 2. GDPR Article Subclass

The General Data Protection Regulation imposes specific obligations that must be operationalized within technical and organizational measures. The ontology defines a class structure where a specific compliance object (`X`) is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` (Information Content Entity) and is further characterized by the property `sdg:gdprArticle`, linking it to a specific regulatory article class (`Y`).

This structure allows for the granular mapping of internal controls to statutory requirements. For example, an internal policy regarding data erasure is not merely a document; it is an instance of a DirectiveICE that specifically satisfies the requirements of GDPR Article 17 (Right to Erasure). The relational schema enforces a foreign key constraint linking the GDPR article instance to an attestation record (`t_attestation_signed_by`). This constraint ensures that no regulatory interpretation or implementation exists in isolation; it must be validated and signed off by a responsible party via the attestation process defined in Section 1.

The integration of these concepts creates a rigid chain of evidence: the Directive (the law) dictates the Article (the requirement), which is instantiated as a Class (the control), which is then bound to an Attestation (the verification).

**Table 2.1: GDPR Article Implementation and Attestation Linkage**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| GDPR-ART-17-01 | RightToErasureImplementation | ATT-2023-001 |
| GDPR-ART-28-01 | ProcessorAppointmentRecord | ATT-2023-004 |
| GDPR-ART-32-01 | SecurityMeasureEncryption | ATT-2023-005 |
| GDPR-ART-35-01 | DPIAProcessingActivity | ATT-2023-002 |

In Table 2.1, the column `x` denotes the specific class or implementation of the control, while `y` references the `id` of the attestation from Table 1.1. For instance, the `RightToErasureImplementation` (GDPR Article 17) is formally linked to the `DataProcessingAgreement` attestation (`ATT-2023-001`). This linkage signifies that the attestation event serves as the authoritative verification record for that specific GDPR implementation.

## 3. SOC 2 Trust Criterion

Parallel to the GDPR framework, the SOC 2 (System and Organization Controls 2) audit framework necessitates a mapping between internal controls and the Trust Services Criteria (TSC). The ontology defines this via the `soc2_trust_criterion` axiom, where a class `X` (the control implementation) is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` and satisfies a specific SOC 2 Trust Criterion `Y`.

The structural similarity to the GDPR axiom is intentional, reflecting the need for a unified governance model that can handle multiple regulatory regimes simultaneously. However, the content of the criteria differs fundamentally, focusing on security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy. The relational schema similarly enforces a dependency on the attestation table (`t_attestation_signed_by`), mandating that any SOC 2 control criterion be formally signed off by a qualified individual.

This mapping is critical for audit readiness. When an auditor queries the system for evidence regarding "CC6.1 - Logical and Physical Access Controls," the system retrieves the relevant class `X` and traces its validity to the specific attestation record `y`, thereby providing the "who, what, and when" of the compliance verification.

**Table 3.1: SOC 2 Trust Criteria and Attestation Dependencies**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| SOC2-CC6.1-01 | LogicalAccessControlPolicy | ATT-2023-005 |
| SOC2-CC7.2-01 | SystemMonitoringDefinition | ATT-2023-003 |
| SOC2-A1.1-01 | AvailabilityBackupProcedure | ATT-2023-003 |
| SOC2-C1.1-01 | ConfidentialityDataClassification | ATT-2023-001 |

The data in Table 3.1 demonstrates how distinct SOC 2 criteria (column `x`) are anchored to specific attestation events (column `y`). For example, the `LogicalAccessControlPolicy` (mapped to Common Criteria 6.1) is verified by the `AccessControlCertification` attestation (`ATT-2023-005`). This structure ensures that the control narrative is not merely theoretical but is substantiated by a recorded, signed event.

## 4. Attestation with Supporting Evidence

A robust governance framework requires that assertions (attestations) be supported by objective evidence. The `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` axiom defines a relationship where an attestation class `X` (a subclass of `bfo:Process`) possesses the property `sdg:withSupportingEvidence`, linking it to a supporting evidence class `Y`.

In this ontology, the "supporting evidence" is modeled as a reference to a specific regulatory implementation or directive instance, specifically those defined in the GDPR subclass table. The relational schema enforces that the evidence (`y`) must exist within the `t_gdpr_article_subclass` table. This creates a hierarchical validation model: the Attestation is the top-level claim, and the GDPR Article implementation is the substantive proof or artifact that supports that claim.

This relationship prevents "orphaned" attestations—signatures that exist without corroborating documentation or implementation details. It enforces the principle that compliance is evidenced by the operationalization of regulatory requirements (the GDPR article instance), which in turn validates the attestation.

**Table 4.1: Attestation Evidence Chain**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| EVID-CHAIN-01 | DataProcessingAgreement | GDPR-ART-28-01 |
| EVID-CHAIN-02 | PrivacyImpactAssessment | GDPR-ART-35-01 |
| EVID-CHAIN-03 | AccessControlCertification | GDPR-ART-32-01 |

In this table, `x` represents the attestation process (or class thereof), and `y` represents the specific GDPR article instance serving as evidence. For instance, the `DataProcessingAgreement` attestation is supported by the evidence found in the `ProcessorAppointmentRecord` (`GDPR-ART-28-01`). This linkage confirms that the act of signing the agreement was performed based on the existence and review of the specific article implementation record.

## 5. Audit Subclass

The final concept in this governance ontology is the Audit function. While attestations represent point-in-time verification by internal actors, the Audit represents a comprehensive review process. The `audit_subclass` axiom defines a class `X` as a subclass of `bfo:Process` that specifically audits a `cco:DirectiveICE`.

This distinction is vital. An audit is a meta-process that evaluates other directives and controls. It is the mechanism by which the organization verifies that the DirectiveICEs (such as the GDPR or SOC 2 implementations discussed previously) are functioning as intended. The schema for this axiom is straightforward, identifying the audit class, but in practice, this class would contain complex logic for sampling, testing, and reporting on the directives defined in the other tables.

The audit process consumes the outputs of the attestation and evidence chains. It validates that the attestations were signed by authorized persons, that the evidence (GDPR articles) is current and accurate, and that the SOC 2 criteria are met. The audit subclass serves as the encapsulating entity for this verification logic.

**Table 5.1: Audit Process Definitions**

| id | x |
| :--- | :--- |

Table 5.1 catalogs the specific audit processes defined within the system. These are not merely documents but active processes (`bfo:Process`) designed to interrogate the state of the directives. For example, `AUD-EXT-2023` represents the process of the external SOC 2 audit, which would systematically query the `t_soc2_trust_criterion` and `t_attestation_signed_by` tables to validate the organization's trust posture.
