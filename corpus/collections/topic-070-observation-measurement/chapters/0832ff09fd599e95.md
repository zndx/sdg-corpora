# Chapter 4: Governance Ontology for Compliance Attestation and Audit Verification

This chapter establishes the formal ontological framework governing the intersection of regulatory directives, personnel attestation, and audit verification mechanisms. It delineates the structural relationships between information content entities (ICE) and the processes required to validate them, specifically within the contexts of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the System and Organization Controls (SOC) 2 framework. The definitions provided herein are binding for the implementation of compliance management systems and serve as the logical schema for the underlying relational data structures.

## 1. Attestation Signed By

The foundational unit of governance accountability is the attestation process, formally defined as a subclass of `bfo:Process`. In this ontology, an attestation is not merely a signature but a distinct processual entity that necessitates the involvement of a responsible agent. The axiom `attestation_signed_by` posits that specific classes of attestation processes are characterized by the property `sdg:signedBy`, which restricts the range of values to instances of `cco:Person`.

This construct ensures that governance artifacts are not floating abstractions but are anchored to individual accountability. By defining a class `X` as a "Process that is signed by a Person," the ontology enforces a rigid constraint: any instantiation of `X` must be associated with a valid human agent. This is critical for audit trails, where the provenance of a compliance assertion is as significant as the assertion itself.

### 1.1 Processual Definition and Personnel Constraints

The class `X` represents the specific type of attestation process (e.g., "Executive Sign-Off" or "Officer Certification"). The relationship to `cco:Person` via `sdg:signedBy` indicates that the process is valid only upon the completion of the signing act by a natural person. This excludes automated or synthetic agents from serving as the primary signatory for high-compliance governance events.

The following table enumerates specific classes of attestation processes defined within the system. Each entry represents a distinct category of process (`x`) that requires human signatory validation.

**Table 1.1: Attestation Process Classes**

| id | x |
| :--- | :--- |
| proc_001 | CISO_Attestation_Process |
| proc_002 | DPO_Certification_Process |
| proc_003 | Data_Controller_Acknowledgement |

In the relational schema, `t_attestation_signed_by` serves as the lookup for these process classes. The `id` column provides the primary key, while `x` defines the class name. This table acts as a foreign key target for other directives, ensuring that specific compliance articles are mapped to valid, human-backed attestation workflows.

## 2. GDPR Article Subclass

The regulatory landscape of the European Union is codified within the ontology through the `gdpr_article_subclass` axiom. This axiom defines a class `X` as a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` (Directive Information Content Entity) that satisfies the condition `sdg:gdprArticle some {Y:Class}`. In natural language, this asserts that `X` is a directive that corresponds to a specific GDPR article `Y`.

This mapping allows the governance framework to treat legal requirements as computable entities. By classifying a directive as a specific type of GDPR article, the system can automatically trigger relevant attestation workflows and evidence collection requirements. The ontology distinguishes between the abstract legal text (the Directive ICE) and the specific governance process it invokes.

### 2.1 Mapping Directives to Legal Articles

The critical structural feature of this axiom is the linkage between the Directive (`X`) and the Attestation Process (`Y`). The schema dictates that the `y` slot (the GDPR article class) references the `t_attestation_signed_by` table. This implies a dependency: the instantiation of a GDPR directive within the system is inextricably bound to a specific type of attestation process.

For example, a directive regarding "Data Subject Access Rights" (Article 15) may be linked to a "DPO Certification Process." This binding ensures that when the directive is active, the system enforces the requirement for the designated personnel to sign off on the related compliance artifacts.

**Table 2.1: GDPR Directive and Attestation Mapping**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| dir_gdpr_01 | Art15_RightOfAccess_Directive | proc_002 |
| dir_gdpr_02 | Art32_SecurityOfProcessing_Directive | proc_001 |
| dir_gdpr_03 | Art28_Processor_Directive | proc_003 |

*Table 2.1 illustrates the `t_gdpr_article_subclass` structure. The column `x` represents the specific directive class (e.g., `Art15_RightOfAccess_Directive`). The column `y` is a foreign key referencing the `t_attestation_signed_by` table, linking the legal requirement to the specific attestation process (e.g., `proc_002`, the DPO Certification Process).*

## 3. SOC 2 Trust Criterion

Parallel to the GDPR framework, the ontology incorporates American Institute of Certified Public Accountants (AICPA) Trust Services Criteria through the `soc2_trust_criterion` axiom. This axiom defines a class `X` as a `cco:DirectiveICE` that fulfills `sdg:soc2TrustCriterion some {Y:Class}`. Essentially, `X` is a directive that operationalizes a specific SOC 2 criterion `Y`.

The structural implementation mirrors the GDPR logic but applies to a different regulatory domain. The SOC 2 criteria—covering Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, and Privacy—are modeled as classes that constrain the definition of internal directives. This allows organizations to map their internal policies directly to the control points required for a SOC 2 examination.

### 3.1 Criteria and Attestation Linkage

As with the GDPR axiom, the `soc2_trust_criterion` relies on the attestation infrastructure. The `y` slot, representing the SOC 2 criterion class, references the `t_attestation_signed_by` table. This design choice reflects standard audit practices where specific SOC 2 criteria (e.g., logical access controls) must be formally attested to by responsible personnel (e.g., the CISO or IT Director).

The relational projection of this axiom, `t_soc2_trust_criterion`, therefore contains the directive class (`x`) and a reference to the attestation process (`y`). This enforces the governance principle that compliance criteria are not merely descriptive but performative, requiring active validation by a human agent.

**Table 3.1: SOC 2 Criteria and Directive Mapping**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| dir_soc2_01 | CC6_1_LogicalAccess_Directive | proc_001 |
| dir_soc2_02 | CC6_6_SystemBoundary_Directive | proc_001 |
| dir_soc2_03 | CC7_2_SystemMonitoring_Directive | proc_002 |

*Table 3.1 depicts the `t_soc2_trust_criterion` schema. Here, `x` denotes the internal directive class (e.g., `CC6_1_LogicalAccess_Directive`), and `y` references the attestation process ID (e.g., `proc_001`, linking to the CISO Attestation Process). This ensures that the logical access control directive is validated by the appropriate officer.*

## 4. Attestation with Supporting Evidence

To substantiate the claims made during the attestation process, the ontology defines the `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` axiom. This axiom classifies `X` as a subclass of `bfo:Process` that possesses the property `sdg:withSupportingEvidence some {Y:Class}`. This verbalizes to: `X` is a process that includes supporting evidence `Y`.

This concept introduces the evidentiary layer into the governance framework. An attestation is rendered valid only when it is accompanied by artifacts that prove the compliance state. The evidence (`Y`) is modeled as a class, allowing for types of evidence such as "Audit Logs," "Configuration Snapshots," or "Penetration Test Reports."

### 4.1 Evidence Linkage to GDPR Directives

The relational schema for this axiom, `t_attestation_with_supporting_evidence`, establishes a foreign key constraint where the evidence class (`y`) references the `t_gdpr_article_subclass` table. This creates a hierarchical chain: the Evidence supports the Attestation, and the Evidence is typed by the specific GDPR Directive it validates.

For instance, a process class `X` might be "Quarterly Compliance Review." The supporting evidence `Y` might be "Art32_SecurityOfProcessing_Evidence." This evidence class is derived from the GDPR directive defined in Section 2. This structure ensures that evidence collection is not arbitrary but is strictly typed according to the regulatory requirement it fulfills.

**Table 4.1: Evidence Classes and Directive Dependencies**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ev_001 | Evidence_Logs_AccessControl | dir_gdpr_02 |
| ev_002 | Evidence_Report_DSAR | dir_gdpr_01 |
| ev_003 | Evidence_Contract_Processor | dir_gdpr_03 |

*Table 4.1 represents the `t_attestation_with_supporting_evidence` structure. Column `x` identifies the class of evidence artifact (e.g., `Evidence_Logs_AccessControl`). Column `y` is a foreign key referencing the `t_gdpr_article_subclass` table (e.g., `dir_gdpr_02`), linking the evidence to the specific GDPR Article 32 directive regarding security of processing.*

## 5. Audit Subclass

The final component of the governance ontology is the `audit_subclass`. This axiom defines a class `X` as a subclass of `bfo:Process` that performs the action `sdg:auditsControl` on a `cco:DirectiveICE`. In natural language, `X` is a process that audits a control directive.

This axiom encapsulates the verification mechanism. While attestation (Section 1) is the act of signing off, and evidence (Section 4) provides the proof, the audit is the systematic process of reviewing both. The audit process is directed at the "Directive ICE"—the policy or control statement itself—to verify its design and operating effectiveness.

### 5.1 Audit Process Definition

The `t_audit_subclass` table provides a catalog of audit process types. Unlike previous tables, this schema does not link to external tables via foreign keys in the provided definition, serving instead as a registry of audit activities. However, the semantic meaning implies that these processes operate upon the directives defined in the GDPR and SOC 2 sections.

Examples of audit classes include "Annual SOC 2 Audit" or "GDPR Gap Analysis." By defining these as subclasses of `bfo:Process`, the ontology facilitates the scheduling, execution, and tracking of audit events as distinct workflow instances within the governance framework.

**Table 5.1: Audit Process Classes**

| id | x |
| :--- | :--- |
| aud_001 | Annual_SOC2_Type2_Audit |
| aud_002 | Quarterly_GDPR_Compliance_Review |
| aud_003 | Adhoc_AccessControl_Audit |

*Table 5.1 lists the classes defined in `t_audit_subclass`. Each `x` value represents a specific type of audit process (e.g., `Annual_SOC2_Type2_Audit`). These processes are responsible for auditing the controls defined by the Directives in the preceding sections, completing the governance loop from definition to attestation, evidence, and final verification.*
