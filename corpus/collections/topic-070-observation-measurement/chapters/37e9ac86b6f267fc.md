# Chapter 4: Governance Frameworks and Audit Evidence Verification

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the structural dependencies between regulatory directives, specifically the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and System and Organization Controls (SOC 2), and the evidentiary protocols required for compliance verification. The text establishes a formal ontology for mapping governance artifacts to their corresponding legal and procedural authorities, ensuring that audit trails are both semantically rigorous and evidentiarily sound. It further defines the relational constraints governing signatory attestations, trust criteria, and the hierarchical linkage of supporting documentation to primary regulatory articles.

## 4.2 Signatory Authority and Attestation Protocols

### 4.2.1 Definition and Verbalization

In the context of governance frameworks, the validity of any directive or control is contingent upon the explicit authorization by a responsible natural person. The axiom `attestation_signed_by` formalizes this requirement. It asserts that a specific class of attestation ({X:Class}) is a subclass of a process (bfo:0000015) and necessitates that this process is signed by a person (cco:Person). Verbalized, {X} is something that is signed by a person. This creates the foundational link between an administrative action and its human agent, establishing non-repudiation and accountability within the compliance record.

### 4.2.2 Implementation and Relational Structure

The implementation of this axiom requires a registry where every attestation instance is uniquely identified and associated with its functional class. The primary key (`id`) serves as the immutable reference for the attestation event, while the attribute `x` denotes the classification of the attestation (e.g., Data Processing Agreement, Privacy Impact Assessment). The relational schema is designed to ensure that no attestation exists without a defined class and that downstream references to this attestation are strictly controlled via foreign key constraints.

## 4.3 GDPR Directive Integration

### 4.3.1 Definition and Verbalization

The application of the General Data Protection Regulation within an enterprise architecture is modeled through the `gdpr_article_subclass` axiom. This axiom posits that a regulatory class ({X:Class}) is a subclass of a Directive Information Content Entity (cco:DirectiveICE) and specifically addresses a GDPR Article ({Y:Class}). Verbalized, {X} is something that GDPR article {Y} addresses. This mapping allows the organization to abstract specific legal articles (e.g., Article 17, Right to Erasure) into implementable technical or procedural directives.

### 4.3.2 Dependency on Signatory Attestation

Crucially, the ontology enforces a dependency between the regulatory directive and the signatory process. The relational schema for `t_gdpr_article_subclass` mandates that the `y` attribute (the GDPR Article reference) must exist as a valid entry in the `t_attestation_signed_by` table. This structural constraint ensures that the invocation of a specific GDPR Article is always anchored to a specific, signed authorization event. It prevents the theoretical existence of a compliance requirement without a corresponding responsible party having executed the necessary attestation.

## 4.4 SOC 2 Trust Criteria Alignment

### 4.4.1 Definition and Verbalization

Parallel to the GDPR framework, the Trust Services Criteria (TSC) utilized in SOC 2 examinations are integrated via the `soc2_trust_criterion` axiom. This axiom defines a class ({X:Class}) as a subclass of a Directive Information Content Entity (cco:DirectiveICE) that fulfills a specific SOC 2 Trust Criterion ({Y:Class}). Verbalized, {X} is something that SOC 2 trust criterion {Y} fulfills. This facilitates the mapping of internal control objectives to the standardized criteria defined by the AICPA (e.g., CC6.1, Logical and Physical Access Controls).

### 4.4.2 Relational Mapping and Evidence

The relational schema for `t_soc2_trust_criterion` mirrors the dependency structure found in the GDPR integration. The `y` attribute, representing the specific Trust Criterion, is constrained by a foreign key referencing the `t_attestation_signed_by` table. This design choice reflects the audit requirement that control design and implementation must be reviewed and signed off by a qualified authority (e.g., a Control Owner or Compliance Officer). The link ensures that the abstract criterion is tied to a concrete, attested governance process.

## 4.5 Evidence Corroboration and Support

### 4.5.1 Definition and Verbalization

A compliance framework is only as robust as the evidence substantiating its claims. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` provides the mechanism for linking a process ({X:Class})—specifically a bfo:0000015 (Process)—to its supporting evidence ({Y:Class}). Verbalized, {X} is something that has supporting evidence {Y}. This axiom moves the compliance model from procedural assertion to evidentiary verification, requiring that every attestation or process be backed by concrete artifacts such as logs, screenshots, configuration exports, or review notes.

### 4.5.2 Hierarchical Evidence Chain

The relational schema for `t_attestation_with_supporting_evidence` establishes a hierarchical chain of custody. The `y` attribute, representing the supporting evidence class, is linked via a foreign key to the `t_gdpr_article_subclass` table. This implies that the evidence is not merely attached to a generic attestation but is specifically validating the implementation of a GDPR Article directive. For instance, a configuration file (Evidence) supports the "Right to Erasure" directive (GDPR Article), which in turn is linked to a "Data Processing Agreement" (Attestation). This structure ensures that evidence is contextually relevant and directly supports specific regulatory obligations.

## 4.6 Audit Control Governance

### 4.6.1 Definition and Verbalization

The final component of the governance ontology is the `audit_subclass` axiom. This axiom defines a class ({X:Class}) as a process (bfo:0000015) that audits a Directive Information Content Entity (cco:DirectiveICE). Verbalized, {X} is something that audits control directive information content entity. This represents the meta-governance layer—the audit function itself—which is responsible for examining the validity of the directives, attestations, and evidence described in previous sections.

### 4.6.2 Audit Independence and Scope

The relational schema for `t_audit_subclass` is straightforward, consisting of an identifier and the class of the audit process. However, its semantic role is critical. It defines the scope of the audit activity. While the audit process itself is a distinct entity, its function is to validate the integrity of the `cco:DirectiveICE` instances (the GDPR and SOC 2 directives). In practice, this table would enumerate the specific audit procedures (e.g., "Quarterly Access Review," "Annual SOC 2 Audit") that serve as the primary vehicles for compliance verification.
