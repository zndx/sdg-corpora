# Chapter 4: Formalization of Governance Directives and Attestation Protocols

This chapter delineates the structural and semantic relationships governing regulatory compliance frameworks, specifically focusing on the intersection of General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) mandates, System and Organization Controls (SOC 2) trust criteria, and the attestation processes that substantiate organizational adherence. The content herein provides a rigorous examination of the ontological commitments required to map these legal and operational requirements into a coherent governance architecture. Particular emphasis is placed on the relational dependencies between directive classes, attestation processes, and the evidentiary artifacts that serve as the basis for audit verification.

## 1. GDPR Article Subclass

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) establishes a complex hierarchy of legal norms, wherein specific articles function as subclasses of broader directive information content entities. Within the formal ontology, the concept of a GDPR Article Subclass is defined by the axiom that such an entity is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` and possesses a specific relationship to a target class Y, denoted as `sdg:gdprArticle`. This relationship asserts that the governance directive (X) is substantively characterized or constrained by a specific GDPR article (Y).

To maintain referential integrity within the governance database, the instantiation of a GDPR Article Subclass requires a valid link to an attestation record. This dependency ensures that every regulatory citation is anchored to a specific, signed governance process. The relational schema for this entity enforces a foreign key constraint, mandating that the `y` slot (the article identifier) must correspond to a valid identifier in the attestation table.

**Exhibit 1.1: Relational Mapping of GDPR Article Subclasses**

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

*Exhibit 1.1 illustrates the instantiation of specific GDPR articles as directive classes. The primary key (`id`) uniquely identifies the regulatory provision, while the `x` column denotes the specific directive class. The `y` column establishes the mandatory foreign key relationship to the attestation record, ensuring that the legal provision is operationally bound to a signed attestation process.*

## 2. Attestation Signed By

Attestation serves as the foundational process by which an organization formally validates its compliance posture. Ontologically, an Attestation Signed By is defined as a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (a Process) that necessitates the involvement of a `cco:Person` via the `sdg:signedBy` property. This axiom formalizes the delegation of responsibility, asserting that the attestation process is not merely an automated event but is grounded in the agency of a designated natural person.

The relational representation of this concept isolates the attestation entity and its class type. While the ontology links the attestation to a person, the provided schema focuses on the attestation instance itself (`x`) and its unique identifier (`id`). This structure acts as the parent node for other governance artifacts, such as GDPR articles and SOC 2 criteria, which reference the attestation to validate their own existence within the compliance framework.

**Exhibit 1.2: Registry of Attestation Processes**

| id | x |
| :--- | :--- |
| ATT_SIGN_001 | DPOAttestationProcess |
| ATT_SIGN_002 | CISOAttestationProcess |
| ATT_SIGN_003 | PrivacyOfficerAttestation |
| ATT_SIGN_004 | ComplianceOfficerAttestation |
| ATT_SIGN_005 | DataGovernanceManagerAttestation |

*Exhibit 1.2 catalogs the distinct attestation processes utilized within the governance framework. The `x` column specifies the class of the attestation process (e.g., the specific role responsible for signing), while the `id` provides the unique reference point. These identifiers serve as the target for foreign key constraints defined in subsequent tables, creating a normalized structure where regulatory articles and trust criteria are dependent on these attestations.*

## 3. SOC 2 Trust Criterion

The SOC 2 framework provides a baseline of trust service criteria, organized around security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy. In this ontology, a SOC 2 Trust Criterion is modeled as a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` that incorporates a specific trust principle via the `sdg:soc2TrustCriterion` property. This allows the organization to map internal control directives directly to the external criteria defined by the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants (AICPA).

Structurally, this entity mirrors the GDPR Article Subclass in its dependency on the attestation layer. The schema dictates that the `y` slot, representing the specific trust criterion, must reference a valid attestation record (`t_attestation_signed_by`). This design enforces the principle that trust criteria are not abstract concepts but are operationalized only when explicitly linked to a verification process signed by a responsible agent.

**Exhibit 1.3: SOC 2 Criteria and Attestation Linkages**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| SOC2_CC6.1 | LogicalAccessControlDirective | ATT_SIGN_002 |

| SOC2_C1.2 | ConfidentialityDataClassification | ATT_SIGN_001 |
| SOC2_P1.1 | PrivacyNoticeManagement | ATT_SIGN_004 |
| SOC2_CC3.2 | ChangeManagementDirective | ATT_SIGN_005 |

*Exhibit 1.3 demonstrates the alignment of organizational directives with SOC 2 trust criteria. The `x` column defines the internal control class addressing the criterion, while `y` links the control to the specific attestation process (e.g., `ATT_SIGN_002` for the CISO attestation). This mapping ensures that every trust service criterion claimed by the organization is substantiated by a corresponding, signed attestation.*

## 4. Attestation with Supporting Evidence

A robust compliance framework requires that assertions of control effectiveness be supported by objective evidence. The axiom for Attestation with Supporting Evidence defines a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process) that includes the property `sdg:withSupportingEvidence`. This property links the attestation process to a specific evidence class (Y), which may consist of logs, reports, or other artifacts generated during operations.

The relational schema for this entity establishes a critical dependency chain: the evidence (`y`) referenced in the attestation must itself correspond to a valid GDPR Article Subclass ID. This constraint implies that the "supporting evidence" is categorized or indexed according to the specific regulatory provision it addresses. For instance, a log file serving as evidence for a Right to Erasure request would be linked to the `GDPR_ART_17` identifier. This structure facilitates audit trails by connecting the process of attestation directly to the regulatory context of the evidence.

**Exhibit 1.4: Evidence Mapping for Attestation Processes**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| EVID_ERASURE_01 | ErasureRequestLogAttestation | GDPR_ART_17 |
| EVID_DESIGN_01 | PrivacyByDesignReviewAttestation | GDPR_ART_25 |
| EVID_ENC_01 | EncryptionKeyManagementAttestation | GDPR_ART_32 |
| EVID_DPIA_01 | DPIAReportApprovalAttestation | GDPR_ART_35 |
| EVID_TRANSFER_01 | SCCExecutionAttestation | GDPR_ART_45 |

*Exhibit 1.4 details the binding of specific attestation processes to their supporting evidence, categorized by GDPR article. The `x` column identifies the specific attestation class (e.g., the act of reviewing an erasure log), while the `y` column references the `id` of the relevant GDPR Article Subclass from Exhibit 1.1. This foreign key relationship ensures that the evidence is legally contextualized within the broader directive framework.*

## 5. Audit Subclass

The final component of the governance architecture is the Audit Subclass. This entity is defined as a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process) that performs the action of auditing a control directive (`cco:DirectiveICE`). The axiom `sdg:auditsControl some cco:DirectiveICE` establishes the audit process as the mechanism by which the effectiveness and implementation of directives are verified.

In the relational schema, the Audit Subclass is represented as a standalone table containing the audit identifier and the class of the audit process. While the ontology defines the relationship to the directive being audited, the schema focuses on the classification of the audit itself. This entity acts as a distinct process layer, separate from but related to, the attestation layer; while attestation represents a declaration of compliance, the audit represents the investigative or verification process that validates such declarations.

**Exhibit 1.5: Classification of Audit Processes**

| id | x |
| :--- | :--- |
| AUD_INT_001 | InternalControlAudit |
| AUD_EXT_001 | ThirdPartyComplianceAudit |
| AUD_SYS_001 | SystemConfigurationAudit |
| AUD_LOG_001 | AccessLogAudit |
| AUD_NET_001 | NetworkSecurityAudit |

*Exhibit 1.5 provides a taxonomy of audit processes utilized within the framework. The `x` column denotes the specific class of audit being performed. These audit classes serve as the operational vehicles through which `cco:DirectiveICE` entities—such as the GDPR directives and SOC 2 criteria defined in previous sections—are examined for efficacy and implementation fidelity.*
