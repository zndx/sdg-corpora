# Chapter 4: Governance Ontology and Compliance Mapping

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures governing regulatory compliance, audit protocols, and evidence management within the enterprise information security framework. It establishes the axiomatic relationships between internal directives, external statutory requirements (specifically the General Data Protection Regulation), and industry-standard trust criteria (SOC 2). The content herein provides a rigorous, evidence-anchored methodology for mapping organizational controls to legal and compliance obligations, ensuring traceability from high-level governance policies down to specific attestations and supporting artifacts.

## 4.1 Attestation and Signatory Authority

In the context of governance frameworks, the validity of any control or directive is contingent upon the verification by an authorized agent. The concept of attestation serves as the foundational bridge between abstract policy and operational accountability. Formally, an attestation is defined as a process that must be signed by a person. This axiom ensures that no automated or disembodied process is considered valid without explicit human accountability, anchoring the governance chain in individual responsibility.

The following table embodies the axiom `attestation_signed_by`, representing the entities responsible for validating controls. The primary key `id` serves as the unique identifier for the attestation event, while `x` denotes the class or type of attestation being executed. This structure acts as the parent record for subsequent compliance mappings, ensuring that all regulatory citations are traceable back to a specific human signatory.

## 4.2 Regulatory Alignment: GDPR Articles

To operationalize the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), organizations must map their internal directives to specific articles of the regulation. An internal directive is not merely a suggestion; it is a subclass of a `DirectiveICE` (Information Content Entity) that specifically addresses a GDPR Article. This relationship creates a formal binding between the organization's governing documents and the legal text, ensuring that for every internal rule, there is a corresponding legal basis.

The table below illustrates the `gdpr_article_subclass` axiom. It maps internal directive classes (`x`) to specific GDPR Articles (`y`). The primary key is the directive identifier, and the foreign key (`y`) references the attestation table (`t_attestation_signed_by`), signifying that the interpretation or application of this specific Article is validated by the signatory identified in the corresponding attestation record. This linkage ensures that the legal interpretation is authorized and recorded.

## 4.3 Trust Services Criteria Integration

Organizations operating under multi-framework compliance regimes must demonstrate that their directives satisfy the Trust Services Criteria (TSC) defined in the AICPA SOC 2 framework. A directive is defined as a subclass of a `DirectiveICE` that satisfies a specific SOC 2 Trust Criterion. This axiom allows for the convergence of controls, where a single internal directive may simultaneously satisfy a GDPR requirement and a SOC 2 criterion, thereby reducing redundancy and increasing audit efficiency.

The following table represents the `soc2_trust_criterion` axiom. It lists internal directives (`x`) and the specific SOC 2 criteria (`y`) they fulfill. The foreign key constraint linking `y` to the attestation table (`t_attestation_signed_by`) indicates that the satisfaction of the trust criterion is confirmed by the referenced signatory. This structure is critical for audit readiness, as it provides an immediate lineage from the criterion to the individual responsible for attesting to its implementation.

## 4.4 Evidentiary Support and Validation

A claim of compliance is insufficient without corroborating evidence. In formal ontology terms, an attestation is a process that must possess supporting evidence. This evidence acts as the ground truth for the attestation, transforming a subjective assertion into an objective, verifiable fact. The evidence must be specific, relevant, and directly linked to the regulatory or compliance requirement it supports.

The table below embodies the `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` axiom. It categorizes types of supporting evidence (`x`) and links them to the specific GDPR directive subclass (`y`) they validate. The foreign key constraint ensures that every piece of evidence is tied to a valid directive instance (from `t_gdpr_article_subclass`), which in turn is tied to a legal article and a signatory. This creates a rigid chain of custody: Evidence -> Directive -> Article -> Signatory.

## 4.5 Audit Control Mechanisms

The final component of the governance ontology is the audit process itself. An audit is defined as a process that audits a control directive. This recursive relationship ensures that governance frameworks are subject to independent review. The audit process is distinct from the attestation; while attestation is a declaration of validity, the audit is the investigative process that verifies the integrity of the directive and its associated evidence.

The table provided represents the `audit_subclass` axiom. It identifies specific audit processes (`x`) that are designed to review the directives defined in previous sections. As a standalone table with no foreign key dependencies in this schema subset, it represents the independent nature of the audit function.
