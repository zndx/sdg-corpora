# Chapter 4: Ontological Mapping of Compliance Directives and Governance Processes

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter provides a formal specification of the ontological structures governing the intersection of regulatory directives and procedural attestations within an enterprise governance framework. The content herein defines the semantic relationships between specific legal instruments—namely the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and System and Organization Controls (SOC 2)—and the procedural mechanisms required to validate their implementation. By mapping these concepts to the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) and the Common Core Ontologies (CCO), this document establishes a rigorous, evidence-anchored data model suitable for automated compliance auditing and high-assurance reporting. The definitions provided are deterministic, ensuring that every instance of a governance process can be traced back to a specific directive and a responsible agent.

## 4.2 GDPR Article Subclasses

The first structural component involves the classification of regulatory directives as subclasses of Information Content Entities (ICE). Specifically, we address the axiom where a class `X` is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` and satisfies the restriction of being a GDPR article `Y`. In formal terms, `{X}` is something that GDPR article `{Y}`. This relationship is not merely taxonomic; it establishes a binding dependency where the existence and validity of the directive are contingent upon its alignment with specific statutory articles.

In the context of relational data modeling, this axiom is instantiated through a schema that links the directive to a specific attestation process. The primary key identifies the specific directive instance, while the foreign key enforces integrity by ensuring that the directive is mapped to a valid, signed attestation record. This structure prevents the existence of "orphan" directives that lack legal grounding or executive accountability.

## 4.3 Attestation Signed By

Governance frameworks require the formalization of approval processes. The axiom `attestation_signed_by` posits that a class `X` is a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (a Process) and is characterized by the property `sdg:signedBy` referencing a `cco:Person`. Verbalized, `{X}` is something that signed by person. This concept moves beyond the static definition of a document to the dynamic act of endorsement, capturing the agency and temporal reality of the governance event.

This table serves as a critical dependency for other governance artifacts. Because attestation is a process, it occupies a distinct temporal space separate from the directive itself. The relational schema for this axiom is straightforward, consisting of an identifier and the process definition. However, its role as a parent table in the schema (referenced by both GDPR and SOC 2 mappings) underscores its centrality. It acts as the ledger of accountability, ensuring that every compliance artifact can be traced back to a human agent.

## 4.4 SOC 2 Trust Criteria

Parallel to the GDPR framework, the System and Organization Controls (SOC 2) criteria provide a functional baseline for information security. The axiom `soc2_trust_criterion` defines a class `X` as a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` that fulfills a specific SOC 2 trust criterion `Y`. Thus, `{X}` is something that SOC 2 trust criterion `{Y}`. This mapping allows the organization to align its internal policies with external audit standards, specifically the Trust Services Criteria (TSC) regarding security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy.

The relational projection of this axiom mirrors the GDPR structure, utilizing the same foreign key dependency to the attestation table. This design choice reflects a unified governance model where distinct regulatory regimes (GDPR and SOC 2) converge on common procedural checkpoints. By linking the SOC 2 criterion to an attestation ID, the model ensures that the criteria are not merely theoretical but have been reviewed and signed off by the appropriate governance authority.

## 4.5 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

A robust compliance framework distinguishes between the act of attestation and the evidentiary basis supporting that act. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` asserts that a class `X` (a Process) possesses the property `sdg:withSupportingEvidence` pointing to a class `Y`. In natural language, `{X}` is something that with supporting evidence `{Y}`. This axiom is critical for audit readiness, as it mandates that every attestation must be anchored to verifiable proof, such as logs, screenshots, or audit reports.

The schema for this axiom establishes a dependency where the evidence (`y`) must correspond to a valid GDPR article subclass. This implies that the evidence is not generic but is specifically generated to demonstrate compliance with a particular article. For instance, a log review process (the attestation) would be supported by the specific log file (the evidence) that proves adherence to a GDPR Article regarding data access.

The table `t_attestation_with_supporting_evidence` embodies this axiom. The primary key is `id`. The `x` column represents the attestation process, and the `y` column is a foreign key referencing `t_gdpr_article_subclass`. This structure ensures that the evidence provided is directly relevant to the specific regulatory article under review.

| evi_supp_02 | EncryptionKeyManagementAudit | dir_gdpr_art_32 |

*Table 4.4 maps specific evidence-gathering processes (e.g., Quarterly Access Log Review) to the GDPR directives they support. The reference to `dir_gdpr_art_17` indicates that the log review serves as the primary evidence for the directive concerning the Right of Access.*

## 4.6 Audit Subclass

The final concept in this ontological chapter is the Audit. The axiom `audit_subclass` defines a class `X` as a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process) that `sdg:auditsControl` a `cco:DirectiveICE`. Verbalized, `{X}` is something that audits control directive information content entity. This concept encapsulates the verification mechanism itself—the systematic process of examining the directives and their implementation to ensure conformity.

Unlike the previous tables, the audit subclass does not require a foreign key to other tables in this specific schema segment, as it acts as the independent verification agent. However, it is semantically linked to the directives defined earlier. The audit process is the "meta-process" that evaluates the efficacy of the directives and the validity of the attestations. In a mature governance framework, the rows in this table represent the scheduled or ad-hoc audit events that scrutinize the controls defined in the GDPR and SOC 2 sections.
