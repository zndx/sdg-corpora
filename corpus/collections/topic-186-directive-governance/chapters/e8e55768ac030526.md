# Chapter 4: Spatial Governance and Evidence-Based Compliance

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the formal protocols for integrating spatial data artifacts within governance frameworks, specifically focusing on the verification of physical locations through evidence-backed attestations. The guidelines herein establish a rigorous ontology for managing the relationships between physical infrastructure (Places), their logical identifiers (Addresses), and the administrative processes (Attestations, Audits) that validate their existence and compliance status. Adherence to these axioms ensures that spatial data is not merely descriptive but functions as a legally binding component of the regulatory record, supporting high-fidelity audit trails and directive enforcement.

## 4.2 Artifact-Address Correlation

### 4.2.1 Conceptual Framework
In the context of asset management and regulatory compliance, a physical site or facility is modeled as an `cco:Artifact`. This classification elevates the "Place" from a mere geometric locus to a distinct entity within the information architecture, possessing properties that can be audited and governed. The primary axiom governing this relationship asserts that every such Artifact must possess a `sdg:hasPostalAddress` property. This property does not merely store a string; it links the Artifact to a distinct class entity representing the Address itself.

Formally, the axiom is expressed as:
**Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:Artifact, sdg:hasPostalAddress some {Y:Class}**

This structure ensures that the identity of a Place is inextricably bound to its postal designation. In practice, this means that a facility cannot exist within the system without a valid, registered address object. Furthermore, the relational schema imposes a foreign key constraint wherein the Address (`Y`) must reference a valid Attestation record. This design choice enforces a rule of evidence: an address is not valid unless it is the subject of a specific verification process (Attestation).

## 4.3 Attestation and Supporting Evidence

### 4.3.1 The Process of Verification
While the previous section established the existence of a Place and its Address, the validity of that Address is contingent upon a verification process. This concept is captured by the attestation axiom, which classifies the verification event as a `bfo:0000015` (Process). An Attestation is defined as a process that possesses `sdg:withSupportingEvidence`. This creates a chain of custody for truth: a Place has an Address, and that Address is validated by an Attestation, which in turn is substantiated by Evidence.

The axiom is formalized as:
**Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:withSupportingEvidence some {Y:Class}**

In this framework, the "Supporting Evidence" (`Y`) is not a document file in the traditional sense, but rather a reference back to a spatial entity (a Place/Address). This circular reference—where an Address is validated by an Attestation that points to another Place (or the same Place)—models the real-world practice of site surveys. For example, an address verification for a new facility (Attestation) might be supported by the evidence of a geodetic marker located at a separate, established reference point (Place). This rigid structure prevents the entry of "orphan" addresses that cannot be traced back to physical reality.

## 4.4 Geospatial Coordinate Attribution

### 4.4.1 Precision and Artifact Identity
Beyond postal addresses, the precise geolocation of an Artifact is required for jurisdictional and operational definitions. The axiom for geospatial coordinates extends the definition of the `cco:Artifact` (Place) to include the property `sdg:hasGeoCoordinates`. This property links the Place to a coordinate set, treated here as a distinct class entity.

The axiom is expressed as:
**Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:Artifact, sdg:hasGeoCoordinates some {Y:Class}**

Similar to the address relationship, the coordinate entity (`Y`) is not merely a pair of floating-point numbers but an object that must be justified. The relational schema dictates that the coordinate entity references an Attestation. This implies that coordinates are not self-evident; they must be the output of a measurement or calibration process (the Attestation). This layer of abstraction is crucial for high-stakes environments where "GPS coordinates" must be defensible—e.g., the coordinates defining a hazardous zone must be traceable to a specific surveyor's report (Attestation).

## 4.5 Audit Findings and Spatial Resolution

### 4.5.1 Governance and Non-Compliance
Audits are the primary mechanism for enforcing governance standards. An Audit is modeled as a `bfo:0000015` (Process) that produces `sdg:hasFindings`. The critical distinction in this ontology is that the "Findings" are not text descriptions but references to spatial entities (Places/Addresses). This forces the audit process to be spatially resolved; a non-compliance finding cannot exist in the abstract—it must be pinned to a specific Artifact.

The axiom is defined as:
**Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:hasFindings some {Y:Class}**

By linking the Finding (`Y`) to a Place (`t_schemaorg_place_address`), the system ensures that remediation efforts are directed at the correct physical asset. If an audit reveals a safety violation, the finding is recorded as a reference to the specific facility ID. This eliminates ambiguity in audit reports, ensuring that "the leak in Sector 7G" is formally recorded as a finding associated with `place_facility_alpha`.

## 4.6 Directive Specification and Target Application

### 4.6.1 Regulatory Logic
The final concept governs the application of regulatory rules. A Directive is modeled as a `cco:DirectiveICE` that is `EquivalentTo` a specification and a target. The axiom states:
**Class: {X:Class} EquivalentTo: cco:DirectiveICE and (sdg:specifies some {Y:Class}) and (sdg:appliesTo some {Z:Class})**

This structure creates a triple-binding for regulations: The Directive (`X`) specifies a Requirement (`Y`) and applies it to a Target (`Z`). In this specific schema, the Requirement (`Y`) is projected onto the spatial domain—it references a Place/Address. This implies that the *content* of the directive is often spatially defined (e.g., "the buffer zone around Facility Alpha"). The Target (`Z`) represents the broader class or entity affected (e.g., "All Personnel", "Operations Team").
