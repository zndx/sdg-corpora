# Chapter 4: Evidence-Based Compliance and Data Lineage Verification

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the structural and semantic requirements for establishing evidentiary trails within automated audit frameworks. It addresses the intersection of ontological rigor and regulatory compliance, specifically focusing on how artifacts, processes, and directives are mapped to support descriptive claims. The principles outlined herein are essential for ensuring that digital evidence withstands scrutiny during internal reviews and external regulatory examinations. By formalizing the relationships between data lineage, system anomalies, and statutory requirements, organizations can mitigate the risk of inaccurate determinations and downstream rework. The content is grounded in the necessity to produce high-quality, verifiable data structures that support the "Right to a Fair and Just Tax System" and analogous rights in data governance.

## 4.2 Trace Support for Descriptive Claims

In the context of formal audits, a "trace" is not merely a log entry but a distinct process entity that substantiates a specific assertion. The axiom `trace_supports_claim` establishes a fundamental relationship wherein a process (specifically, a subclass of `bfo:0000015`, denoting a process) functions as the evidentiary basis for a `DescriptiveICE` (Descriptive Information Content Entity). This relationship is critical when auditors must verify that a reported state of affairs is supported by continuous, observable system behaviors.

To validate a claim—such as "the transaction was processed within the regulatory SLA"—the auditor must identify a trace that is temporally and causally linked to the event in question. This trace must be immutable and distinct from the claim itself, serving as an independent witness to the process.

## 4.3 Kernel Anomaly Evidence

While standard traces validate expected behaviors, kernel anomalies represent deviations that must also be formally captured to support claims regarding system integrity or security incidents. The axiom `kernel_anomaly_supports_claim` posits that an artifact—specifically a kernel-level anomaly—can support a descriptive claim. This is distinct from standard process traces; kernel anomalies are artifacts produced by the system's core, often indicating instability, intrusion attempts, or hardware failures.

When a compliance claim asserts that "the system detected and logged critical security exceptions," the evidence must be an instance of a kernel anomaly. These artifacts are often binary dumps or stack traces that require specialized parsing but serve as the definitive proof of the claimed event.

### 4.3.1 Classification of Kernel Artifacts

The table below defines the classes of artifacts that qualify as kernel anomalies under this axiom. These artifacts are treated as distinct entities (`cco:Artifact`) that possess the properties necessary to support a claim.

**Table 4.2: Kernel Anomaly Artifacts Supporting Claims**

| id | x |
| :--- | :--- |
| KA-101 | KernelPanicArtifact |
| KA-102 | NullPointerDereferenceArtifact |
| KA-103 | DeadlockDetectionArtifact |
| KA-104 | BufferOverflowArtifact |
| KA-105 | InterruptConflictArtifact |

The `KernelPanicArtifact` (KA-101), for example, is a class of artifact that supports the descriptive claim "Operating System Halted Unexpectedly." By classifying these artifacts formally, the audit framework can automatically associate them with relevant incident reports without manual intervention.

## 4.4 Column Lineage for Compliance Claims

Data governance frameworks require that the transformation of data be transparent and verifiable. The axiom `column_lineage_for_compliance_claim` addresses this by defining a relationship where an artifact (specifically, a column lineage record) supports a descriptive claim about data provenance. This is essential for demonstrating compliance with regulations such as GDPR or PCI DSS, where the origin and transformation path of sensitive data are subject to strict controls.

A column lineage artifact maps a source data element to its destination, documenting the transformations applied. When an organization claims that "PII data was masked prior to reporting," the evidence is a lineage artifact showing the transformation from a raw column to a masked column.

### 4.4.1 Lineage Artifacts and Compliance

The following table enumerates the artifact classes responsible for maintaining column lineage. These artifacts serve as the bridge between technical data transformations and high-level compliance claims.

The `PseudonymizationArtifact` (CL-205) is particularly relevant for privacy compliance. It is an artifact that supports the claim "Personal identifiers were replaced with pseudonyms," thereby validating adherence to data protection mandates.

## 4.5 PCI DSS Requirement Mapping

Regulatory compliance often requires a direct mapping between internal directives and external standards. The axiom `pci_dss_requirement` formalizes this by asserting that a Directive Information Content Entity (`cco:DirectiveICE`) is the subject of a specific PCI DSS requirement. This mapping allows an organization to demonstrate that its internal policies are designed to satisfy explicit external obligations.

For example, an internal directive titled "Password Complexity Policy" must be mapped to the corresponding PCI DSS requirement (e.g., Requirement 8.2.3). This ontological structure ensures that every internal directive can be traced back to a regulatory clause, facilitating gap analysis and audit readiness.

### 4.5.1 Directive-to-Requirement Mapping

The table below illustrates the mapping between internal directive classes and the specific PCI DSS requirements they fulfill. The `y` column contains the requirement identifier, acting as a foreign key to the regulatory standard.

**Table 4.4: PCI DSS Requirement Mappings**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| PCI-01 | AccessControlDirective | Req. 7 |
| PCI-02 | AuthenticationMechanismDirective | Req. 8.2 |
| PCI-03 | FirewallConfigurationDirective | Req. 1.2 |
| PCI-04 | WirelessAccessDirective | Req. 1.3 |
| PCI-05 | LoggingMechanismDirective | Req. 10.2 |
| PCI-06 | VulnerabilityScanDirective | Req. 11.2 |

In this schema, `AccessControlDirective` (PCI-01) is the class of directive that implements "Req. 7" (Restrict access to system components and cardholder data). This explicit linkage prevents ambiguity regarding which policy addresses which compliance obligation.

## 4.6 Organizational Tax Identification

Entity resolution is a foundational aspect of compliance, particularly in financial reporting. The axiom `schemaorg_org_taxid` establishes that an artifact (typically an organizational profile or registration record) possesses a Tax ID. This relationship is vital for verifying the legal status of counterparties and ensuring that tax reporting obligations are met correctly.

In an audit scenario, a claim that "Vendor X is a registered tax entity" must be supported by an artifact containing the valid Tax ID. This axiom integrates the Schema.org vocabulary for tax identification into the broader compliance ontology, enabling automated validation of entity data against external tax authority databases.
