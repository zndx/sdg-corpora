# Chapter 4: Formal Specification of Belief Structures and Mass Functions

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter establishes the formal ontological criteria for the classification and management of Mass Functions within information systems. As of the current revision of the governance framework, the definition of a Mass Function is strictly axiomatized to ensure interoperability between descriptive data artifacts and probabilistic reasoning engines. The purpose of this section is to delineate the necessary and sufficient conditions for an entity to be recognized as a Mass Function, specifically its equivalence to a Descriptive Information Content Entity (ICE) and its requisite relationships with a Frame of Discernment and specific Mass Assignments. Compliance with these structural definitions is mandatory for all systems performing evidence aggregation under the specified protocol.

## 4.2 The Descriptive Information Content Entity (Descriptive ICE)

### 4.2.1 Definition and Context

In accordance with the Common Core Ontologies (CCO), a Descriptive Information Content Entity is defined as a generically dependent continuant that is about some entity or entities. Within the context of belief structure formalization, the Descriptive ICE serves as the informational container. It is not the physical realization (e.g., the paper document or the digital file sector), but rather the content itself—the proposition or the data pattern—that conveys meaning.

For a Mass Function to be valid within this system, it must first be classified as a Descriptive ICE. This classification ensures that the mathematical construct of the mass function is treated as a distinct, describable artifact capable of being archived, audited, and transmitted. The Descriptive ICE acts as the bearer of the information regarding the distribution of belief. It is imperative to distinguish the *information content* (the Descriptive ICE) from the *information carrier* (the hardware or software medium).

### 4.2.2 Role in Belief Representation

The Descriptive ICE encapsulates the state of knowledge regarding a system at a specific point in time. In an audit context, this means that the Mass Function, as a Descriptive ICE, is the object of scrutiny. It must be immutable once recorded and must reference the entities it describes with unambiguous identifiers. The requirement that a Mass Function be a Descriptive ICE anchors the abstract mathematical theory of evidence in the concrete reality of information management, ensuring that every probabilistic assertion is traceable to a specific informational artifact.

## 4.3 The Frame of Discernment

### 4.3.1 Structural Constraints

The Frame of Discernment represents the set of mutually exclusive and exhaustive hypotheses (or propositions) that define the universe of discourse for a specific belief assessment. Formally, the Mass Function must be `overFrameOfDiscernment` some Class Y. This relationship establishes the domain of validity for the Mass Function. Without a defined Frame of Discernment, a Mass Assignment is mathematically undefined and operationally invalid.

The Frame of Discernment, denoted as Class Y in the ontology, functions as the boundary condition for the evidence. It enumerates the possible states of the world that the Mass Function is attempting to describe. For instance, in a diagnostic system, the Frame of Discernment might consist of the set {Normal, Degraded, Failed}. The Mass Function, as a Descriptive ICE, exists *relative* to this Frame; it describes the distribution of belief *over* these specific options.

### 4.3.2 Operational Implementation

In practical deployment, the Frame of Discernment must be instantiated as a controlled vocabulary or an enumerated class within the system schema. The relationship `sdg:overFrameOfDiscernment` acts as a foreign key constraint, ensuring that no Mass Function can assign belief to a proposition that lies outside its defined scope. This constraint prevents logical errors and category mismatches during data fusion. All Mass Functions associated with a specific analytical subsystem must share a common Frame of Discernment to allow for valid combination rules (such as Dempster's rule of combination) to be applied.

## 4.4 Mass Assignment and the Axiomatic Equivalence

### 4.4.1 The Axiom of Equivalence

The core definition of the Mass Function is provided by the axiom of equivalence. This axiom asserts that a Class X (the Mass Function) is equivalent to the intersection of three constraints:
1.  It is a `cco:DescriptiveICE`.
2.  It is `sdg:overFrameOfDiscernment` some Class Y.
3.  It `sdg:assignsMassTo` some Class Z.

This equivalence is not merely correlational; it is definitional. Any entity within the system that satisfies these three conditions is, by definition, a Mass Function. This tripartite structure ensures that a Mass Function is never treated as a floating numerical value, but always as a grounded informational artifact (Descriptive ICE) with a defined scope (Frame of Discernment) and specific targets (Mass Assignments).

### 4.4.2 The Assigns Mass To Relationship

The slot `sdg:assignsMassTo` links the Mass Function (Class X) to a specific target Class Z. Class Z typically represents a focal element or a specific subset of the hypotheses within the Frame of Discernment. While the Frame of Discernment (Y) defines the total universe of possibilities, the Mass Assignment (Z) identifies the specific recipient of the belief mass.

It is critical to note that the value assigned is the *mass* (a basic probability assignment), not a traditional probability in the Bayesian sense. The mass assigned to Z represents the belief strictly allocated to that proposition and nothing more, without apportionment to its subsets. The relationship `sdg:assignsMassTo` captures this directed allocation. In a relational database context, this necessitates a structure where the Mass Function (X) is the primary entity, linked to its defining Frame (Y), and containing multiple records of assignments (Z), each carrying a specific mass value.

## 4.5 Compliance and Verification

To ensure compliance with the established ontology, all system implementations must verify that every Mass Function record contains valid references to both a Frame of Discernment and a Mass Assignment target. The integrity of the belief structure relies on the strict enforcement of the `sdg:overFrameOfDiscernment` and `sdg:assignsMassTo` relationships. Any Mass Function lacking a valid link to Class Y or Class Z must be flagged as an ontological violation and rejected by the inference engine.

Furthermore, audit trails must preserve the identity of the Descriptive ICE (Class X) across all transformations. Because the Mass Function is defined as an Information Content Entity, version control is applicable. Modifications to the mass assignments (Z) or the redefinition of the Frame (Y) necessitate the creation of a new Mass Function instance (new X), rather than the in-place mutation of the existing record. This immutability is a foundational requirement for maintaining the evidentiary value of the data artifacts.
