# Chapter 12: Formalization of Evidence Structures via Mass Function Equivalence

This chapter delineates the formal criteria for classifying Information Content Entities (ICEs) as mass functions within a compliant governance framework. It establishes the necessary equivalence between descriptive data structures and the mathematical constructs of evidence theory, specifically focusing on the definition of frames of discernment and the assignment of belief mass. The objective is to ensure that all evidentiary data artifacts adhere to the structural axioms required for probabilistic reasoning in automated audit systems and decision-support architectures.

## 12.1 The Descriptive Information Content Entity (DescriptiveICE)

In the context of information systems auditing and semantic data modeling, the foundational unit of analysis is the Information Content Entity (ICE). However, not all ICEs possess the properties required to serve as vehicles for evidentiary reasoning. We restrict our domain to the subclass `cco:DescriptiveICE`. A DescriptiveICE is defined as an information entity that is about some entity or phenomenon. Unlike prescriptive entities (which define rules or norms) or structural entities (which define relationships), a DescriptiveICE serves as a container for observations, measurements, or assertions derived from empirical processes or logical deduction.

For a DescriptiveICE to be utilized in high-assurance environments—such as financial reporting or industrial safety monitoring—it must be rigorously typed. The mere presence of descriptive text or numerical data is insufficient. The entity must be explicitly encoded such that it can be subjected to automated validation rules. In compliance frameworks, this often translates to a requirement that the data structure not only holds a value but also carries metadata regarding its provenance, timestamp, and the context of its generation.

Consider the audit trail of a high-frequency trading platform. A log entry stating "Trade executed at 14:00:01" is a descriptive artifact. However, to function within a semantic reasoning engine, this entry must be instantiated as a `cco:DescriptiveICE`. This classification allows the system to distinguish between the *record* of the trade and the *trade event* itself. The DescriptiveICE acts as the immutable digital fingerprint of the event, preserving the state of the world at a specific temporal coordinate. This distinction is critical when the ICE is subsequently used to update belief states regarding market volatility or system integrity.

## 12.2 The Frame of Discernment

To transform a static DescriptiveICE into an active component of belief revision, it must be anchored to a `sdg:overFrameOfDiscernment`. The Frame of Discernment, a concept borrowed from the Dempster-Shafer theory of evidence, represents the set of mutually exclusive and exhaustive hypotheses relevant to a specific domain of inquiry.

In the ontology, the relationship `sdg:overFrameOfDiscernment` is an object property that links a DescriptiveICE to a specific class defining the universe of possible states. This linkage is not merely semantic; it is a functional constraint that dictates the operational logic of the reasoning engine. When a DescriptiveICE is asserted to be over a Frame of Discernment $\Theta$, it signifies that the content of that entity is intended to modify the probability distribution over the power set of $\Theta$.

For example, in a regulatory audit concerning "Revenue Recognition," the Frame of Discernment might consist of the set $\{ \text{Compliant}, \text{Non-Compliant}, \text{Inconclusive} \}$. A specific audit report (the DescriptiveICE) is defined as being *over* this frame. This means the report's content is interpreted exclusively through the lens of these three hypotheses. The report cannot arbitrarily introduce a fourth state (e.g., "Pending Review") unless the Frame of Discernment itself is redefined to include it. This constraint ensures that all incoming evidence is mapped to a standardized, governed taxonomy, preventing semantic drift and ensuring that downstream analytics remain consistent with the defined governance model.

## 12.3 Mass Assignment and Belief Quantification

The final component in the axiomatization of the evidence structure is the act of assigning mass. The property `sdg:assignsMassTo` links the DescriptiveICE to a specific subset of the Frame of Discernment. In evidence theory, "mass" (or basic probability assignment) represents the degree of belief allocated specifically to a given set of hypotheses, based on the evidence at hand, without that belief being apportioned to any subsets thereof.

It is imperative to distinguish mass from probability. While probability must sum to 1 over the singleton elements of the frame, mass is allocated to the power set of the frame. A DescriptiveICE may assign mass to a composite hypothesis (e.g., $\{ \text{Compliant} \cup \text{Non-Compliant} \}$), representing ignorance or uncertainty that cannot be resolved with the current evidence.

In a technical implementation, the `sdg:assignsMassTo` property serves as the pointer to the target hypothesis set. The actual numerical value of the mass is typically stored as a data property associated with this relationship, but the structural definition of the entity relies on establishing this link. The axiom asserts that for an entity to function as a Mass Function, it *must* assign mass to *some* target class $Z$. This target class $Z$ is a subset of the Frame of Discernment $Y$. This tripartite structure—the Entity ($X$), the Frame ($Y$), and the Target ($Z$)—forms the atomic unit of evidentiary logic in the system.

## 12.4 The Mass Function Equivalence Axiom

The integration of the concepts described in Sections 12.1 through 12.3 is formalized through the Mass Function Equivalence Axiom. This axiom defines a specific class of DescriptiveICE that satisfies the necessary and sufficient conditions to act as a Mass Function. The axiom is expressed as follows:

**Class:** $X$ **EquivalentTo:** `cco:DescriptiveICE` **and** (`sdg:overFrameOfDiscernment` **some** $Y$) **and** (`sdg:assignsMassTo` **some** $Z$)

This logical construct dictates that any entity $X$ is recognized as a Mass Function if and only if it is a DescriptiveICE, it is defined over a specific Frame of Discernment $Y$, and it actively assigns mass to a specific subset $Z$ of that frame. This equivalence serves as the validation rule for the ontology. If an incoming data artifact lacks one of these components—for instance, if it describes an event but does not specify the hypothesis set it pertains to—it fails the classification check and is rejected by the reasoning engine as an "unstructured" or "non-evidentiary" artifact.

This formalization allows for the creation of complex, layered evidence structures. By defining specific classes for $X$, $Y$, and $Z$, governance frameworks can enforce strict typing on evidence. For instance, a "Safety Inspection Report" ($X$) might be restricted to being over the "Operational Safety Frame" ($Y$) and assigning mass only to "Hazardous Conditions" ($Z$). This prevents a safety report from being erroneously interpreted as a financial compliance document, thereby maintaining the integrity of the audit trail.

## 12.5 Application in Industrial Control Systems

The utility of the Mass Function Equivalence Axiom extends beyond administrative auditing into the realm of industrial control systems (ICS) and sensor fusion. In environments where sensors generate continuous streams of data, distinguishing between a raw signal and an evidentiary assertion is crucial for automated decision-making.

In this domain, a DescriptiveICE might be a processed packet of sensor data rather than a text document. For instance, a vibration sensor output is not merely a voltage reading; when processed, it becomes a `VibrationAnalysisLog`. This log is a `cco:DescriptiveICE`. To be useful for predictive maintenance, it must be defined over a `MechanicalHealthFrame` ($Y$), containing hypotheses such as $\{ \text{Normal}, \text{Degraded}, \text{Failure} \}$. The log assigns mass to a specific subset, such as `BearingWearSet` ($Z$).

By enforcing the axiom, the system prevents "orphan" data—readings that cannot be interpreted—from triggering alerts. Only those data packets that conform to the defined Mass Function structure are allowed to update the system's belief regarding the health of the machinery. This filtering mechanism is essential for reducing false positives in safety-critical environments.

## 12.6 Verification and Compliance

The rigorous application of the Mass Function Equivalence Axiom provides a mechanism for automated compliance verification. By querying the `t_mass_function_equiv_frame_and_assignment` table, auditors and system architects can verify that every evidentiary class defined in the system is properly grounded.

A verification query would check for the existence of any `x` (Mass Function class) that does not have a corresponding `y` (Frame) or `z` (Target). The presence of such orphaned records would indicate a violation of the ontology's integrity constraints. Furthermore, this structure facilitates impact analysis; if a specific Frame of Discernment ($Y$) is deprecated or modified, the relational schema allows for the immediate identification of all affected Mass Functions ($X$) and their assignments ($Z$), ensuring that governance changes are propagated consistently throughout the information system.

In summary, the formalization of Mass Functions through the equivalence of DescriptiveICE, Frames of Discernment, and Mass Assignments provides a robust, mathematically sound foundation for managing uncertainty in complex technical environments. The deterministic projection of this ontology into relational schemas ensures that these abstract concepts are implemented with the rigor required for high-stakes auditing and control applications.
