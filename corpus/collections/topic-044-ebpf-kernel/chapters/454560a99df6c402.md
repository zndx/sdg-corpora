# Chapter 7: Audit Lifecycle and Evidence Correlation in Governance Frameworks

This chapter delineates the procedural dependencies between audit processes, verification activities, and attestation protocols within the regulatory compliance architecture. It establishes the ontological relationships governing the generation of findings and the aggregation of supporting evidence, ensuring that all governance activities are traceable, verifiable, and anchored in documented artifacts. The definitions provided herein are binding for all internal audit units and external assurance providers engaged in the fiscal year 2024-2025 compliance cycle.

## 7.1 Audit Processes and Findings Generation

In accordance with the Governance Framework Directive 4.2, an Audit is formally defined as a systematic process designed to assess the efficacy of internal controls and regulatory adherence. Per the governing ontology, an Audit is not merely an event but a processual entity that necessitates the creation of specific outputs. Specifically, the axiom **{Audit} is something that has findings {Finding}** establishes the structural dependency where the existence of an Audit implies the existence of one or more Findings.

### 7.1.1 Classification of Findings
Findings generated during an audit cycle must be classified according to severity and scope. The audit report is considered incomplete until all identified observations are formalized as Findings within the central repository. The relationship between the Audit process and the resultant Finding is immutable; a Finding cannot exist independently of a source Audit process.

## 7.2 Verification Protocols and Evidence Production

Verification activities constitute the operational layer of the compliance framework. While audits define the scope, verification activities perform the actual testing of controls. The ontology defines a Verification as a process that actively generates artifacts. Formally, **{Verification} is something that produces evidence {Evidence}**. This distinction is critical: evidence does not exist prior to the verification act; it is the output of the verification procedure.

### 7.2.1 Evidence Chain of Custody
All verification procedures must be documented with a precise timestamp and operator identifier. The evidence produced must be immutable and stored in the WORM (Write Once, Read Many) repository. The relationship between the verification action and the evidence is direct and causal.

## 7.3 Attestation and Evidence Correlation

The final stage of the governance lifecycle is Attestation. Attestation is the formal declaration by an authorized officer that a specific set of controls has been reviewed and found to be operating effectively. Unlike verification, which produces evidence, attestation *consumes* evidence to support a claim. The axiom states: **{Attestation} is something that with supporting evidence {Evidence}**.

### 7.3.1 Requirements for Valid Attestation
An attestation is only valid if it is linked to supporting evidence that is sufficient and appropriate. Officers are prohibited from signing attestations if the `withSupportingEvidence` relation is null or references expired evidence artifacts. The evidence referenced must directly support the scope of the attestation.
