# Chapter 4: Formalization of Directive Governance and PCI-DSS Compliance Mapping

This chapter delineates the structural architecture for mapping high-level governance directives to specific regulatory requirements, with a primary focus on the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI-DSS). The content herein establishes the ontological relationships between abstract policy classes, their specific enforcement mechanisms, and the jurisdictional scopes that govern their application. It is intended for compliance officers, data architects, and information security auditors who require a rigorous, schema-based approach to regulatory mapping. The following sections decompose the governance framework into five distinct axiomatic components, providing both the logical definition and the relational instantiation of each concept.

## 4.1 Requirement Specification and Target Equivalence

The foundational unit of governance within this framework is the Requirement Class, defined not merely by its existence but by its functional equivalence to a directive that specifies a control and applies to a specific target. Axiomatically, we define a Class X as equivalent to a Directive Information Content Entity (ICE) if it specifies some requirement Y and applies to some scope Z. This equivalence ensures that a "requirement" is never treated as a static string of text, but as an active directive with a defined object (the requirement it specifies) and a target (the system or process it governs).

In practice, this structure prevents ambiguity in compliance audits. When an auditor queries for a specific requirement, the system retrieves not only the requirement text but also the directive context and the specific target environment. This is critical for maintaining traceability between high-level policy mandates and operational realities.

The following table, `t_requirement_equiv_specifies_target`, embodies this axiom. It establishes the primary keys for specific directive instances and links them via foreign keys to the specific PCI-DSS requirements they codify. The structure ensures that every directive entry (`x`) is intrinsically bound to a specific requirement definition (`y`) and a target scope (`z`).

**Table 4.1: Directive Requirement Mapping**
*This table illustrates the equivalence of directive classes to specific requirements and their application targets. The foreign key `y` enforces referential integrity to the PCI requirement definitions.*

| id | x | y | z |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| DIR-REQ-001 | FirewallConfigurationDirective | PCI-REQ-001 | UntrustedNetworkZone |
| DIR-REQ-002 | VendorAccessDirective | PCI-REQ-002 | ThirdPartyRemoteAccess |

**Analysis of Table 4.1:**
As evidenced in the row `DIR-REQ-001`, the entity `FirewallConfigurationDirective` is formally equivalent to a directive that specifies `PCI-REQ-001` and applies specifically to the `UntrustedNetworkZone`. This mapping satisfies the axiom that the directive class is defined by its specification of a requirement and its application to a target. The data indicates a strict one-to-one mapping in this instance, though the ontology allows for broader application.

## 4.2 PCI-DSS Requirement Subclassification

While the previous section establishes the general structure of directives, this section addresses the specific domain of Payment Card Industry compliance. Axiom 2 posits that a Class X is a subclass of the Directive ICE and is specifically characterized by a `pciDssRequirement` relationship to some Class Y. Verbalized, X is something that acts as a PCI DSS requirement Y. This subclassification allows the ontology to distinguish generic governance directives from those specifically mandated by the PCI Security Standards Council.

The relational implementation of this axiom involves a recursive structure where the specific PCI requirement instance references the general directive requirement established in Axiom 1. This creates a tight coupling between the abstract concept of a "requirement" and its specific regulatory instantiation. It ensures that every PCI-DSS requirement is treated as a distinct, governed entity within the broader compliance framework.

**Analysis of Table 4.2:**
The row corresponding to `PCI-REQ-001` identifies the entity as `Requirement1` (referring to PCI-DSS Requirement 1 regarding firewall installation). The foreign key `y` points to `DIR-REQ-001`. This linkage validates the axiom that the PCI requirement is a subclass of the directive defined previously. It confirms that `Requirement1` is the specific regulatory manifestation of the `FirewallConfigurationDirective`. This structure is essential for automated compliance tools that must toggle between regulatory language and internal directive terminology.

## 4.3 Jurisdiction and Scope Governance

Directives do not exist in a vacuum; they are constrained by legal jurisdictions and operational scopes. Axiom 3 defines a Class X (Policy) as equivalent to a Directive ICE that possesses a specific jurisdiction Y and governs a specific scope Z. This axiom is critical for multinational organizations where a single technical control may be mandated by different policies in different regions (e.g., GDPR vs. CCPA), or where the scope of application (e.g., production vs. development) alters the compliance status.

In this schema, the jurisdiction slot (`y`) is modeled as a reference to a PCI-DSS requirement. This implies that the "jurisdiction" or the enforcing authority for a specific policy is effectively defined or anchored by the specific requirement it enforces. While typically a jurisdiction might be a legal entity, in this specific technical ontology, the jurisdictional constraint is functionally tied to the requirement ID.

**Table 4.3: Policy Jurisdiction and Scope**
*This table defines the policies, their enforcing jurisdictions (linked to PCI requirements), and the scopes they govern. The foreign key constraint on `y` ensures that the jurisdiction is valid within the context of established requirements.*

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| POL-JUR-001 | CorporateDataSovereigntyPolicy | PCI-REQ-001 | EUCustomerDatabase |
| POL-JUR-002 | ThirdPartyRiskManagementPolicy | PCI-REQ-002 | VendorAccessGateway |
| POL-JUR-003 | EncryptionInTransitPolicy | PCI-REQ-003 | PublicInternetInterface |
| POL-JUR-004 | DataAtRestProtectionPolicy | PCI-REQ-004 | SecureBackupArchive |
| POL-JUR-005 | IntegrityMonitoringPolicy | PCI-REQ-005 | SystemLogRepository |

**Analysis of Table 4.3:**
The entry `POL-JUR-001` represents a `CorporateDataSovereigntyPolicy`. Its jurisdiction is defined by the reference to `PCI-REQ-001` (Requirement 1), and it governs the scope of the `EUCustomerDatabase`. This mapping suggests that the authority enforcing this policy is derived directly from the mandate of PCI Requirement 1. By anchoring the jurisdiction to the requirement ID, the schema ensures that policies are not created in isolation of the technical controls they intend to govern.

## 4.4 Attestation and Personnel Accountability

Governance frameworks must account for the human element of compliance—the verification and sign-off of controls. Axiom 4 defines a Class X (Attestation) as a subclass of a Process (BFO:0000015) that is signed by a Person. This moves the ontology from purely informational entities (directives and requirements) to processual entities that occur in time and involve agency.

Attestation serves as the bridge between the static definition of a requirement and the dynamic confirmation of its implementation. In an audit context, the existence of a directive is necessary but insufficient; evidence of the attestation process (a specific process signed by a responsible person) is required to demonstrate compliance.

**Table 4.4: Attestation Processes**
*This table catalogs the attestation processes. The primary key is the process ID, and the value `x` represents the class of the attestation process. Note that while the ontology links to a Person, the relational schema provided focuses on the process classification itself.*

| id | x |

| ATT-SGN-001 | Q1ComplianceReviewProcess |
| ATT-SGN-002 | AnnualPenetrationTestSignOff |
| ATT-SGN-003 | QuarterlyVulnerabilityScanAttestation |
| ATT-SGN-004 | PolicyChangeApprovalProcess |
| ATT-SGN-005 | IncidentResponseVerificationProcess |

**Analysis of Table 4.4:**
The table lists specific process instances such as `Q1ComplianceReviewProcess` (`ATT-SGN-001`). According to the axiom, this process is a subclass of a BFO Process and is characterized by the property `signedBy` pointing to a `cco:Person`. While the specific person's name is not a column in this specific table (being likely stored in a separate `Person` table not defined in this schema), the entity `ATT-SGN-001` represents the event of signing. This allows the system to query for all outstanding attestations or to generate a report of all processes requiring signature by a specific role.

## 4.5 Policy Enforcement Mechanisms

The final axiom addresses the operationalization of governance: enforcement. Axiom 5 states that a Class X (Policy) is a Directive ICE that is enforced by a minimum of one Class Y. The "min 1" cardinality is a critical constraint; it prohibits the existence of "zombie policies"—directives that are defined but have no mechanism or agent responsible for their enforcement.

In the relational schema, the enforcing entity (`y`) is linked to the PCI-DSS requirement table. This implies that the enforcement of a policy is achieved through the implementation or monitoring of a specific requirement. In other words, the policy is the abstract rule, and the PCI requirement acts as the concrete enforcement mechanism or the metric by which enforcement is measured.

**Table 4.5: Policy Enforcement Definitions**
*This table maps policies to their enforcing requirements. The constraint that `y` references `t_pci_dss_requirement` ensures that every policy listed here is actively enforced by a valid, defined PCI requirement.*

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| POL-ENF-001 | NetworkAccessControlPolicy | PCI-REQ-001 |
| POL-ENF-002 | RemoteAccessManagementPolicy | PCI-REQ-002 |
| POL-ENF-003 | SecureTransmissionPolicy | PCI-REQ-003 |
| POL-ENF-004 | DataStorageEncryptionPolicy | PCI-REQ-004 |
| POL-ENF-005 | AuditLogRetentionPolicy | PCI-REQ-005 |

**Analysis of Table 4.5:**
The row `POL-ENF-001` defines a `NetworkAccessControlPolicy`. The enforcement of this policy is tied to `PCI-REQ-001`. This structure allows governance officers to validate that for every high-level policy (e.g., "We must secure the network"), there is a corresponding, enforceable technical requirement (e.g., "Install and maintain a firewall configuration") that serves as the mechanism of enforcement. The "min 1" constraint is satisfied by the presence of the foreign key entry; a null value or missing reference would violate the axiom.
