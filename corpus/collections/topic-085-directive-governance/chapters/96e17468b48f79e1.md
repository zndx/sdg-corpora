# Chapter 4: Governance Frameworks and Regulatory Compliance Mapping

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the structural relationships between regulatory directives, technical controls, and risk management taxonomies within a unified governance framework. The primary objective is to establish a precise ontology for mapping General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) articles to specific control classes and their associated threat vectors. By formalizing these relationships through axiomatic constraints, organizations can ensure that their compliance artifacts—ranging from audit reports to technical implementation guides—are anchored in a verifiable evidence chain. The following sections decompose these relationships into five core axioms, providing both the semantic definition and the relational schema necessary for automated validation and reporting.

## 4.2 GDPR Article Subclass Relationships

The foundational element of the compliance framework is the formal association between a control class and a specific regulatory provision. In the context of the ontology, this is defined by the axiom `gdpr_article_subclass`, which asserts that a specific class of controls (`X`) is intrinsically linked to a GDPR article (`Y`). This relationship is not merely semantic but serves as a binding constraint for auditability: any instance of the control class `X` must necessarily invoke the regulatory context of article `Y`.

This mapping is critical for establishing the "Regulatory Basis" field in governance documentation. When an auditor reviews a technical control, they must be able to trace it back to the specific legal mandate requiring its implementation. The relational schema for this axiom enforces a circular dependency with threat classes, ensuring that the regulatory article is not defined in isolation but is strictly tied to the threat landscape it addresses.

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Risk_UnauthAccess | AccessControl | Map_Art32_Access |
| Risk_DataExfiltration | EncryptionControl | Map_Art32_Enc |
| Risk_HighRightsImpact | ImpactAssessment | Map_Art35_DPIA |
| Risk_PrivacyBreach | DataMinimization | Map_Art25_DataByDesign |
| Risk_SubjectRights | ConsentManagement | Map_Art7_Consent |

*Table 4.1 illustrates the instantiation of the `gdpr_article_subclass` axiom. The column `x` represents the control class, while `y` represents the specific regulatory mapping identifier. The `id` column serves as the primary key and corresponds to the specific risk profile being addressed, creating a direct link between the risk, the control, and the regulation.*

In practice, this means that the `AccessControl` class is formally defined as something that pertains to the regulatory mapping `Map_Art32_Access`. This abstraction allows the framework to handle complex regulatory texts by treating them as distinct, referenceable objects within the database schema.
