# Chapter 4: Ontological Mapping of Regulatory Directives and Control Frameworks

This chapter establishes the formal correspondence between General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) articles and internal control directives within the Information Compliance Environment (ICE). It delineates the structural axioms governing the classification of requirements, the specific risks they address, and the priority assignments necessary for compliance governance. The mapping is presented through a deterministic relational schema, ensuring that every regulatory citation is anchored to a specific control class and threat mitigation strategy.

## 4.1 GDPR Article Subclassifications

The foundational unit of regulatory compliance within the ICE ontology is the alignment of internal directives with specific statutory provisions. Axiomatically, a class is defined as a compliant entity only if it is a subclass of the `cco:DirectiveICE` and explicitly implements a provision from the GDPR. This relationship is not merely associative but structural; the directive inherits the compliance obligations of the article.

The verbalization of this axiom asserts that a specific class (the directive) is something that GDPR article (the statutory provision). This creates a direct lineage from the abstract legal requirement to the concrete technical control. To maintain referential integrity, the schema enforces a dependency where the regulatory article (`y`) must correspond to an existing control class defined in the threat addressing schema. This recursive validation ensures that no directive is created without a corresponding control mechanism capable of addressing the mandated risk.

## 4.2 Control Constraints and Risk Addressing

In the governance of information security, the specificity of a control is paramount. Axiom 2 defines a class of controls that address a risk *only* within the confines of a specific regulatory article. This exclusivity constraint is critical for audit trails, as it prevents "scope creep" where a generalized control might be cited as a mitigation for disparate, unrelated risks.

The verbalization states that the control class is something that addresses risk {Y} exclusively. In this context, the "risk" is the specific compliance gap defined by the GDPR article. The relational schema for this axiom (`t_control_only_addresses_threat_class`) enforces a strict circular dependency with the article subclassification table. The risk addressed (`y`) must exist as a valid entry in the `t_gdpr_article_subclass` table, ensuring that the control is strictly bound to a defined regulatory requirement. Conversely, the article subclassification table references this control table, creating a tight coupling between the mandate and the exclusive remedy.

Accordingly, a control defined under this axiom:
*   Does not provide mitigation for threats outside the scope of the referenced article;
*   Is not a "significant regulatory action" applicable to broad domains;
*   Is subject to validation only against the specific risk class identified in the mapping.

## 4.3 General Risk Addressing

While exclusive controls address specific statutory gaps, the governance framework must accommodate controls that address risks *some* of which may be regulatory in nature, or which address a subset of obligations within a broader article. Axiom 3 relaxes the exclusivity constraint, allowing for a more flexible mapping where a directive addresses a risk, but not necessarily to the exclusion of all others.

This distinction is vital for technical infrastructure controls (e.g., firewalls, access management systems) that support multiple compliance articles simultaneously. The verbalization indicates that the class is something that addresses risk {Y}. The schema (`t_control_addresses_risk`) maintains the link to the GDPR article subclassification, ensuring that while the control may be multi-purpose, its compliance relevance is explicitly anchored to the specific risk defined in the article mapping.

## 4.4 Control Subclassifications and Mitigation

The functional objective of any directive within the ICE ontology is the mitigation of identified threats. Axiom 4 formalizes the mitigation relationship, asserting that a control class is something that mitigates a specific threat class. This moves beyond simple "addressing" (which may imply acknowledgment or policy alignment) to "mitigating" (which implies active reduction of risk likelihood or impact).

The schema (`t_control_subclass`) connects the mitigation action directly to the regulatory article mapping. By referencing the `t_gdpr_article_subclass` table, the ontology asserts that the mitigation is valid only insofar as it reduces the risk associated with that specific GDPR article. This structure allows auditors to trace a technical control (e.g., an intrusion detection system) up through the risk it mitigates to the specific regulatory article it satisfies.

## 4.5 Requirement Prioritization

Resource allocation in compliance governance is driven by the priority of requirements. Axiom 5 introduces the concept of priority, asserting that a requirement class is something that has a specific priority class. This allows the framework to distinguish between "must-have" mandates and lower-order obligations, facilitating triage during implementation or incident response.

The relational schema (`t_requirement_has_priority`) links the requirement to the regulatory article mapping. This ensures that priorities are not assigned in a vacuum but are anchored to the specific risks defined by the GDPR articles. High-priority assignments typically correlate with articles concerning fundamental rights or immediate breach reporting, whereas lower priorities may be assigned to procedural documentation.
