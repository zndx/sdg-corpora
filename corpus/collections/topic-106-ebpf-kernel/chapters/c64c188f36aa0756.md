# Chapter 4: Mapping Regulatory Directives to Control Frameworks

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter establishes the formal protocols for aligning internal governance artifacts with external regulatory standards. It defines the ontological structures necessary to map organizational directives to specific control requirements within the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) and the ISO/IEC 27001 information security management framework. The objective is to ensure that every internal policy or procedural mandate (Directive) is traceable to a specific, verifiable compliance requirement (Descriptive Information Content Entity). This traceability is essential for audit readiness, gap analysis, and the maintenance of a continuous compliance posture. The following sections detail the axiomatic relationships between these entities, providing the necessary schema to instantiate these mappings within a governance, risk, and compliance (GRC) repository.

## 4.2 Descriptive Information Content Entities

### 4.2.1 Conceptual Definition

At the foundational layer of the compliance ontology lies the Descriptive Information Content Entity. Within the context of this framework, a Descriptive Information Content Entity is any atomic unit of information that describes a control objective, a requirement, or a measurement criterion without itself prescribing an action. It serves as the "what" of compliance—the static text or definition against which organizational behavior is measured. Unlike Directives, which are actionable mandates issued by the organization, Descriptive Information Content Entities represent the external standards or definitions that those Directives seek to fulfill.

Formally, we define this entity via the axiom: **{X} is a descriptive information content entity**. In this structure, {X} represents the specific textual definition of a control or requirement. This entity acts as the target for foreign key relationships from organizational directives, ensuring that internal policies are always anchored to valid, recognized external standards.

## 4.3 PCI DSS Requirement Directives

### 4.3.1 Conceptual Definition

The PCI DSS Requirement Directive represents a specific class of organizational mandate that explicitly references a requirement from the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS). This relationship is critical for organizations handling payment card data, as it provides the evidentiary link between an internal policy (e.g., "Password Management Policy") and the specific regulatory text that necessitates it (e.g., "Require all users to change their passwords...").

The axiom governing this relationship is: **{X} is something that pci dss requirement {Y}**. Here, {X} denotes the internal organizational directive (classified as a `cco:DirectiveICE`), and {Y} denotes the specific PCI DSS requirement text (a `cco:DescriptiveICE`). This relationship ensures that for every internal rule claiming PCI DSS relevance, there is a documented, auditable link to the standard.

### 4.3.2 Audit Implications and Mapping

From an audit perspective, the absence of a valid mapping in this structure constitutes a finding of "orphaned policy" or "undefined control." Auditors will verify that the {Y} value in the mapping corresponds exactly to a valid entry in the `t_measurement_basic` table. This prevents organizations from claiming compliance with vague or non-existent requirements. Furthermore, this structure facilitates automated compliance scanning by allowing GRC tools to parse the {Y} value and compare it against the latest version of the PCI DSS standard.

**Table 4.2: Mapping of Organizational Directives to PCI DSS Requirements**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| p_001 | User Credential Management Directive | m_001 |
| p_002 | Data Transmission Security Policy | m_002 |
| p_003 | Least Privilege Access Standard | m_003 |

*Table 4.2 embodies the `pci_dss_requirement` axiom. Column `x` lists the internal Directives, while column `y` references the `id` of the requirement text from Table 4.1. For instance, the "User Credential Management Directive" (p_001) is substantiated by the requirement "Require all users to change their passwords..." (m_001).*

## 4.4 ISO 27001 Annex A Subclassifications

### 4.4.1 Conceptual Definition

The ISO 27001 Annex A Subclassification extends the governance model to encompass the ISO/IEC 27001 information security standard. Specifically, this relationship maps internal directives to controls listed in Annex A of the standard. While similar to the PCI DSS mapping, this relationship is distinct in that Annex A controls are often broader and more objective-based, serving as a comprehensive suite of best practices for information security management.

The axiom for this concept is: **{X} is something that iso27001 annex a {Y}**. In this formulation, {X} is the internal directive, and {Y} is the Annex A control objective or control text. This mapping is essential for organizations seeking ISO 27001 certification, as the certification audit relies heavily on evidence that the organization's Statement of Applicability (SoA) is implemented through actual internal directives.

### 4.4.2 Governance Framework Integration

Integrating ISO 27001 Annex A controls into the ontology allows for a unified view of security governance. By treating these controls as Descriptive Information Content Entities (referenced via {Y}), the organization can perform cross-standard analysis. For example, a single internal directive regarding access control might map to both a PCI DSS requirement and an ISO 27001 Annex A control. This intersection highlights areas where multiple regulatory burdens can be satisfied through a single, cohesive control framework, thereby optimizing the compliance workload.
