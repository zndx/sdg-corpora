# Chapter 4: Ontological Mapping of Governance Artifacts and Constraint Logic

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures required to define, classify, and constrain governance artifacts within a regulated environment. It provides a rigorous examination of the axioms governing artifact composition, directive specification, and cardinality constraints. The objective is to establish a deterministic mapping between high-level semantic definitions and their relational schema implementations, ensuring data integrity for audit readiness and compliance reporting. The following sections present the foundational concepts, supported by data tables that instantiate these axioms using realistic compliance scenarios.

## 1. Artifact Composition and Part-Whole Relations

In the domain of governance records management, an entity is not merely a file; it is a composite structure that must satisfy specific existential criteria to be classified as a valid Artifact. The ontological axiom `equiv_intersect_artifact_with_part` formalizes this by asserting that a Class X is equivalent to the intersection of the `cco:Artifact` class and the condition of possessing a specific part Y. This definition ensures that an object cannot be recognized as a governed artifact unless it is materially composed of the requisite components.

This part-whole relationship, utilizing the `bfo:0000051` (has part) property, is critical for audit trails. It mandates that the integrity of the whole is dependent on the presence and validity of its parts. For instance, a "Policy Document" is only valid as an artifact if it contains "Control Clauses." The relational schema for this axiom enforces a foreign key constraint (`y`) linking the artifact to its constituent requirement, thereby preventing orphaned records and ensuring referential integrity within the compliance database.

## 2. Directive Specification and Target Application

Once the structural validity of an artifact is established via its composition, the semantic content of that artifact must be analyzed. The axiom `requirement_equiv_specifies_target` defines the nature of a Directive (Class X) as an entity that must specify a target (Class Y) and apply to a scope (Class Z). This tripartite structure is the backbone of compliance logic; a directive is inert and unenforceable unless it explicitly links a governance control to a specific operational target.

In this context, `cco:DirectiveICE` represents the information carrier entity, while the properties `sdg:specifies` and `sdg:appliesTo` create the necessary semantic bonds. The relational projection of this axiom requires that the directive (the 'part' identified in the previous section) must reference the artifact it belongs to, creating a recursive validation loop. Furthermore, the directive must identify a scope (Z), such as a specific department, data classification, or geographic region, to which the compliance obligation is bound.

## 3. Maximum Cardinality Constraints in Governance

To prevent over-provisioning of access rights or the accumulation of redundant controls, the governance framework must enforce upper bounds on entity relationships. The axiom `cardinality_max_three_generic` asserts that a Class X is a subclass of an ObjectProperty p restricted to a maximum of 3 instances of Class Y. This constraint is essential for maintaining the principle of least privilege and ensuring that governance structures do not become unwieldy or non-compliant due to excessive associations.

For example, a specific governance role may be restricted to having a maximum of three active delegation relationships. Violating this cardinality constraint—such as assigning four delegates—would render the state of the entity invalid according to the ontology. The relational schema captures this constraint definition by linking the constrained class (X) to the specific requirement (Y) that mandates the limit. This allows the compliance engine to query all entities subject to "max 3" rules and validate their current relationship counts against the defined threshold.

## 4. Minimum Cardinality Constraints and Compliance Thresholds

Conversely to maximum limits, governance frameworks frequently mandate minimum thresholds to ensure resilience, redundancy, and oversight. The axiom `cardinality_min_two_generic` stipulates that a Class X is a subclass of an ObjectProperty p restricted to a minimum of 2 instances of Class Y. This type of axiom is often utilized to enforce "four-eyes principles" (dual control) or to ensure that critical resources have at least two designated custodians.

Failure to satisfy this minimum cardinality constitutes a critical compliance deficiency. For instance, if a cryptographic key management policy requires that every key have at least two custodians, a key with only one assigned custodian would trigger an ontology-based validation error. The relational structure treats these constraints as distinct entities, linked back to the directive that authorizes them. This architecture facilitates automated reporting on "at-risk" entities that fall below the required minimum count of necessary associations.
