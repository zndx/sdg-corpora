# Chapter 4: Governance Frameworks and Compliance Verification Protocols

This chapter establishes the formal ontological structures necessary for the rigorous definition of governance, risk, and compliance (GRC) protocols within enterprise information systems. It delineates the axiomatic relationships between directive instruments, procedural executions, and responsible agents, providing a deterministic framework for auditing and attestation. The definitions herein are grounded in formal logic to ensure unambiguous interpretation across regulatory domains, specifically focusing on the intersection of process verification and directive adherence.

## 4.1 Attestation and Signatory Authority

ATTESTATION PROTOCOL 4-1

DEFINITION: ATTESTATION SIGNED BY

The concept of `AttestationSignedBy` refers to a specific class of process (bfo:0000015) characterized by the mandatory action of affixing a signature or digital endorsement by a responsible natural person (cco:Person). In accordance with governance frameworks, an attestation is not merely a record but a processual event that validates the integrity of a preceding action or state. The entity initiating the attestation is distinct from the entity signing it; however, the validity of the attestation is contingent upon the linkage between the process and the signatory.

VERBALIZATION: {X} is something that signed by person.

In practice, this axiom mandates that any class classified as an attestation must possess a property linking it to an individual. This ensures non-repudiation and assigns liability. The following table enumerates specific attestation classes utilized within the secure data handling environment, identifying the process class and the unique identifier assigned for tracking purposes.

### 4.1.1 Minimum Signatory Requirements

DEFINITION: ATTESTATION MIN ONE SIGNER

To prevent the execution of null or automated attestations without human oversight, the class `AttestationMinOneSigner` is defined as a subclass of process that imposes a cardinality constraint. Specifically, this class necessitates that the process of attestation is signed by at least one (min 1) person. This axiom enforces the "human-in-the-loop" requirement for high-compliance activities.

VERBALIZATION: {X} is a process.

This constraint is critical for maintaining the audit trail. It logically follows that if a process is defined as an attestation requiring a signature, it must satisfy the minimum cardinality of one signatory to be valid. ## 4.4 Directive Equivalence and Targeting

COMPLIANCE FRAMEWORK #09-2024

SYSTEM DIRECTIVES AND GOVERNANCE

TITLE 700 ONTOLOGICAL EQUIVALENCE AND TARGETING

CHAPTER 8 COMPLEX DIRECTIVE STRUCTURES

8-700 DEFINITIONS

REQUIREMENT EQUIVALENT SPECIFIES TARGET means a complex class definition wherein a Directive (cco:DirectiveICE) is defined as equivalent to the intersection of two conditions: the specification of a particular requirement (sdg:specifies some {Y}) and the application to a particular target scope or environment (sdg:appliesTo some {Z}). This axiom allows for the precise definition of directives that are only valid when they both specify a control and target a specific asset class.

VERBALIZATION: {X} is equivalent to a directive that specifies {Y} and applies to {Z}.

This equivalence is fundamental to the "long-tail" governance family, where directives are highly specific. The 'y' slot in this structure references the PCI DSS requirement mapping, creating a circular dependency that ensures mutual consistency: a directive must map to a requirement, and that requirement must be defined by a directive targeting a specific environment.
