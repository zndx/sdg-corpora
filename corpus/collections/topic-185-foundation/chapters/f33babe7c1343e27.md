# Chapter 7: Formal Ontology of Commerce Artifacts and Compliance Governance

This chapter establishes the ontological framework required to rigorously map commercial artifacts to their regulatory and descriptive attributes. It defines the structural relationships between physical locations, pricing mechanisms, organizational identifiers, and compliance directives within a unified information model. Furthermore, it elucidates the evidentiary role of process traces in supporting descriptive claims within an audit context, providing the necessary logical scaffolding for high-fidelity compliance reporting.

## 1. Artifact Localization and Postal Addressing

In the context of digital commerce governance, the precise identification of physical artifacts is a prerequisite for jurisdictional compliance and logistical execution. The ontology defines a specific axiom, `schemaorg_place_address`, which formalizes the relationship between a material entity and its postal designation. According to the Manchester syntax axiom `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:Artifact, sdg:hasPostalAddress some {Y:Class}`, any entity classified as an Artifact (`X`) must possess a defined relationship to a Postal Address (`Y`). This relationship is not merely associative but constitutive of the artifact's identity within the system of record.

### 1.1 The Artifact-Address Relationship

The concept of an `Artifact` within the Common Core Ontologies (CCO) refers to a material entity that is created by an agent through a process of manufacture. When this artifact is a fixed location, such as a data center, a retail outlet, or a warehouse, it requires a `PostalAddress` to be functionally operable within shipping and tax calculation subsystems. The axiom asserts that the address is a dependent entity; without the artifact, the specific instance of the address (as used in this context) lacks a referent in the domain of commerce.

The relational schema derived from this axiom enforces that every artifact entry must reference a valid postal address string. This ensures that "orphan" locations—places without valid logistical identifiers—cannot exist within the database. The primary key (`id`) serves as the unique identifier for the relationship instance, while `x` represents the Artifact and `y` represents the Postal Address class instance.

## 2. Regulatory Directives and PCI DSS Requirements

Governance frameworks rely heavily on the mapping of internal directives to external regulatory standards. The axiom `pci_dss_requirement` provides the formal logic for this mapping. Expressed in Manchester syntax as `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:pciDssRequirement some {Y:Class}`, this axiom defines that a Directive Information Content Entity (`X`) is a subclass of artifacts that specifically fulfill or reference a PCI DSS Requirement (`Y`).

### 2.1 Directive ICE and Compliance Mapping

A `DirectiveICE` (Information Content Entity) is a prescriptive document or policy artifact that dictates behavior. In the context of Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) compliance, an organization’s internal policies (e.g., "Password Rotation Policy") must be demonstrably linked to specific requirements in the regulatory standard (e.g., "Req 8.2.3"). The axiom `pci_dss_requirement` creates a foreign key relationship between the internal governance document and the external control framework.

This structure allows auditors to query the ontology for all directives that support a specific compliance requirement. For instance, to validate compliance with Requirement 3.1 ("Keep cardholder data storage to a minimum"), the system retrieves all DirectiveICE instances where `y` is `PCI_DSS_Req_3.1`. The relational schema `t_pci_dss_requirement` captures this mapping, ensuring that every internal directive is anchored to a specific regulatory clause.

## 3. Commercial Artifacts and Pricing Structures

The financial aspect of commerce artifacts is governed by the `schemaorg_product_price` axiom. The Manchester syntax `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:Artifact, sdg:hasPrice some {Y:Class}` verbalizes to: `{X} is something that has price {Y}`. This axiom treats price not as a primitive data type, but as a distinct class entity associated with a commercial artifact.

### 3.1 Price as a Dependent Entity

In this ontological model, price is an attribute that qualifies the artifact. By defining `Y` as a class rather than a simple floating-point value, the framework allows for complex price definitions, including currencies, tax-inclusive statuses, and time-based validity windows. The artifact `X` (which could be a physical good or a digital service) is the bearer of the price entity.

The relational projection `t_schemaorg_product_price` enforces the association between the product and its price. This separation of concerns allows for multiple price points to be associated with a single artifact over time (temporal pricing) without altering the core identity of the artifact itself. The primary key `id` distinguishes between different pricing instances for the same product.

## 4. Organizational Identification and Taxation

To facilitate legal and financial transparency, the ontology includes the `schemaorg_org_taxid` axiom. Formally defined as `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:Artifact, sdg:hasTaxID some {Y:Class}`, this axiom asserts that an organizational artifact (`X`) possesses a Tax ID (`Y`). This is a fundamental requirement for B2B transactions, tax reporting, and entity verification.

### 4.1 The Tax ID Property

The Tax ID serves as a unique identifier issued by a government jurisdiction. In this model, the organization is treated as an `Artifact`—a social construct created by agents. The `hasTaxID` property links this social artifact to its formal identifier. This linkage is crucial for validating the legitimacy of a vendor or customer during the onboarding process (KYC/KYB) and for generating year-end tax documentation.

The schema `t_schemaorg_org_taxid` requires that the Tax ID (`y`) be a non-null value. This constraint prevents the registration of organizational entities within the commercial system without providing the necessary fiscal identification. It supports the "single source of truth" principle by centralizing tax identification data.

## 5. Audit Trails and Evidentiary Support

The final concept, `trace_supports_claim`, addresses the epistemological foundation of auditing. The axiom `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:supportsClaim some cco:DescriptiveICE` posits that a process or independent continuant (`X`) supports a specific claim (`cco:DescriptiveICE`). This captures the logical link between evidence (the trace) and the assertion it proves.

### 5.1 Traces and Descriptive Claims

In the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO), `bfo:0000015` refers to a process. A "trace" in this context is a process that generates or preserves evidence. This evidence is used to support a "Descriptive Information Content Entity" (DescriptiveICE)—a claim about the state of the world. For example, a system log entry (the trace/process) supports the claim that "User A accessed File B at time T."

The relational schema `t_trace_supports_claim` catalogs these traces. While the schema provided is simplified (containing only `id` and `x`), in a broader implementation, `x` would function as a foreign key to the specific process instance that acts as evidence. This table serves as a registry of admissible evidence within the audit framework.
