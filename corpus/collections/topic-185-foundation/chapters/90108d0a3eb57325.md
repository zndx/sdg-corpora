# Chapter 4: Ontological Mapping of Compliance Artifacts in Enterprise Systems

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures required to map physical and informational entities within a regulated enterprise environment. It establishes the axiomatic relationships between artifacts, directives, and processes, providing a framework for automated compliance auditing and data governance. The following sections define five core concepts derived from the Common Core Ontologies (CCO) and the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO), instantiated through relational schemas that support high-fidelity traceability.

## 4.1 Geospatial Artifacts and Postal Addressing

In the context of supply chain governance, the precise definition of location is paramount. An entity is not merely a coordinate but a distinct artifact within the system of record. The axiom `schemaorg_place_address` posits that a specific class of artifact (X) necessarily possesses a postal address (Y). This relationship is foundational for Know Your Customer (KYC) protocols, vendor onboarding, and logistics routing, where the artifact represents the facility, warehouse, or legal registered office.

The ontological constraint here ensures that an artifact cannot exist within the registry without a corresponding, valid postal address string. This binding enforces data integrity at the class level, preventing the creation of "orphan" location records. In practice, this means that when a `Warehouse` class is instantiated, the system mandates the population of the `sdg:hasPostalAddress` slot with a structured string conforming to postal standards.

## 4.2 Regulatory Directives and PCI DSS Requirements

Compliance frameworks are operationalized through the binding of internal directives to external regulatory standards. The axiom `pci_dss_requirement` defines that a directive (X), classified as an Information Content Entity (ICE) under the CCO, must satisfy a specific PCI DSS requirement (Y). This mapping transforms abstract legal mandates into executable governance rules within the enterprise architecture.

Here, the `DirectiveICE` represents the internal policy document or control statement, while the `Y` slot captures the specific external requirement identifier (e.g., "Req 8.2.5"). This structure facilitates automated gap analysis; by querying the directive class, an auditor can immediately retrieve the specific PCI DSS controls it purports to satisfy. It ensures that every internal governance artifact is traceable to a regulatory source.

## 4.3 Pricing Artifacts and Financial Reporting

Financial integrity within an ERP system relies on the explicit association of product artifacts with their pricing attributes. The axiom `schemaorg_product_price` asserts that a commercial artifact (X) possesses a price (Y). In this ontology, the artifact is the SKU or product entity, while the price is a data property representing the monetary value at a given point in time.

This relationship is critical for revenue recognition and tax compliance. By treating the price as a necessary property of the artifact class, the system prevents the listing of sellable goods without a defined financial value. This axiom supports the generation of accurate invoices and facilitates the reconciliation of inventory values against general ledger accounts.

## 4.4 Organizational Identity and Tax Identification

Vendor validation and fiscal compliance require the unambiguous identification of legal entities. The axiom `schemaorg_org_taxid` stipulates that an organizational artifact (X) possesses a Tax ID (Y). This axiom treats the organization itself as an informational artifact, binding it to a government-issued identifier.

This constraint is vital for Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and tax reporting. It ensures that no counterparty can be entered into the financial system without a valid tax identifier. The relationship enforces a "single source of truth" regarding legal status, enabling the automated verification of entities against global tax databases.

## 4.5 Process Traces and Evidentiary Claims

The final concept addresses the dynamic aspect of compliance: the execution of processes that generate evidence. The axiom `trace_supports_claim` defines that a process (X), specifically a BFO process (bfo:0000015), supports a claim represented by a Descriptive Information Content Entity (ICE). This bridges the gap between operational activity (what happened) and compliance assertions (what is proven).

In this model, the process is the temporal occurrence (e.g., a nightly backup or a vulnerability scan), and the claim is the assertion that a specific control is operating effectively (e.g., "Data is encrypted"). This axiom is the mechanism for "continuous control monitoring," allowing the system to automatically validate compliance claims by checking for the existence of the supporting process trace.
