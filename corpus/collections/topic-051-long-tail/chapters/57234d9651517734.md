# Chapter 7: Governance of Digital and Physical Artifacts in Compliance Frameworks

This chapter delineates the protocols for managing artifacts and their descriptive counterparts within a unified governance framework. It establishes the axiomatic relationships between physical assets, their monetary attributes, and the information content entities (ICE) that describe them, ensuring traceability and auditability in regulated environments. The following sections provide a rigorous examination of the semantic structures required to maintain data integrity across financial, operational, and locational domains.

## 7.1 Semantic Tagging of Descriptive Information Content Entities

The classification of Information Content Entities (ICE) via topical tags serves as the primary mechanism for semantic retrieval in compliance audits. In accordance with the axiom `table_topic_tag`, any class `X` defined as a DescriptiveICE must be semantically linked to a specific topic class `Y`. This relationship ensures that unstructured or semi-structured data assets, such as audit reports or regulatory tables, are discoverable based on their subject matter rather than solely their filename or metadata headers. The assertion `{X} is something that has table topic {Y}` mandates that every data object undergoes a mandatory classification step prior to ingestion into the central repository.

For instance, a specific data table containing quarterly financial results must be explicitly tagged with the topic "Financial Audit" to distinguish it from operational logs. This tagging is not merely cosmetic but serves as a functional constraint for query engines enforcing row-level security. Without this binding, the data object remains orphaned within the semantic graph, rendering it non-compliant with data governance standards.

The primary key is the unique identifier `id`, which anchors the relationship between the specific DescriptiveICE (`x`) and its required topic (`y`). The `y` column acts as a foreign key reference to a controlled vocabulary of valid topics, ensuring that only standardized classifications are utilized.

## 7.2 Provenance Tracking and Source Attribution

Traceability is paramount in regulatory environments where the validity of a conclusion depends on the integrity of the source data. The axiom `table_provenance_source` formalizes this by requiring that any derived DescriptiveICE `X` must explicitly cite its source table `Y`. The verbalization `{X} is something that sourced from table {Y}` establishes a directed acyclic graph of data lineage, preventing the use of unverified or "black box" data in official reporting.

This axiom is particularly critical when aggregating data from disparate subsystems. For example, a "Summary of Liabilities" table generated for the Board of Education must be verifiably sourced from the "General Ledger" and "Accounts Payable" sub-ledgers. The governance framework mandates that this link be immutable; once a source is attributed, altering the source requires the creation of a new version of the derived entity to preserve the historical audit trail.

The implementation of this axiom relies on the `t_table_provenance_source` schema. The `id` serves as the primary key for the provenance record itself, while `x` represents the derived report and `y` represents the source table. This structure allows auditors to traverse backward from high-level summaries to granular transactional data instantaneously.

## 7.3 Valuation Protocols for Physical Artifacts

The assignment of monetary value to physical artifacts is governed by the `schemaorg_product_price` axiom. This principle asserts that any Artifact `X` must possess a price attribute `Y`, formalized as `{X} is something that has price {Y}`. In the context of fixed asset accounting and inventory management, this relationship is not static; it must reflect the current valuation standards, whether First-In, First-Out (FIFO), Last-In, First-Out (LIFO), or net realizable value.

The application of this axiom extends beyond simple retail pricing. In industrial automation and procurement contexts, the "price" attribute encompasses unit costs, bulk pricing tiers, and total landed costs. For example, a specific industrial component, such as a 24-inch W Touch-Screen, must be cataloged with its precise acquisition price to ensure that capital depreciation schedules are calculated correctly. The absence of a valid price attribute renders the asset financially invisible, violating compliance with Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP).

The `t_schemaorg_product_price` table below demonstrates this mapping. The `id` uniquely identifies the price entry, `x` references the specific artifact, and `y` contains the price value. This structure supports multiple price points for a single artifact over time, provided new `id` entries are generated for temporal accuracy.

## 7.4 Brand Identification and Manufacturer Attribution

Standardization of hardware and software inventory requires rigorous brand attribution as defined by the `schemaorg_product_brand` axiom. This axiom posits that an Artifact `X` is characterized by its association with a Brand `Y` (`{X} is something that has brand {Y}`). This relationship is foundational for warranty management, supply chain risk assessment, and compatibility verification.

In technical specifications, such as those for industrial automation products, the brand is often a determinant of interoperability. For instance, a processor listed as "Intel® Celeron 3765U" carries specific thermal and power requirements that differ from other manufacturers. The governance framework requires that the brand be treated as a distinct entity class, not merely a string property, to facilitate aggregation of all assets by manufacturer for bulk lifecycle management.

The `t_schemaorg_product_brand` schema enforces this relationship. The primary key `id` distinguishes each brand assignment, `x` identifies the artifact, and `y` links to the canonical brand entity. This ensures that "Intel" is referenced consistently across all inventory items, preventing discrepancies such as "Intel Corp," "Intel," or "INTC."

## 7.5 Geolocation and Postal Addressing of Artifacts

The physical deployment of assets is tracked through the `schemaorg_place_address` axiom. This axiom asserts that an Artifact `X` (or a Place acting as an artifact container) has a Postal Address `Y` (`{X} is something that has postal address {Y}`). This is critical for regulatory frameworks that govern jurisdiction, tax liability, and site-specific compliance mandates (e.g., OSHA regulations varying by state or municipality).

For example, an "Independent Auditors' Report" or a physical asset ledger associated with "Unified School District #503" in Parsons, Kansas, must be anchored to the specific postal address of that entity. This linkage allows the system to automatically apply the correct state-level regulatory codes to the assets located at that site. It facilitates granular reporting, such as "All assets located at 3500 Belmont Blvd, Parsons, KS," ensuring that site audits are scoped correctly.

The `t_schemaorg_place_address` table captures this data. The `id` provides a unique reference for the location record, `x` represents the entity or artifact being located, and `y` contains the standardized postal address string. This structure supports geospatial analysis and logistical planning by anchoring digital records to physical coordinates.
