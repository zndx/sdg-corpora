# Chapter 4: Ontological Mapping in Regulatory Compliance Reporting

This chapter delineates the formal procedures for mapping raw data assets to the Common Core Ontologies (CCO) and Schema.org (SDG) extensions within a regulated data governance environment. It provides the necessary axiomatic definitions for establishing traceability, provenance, and semantic relationships between information content entities and physical artifacts. The objective is to ensure that all tabular data, product listings, and location identifiers adhere to a strict relational schema that supports automated auditing and regulatory compliance.

## 4.1 Semantic Tagging of Descriptive Information Content Entities

In the context of data governance, a Descriptive Information Content Entity (ICE) is defined as any distinct data container—such as a spreadsheet, database view, or flat file—that encapsulates information about other entities. To ensure these entities are discoverable and classifiable within the enterprise data lake, they must be semantically tagged. This process is governed by the axiom that a Descriptive ICE is a subclass of entities that possess a specific Table Topic.

The formalization of this relationship ensures that unstructured or semi-structured data sources can be queried based on their thematic content rather than their filename or storage path. For instance, a tabular dataset containing quarterly financial figures must be explicitly linked to the concept of "Financial Reporting" to be valid for audit purposes. This mapping prevents ambiguity in data retrieval and enforces a consistent taxonomy across the organization's digital infrastructure.

The following relational schema enforces this tagging requirement. The primary key is the unique identifier for the tag instance, while the `x` column represents the specific Descriptive ICE (the table) and the `y` column represents the mandatory topic class.

## 4.2 Provenance Tracking and Lineage

Regulatory frameworks such as GDPR and SOX mandate that data consumers must be able to trace the origin of reported figures. This requirement is addressed through the provenance axiom, which asserts that a Descriptive ICE is derived from or sourced from a specific source table. This relationship establishes the lineage of data, allowing auditors to verify that a summary report is accurately compiled from authorized, raw transaction logs.

The implementation of this axiom creates a directed graph of data flow. The source table (`y`) acts as the authoritative node, while the derived table (`x`) is the dependent node. This structure is critical for impact analysis; if a schema change occurs in the source system, the provenance table allows downstream systems to immediately identify which reports require re-validation or re-generation. Without this explicit mapping, organizations risk utilizing "orphaned" data whose origin cannot be verified, leading to significant compliance liabilities.

The relational structure below captures these lineage dependencies. Each row represents a distinct sourcing event where a specific downstream entity (`x`) is populated by data from an upstream entity (`y`).

## 4.3 Artifact Pricing and Valuation

Moving from abstract information entities to tangible goods, the ontology defines Artifacts as physical or digital objects of value. In commercial and regulatory reporting, the most critical attribute of an Artifact is its Price. The axiom governing this relationship asserts that an Artifact is a subclass of entities possessing a Price attribute, typically defined using the Schema.org vocabulary.

This mapping facilitates the automated generation of pricing catalogs, tax calculations, and customs declarations. By formally linking an Artifact (e.g., a specific SKU or product model) to its Price, the system ensures that financial reporting engines utilize the correct, authorized valuation. It is imperative to note that the Price entity (`y`) is treated as a distinct class or structured value (e.g., containing currency and amount) rather than a simple string, thereby supporting multi-currency operations and historical price tracking.

## 4.4 Brand Attribution and Intellectual Property

For the purposes of supply chain governance and vendor management, every Artifact must be associated with a Brand. This axiom asserts that an Artifact has a Brand, linking the physical object to the legal entity or commercial identity responsible for its production. This relationship is essential for enforcing warranty claims, managing recall procedures, and adhering to vendor-specific compliance standards.

In a technical audit, the Brand attribute serves as a filtering criterion. For example, if a specific vendor is placed on a restricted entity list, the ontology allows the system to immediately identify all Artifacts (`x`) associated with that Brand (`y`). This mapping supports the "Know Your Vendor" (KYV) protocols required in modern procurement regulations. The Brand entity is not merely a label but a reference to a corporate identifier within the governance framework.

## 4.5 Geospatial Addressing of Artifacts

The final axiom in this chapter addresses the physical location of Artifacts. It asserts that an Artifact (specifically a Place or an object located at a place) possesses a Postal Address. This linkage is fundamental for logistics, tax jurisdiction determination, and physical asset tracking. By utilizing the Schema.org `hasPostalAddress` property, the ontology integrates physical geography with digital inventory records.

Accurate address mapping ensures that artifacts are correctly assigned to specific tax zones, shipping routes, and regulatory jurisdictions (e.g., identifying whether an artifact is located in an enterprise zone or a high-security area). The Postal Address entity (`y`) must conform to standard postal formats to ensure interoperability with external mapping and logistics services. This axiom completes the digital twin representation of the asset, defining not just what it is and what it costs, but where it physically resides.
