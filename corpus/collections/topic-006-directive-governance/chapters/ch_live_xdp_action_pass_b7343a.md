Exit codes, identifiers, roles, statuses, subjects, and targets constitute the structural vocabulary of an evidence-tracking and relationship-mapping system, each serving a distinct governance function. Identifiers provide the immutable keys that anchor every entity and association within the data fabric. Records in the XDP action pass registry carry identifiers such as PASS-0001 through PASS-0004, each bound to a specific data processing pipeline—prod-ingest-xdp, edge-telemetry-xdp, or compliance-xdp—while baseline metric definitions are keyed by METR-0001 through METR-0004, referencing baselines like Standard_Daylight_Cycle, Power_Consumption, Structural_Stress, and Steady_State_Vibration. Evidence records generated by verification activities are identified by EVID-0001 through EVID-0004, and the system uses these identifiers consistently across all tables to maintain referential integrity. The exit code field, as observed in the verification-to-evidence mapping table, assigns a numeric code to each verification outcome—values such as 568, 821, 938, and 88 appear alongside compliance audit records—providing a machine-readable signal that can be aggregated, thresholded, or correlated with downstream remediation workflows.

The subject-target relationship model forms the backbone of the system's association layer, encoding how entities relate to one another through explicitly typed links. In the XDP action pass-to-action junction table, the xdp_id column designates the subject of the relationship while x_d_p_action_id designates the target, creating directed edges that connect a pipeline pass to the action it performs. For instance, PASS-0001 serves as the subject linked to a target action, and PASS-0005 appears as a subject in two separate relationship rows, each pointing to different targets—PASS-0003 and PASS-0005 respectively. The same subject-target pattern recurs in the baseline-for-metric junction table, where baseline_id functions as the subject and baseline_for_id as the target, establishing which metric a given baseline is intended to govern. This bidirectional clarity—knowing which end of a relationship is the subject and which is the target—enables precise traversal queries and unambiguous audit trails.

Roles overlay a semantic layer onto the subject-target relationships, specifying the nature of each entity's participation within a given association. The role column in both junction tables takes values from a constrained vocabulary: observer, owner, and contributor. In the XDP action pass context, a pass may hold the owner role relative to an action it executes, assume the observer role when passively monitoring an action, or serve as a contributor when it supplies data or configuration to the action's execution. Similarly, in the baseline-for-metric context, a baseline can be the owner of a metric it defines, a contributor to a metric it partially informs, or an observer of a metric it tracks without direct control. These role distinctions are critical for access control, responsibility attribution, and impact analysis—knowing that a baseline holds the contributor role to a particular metric, for example, signals a dependency that is weaker than ownership but stronger than passive observation.

The status field captures the lifecycle state of verification and evidence records, providing a high-level indicator of operational health. Within the verification-to-evidence table, statuses of complete, failed, and pending are assigned to evidence records produced by verification activities such as Compliance audit Q3. A status of complete indicates that the verification has concluded successfully and the associated evidence—such as PASS-0001 or PASS-0003—is available for downstream consumption. A status of failed signals that the verification did not meet its success criteria, as seen with evidence records PASS-0001 and PASS-0005, each carrying distinct exit codes (821 and 938 respectively) that may encode the nature of the failure. A status of pending denotes evidence that has been generated but not yet finalized, as with PASS-0003 under exit code 88, suggesting that the verification process is still in progress or awaiting review.

Together, these six concepts—exitcode, identifier, role, status, subject, and target—form a cohesive governance framework that supports traceability, accountability, and operational awareness. Identifiers ensure that every entity and relationship can be uniquely referenced; subject-target pairs define the topology of associations; roles clarify the capacity in which entities participate; status provides a lifecycle signal; and exit codes deliver granular outcome information. The system's design, as evidenced by the consistent use of these fields across the XDP action pass registry, the baseline-for-metric mappings, and the verification-to-evidence linkage, reflects a deliberate architecture for managing complex dependencies and audit requirements in a data-intensive environment.
