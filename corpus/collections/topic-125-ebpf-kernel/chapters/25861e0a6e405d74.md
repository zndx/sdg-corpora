# Chapter 5: Relational Mapping of Artifacts, Processes, and Spatial Attributes in Governance Frameworks

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures required to establish a compliant governance framework for physical asset management. It provides a rigorous examination of three core axioms derived from the Common Core Ontologies (CCO) and the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO), specifically addressing the intersection of artifact participation, spatial location, and custodial ownership. The objective is to define a data architecture that supports high-fidelity audit trails, ensuring that every physical entity within the registry is anchored to a verifiable process and a responsible agent. The following sections detail the implementation of these axioms within a relational database management system (RDBMS), adhering to strict foreign key constraints to maintain referential integrity and evidentiary continuity.

## 1. The Observation Process and Artifact Participation

In the context of regulatory compliance and asset governance, the fundamental unit of auditability is the *observation*. An observation is not merely a passive recording but an active process (BFO:0000015) that necessitates the participation of one or more artifacts. Axiom 2 establishes the critical relationship between a temporal process and the material entities involved therein. Formally, the axiom states that an observation (Class X) is a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process) and bears the `bfo:0000057` (has participant) relationship to some instance of `cco:Artifact`.

This distinction is vital for governance frameworks because it decouples the *event* of recording from the *object* being recorded. By treating the observation as a distinct process, the system can log metadata regarding the audit event—such as the timestamp, the auditor, and the jurisdictional scope—without conflating it with the intrinsic properties of the artifact itself. The relational projection of this axiom creates a primary registry of all observation events. Every subsequent claim about location or ownership must eventually reference an entry in this registry, ensuring that no attribute exists in a vacuum; every attribute must be the result of a specific, recorded process.

The following table embodies this axiom, listing the unique identifiers for various observation processes and the specific artifacts participating in those processes. The primary key (`id`) serves as the authoritative reference for all dependent tables.

The `id` column provides the stable identifier used to enforce foreign key constraints in downstream relational mappings, ensuring that every spatial or custodial claim is traceable to a specific observation event.

## 2. Spatial Anchoring of Artifacts via Postal Addresses

Once the observation process is formalized, the governance framework must address the spatial dimension of the artifacts. Axiom 1 defines the constraint that an artifact (Class X) is a subclass of `cco:Artifact` and possesses a `sdg:hasPostalAddress` relationship with a second entity (Class Y). In a strict ontological sense, this asserts that physical entities cannot exist within the governance domain without a defined, mappable location.

The implementation of this axiom within the relational schema introduces a specific structural requirement: the address entity (Y) is not stored as a simple string literal but is referenced as a foreign key pointing to the Observation table. This design choice reflects the "evidence-anchored" nature of the framework. An address is not merely a label; it is a datum that was established, verified, or recorded during a specific observation process. By linking the address to the observation table, the system enforces the provenance of the location data. If an observation is invalidated or retracted, the associated address linkage is automatically flagged by the referential integrity constraints of the database.

The table below illustrates this mapping. It connects specific artifacts to their respective postal addresses, where the address itself is identified by the `id` of the observation that validated it. This structure allows for complex historical tracking, such as logging multiple addresses for a single artifact over time, provided each address is associated with a distinct observation event.

**Table 2-1: Spatial Mapping of Artifacts to Validated Addresses**

| id | x | y |
|---|---|---|
| `loc_link_001` | `ServerRack_Alpha_A01` | `obs_reg_2024_001` |
| `loc_link_002` | `Vehicle_Fleet_Beta` | `obs_reg_2024_002` |
| `loc_link_003` | `Storage_Unit_Gamma` | `obs_audit_2023_089` |

*Table 2-1 Notes:* Column `x` denotes the artifact being located. Column `y` is a foreign key referencing `t_observation_with_participant(id)`, representing the specific observation record that contains or validates the postal address data for that artifact.

## 3. Custodial Requirements and Minimum Ownership Constraints

The final component of the governance triad concerns the assignment of responsibility and liability. Axiom 3 stipulates that an artifact (Class X) is a subclass of `cco:Artifact` and must have a minimum of one owner (Class Y), defined by the `sdg:hasOwner` property. The "min 1" cardinality constraint is a critical compliance rule; it prohibits the existence of "orphaned" assets within the system. Every tracked entity must be linked to a legal or functional agent responsible for its custody, maintenance, and eventual decommissioning.

Similar to the spatial mapping, the relational schema for ownership treats the owner entity (Y) as a dependent of the observation table. This implies that the "Owner" is not a static string but a role or agent definition established within the context of a specific observation or registration process. This approach accommodates dynamic organizational structures where ownership may transfer between departments or external contractors. By referencing the observation ID, the system captures not just *who* owns the asset, but *when* and *via which process* that ownership was established.

The table below demonstrates the enforcement of this axiom. It lists artifacts and their corresponding owners, with the owner field strictly referencing the observation registry. This ensures that the claim of ownership is always auditable and temporally anchored to the moment the custodial relationship was recorded.
