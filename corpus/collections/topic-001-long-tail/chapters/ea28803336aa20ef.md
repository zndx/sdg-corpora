# Chapter 7: Governance Frameworks for Verification, Audit, and Attestation Protocols

This chapter delineates the foundational ontological structures governing audit and verification protocols within the enterprise governance framework. It establishes the necessary conditions for process classification, evidence generation, and signatory authorization pursuant to the Directive Governance Family (03_directive_governance). The definitions provided herein are binding for all subsequent compliance reporting, financial statement generation, and regulatory audit activities. Adherence to these axioms ensures traceability, non-repudiation, and semantic consistency across all control layers.

## 1. Basic Verification Processes

### 1.1 Definition and Scope
Under the governance framework, a verification process is formally defined as a specific subclass of a `bfo:Process`. This classification (template_id: `verification_basic`) serves as the ontological root for all activities that confirm the validity of a state or assertion without necessarily producing a distinct evidentiary artifact as a primary output. While distinct from full audits, basic verification processes constitute the operational unit of compliance.

The axiom asserts that any entity classified as a verification process is inherently a process occurring over time. This temporal distinction is critical for governance reporting, as it separates static data entities from dynamic control activities. ## 2. Evidence Production in Verification

### 2.1 The Evidence Imperative
While basic verification defines the activity, the regulatory framework mandates that material verification processes must result in tangible outputs. This requirement is codified in the `verification_min_one_evidence` axiom. This constraint elevates a standard process to a verified event by requiring the production of at least one evidence object (`min 1 {Y:Class}`).

In this context, "evidence" refers to any persistable record—digital or physical—that substantiates the execution of the verification. The relationship between the process (`X`) and the evidence (`Y`) is deterministic; the process must be capable of generating the evidence class as a defined output. This structure ensures that for every compliance claim, there exists a retrievable artifact anchoring the claim to reality.

## 3. Audit Classification

### 3.1 Distinction from Verification
Audits are distinct from routine verification processes in terms of scope, independence, and objective. While verification is often operational and continuous, auditing is a periodic, systematic review. Ontologically, however, an audit remains a `bfo:Process`. The `audit_basic` axiom establishes this classification, ensuring that all audit activities inherit the temporal and procedural properties of the parent Process class.

This classification is critical for resource allocation and scheduling within the governance framework. By defining audits as a specific class of process, the framework can apply specific retention policies, access controls, and workflow rules that differ from those applied to standard verifications.

## 4. Attestation Mechanisms

### 4.1 Definition of Attestation
Attestation is the formal act of signing or validating a document or process state. Within the ontology, attestation is modeled as a process (`attestation_basic`) that culminates in a declaration of truth or validity. Unlike verification, which may be automated, attestation implies a cognitive validation step, often linked to professional or legal liability.

The `attestation_basic` axiom classifies these events as processes. This allows the governance framework to track the lifecycle of an attestation—from initiation to completion—just as it would any other operational process. This is particularly relevant for tracking the status of outstanding sign-offs for regulatory filings.

## 5. Signatory Requirements for Attestation

### 5.1 The Agency Constraint
A critical constraint within the governance framework is the requirement that attestation processes must have an agent. The `attestation_min_one_signer` axiom stipulates that any attestation process (`X`) must be signed by at least one Person (`cco:Person`). This enforces the principle of agency; a process cannot attest to its own validity, nor can a purely automated system execute a legal attestation without human intervention.

This constraint is the mechanism by which the framework enforces accountability. By linking the attestation process to the requirement for a signer, the ontology ensures that there is always a natural person associated with the validity of the attested state. This is the foundation for non-repudiation in the compliance ecosystem.
