# Chapter 4: Ontological Structures in Regulatory Compliance and Evidence Management

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the formal ontological structures underpinning modern regulatory compliance frameworks, specifically focusing on the intersection of General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) mandates, jurisdictional policies, and evidence-based attestations. It provides a rigorous analysis of the relational mappings required to validate compliance controls within a governed information environment. The content herein is intended for compliance officers, data governors, and audit practitioners seeking to implement a verifiable chain of custody for legal bases and supporting evidence. The definitions provided are derived from the upper-level ontology of Directive Governance and are projected into relational schemas to facilitate database implementation and automated auditing. Each section introduces a core axiom, verbalizes its semantic intent, and provides the corresponding data table populated with representative instances from a hypothetical multinational compliance audit.

## 4.2 The GDPR Lawful Basis Axiom

### 4.2.1 Conceptual Definition

The foundation of any data processing activity within the scope of the GDPR is the establishment of a lawful basis. Formally, this is expressed through the axiom `gdpr_lawful_basis`. In the Manchester syntax, this is defined as `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:hasLawfulBasis some {Y:Class}`. Verbalized, this asserts that a specific directive or information control entity ({X}) is a type of Directive that possesses a lawful basis ({Y}).

In practice, this axiom enforces the constraint that no data processing directive may exist in a compliant state unless it is explicitly linked to a valid legal justification. These justifications, such as Contract, Legal Obligation, or Consent, are not merely metadata tags but are distinct classes within the ontology that must be instantiated and referenced. This structure ensures that the "lawfulness" of a processing activity is an object of the governance model, subject to querying and validation rather than a static, unverified text field.

### 4.2.2 Relational Projection and Data

The relational schema for this axiom is embodied in the table `t_gdpr_lawful_basis`. The primary key is the unique identifier (`id`) of the relationship instance. The column `x` represents the Directive (the processing activity or policy), while `y` represents the Lawful Basis class. A critical referential integrity constraint is enforced: the Lawful Basis (`y`) must exist as a primary key within the `t_policy_with_jurisdiction` table. This circular dependency (where a basis is also treated as a policy with jurisdiction) reflects the complex regulatory reality where legal bases often have their own territorial scope and applicability definitions.

The following table illustrates instances where specific data processing directives are anchored to their respective lawful bases.

## 4.3 Policy with Jurisdiction

### 4.3.1 Conceptual Definition

While the lawful basis provides the legal *why*, the jurisdiction provides the legal *where*. The axiom `policy_with_jurisdiction` is defined as `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:hasJurisdiction some {Y:Class}`. This asserts that a Directive ({X}) has a Jurisdiction ({Y}). Jurisdiction in this context is not merely a geographic boundary but a regulatory class defining the scope of authority and the specific legal framework (e.g., GDPR, CCPA) that applies to the directive.

This axiom is essential for multi-national entities that must navigate a patchwork of regulatory requirements. It allows the governance framework to segment controls based on the applicable legal regime. For instance, a directive processing employee data may have different jurisdictional constraints depending on whether the employee is located in the European Union or the United States. The ontology treats Jurisdiction as a first-class object, allowing for hierarchical definitions (e.g., "Global" encompassing "EU" and "US").

### 4.3.2 Relational Projection and Data

The schema `t_policy_with_jurisdiction` captures these relationships. The column `x` holds the Directive, and `y` holds the Jurisdiction class. To maintain the integrity of the regulatory graph, the schema dictates that the Jurisdiction (`y`) must reference a valid entry in the `t_gdpr_lawful_basis` table. This constraint implies that a jurisdictional scope is, in itself, defined or validated by a lawful basis relationship, creating a tightly coupled loop of legal validity.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

*Note: The `y` column values (e.g., LB_REL_001) correspond to the identifiers of the lawful basis records, establishing the required foreign key constraint. This linkage ensures that the jurisdiction is not defined in a vacuum but is intrinsically linked to the legal basis that validates it.*

## 4.4 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

### 4.4.1 Conceptual Definition

Compliance is not merely theoretical; it requires demonstrable proof. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` addresses this requirement. Defined as `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:withSupportingEvidence some {Y:Class}`, it asserts that a Process ({X})—specifically an attestation process—is accompanied by Supporting Evidence ({Y}).

In the context of an audit, an attestation is a formal declaration that a control is operating effectively. This axiom mandates that such a declaration cannot exist without an evidentiary anchor. The evidence could be a log file, a system configuration screenshot, a signed contract, or a review report. By linking the attestation process directly to the evidence class via the ontology, the framework automates the evidence collection chain, ensuring that every compliance claim is backed by retrievable, auditable artifacts.

### 4.4.2 Relational Projection and Data

The table `t_attestation_with_supporting_evidence` implements this structure. The `x` column identifies the specific attestation process instance, while the `y` column identifies the evidence class. The schema enforces a foreign key constraint linking the evidence (`y`) to the `t_gdpr_lawful_basis` table. This design choice suggests that the "supporting evidence" is often the documentation or realization of the lawful basis itself (e.g., the signed consent form serves as the evidence for the "Consent" basis).

## 4.5 Control with Evidence Requirement

### 4.5.1 Conceptual Definition

Proactive governance involves defining what evidence must be produced *before* an attestation can even occur. This is governed by the axiom `control_with_evidence_requirement`, expressed as `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:requiresEvidence some {Y:Class}`. This asserts that a Control ({X})—a directive or automated rule—requires Evidence ({Y}) to function or to be verified.

This axiom shifts the focus from the retrospective (attestation) to the prospective (control design). It answers the question: "What artifacts must this system generate to be considered compliant?" By defining evidence requirements at the control level, the organization ensures that logging, monitoring, and documentation capabilities are built into the system architecture (Privacy by Design). It transforms evidence from an afterthought into a functional requirement of the control itself.

### 4.5.2 Relational Projection and Data

The `t_control_with_evidence_requirement` table maps controls to their required evidence. The `x` column represents the Control Directive, and the `y` column represents the type of evidence required. Consistent with the other tables in this chapter, the evidence (`y`) is validated against the `t_gdpr_lawful_basis` table. This reinforces the concept that the evidence required by a control is fundamentally tied to the legal basis the control is designed to protect.

## 4.6 Attestation Signed By

### 4.6.1 Conceptual Definition

The final link in the compliance chain is the authorization of the attestation. The axiom `attestation_signed_by` is defined as `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:signedBy some cco:Person`. This asserts that an Attestation Process ({X}) must be signed by a Person.

This axiom introduces the principle of individual accountability. While systems generate logs and controls produce evidence, a valid attestation requires human intervention—a conscious verification by a qualified natural person. This prevents the "compliance by automation" fallacy where compliance is assumed solely because a system is running. It mandates that a responsible agent reviews the evidence and the control status and formally signs off on the attestation.

### 4.6.2 Relational Projection and Data

The table `t_attestation_signed_by` is a registry of attestations that have completed the signing protocol. The schema includes the `id` and the attestation process `x`. Notably, the schema provided does not include a column for the signer's identity (`y`), implying that the existence of a row in this table serves as a boolean flag or a state marker indicating that the signing event has occurred for the attestation instance `x`. The detailed identity of the signer would typically be managed in an auxiliary personnel table or an audit log not depicted in this specific schema projection.
