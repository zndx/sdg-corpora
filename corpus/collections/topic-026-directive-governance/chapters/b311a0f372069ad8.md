# Chapter 4: Ontological Mapping of Compliance Directives and Evidence Chains

This chapter delineates the formalization of governance frameworks through the lens of a specific compliance ontology. It bridges the gap between abstract regulatory requirements and concrete relational data structures, providing a rigorous method for capturing the semantics of lawful bases, jurisdictional scopes, and evidence chains. The discussion focuses on the translation of OWL axioms into database schemas, ensuring that audit trails maintain referential integrity and semantic precision. By grounding these concepts in the `cco` (Common Core Ontologies) and `bfo` (Basic Formal Ontology) upper-level structures, we establish a robust architecture for managing compliance artifacts within enterprise systems.

## 4.1 GDPR Lawful Basis

The foundation of any data governance architecture operating within the European regulatory sphere is the concept of the "Lawful Basis." Formally, this is defined by the axiom `gdpr_lawful_basis`, which asserts that a specific directive or processing activity (Class X) is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` and must possess a lawful basis (Class Y). This relationship is not merely associative but foundational; without a valid lawful basis, the directive itself is ontologically invalid in the context of the regulation.

In practical terms, this axiom mandates that for every instance of a data processing directive, the system must record a specific legal justification—such as Contract, Legal Obligation, or Consent. The relational projection of this axiom creates a dependency where the lawful basis itself is treated as a distinct entity, often linked to broader policy definitions.

The following table embodies the `gdpr_lawful_basis` axiom. The primary key is the unique identifier for the basis assertion (`id`), while the column `y` represents the lawful basis class. The schema enforces a foreign key constraint linking the lawful basis (`y`) to a policy with jurisdiction, ensuring that the legal grounding is itself tied to a specific governance regime.

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| LB_EU_001 | CustomerDataProcessingDirective | POL_EU_GDPR_2024 |
| LB_EU_002 | EmployeeMonitoringDirective | POL_EU_GDPR_2024 |
| LB_US_001 | CrossBorderTransferDirective | POL_US_CCPA_2023 |
| LB_GLOBAL_001 | IncidentResponseDirective | POL_GLOBAL_ISO27001 |

*Table 4.1: Implementation of the `gdpr_lawful_basis` axiom, linking specific directives (x) to their required lawful bases (y).*

## 4.2 Policy with Jurisdiction

While the lawful basis provides the legal grounding, the `policy_with_jurisdiction` axiom defines the spatial and regulatory scope of the governance artifact. This axiom asserts that a directive (Class X) has a jurisdiction (Class Y). In the context of international compliance, distinguishing between the directive itself and the jurisdiction it governs is critical for avoiding regulatory creep and ensuring that data handling procedures align with local laws.

The relational schema for this axiom presents a recursive structure often found in complex governance models. Here, the jurisdiction (`y`) is not a simple string literal but a reference to a `gdpr_lawful_basis` identifier. This design choice implies that the jurisdiction is defined or activated by the specific lawful basis applicable to that region. For instance, the "EU" jurisdiction might be formally defined in the system by the specific GDPR lawful basis it enforces.

The table below illustrates the `policy_with_jurisdiction` axiom. The primary key (`id`) represents the unique policy instance, while the foreign key (`y`) references the `t_gdpr_lawful_basis` table. This structure ensures that a jurisdiction cannot exist in isolation; it must be anchored to a valid lawful basis claim.

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| POL_EU_GDPR_2024 | EUDataRetentionPolicy | LB_EU_001 |
| POL_EU_GDPR_2024 | EUDataRetentionPolicy | LB_EU_002 |
| POL_US_CCPA_2023 | USConsumerPrivacyPolicy | LB_US_001 |
| POL_GLOBAL_ISO27001 | GlobalSecurityPolicy | LB_GLOBAL_001 |

*Table 4.2: Implementation of the `policy_with_jurisdiction` axiom, mapping policies (x) to their defined jurisdictions (y), which are resolved via lawful basis identifiers.*

## 4.3 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

Moving from static directives to dynamic processes, the `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` axiom addresses the verification of compliance. Attestation is a process (subclass of `bfo:0000015`, a Process Entity) wherein an auditor or responsible party validates that controls are operating effectively. This axiom asserts that an attestation (Class X) must be accompanied by supporting evidence (Class Y).

In audit methodologies, the "assertion" of compliance is meaningless without "evidence." This axiom formalizes that relationship by treating evidence as a mandatory object property of the attestation process. The relational schema links the attestation record directly to the evidence artifact, which, in this specific ontology derivation, is modeled as a reference to a lawful basis record. This suggests that the ultimate evidence for a compliance attestation is the demonstration that a valid lawful basis exists and is maintained.

The table `t_attestation_with_supporting_evidence` captures this relationship. The foreign key constraint ensures that the evidence (`y`) cited in the attestation corresponds to a valid entry in the `t_gdpr_lawful_basis` table.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ATT_Q3_2023_01 | Q3_Audit_Process | LB_EU_001 |
| ATT_Q3_2023_02 | HR_Monitoring_Audit | LB_EU_002 |
| ATT_Q4_2023_01 | Data_Transfer_Review | LB_US_001 |
| ATT_ANNUAL_01 | ISO_Certification_Audit | LB_GLOBAL_001 |

*Table 4.3: Implementation of the `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` axiom, linking specific attestation processes (x) to the supporting evidence records (y) derived from lawful bases.*

## 4.4 Control with Evidence Requirement

The `control_with_evidence_requirement` axiom shifts the focus to the design of the governance framework itself. It asserts that a directive or control (Class X) requires evidence (Class Y). This is distinct from the *attestation* of evidence; rather, this is a prescriptive rule defining the *metadata* of the control. It answers the question: "What artifacts must this control produce to be considered effective?"

By modeling this as a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE`, the ontology treats the requirement for evidence as a directive in its own right. The relational projection links the control definition to the type of evidence it requires. As with the attestation model, the evidence requirement is resolved against the lawful basis table, reinforcing the concept that the primary artifact of compliance in this framework is the lawful basis record.

The table below demonstrates this dependency. Each control (`x`) is associated with a specific evidence requirement (`y`), ensuring that control implementations are auditable against a predefined standard of proof.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| CTRL_ACC_01 | AccessControlDirective | LB_EU_001 |
| CTRL_HR_01 | DPA_Assessment_Directive | LB_EU_002 |
| CTRL_XFR_01 | DataTransferProtocol | LB_US_001 |
| CTRL_SEC_01 | EvidenceRetentionPolicy | LB_GLOBAL_001 |

*Table 4.4: Implementation of the `control_with_evidence_requirement` axiom, defining the evidentiary prerequisites (y) for various governance controls (x).*

## 4.5 Attestation Signed By

The final concept in this chapter addresses the accountability of the attestation process. The `attestation_signed_by` axiom asserts that an attestation process (Class X) must be signed by a `cco:Person`. This introduces the agentive element into the compliance model, ensuring that processes are not merely automated events but are formally authorized by human agents with responsibility.

In the relational schema, this is represented as a direct link between the attestation instance and the person entity. While the schema provided does not include a separate table for persons (treating `signedBy` as a property of the attestation table), the axiom implies the existence of a `cco:Person` class within the broader ontology. This constraint is vital for non-repudiation, creating a cryptographic or procedural chain of custody for the attestation.

The table `t_attestation_signed_by` lists the attestation instances alongside the identifiers of the signatories. This structure allows for rapid querying of all attestations for which a specific individual is responsible, a key requirement during regulatory investigations or internal reviews.

| :--- | :--- |
| SIG_J_DOE | ATT_Q3_2023_01 |
| SIG_J_SMITH | ATT_Q3_2023_02 |
| SIG_M_ROE | ATT_Q4_2023_01 |

*Table 4.5: Implementation of the `attestation_signed_by` axiom, associating attestation process instances (x) with the responsible signatories.*
