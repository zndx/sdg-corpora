# Chapter 7: Regulatory Compliance and Evidence Governance Frameworks

## 7.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the structural dependencies between regulatory directives, jurisdictional constraints, and evidentiary artifacts within a compliant governance framework. It establishes the ontological relationships required to validate data processing activities against legal standards, specifically focusing on the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) as a primary use case. The following sections provide a formal analysis of the axiomatically defined concepts governing lawful bases, policy jurisdictions, attestation protocols, and control mechanisms. The objective is to define a rigorous schema for mapping compliance obligations to executable technical controls and documented evidence, ensuring auditability and regulatory adherence.

## 7.2 GDPR Lawful Basis

The foundational axiom of any data processing architecture within the European regulatory sphere is the establishment of a lawful basis. Formally defined by the axiom `gdpr_lawful_basis`, this concept asserts that a processing class (`X`) is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` and must possess a lawful basis (`Y`). This relationship is not merely semantic but serves as the primary constraint for data legitimacy. In practice, this means that no data processing operation may exist in the system state without a valid, referenced legal justification, such as Contractual Necessity, Legal Obligation, or Consent.

The relational projection of this axiom creates a dependency where the lawful basis itself must reference a valid policy jurisdiction. This circular dependency enforces that lawful bases are not abstract concepts but are grounded in specific, enforceable policy domains. The table below illustrates the mapping of specific processing activities to their requisite lawful bases, which in turn validate against jurisdictional policies.

**Table 7.1: Mapping of Processing Activities to Lawful Bases (t_gdpr_lawful_basis)**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| LB_CONTRACT_01 | CustomerOrderProcessing | POL_EU_COMMERCE |
| LB_LEG_OBL_02 | EmployeeTaxReporting | POL_FISCAL_DOMESTIC |
| LB_CONSENT_03 | DirectMarketingCampaigns | POL_PRIVACY_GLOBAL |
| LB_VITAL_04 | EmergencyMedicalServices | POL_HEALTH_REGIONAL |
| LB_LEG_05 | LitigationHoldPreservation | POL_LEGAL_CORPORATE |

In the context of Table 7.1, the entity `CustomerOrderProcessing` (x) is validated by the lawful basis `LB_CONTRACT_01`. This basis derives its authority from `POL_EU_COMMERCE` (y), which represents the governing policy jurisdiction. This structure ensures that if the jurisdictional policy is revoked or modified, the lawful basis—and consequently the processing activity—must be re-evaluated for validity.

## 7.3 Policy with Jurisdiction

While the lawful basis provides the legal justification for processing, the `policy_with_jurisdiction` axiom defines the territorial and functional scope of the governance framework. This axiom asserts that a directive (`X`) has a jurisdiction (`Y`). The jurisdiction acts as the boundary condition for the policy, defining the legal geography (e.g., EEA, California, Global) and the regulatory regime to which the organization is subject.

The relational schema for this axiom requires that the jurisdiction (`y`) reference a valid entry in the lawful basis table. This recursive structure implies that jurisdictions are activated or defined by the presence of specific lawful obligations. In a governance framework, this prevents the creation of "orphan" jurisdictions that have no bearing on actual data processing activities. It enforces a strict mapping where a jurisdiction exists in the system only insofar as it underpins a lawful basis for a specific directive.

**Table 7.2: Jurisdictional Scope of Governance Policies (t_policy_with_jurisdiction)**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| POL_EU_COMMERCE | E-CommerceDataDirective | LB_CONTRACT_01 |
| POL_FISCAL_DOMESTIC | DomesticTaxCompliancePolicy | LB_LEG_OBL_02 |
| POL_PRIVACY_GLOBAL | GlobalPrivacyNoticeFramework | LB_CONSENT_03 |
| POL_HEALTH_REGIONAL | RegionalHealthDataProtocol | LB_VITAL_04 |
| POL_LEGAL_CORPORATE | CorporateLegalHoldDirective | LB_LEG_05 |

As shown in Table 7.2, the `E-CommerceDataDirective` (x) asserts its jurisdiction over the `LB_CONTRACT_01` (y) entity. This confirms that the directive's authority is specifically anchored to the lawful basis of contract fulfillment. This granular mapping allows compliance officers to query the system for all directives active within a specific jurisdiction or to trace the lineage of a specific data processing event back to the governing policy.

## 7.4 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

To operationalize compliance, organizations must demonstrate that controls are operating effectively. The `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` axiom addresses this by defining a process (`X`) that is substantiated by supporting evidence (`Y`). Here, `X` represents a specific attestation event (e.g., a quarterly audit sign-off or a continuous control monitoring result), which is a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (a Process). The evidence (`Y`) provides the empirical proof required to satisfy the assertion.

In the relational model, the evidence component (`y`) must reference a lawful basis. This constraint ensures that attestations are not merely generic assertions of "good behavior" but are directly tied to the specific legal requirements defined in Section 7.2. An attestation regarding "Contractual Necessity" must be supported by evidence explicitly linked to that lawful basis, thereby closing the loop between legal theory and operational reality.

**Table 7.3: Attestation Log and Evidence Linkage (t_attestation_with_supporting_evidence)**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ATT_Q3_2023_01 | Q3_Compliance_Audit_Review | LB_CONTRACT_01 |
| ATT_TAX_2023_04 | Annual_Tax_Certification | LB_LEG_OBL_02 |
| ATT_MKTG_0024 | Marketing_Consent_Audit | LB_CONSENT_03 |
| ATT_HLTH_99 | Emergency_Response_Drill_Log | LB_VITAL_04 |
| ATT_LEGAL_DISC | Discovery_Response_Attestation | LB_LEG_05 |

Table 7.3 demonstrates that the `Q3_Compliance_Audit_Review` (x) is substantiated by evidence tied to `LB_CONTRACT_01` (y). This implies that the audit artifacts (logs, screenshots, configuration exports) contained within the evidence package specifically validate the existence and execution of the contractual lawful basis. This linkage is critical for audit readiness, as it allows automated tools to retrieve the exact evidence packet supporting a specific compliance claim.

## 7.5 Control with Evidence Requirement

Complementary to attestation is the definition of the controls themselves. The `control_with_evidence_requirement` axiom stipulates that a directive (`X`) requires evidence (`Y`). This is distinct from the attestation process; rather, it defines the design of the control. A control is not merely a procedure but a directive that mandates the collection of specific artifacts.

This axiom enforces a "privacy by design" approach at the schema level. When a control is instantiated (e.g., "Access Logging"), the system must identify the type of evidence it generates (e.g., "Access Logs"). The schema requires that this evidence type (`y`) be mapped to a lawful basis, ensuring that every control in existence serves a specific regulatory purpose and generates the necessary proof of compliance.

**Table 7.4: Control Directives and Evidence Specifications (t_control_with_evidence_requirement)**

| id | x | y |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| CTRL_ACCESS_01 | User_Access_Logging_Control | LB_CONTRACT_01 |
| CTRL_RETENTION_02 | Data_Retention_Schedule_Control | LB_LEG_OBL_02 |
| CTRL_PREF_03 | Consent_Preference_Management | LB_CONSENT_03 |
| CTRL_ER_04 | Emergency_Rights_Access_Control | LB_VITAL_04 |
| CTRL_LEGAL_05 | Legal_Hold_Identification_Control | LB_LEG_05 |

In Table 7.4, the `User_Access_Logging_Control` (x) is defined as requiring evidence associated with `LB_CONTRACT_01` (y). This ensures that the logs generated by this control are recognized as valid evidence for contract-based processing. If a lawful basis were to expire, the system could flag all associated controls as requiring deactivation or reconfiguration, thereby automating the compliance lifecycle management.

## 7.6 Attestation Signed By

Finally, the accountability framework is completed by the `attestation_signed_by` axiom. This axiom asserts that an attestation process (`X`), a subclass of `bfo:0000015`, must be signed by a person (`cco:Person`). This introduces the element of human agency and responsibility into the automated governance framework. While systems can collect evidence and execute controls, the formal attestation of compliance requires the authorization of a qualified individual.

This relational structure is simpler, serving as a junction table between the attestation event and the responsible party. It ensures that every attestation logged in the system is associated with a unique identifier for the signing authority, creating an immutable chain of custody for compliance assertions.

**Table 7.5: Attestation Signatory Registry (t_attestation_signed_by)**

| :--- | :--- |

Table 7.5 identifies the specific individuals responsible for the attestations listed in Table 7.3. For instance, `ATT_Q3_2023_01` is signed by `DPO_Jane_Smith_8821`. This mapping allows the organization to query all attestations signed by a specific officer, a requirement often necessary during internal investigations or regulatory inquiries. It ties the process-oriented view of compliance directly to the human governance structure.
