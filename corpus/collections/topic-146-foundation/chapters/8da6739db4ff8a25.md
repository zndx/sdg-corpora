# Chapter 7: Artifact Taxonomy and Brand Attribution in Enterprise Asset Management

## 7.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the formal taxonomic structures required for the accurate classification, governance, and auditing of physical and digital assets within a regulated enterprise environment. It establishes the necessary ontological frameworks to ensure that hardware and software inventories are not merely descriptive lists but semantically rich data structures capable of supporting automated compliance checks and forensic analysis. The guidelines herein are mandated for use by internal audit committees, IT governance bodies, and compliance officers tasked with maintaining the integrity of the Configuration Management Database (CMDB). Adherence to these protocols ensures that asset attribution—specifically the relationship between an artifact and its originating commercial brand—is captured with sufficient granularity to satisfy regulatory standards such as ISO/IEC 27001 and the Sarbanes-Oxley Act.

## 7.2 The Ontological Foundation of the Artifact

In the context of information systems governance, the term "artifact" is frequently applied with insufficient rigor, leading to ambiguity in asset registers and depreciation schedules. To mitigate this risk, this framework adopts the Common Core Ontologies (CCO) definition of an artifact. Formally, an artifact is defined as a distinct entity that is the product of a deliberate manufacturing process, possessing specific physical boundaries and functional characteristics. This definition excludes abstract information entities or purely logical constructs unless they are instantiated in a physical medium or a distinct software container.

The classification of an entity as an `cco:Artifact` is the prerequisite for any subsequent governance action, including procurement validation, lifecycle management, and disposal. Without this foundational classification, the asset cannot be effectively linked to supply chain data, warranty information, or security vulnerability databases. Consequently, the first step in any audit engagement involving asset management must be the verification that the inventory items under review satisfy the axiomatic requirements of the Artifact class.

### 7.2.1 Artifact Identification and Inventory Control

Effective governance requires that every artifact registered in the enterprise systems be uniquely identifiable and mapped to a specific class within the ontology. This mapping facilitates the application of rule-based controls; for instance, a "ServerChassis" class may be subject to different physical security protocols than a "MobileDevice" class. The granularity of this classification directly impacts the organization's ability to generate accurate risk profiles and resource allocation reports. Auditors must verify that the declared class of an artifact aligns with its physical reality and functional utilization, rejecting any entries where the classification appears to be a generic placeholder rather than a specific taxonomic designation.

## 7.3 Brand Attribution and the `schemaorg_product_brand` Axiom

Once an entity has been established as an artifact, the next critical governance requirement is the attribution of its commercial provenance. This relationship is governed by the axiom `schemaorg_product_brand`, which posits that a specific class of artifact (`X`) necessarily possesses a brand relationship (`sdg:hasBrand`) with a specific commercial entity or brand class (`Y`). This relationship is not merely descriptive but structural; it encodes the supply chain lineage required for warranty validation, patch management, and vendor risk assessment.

The Manchester syntax for this axiom is expressed as: `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:Artifact, sdg:hasBrand some {Y:Class}`. In natural language, this asserts that `{X}` is something that has brand `{Y}`. This constraint ensures that one cannot define a class of artifacts—such as a specific model of network switch—without simultaneously defining the commercial entity responsible for its manufacture. This linkage is vital for maintaining the "Single Source of Truth" regarding vendor liability and support entitlements.

### 7.3.2 Audit Verification of Brand Consistency

During quarterly audits, compliance officers must extract the artifact inventory and cross-reference it against the `t_schemaorg_product_brand` table. Discrepancies often arise when legacy equipment is rebranded or when white-box solutions are deployed without proper authorization. The audit protocol requires that any artifact instance where the `Brand` attribute does not resolve to a valid entry in the `y` column of the reference table be flagged as "Non-Compliant." Furthermore, the auditor must verify that the `x` value (Artifact Class) is consistent with the functional definition of the brand; for instance, a brand known primarily for consumer electronics should not be listed as the primary brand for an industrial-grade `StorageArray` without a specific waiver and risk acceptance addendum.

## 7.5 Remediation and Governance Protocols

Upon identification of discrepancies via the reports outlined in Section 7.4, the following remediation protocols must be initiated within five (5) business days:

1.  **Data Rectification:** If the error is clerical (e.g., a typo in the brand name), the CMDB record must be corrected to reflect the valid `y` value found in `t_schemaorg_product_brand`.
2.  **Asset Decommissioning:** If the artifact is genuinely orphaned (no valid brand mapping exists), the asset must be removed from the production network and scheduled for secure disposal, as it represents an unmanaged security vector.
3.  **Schema Update:** If the artifact is from a new, approved vendor not currently in the schema, a formal change request must be submitted to the Data Governance Board to add the new `{X, Y}` pair to the `t_schemaorg_product_brand` table. This update must include a risk assessment of the new vendor.

These protocols ensure that the ontology remains a living reflection of the enterprise's physical reality, rather than a static historical document. By maintaining the integrity of the `schemaorg_product_brand` relationship, the organization ensures that its asset management capabilities remain robust, auditable, and aligned with industry best practices.
