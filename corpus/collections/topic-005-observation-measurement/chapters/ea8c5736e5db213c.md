### 4.0 Preamble and Scope

This chapter establishes the formal ontological structures required to maintain rigorous governance over information processing activities within the enterprise. It delineates the relationships between regulatory directives, personnel accountability, and the evidentiary artifacts necessary for compliance verification. The framework provided herein is designed to align with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and broader audit standards, ensuring that all processing activities are traceable to specific human agents and supported by verifiable evidence. The following sections define the core axioms governing attestation, regulatory mapping, evidence management, data subject rights, and auditing protocols.

---

### 4.1 Attestation and Personnel Liability

**4.1.1 Definition and Scope**

In the context of information governance, an attestation is not merely a signature but a formal process instance (`bfo:Process`) that validates the integrity of a control or a data set. The foundational axiom for this concept asserts that any valid attestation must be signed by a natural person. This creates an immutable link between a governance process and a responsible agent, ensuring that accountability is preserved. The ontology defines this as `attestation_signed_by`, where the attestation entity is a subclass of a process that is signed by a member of the class `cco:Person`.

This mechanism prevents the abstraction of responsibility. By anchoring the attestation to a specific individual, the organization ensures that there is always a human node capable of verifying the accuracy of the declared state. This is critical for audit trails, as it allows investigators to trace the origin of a specific compliance assertion back to the source of authority.

**4.1.2 Relational Implementation**

The relational schema for this concept enforces that every attestation record is uniquely identified and explicitly linked to the class of the entity performing the signing. The primary key serves as the unique identifier for the attestation event, while the `x` slot denotes the class of the attestation process itself.

*Table 4.1: Attestation Signed By Registry*

| ID (PK) | X (Attestation Class) |
| :--- | :--- |
| ATT-2023-001 | DataProtectionOfficerCertification |
| ATT-2023-002 | ThirdPartyProcessorAgreement |
| ATT-2023-003 | InternalAuditSignOff |

**4.1.3 Operational Context**

When a Data Protection Officer (DPO) certifies a Record of Processing Activities (ROPA), they are instantiating the class `DataProtectionOfficerCertification`. This action creates a row in the registry (e.g., ID `ATT-2023-001`). The system records that this specific process instance was executed by a person. In the event of a regulatory inquiry, this entry serves as the primary evidence of who authorized the specific data handling configuration.

### 4.2 Regulatory Directives and Article Mapping

**4.2.1 Definition and Scope**

To ensure automated compliance and semantic reasoning, governance frameworks must map internal policy documents to external legal statutes. The `gdpr_article_subclass` axiom provides the structure for this mapping. It defines a class of Directives (`cco:DirectiveICE`) that specifically references a GDPR Article.

This relationship is hierarchical and directional. The Directive (Class X) is the internal implementation of the legal requirement (Class Y). By formalizing this relationship, the organization can query its ontology to identify which internal directives satisfy specific legal articles. For instance, an internal "Data Retention Policy" would be modeled as a subclass of a Directive that has the GDPR Article as a property.

**4.2.2 Relational Implementation**

The schema for this concept requires a foreign key constraint that links the specific legal article (Y) back to an attestation record. This constraint ensures that the legal citation is not merely theoretical but is actively instantiated within a signed attestation process. This linkage enforces the principle that legal interpretations must be formally adopted and signed off by a responsible authority.

*Table 4.2: GDPR Article Subclass Mapping*

| ID (PK) | X (Directive Class) | Y (Article ID/FK) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| DIR-ART-32 | SystemSecurityDirective | ATT-2023-001 |
| DIR-ART-15 | RightOfAccessDirective | ATT-2023-003 |
| DIR-ART-28 | DataProcessorControllerDirective | ATT-2023-002 |

**4.2.3 Operational Context**

Consider the `SystemSecurityDirective` (ID `DIR-ART-32`). This internal directive defines the technical measures required to secure personal data. According to the table, this directive maps to the legal concept of GDPR Article 32 (Security of processing). The foreign key `ATT-2023-001` indicates that this specific mapping and interpretation were validated during the `DataProtectionOfficerCertification` process. This creates a chain of custody: the Law -> The Directive -> The Attestation -> The Person.

### 4.3 Evidentiary Support for Attestation

**4.3.1 Definition and Scope**

A governance framework is only as robust as the evidence supporting its claims. The `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` axiom formalizes the requirement that every attestation process must be backed by concrete artifacts. These artifacts—logs, screenshots, configuration exports, or external audit reports—are modeled as classes (`Y`) that support the attestation process (`X`).

This concept transforms the attestation from a declarative statement ("I declare this is true") into an evidentiary assertion ("I declare this is true, supported by artifact Z"). In semantic terms, the attestation process is a subclass of `bfo:Process` that possesses the property of having supporting evidence.

**4.3.2 Relational Implementation**

The relational structure for this axiom is strictly hierarchical. The evidence (Y) must reference a valid GDPR Article Subclass record. This constraint ensures that evidence is not collected in a vacuum; it must be evidence *of* a specific directive's implementation. This prevents "orphaned" evidence that cannot be tied back to a specific compliance requirement.

*Table 4.3: Attestation with Supporting Evidence Log*

| ID (PK) | X (Attestation Process) | Y (Directive ID/FK) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| EVI-SEC-01 | PenetrationTestAttestation | DIR-ART-32 |
| EVI-ACC-01 | AccessLogReviewAttestation | DIR-ART-15 |
| EVI-DPA-01 | ProcessorDPAReview | DIR-ART-28 |

**4.3.3 Operational Context**

The row `EVI-SEC-01` represents a `PenetrationTestAttestation`. This is the process where an internal reviewer signs off on the results of a security test. The supporting evidence for this attestation is linked to `DIR-ART-32` (the System Security Directive). Consequently, an auditor reviewing this record can traverse the path: `EVI-SEC-01` -> `DIR-ART-32` -> `ATT-2023-001`. This confirms that the penetration test results serve as evidence for the security directive, which was in turn signed by the DPO.

### 4.4 Data Subject Rights and Granting Mechanisms

**4.4.1 Definition and Scope**

The GDPR grants specific rights to individuals (data subjects), such as the right to access, rectification, or erasure of their data. The `gdpr_data_subject_right` axiom models the internal mechanisms that operationalize these rights. It defines a Directive (`X`) that explicitly grants a specific Data Subject Right (`Y`).

This abstraction is crucial for workflow automation. By defining "Right to Access" as a class, the system can trigger specific workflows—such as data retrieval and redaction routines—whenever an instance of this class is invoked. The ontology ensures that the organization's internal procedures are semantically bound to the rights afforded by the regulation.

**4.4.2 Relational Implementation**

The schema enforces that the specific Right being granted (Y) must be linked to an attestation record. This implies that the organization's decision to grant a specific right, or the procedure defining how that right is granted, must be formally attested to by a person. This prevents the "silent" implementation of rights procedures without oversight.

*Table 4.4: GDPR Data Subject Rights Implementation*

| ID (PK) | X (Directive Class) | Y (Right ID/FK) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| DSR-ACCESS-01 | SubjectAccessRequestProcedure | ATT-2023-003 |
| DSR-ERASURE-01 | DataErasureProtocol | ATT-2023-001 |
| DSR-PORT-01 | DataPortabilityDirective | ATT-2023-003 |

**4.4.3 Operational Context**

The `SubjectAccessRequestProcedure` (ID `DSR-ACCESS-01`) is the internal directive governing how the organization handles requests from individuals to see their data. This procedure grants the "Right of Access." The foreign key `ATT-2023-003` links this right to the `InternalAuditSignOff` attestation. This indicates that the specific workflow for handling access requests has been reviewed and signed off during the internal audit cycle, ensuring it meets the necessary standards.

### 4.5 Audit Subclass and Control Verification

**4.5.1 Definition and Scope**

The final component of the governance framework is the audit mechanism itself. The `audit_subclass` axiom defines the Audit as a process (`bfo:Process`) that specifically audits a Directive (`cco:DirectiveICE`). This recursive relationship ensures that governance is self-correcting; the directives established to ensure compliance are themselves subject to verification through audit processes.

This distinction separates a general "process" from a specific "audit process." An audit process is characterized by its object: it must target a directive. This semantic constraint ensures that audit resources are focused on governance artifacts rather than arbitrary operational tasks.

**4.5.2 Relational Implementation**

The table structure for the audit subclass is straightforward, consisting of an identifier and the class of the audit process. While it does not explicitly store the target directive in this specific table schema, the ontology definition (`sdg:auditsControl some cco:DirectiveICE`) implies that the instances of `X` are defined by their relationship to the directives defined in previous sections.

*Table 4.5: Audit Subclass Registry*

| ID (PK) | X (Audit Process Class) |
| :--- | :--- |
| AUD-001 | QuarterlyComplianceAudit |
| AUD-002 | AdHocSecurityAudit |
| AUD-003 | AnnualGovernanceReview |

**4.5.3 Operational Context**

The `QuarterlyComplianceAudit` (ID `AUD-001`) is a process class defined by its objective to audit the directives established in Sections 4.2 and 4.4. When this audit is executed, it would logically target the directives found in `t_gdpr_article_subclass` and `t_gdpr_data_subject_right`. The output of this audit process would likely generate new entries in the `t_attestation_with_supporting_evidence` table, closing the loop on the governance lifecycle.
