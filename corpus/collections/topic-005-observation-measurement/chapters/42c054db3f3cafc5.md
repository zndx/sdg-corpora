# Chapter 7: Governance Ontology and Compliance Verification Mechanisms

This chapter establishes the formal ontological framework governing regulatory compliance within the enterprise information system. It delineates the structural dependencies between attestation processes, statutory directives, and audit controls, ensuring that all governance activities are traceable to specific regulatory requirements. The definitions and relational mappings provided herein are essential for maintaining the integrity of the governance framework and ensuring adherence to the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and other relevant standards.

## 7.1 Attestation and Signatory Authority

The foundational unit of governance within the framework is the attestation. An attestation is formally defined as a process that is signed by a person. This process serves the dual function of verifying the completion of a compliance activity and binding a specific natural person to the veracity of that activity. In the context of the ontology, the entity `{X}` represents the attestation class or instance, which is a subclass of `bfo:0000015` (Process). The relationship `sdg:signedBy` links this process to an instance of `cco:Person`.

This mechanism is critical for establishing non-repudiation. By anchoring the attestation to a specific individual, the organization ensures that every compliance claim is auditable and attributable. The attestation is not merely a procedural formality; it is a distinct process node within the governance graph that triggers downstream workflows for evidence retention and audit scheduling.

### 7.1.1 Implementation Guidelines
When implementing the attestation node, the system must capture the unique identifier of the person signing the document. The signatory must possess the organizational authority commensurate with the scope of the attestation. For instance, a system-wide data processing impact assessment requires a signature from a Data Protection Officer (DPO), whereas a departmental access log review may be attested to by a line manager.

The relational schema for this concept isolates the attestation identifier (`id`) and the class or instance name (`x`). The primary key ensures that each attestation event is treated as a discrete, immutable record.

## 7.2 Regulatory Article Alignment

To ensure that internal directives maintain legal validity, they must be explicitly mapped to statutory instruments. The ontology defines a subclass relationship where `{X}` (a Directive) is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` and possesses the property `sdg:gdprArticle` referencing `{Y}` (a specific Article Class). This axiom enforces the requirement that every internal governance directive must be justified by one or more articles of the GDPR.

This alignment serves as the bridge between abstract legal requirements and concrete operational controls. By formalizing this relationship, the organization can automate the gap analysis process. If a new directive is introduced without a valid `gdprArticle` mapping, the validation engine will flag it as non-compliant.

### 7.2.1 Relational Dependencies
The data structure for this axiom enforces a strict foreign key constraint. The `y` column, representing the GDPR Article, must reference an existing identifier from the attestation table (`t_attestation_signed_by`). This design choice implies that the "Article" in this context is treated as a specific instance or interpretation that is itself anchored by a foundational attestation—perhaps the legal review of the article's applicability.

## 7.3 Evidence Integration and Verification

A claim of compliance is insufficient without substantiating evidence. The axiom for attestation with supporting evidence defines `{X}` (a Process) as having the property `sdg:withSupportingEvidence` referencing `{Y}` (an Evidence Class). This creates a compositional hierarchy where the attestation process acts as the parent node for various child nodes representing logs, screenshots, policy documents, or system exports.

This structure is essential for audit readiness. When an auditor queries a specific control, the system must be able to traverse the relationship from the Attestation to the Evidence. The ontology treats evidence as a first-class object, requiring its own classification and metadata, rather than a simple file attachment.

### 7.3.1 Evidence Chain of Custody
The relational schema enforces that evidence (`y`) is linked to a specific GDPR Article directive (`id` from `t_gdpr_article_subclass`). This ensures that evidence is not collected in a vacuum but is gathered specifically to satisfy the requirements of a directive mapped to a regulatory article. For example, a system log (Evidence) is collected specifically to satisfy the Security of Processing Directive (mapped to Article 32).

## 7.4 Data Subject Rights Implementation

The operationalization of GDPR rights is managed through specific directives that grant these rights to data subjects. The ontology defines `{X}` (a Directive) as granting a Data Subject Right `{Y}`. This axiom utilizes the property `sdg:grantsDataSubjectRight` to link the organizational mechanism (the directive) to the fundamental right being exercised.

This section of the framework is distinct from the Article mapping (Section 7.2) because it focuses on the *granting* mechanism rather than the *regulatory basis*. While a directive may be based on Article 17 (Right to Erasure), the specific class of right granted is the "Erasure Right."

### 7.4.2 Granting Mechanism Constraints
The schema requires that the granted right (`y`) references an attestation record (`id` from `t_attestation_signed_by`). This constraint ensures that the granting of a right is always logged and signed off. In practice, this means that when a Data Subject Right is granted (e.g., a user's data is deleted), the system must generate an attestation record (signed by the administrator or the system automator) which serves as the key for the rights grant record.

## 7.5 Audit Control Subclassification

The final component of the governance loop is the audit process. The ontology defines `{X}` (a Process) as a subclass of `bfo:0000015` that audits a `cco:DirectiveICE`. This axiom establishes the audit as a distinct process type that targets the directives defined in previous sections.

Audits function as the feedback mechanism in the control system. They are passive observations of active directives. The ontology requires that every audit process explicitly targets a specific directive. This prevents "generic" audits that lack scope and ensures that audit findings can be directly mapped back to specific governance failures or successes in the directive layer.

### 7.5.1 Audit Execution
The audit process does not require a complex foreign key structure in this schema because it acts as the terminal node for the verification chain. It references the Directive class but does not need to be instantiated by an attestation in this specific model (though in a broader context, the audit report itself would likely be an attestation).
