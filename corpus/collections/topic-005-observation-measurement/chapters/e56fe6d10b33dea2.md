# Chapter 4: Governance Frameworks and Compliance Attestation in Data Protection

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the formal ontology underpinning the governance framework for data protection compliance within the enterprise. It establishes the structural definitions for key compliance artifacts, specifically focusing on the intersection of process attestations, regulatory directives, and evidence management. The content herein is intended for compliance officers, data protection officers (DPOs), and information systems auditors who require a precise, axiomatically derived mapping of organizational controls to legal obligations. The definitions provided are grounded in the Web Ontology Language (OWL) and projected into relational schemas to facilitate system implementation and audit trail verification.

## 4.2 Attestation Signed By

### 4.2.1 Conceptual Definition

The foundational unit of accountability within the governance framework is the attestation. Formally defined by the axiom `attestation_signed_by`, this concept represents a specific class of process (`bfo:0000015`) that necessitates validation by a natural person. In the context of regulatory compliance, an attestation is not merely a digital signature or a checkbox; it is a distinct process entity that carries the weight of legal responsibility. The Manchester syntax for this axiom is expressed as `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:signedBy some cco:Person`.

This definition implies that for any attestation process to be valid within the system, it must be irrevocably linked to an agent with the capacity to bear responsibility—the `cco:Person`. This linkage prevents the delegation of accountability to automated systems or non-legal entities, ensuring that a human stakeholder remains the anchor of governance.

### 4.2.2 Implementation and Relational Structure

In practice, the `attestation_signed_by` axiom is instantiated whenever a senior officer certifies the validity of a control or a data set. For example, the "Third-Party Data Processing Agreement" is not a static document but a process culminating in a signature event. The relational projection of this axiom captures the identity of the attestation class (`X`) while utilizing the primary key (`id`) to serve as the reference point for downstream dependencies.

## 4.3 GDPR Article Subclass

### 4.3.1 Conceptual Definition

To bridge the gap between internal governance artifacts and external legal obligations, the framework utilizes the `gdpr_article_subclass` axiom. This axiom defines a class `X` (a Directive Information Content Entity) that specifically addresses or implements a provision of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), denoted as class `Y`. The formalization is `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:gdprArticle some {Y:Class}`.

This construct allows the organization to map its internal directives—such as policy documents or procedural guidelines—directly to specific regulatory articles (e.g., Article 28 regarding Processor contracts or Article 32 regarding Security of processing). It ensures that every internal directive has a clear, traceable legal basis.

### 4.3.2 Relational Dependencies and Evidence

A critical aspect of this axiom is the foreign key constraint imposed by the relational schema. The `gdpr_article_subclass` table (`t_gdpr_article_subclass`) references the `attestation_signed_by` table via the `y` column. This structural dependency enforces a rule of governance: a GDPR Article implementation (`Y`) cannot exist independently of a signed attestation process. In effect, the legal article is "activated" or "operationalized" only through the mechanism of a signed attestation.

The table below presents the mapping of internal directives to GDPR articles, where the `y` column contains the ID of the attestation that validates the article's implementation.

## 4.4 Attestation with Supporting Evidence

### 4.4.1 Conceptual Definition

Compliance is not established solely through assertion (attestation) or legal mapping (directives); it requires empirical support. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` formalizes this requirement. It defines a class `X` (an attestation process) that includes or is substantiated by evidence class `Y`. The Manchester syntax is `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:withSupportingEvidence some {Y:Class}`.

This concept moves the framework from a declarative model ("we say we do this") to an evidentiary model ("we can prove we do this"). The supporting evidence can range from system logs and audit reports to vulnerability scan results or training completion certificates. By embedding this within the ontology, the organization creates a chain of custody where every claim is backed by a retrievable artifact.

### 4.4.2 Evidence Chain Integrity

The relational schema for `t_attestation_with_supporting_evidence` enforces a specific hierarchy. The evidence (`y`) must reference an existing GDPR Article implementation (`id` from `t_gdpr_article_subclass`). This means that raw evidence is not accepted in isolation; it must be relevant to a specific legal directive that has already been attested to. This prevents "evidence drift" where data is collected without clear regulatory purpose.

For instance, a "System Access Log" (Evidence) supports the "Security of Processing Directive" (GDPR Article 32), which in turn is validated by the "Internal Policy Review" (Attestation).

## 4.5 GDPR Data Subject Right

### 4.5.1 Conceptual Definition

The core of the GDPR framework is the empowerment of data subjects. The axiom `gdpr_data_subject_right` defines a class `X` (a Directive) that explicitly grants a specific right `Y` to the data subject. The axiom is expressed as `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: cco:DirectiveICE, sdg:grantsDataSubjectRight some {Y:Class}`.

This section of the ontology focuses on the operationalization of rights such as access, rectification, erasure, and portability. By treating the granting of a right as a directive class, the organization can audit its readiness to fulfill these rights. It transforms abstract legal rights into concrete, auditable system capabilities.

### 4.5.2 Operationalizing Rights via Attestation

Structurally, the `t_gdpr_data_subject_right` table links the granted right (`y`) back to a signed attestation (`t_attestation_signed_by`). This reinforces the principle that the organization's commitment to uphold a right is a formal, signed process. It is not enough to have a technical capability to delete data; the *policy* to delete data upon request must be formally attested to by a responsible officer.

The following table details the specific rights granted by the organization's directives and the attestations that underpin them.

## 4.6 Audit Subclass

### 4.6.1 Conceptual Definition

The final concept in this governance framework is the `audit_subclass`. This axiom defines a class `X` (a Process) that has the specific function of auditing a Control Directive Information Content Entity (`cco:DirectiveICE`). The Manchester syntax is `Class: {X:Class} SubClassOf: bfo:0000015, sdg:auditsControl some cco:DirectiveICE`.

This represents the meta-governance layer—the process of checking the processes. While attestations provide self-assurance, audits provide independent or internal verification. The audit process is tasked with examining the directives, the evidence, and the attestations defined in the previous sections to ensure they remain valid and effective over time.

### 4.6.2 The Audit Process

The `t_audit_subclass` table is distinct in that it focuses on the *type* of audit process being performed. It serves as a catalog of the organization's audit mechanisms. While the schema provided does not explicitly link the audit to a specific directive ID via a foreign key column in the table definition, the axiom implies the relationship `auditsControl some cco:DirectiveICE`. In a full implementation, the rows in this table would represent the specific audit events (e.g., "Quarterly Compliance Audit") that are executed to verify the integrity of the data in the preceding tables.
