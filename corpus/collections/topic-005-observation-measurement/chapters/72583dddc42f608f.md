# Chapter 7: Governance Frameworks for GDPR Compliance and Attestation Protocols

## 7.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the structural and procedural requirements for establishing a robust governance framework under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). It provides a technical specification for the ontology of compliance, mapping abstract regulatory obligations to concrete operational processes. The text defines the relational dependencies between attestation protocols, regulatory articles, supporting evidence, and audit mechanisms. Organizations must utilize these definitions to ensure that all data processing activities are not only compliant by design but are also verifiable through a deterministic chain of evidence and authority. The following sections detail the five core axioms that constitute the backbone of the compliance model, instantiated through relational data schemas that enforce referential integrity across governance domains.

## 7.2 Attestation and Delegated Authority

### 7.2.1 Conceptual Definition

The foundational unit of accountability within the governance framework is the attestation. Formally defined as a subclass of `bfo:Process`, an attestation represents the specific act of validating or certifying a control's efficacy. Crucially, this process is not autonomous; it requires the agency of a natural person. The axiom `attestation_signed_by` establishes that every attestation process (`X`) must be signed by a `cco:Person`. This requirement ensures that there is always a responsible human agent accountable for the validity of the compliance claim, preventing the delegation of authority to purely automated systems without oversight.

### 7.2.2 Operational Implementation

In practice, this means that no compliance artifact is considered valid until it is cryptographically or physically signed by a designated governor. Whether the process involves a Subject Access Request (SAR) verification or a third-party data transfer agreement, the system must record the specific identifier of the individual authorizing the process. This linkage creates the initial node in the chain of custody for data governance.

## 7.3 Regulatory Mapping and Article Alignment

### 7.3.1 Conceptual Definition

To operationalize the GDPR, internal governance directives must be explicitly mapped to the regulatory text. The axiom `gdpr_article_subclass` formalizes this by asserting that a specific internal directive (`X`) is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` and pertains to a specific GDPR article (`Y`). This mapping ensures that organizational policies are not vague statements of intent but are technically anchored to specific legal provisions.

### 7.3.2 Operational Implementation

Governance teams are required to maintain a matrix where every internal control directive references the specific GDPR article it addresses. For instance, an internal "Data Erasure Policy" must be explicitly linked to GDPR Article 17 (Right to Erasure). The relational integrity of this axiom is enforced by linking the regulatory article (`y`) to the attestation process defined in the previous section. This dependency implies that the validity of the regulatory mapping is contingent upon the attestation process signed by a responsible person.

## 7.4 Evidence-Based Attestation

### 7.4.1 Conceptual Definition

Compliance is not merely a procedural act; it is an evidentiary one. The axiom `attestation_with_supporting_evidence` posits that any attestation process (`X`) must be supported by specific evidence (`Y`). This evidence class acts as the proof payload that justifies the signatory's assertion. Without this linkage, an attestation is an empty claim lacking the "evidence-anchored" density required for audit readiness.

### 7.4.2 Operational Implementation

When a governor signs off on a process, the system must automatically associate the relevant artifact—be it a log file, a system configuration screenshot, or a physical record. This axiom enforces a "zero-trust" approach to documentation where the claim of compliance is inseparable from the proof of compliance. The structure requires that the evidence (`y`) is linked to a specific directive instance, ensuring that the evidence is relevant to the regulatory obligation being satisfied.

### 7.4.3 Relational Instantiation

## 7.5 Data Subject Rights Implementation

### 7.5.1 Conceptual Definition

The ultimate purpose of the governance framework is to uphold the rights of individuals. The axiom `gdpr_data_subject_right` specifies that a directive (`X`) grants a specific data subject right (`Y`). This axiom operationalizes the abstract rights found in the GDPR into concrete, enforceable mechanisms within the organization's information architecture.

### 7.5.2 Operational Implementation

This section requires the organization to identify which internal directives serve as the vehicle for exercising specific rights (e.g., Access, Rectification, Portability). The structure mandates that the granting of a right (`y`) is traceable back to the attestation process (`id` from Table 7.1). This ensures that the mechanism for granting a right is formally authorized and signed off, preventing ad-hoc or unauthorized disclosures of personal data.

## 7.6 Audit and Control Verification

### 7.6.1 Conceptual Definition

The final component of the framework is the audit function. The axiom `audit_subclass` defines an audit (`X`) as a `bfo:Process` that specifically audits a `cco:DirectiveICE`. This recursive structure—where a process examines the integrity of a directive—ensures continuous improvement and verification. It acts as the meta-layer of governance, validating that the directives, evidence, and attestations described in previous sections are functioning as intended.

### 7.6.2 Operational Implementation

Audits must be scheduled and executed to review the efficacy of the `cco:DirectiveICE` entities. Unlike the other axioms, this is a standalone process class that does not require a foreign key dependency on the other tables in this specific schema segment, representing its independence as a verification tool. However, in a broader implementation, the audit object would target the `id` values of the directives defined in Section 7.3.
