# Chapter 4: Interoperability of Compliance Directives and Risk Taxonomies

## 4.1 Scope and Preamble

This chapter delineates the structural mapping between information security governance frameworks, specifically focusing on the integration of ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A controls, PCI DSS requirements, and enterprise risk management taxonomies. The content herein provides a formal ontology for aligning directive classes (cco:DirectiveICE) with specific control objectives and threat vectors. As regulatory environments converge, it is incumbent upon governance bodies to maintain a rigorous mapping between high-level policy directives and specific technical controls. This chapter establishes the axiomatic relationships between these entities, providing a deterministic schema for auditing compliance across multiple standards. The definitions and tabular representations included herein are designed to support automated validation and manual audit trails, ensuring that the implementation of a directive is traceable to its originating risk and compliance requirement.

## 4.2 ISO 27001 Annex A Subclassifications

The foundational element of the governance framework is the classification of directives according to the ISO/IEC 27001 standard. Annex A of ISO/IEC 27001 provides a comprehensive list of control objectives and controls intended to address information security risks. Within the ontology, a directive is formally defined as a subclass of the `cco:DirectiveICE` that specifically implements or references a control from ISO 27001 Annex A. This relationship is not merely associative but hierarchical; the directive inherits the obligations and properties of the referenced standard control.

The axiom `iso27001_annexa_subclass` posits that for a directive to be compliant with this specific subset of the ontology, it must be linked to a valid Annex A control class. This linkage ensures that all governance artifacts can be traced back to an internationally recognized benchmark. The relational projection of this axiom requires that the Annex A control (`y`) referenced by a directive (`x`) must exist as a valid entity within the threat-addressing schema, thereby creating a dependency between the standard and the risk landscape.

The primary key is the directive `id`, and the foreign key `y` references the threat class identifier, ensuring that every ISO control is associated with a specific risk context.

| id | x (Directive) | y (ISO 27001 Annex A Control) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| DIR-ISO-001 | AccessControlPolicy | ISO_A_9_1 |

The rigorous definition of these mappings allows auditors to verify that internal policies are not merely nominal but are structurally anchored to the standard's control set.

## 4.3 Control and Threat Addressing Classes

While the ISO standard provides the control framework, the operational efficacy of a directive is determined by its ability to mitigate specific threats. The ontology defines a specific class of directives that are restricted to addressing particular risks exclusively. The axiom `control_only_addresses_threat_class` introduces a constraint of necessity (`only`), indicating that the directive's scope is strictly limited to the identified threat class. This prevents scope creep and ensures that resources allocated to the directive are focused solely on the designated risk vector.

This axiom creates a circular dependency with the ISO classification in the relational schema. A control addressing a threat (`y`) must reference an ISO subclass (`y`), and conversely, the ISO subclass references the threat class. This mutual dependency enforces a tight coupling between the standard control and the specific threat it is designed to mitigate. It implies that in this governance model, an ISO control is not defined in a vacuum but is defined by the specific threat it addresses.

**Table 4.2: Control-Only Addresses Threat Class**

The following table embodies Axiom 2 (`control_only_addresses_threat_class`). It defines directives that address specific risks exclusively. The primary key is the `id` of the threat/risk entity, and the foreign key `y` references the `id` of the ISO directive, creating a bidirectional link between the threat and the control.

| id | x (Directive) | y (ISO Directive Reference) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| RISK_UNAUTH_ACCESS | AccessControlPolicy | DIR-ISO-001 |
| RISK_DATA_DISCLOSURE | CryptoControlPolicy | DIR-ISO-002 |
| RISK_PHYSICAL_INTRUSION | PhysicalEntryDirective | DIR-ISO-003 |
| RISK_MALICIOUS_CODE | OpsSecurityDirective | DIR-ISO-004 |
| RISK_NETWORK_EAVESDROP | CommsSecurityDirective | DIR-ISO-005 |

As illustrated in Table 4.2, the risk entity `RISK_UNAUTH_ACCESS` is addressed *only* by the `AccessControlPolicy`. The foreign key `y` points back to `DIR-ISO-001`, the ISO directive defined in Table 4.1. This structure confirms that `AccessControlPolicy` is something that "addresses risk" `RISK_UNAUTH_ACCESS`, and that this relationship is exclusive and deterministic within the schema.

## 4.4 PCI DSS Requirement Integration

Organizations operating within the payment card industry must adhere to the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS). The ontology accommodates this by mapping directives to specific PCI DSS requirements. The axiom `pci_dss_requirement` establishes a relationship where a directive (`x`) is a subclass of `cco:DirectiveICE` that satisfies a specific PCI DSS requirement (`y`).

This mapping is critical for cross-compliance auditing. It allows the governance framework to identify where a single directive may satisfy both an ISO 27001 control and a PCI DSS requirement, or where distinct directives are required to meet separate compliance obligations. The relational schema dictates that the PCI requirement (`y`) must reference a valid threat class, ensuring that the PCI requirement is grounded in the same risk taxonomy used for the ISO controls.

**Table 4.3: PCI DSS Requirement Mappings**

The following table embodies Axiom 3 (`pci_dss_requirement`). It maps directives to specific PCI DSS requirements. The primary key is the directive `id`, and the foreign key `y` references the `id` of the threat class from the threat-addressing table, linking the compliance requirement to the operational risk.

| id | x (Directive) | y (PCI DSS Requirement / Threat Ref) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| DIR-PCI-001 | StrongAuthentication | RISK_UNAUTH_ACCESS |
| DIR-PCI-002 | TransmissionEncryption | RISK_DATA_DISCLOSURE |
| DIR-PCI-003 | MediaAccessControl | RISK_PHYSICAL_INTRUSION |
| DIR-PCI-004 | VulnerabilityManagement | RISK_MALICIOUS_CODE |
| DIR-PCI-005 | WirelessNetworkAccess | RISK_NETWORK_EAVESDROP |

In Table 4.3, the directive `StrongAuthentication` (DIR-PCI-001) is mapped to the PCI DSS requirement regarding access control, which is operationally linked to the `RISK_UNAUTH_ACCESS` entity. This demonstrates that the directive `x` is something that "pci dss requirement" `y`, where `y` serves as the bridge to the underlying risk defined in the previous section.

## 4.5 Requirement Specification and Targeting

A directive must be actionable and specific. The axiom `requirement_equiv_specifies_target` defines a complex equivalence class where a directive (`x`) is equivalent to the intersection of two properties: it specifies a control or mechanism (`y`) and applies to a specific target (`z`). This axiom formalizes the deployment scope of a governance artifact. It is insufficient to merely possess a control; the control must be explicitly targeted at a system, process, or entity class.

This equivalence is foundational for role-based access control (RBAC) and system-specific governance policies. It distinguishes between a general policy statement and a specific, enforceable requirement. The relational structure requires that the specified control (`y`) must be a valid ISO subclass, ensuring that the requirement is anchored in the primary control framework.

**Table 4.4: Requirement Equivalence Specification**

The following table embodies Axiom 4 (`requirement_equiv_specifies_target`). It defines requirements that specify a control and apply to a target. The primary key is the requirement `id`, the foreign key `y` references the ISO directive, and `z` defines the target entity class.

| id | x (Requirement) | y (Specifies Control) | z (Applies To Target) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| REQ-EQ-001 | PasswordPolicy | DIR-ISO-001 | SystemUser |
| REQ-EQ-002 | DiskEncryption | DIR-ISO-002 | StorageMedia |
| REQ-EQ-003 | BadgeReader | DIR-ISO-003 | DataCenter |
| REQ-EQ-004 | AntivirusUpdate | DIR-ISO-004 | Workstation |
| REQ-EQ-005 | VPNConfiguration | DIR-ISO-005 | RemoteUser |

Table 4.4 shows that the requirement `PasswordPolicy` (REQ-EQ-001) specifies the `AccessControlPolicy` (DIR-ISO-001) and applies specifically to the `SystemUser` target. This granular definition ensures that the governance framework can answer not only "what control exists?" but also "where and how is it applied?"

## 4.6 General Risk Addressing

While specific controls address specific threats, the governance framework must also account for general risk mitigation strategies that may not be exclusive to a single threat or standard. The axiom `control_addresses_risk` defines a broader class of directives (`x`) that address a risk (`y`) without the exclusivity constraint found in Section 4.3. This allows for overlapping controls and defense-in-depth strategies where multiple directives may address a single risk, or a single directive may address multiple facets of a risk.

This axiom links the directive back to the ISO subclass, maintaining the anchor to the primary standard while allowing flexibility in risk mitigation. It supports the mapping of compensating controls and custom security measures that align with the organization's risk appetite.

**Table 4.5: General Risk Addressing**

The following table embodies Axiom 5 (`control_addresses_risk`). It maps directives to the risks they address. The primary key is the directive `id`, and the foreign key `y` references the ISO directive `id`, linking the control effort back to the standard framework.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| CTRL-GEN-001 | MultiFactorAuth | DIR-ISO-001 |
| CTRL-GEN-002 | KeyRotation | DIR-ISO-002 |
| CTRL-GEN-003 | VisitorLogs | DIR-ISO-003 |
| CTRL-GEN-004 | PatchManagement | DIR-ISO-004 |
| CTRL-GEN-005 | FirewallRules | DIR-ISO-005 |

In Table 4.5, the control `MultiFactorAuth` (CTRL-GEN-001) addresses the risk associated with `DIR-ISO-001` (Access Control). Unlike the "only" constraint in Table 4.2, this table allows for the possibility that other controls (e.g., `StrongAuthentication` in Table 4.3) may also address this same risk, illustrating the layered nature of the security posture.
