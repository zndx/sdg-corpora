# Chapter 4: Geospatial Attribution and Artifact Compliance

This chapter establishes the mandatory protocols for the geospatial attribution of physical artifacts within the enterprise data governance framework. It delineates the ontological relationship between material assets and their spatial attributes, ensuring data integrity for regulatory reporting and auditability. The guidelines herein mandate the use of standardized coordinate notation for all fixed and movable assets classified as Artifacts. Compliance with these mappings is required for all facility audits, asset tracking exercises, and regulatory submissions.

## 1. The Artifact-Coordinate Relationship

The foundational axiom governing physical asset location asserts that every distinct physical entity classified as an Artifact must possess a defined geospatial property. This relationship is not merely descriptive but constitutive of the artifact's identity within the spatial registry. In accordance with the governing ontology, an Artifact (X) is defined as a subclass of `cco:Artifact` that necessarily bears the property `sdg:hasGeoCoordinates` to some instance of a Coordinate class (Y). This ensures that no physical object exists within the inventory without a verifiable, mathematically precise location.

### 1.1 Definition of the Artifact (X)

The term "Artifact" refers to any discrete, physical object that is the product of a fabrication process or is naturally occurring but managed within the enterprise infrastructure. Formally, the entity X is a Class representing these objects. However, for the purposes of data entry and compliance auditing, X is instantiated as specific, trackable entities. These entities range from immobile infrastructure components, such as server racks and structural pillars, to mobile assets like inspection vehicles and portable sensor arrays.

The classification of an entity as an Artifact triggers the requirement for spatial attribution. It is insufficient to record the mere existence of the object; the system must record its location in a structured format compatible with global positioning standards. This requirement ensures that the artifact can be audited physically based on its digital record. The integrity of the asset inventory relies on the one-to-one mapping between the physical object and its digital twin, anchored by the geospatial coordinate.

### 1.2 Definition of GeoCoordinates (Y)

The GeoCoordinates (Y) represent the quantitative expression of the Artifact's location on the Earth's surface. This is not a relative description (e.g., "next to the loading dock") but an absolute value derived from the WGS 84 datum or other applicable coordinate reference systems. The property `sdg:hasGeoCoordinates` links the Artifact to a specific data structure containing latitude, longitude, and optionally, altitude.

In the context of compliance, the precision of Y is paramount. Regulatory frameworks often mandate specific accuracy thresholds (e.g., within 3 meters) for high-value or hazardous assets. Therefore, the coordinate class Y must encapsulate not just the position, but the metadata regarding the measurement's accuracy and timestamp. This allows auditors to verify that the location data was current at the time of the report and meets the required precision standards for the specific class of Artifact.

### 1.3 Implementation and Data Schema

To operationalize the axiom {X} is something that has geo coordinates {Y}, the following relational schema is enforced. This schema, `t_schemaorg_place_geocoord`, serves as the central repository for linking physical asset identifiers to their spatial proofs.

The table structure is designed to enforce referential integrity. The primary key is the `id` field, which serves as a unique identifier for the specific attribution record. The `x` column contains the identifier or URI of the Artifact class instance, representing the physical object. The `y` column contains the serialized GeoCoordinates, typically a string literal representing a latitude/longitude pair or a GeoJSON fragment. The `NOT NULL` constraint on `y` ensures that the axiom is strictly adhered to; an Artifact record cannot exist without coordinates.

### 1.4 Compliance and Verification Procedures

Maintaining the accuracy of the `t_schemaorg_place_geocoord` table is a critical control objective. Internal audit teams must periodically verify that the coordinates listed in column `y` correspond to the physical location of the Artifact identified in column `x`. Discrepancies between the recorded coordinates and the physical reality constitute a material weakness in asset governance.

Verification protocols typically involve:
1.  **Random Sampling:** Selecting a statistically significant sample of records from the table.
2.  **Physical Validation:** Using GPS-enabled handheld devices to confirm the coordinates at the Artifact's location.
3.  **Data Reconciliation:** Comparing the system coordinates against the physical reading to ensure deviation falls within acceptable error margins (e.g., < 1 meter for indoor assets, < 5 meters for outdoor assets).

Furthermore, the ontology distinguishes between the *Class* of the Artifact and the *Instance* of the Artifact. While the axiom defines the relationship at the class level (all Artifacts have coordinates), the compliance activities occur at the instance level (Server_Rack_A04 is at 33.812092, -117.918974). The schema provided bridges this gap by storing instance-level data that validates the class-level axiom.

### 1.5 Error Handling and Exceptions

In instances where an Artifact is moved or decommissioned, the record in `t_schemaorg_place_geocoord` must be updated immediately. A "stale" coordinate—where the `y` value no longer reflects the location of the `x` value—renders the artifact effectively lost from a tracking perspective, triggering potential regulatory fines or security risks.

Exceptions to the coordinate requirement are rare. If an Artifact is currently in transit or located within a GPS-denied environment (e.g., a shielded server room), a "last known good" coordinate may be used, but this must be flagged with a specific metadata attribute indicating the reduced confidence level. However, the structural requirement of the table—that `y` must be populated—remains absolute. Null values are prohibited to ensure that the spatial query layer of the enterprise application does not fail during retrieval operations.
