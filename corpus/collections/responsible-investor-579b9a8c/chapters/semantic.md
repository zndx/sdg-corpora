## Ontology-Grounded Schema Design for Responsible Investment and Environmental Governance

This chapter documents the relational schema that materializes a domain ontology of responsible investment, corporate environmental governance, and empirical policy evaluation. The domain captures how institutional investors allocate capital to corporations, how those corporations generate environmental externalities, what policies they adopt to mitigate pollution, and how empirical studies evaluate the effectiveness of responsible investment funds. Six base tables encode the core entities and their attributes, while twenty-two join views reconstruct domain facts from the normalized tables. Every table and column name is quoted in backticks throughout.

The domain ontology identifies six entity types. `responsible_investors` represents institutional actors who deploy capital with environmental, social, and governance (ESG) criteria. Each investor carries a surrogate `id`, a business `investor_id`, a `name`, a `wealth` metric, a count of `diversification_opportunities`, a `motivation_type` (one of `pure_altruism`, `warm_glow`, or `reputation`), a boolean `is_green_agent` flag, and foreign keys `corporation_id`, `environmental_externality_id`, and `corporate_policy_id` that anchor the investor to specific corporations, externalities, and policies. The first row illustrates this: investor `Extended Review` (id 1, investor_id `39800621`) holds wealth of `13.45`, has `22` diversification opportunities, is motivated by `pure_altruism`, is flagged as a green agent, and is linked to corporation `bc908e7a-8fcd-11eb-924d-9cd76263cbd0`, externality `1`, and policy `1`.

### Foreign-Key Architecture and Cardinality

The schema employs a hub-and-spoke foreign-key pattern. Each of the five operational tables (`responsible_investors`, `corporations`, `environmental_externalities`, `corporate_policies`, `responsible_investment_funds`) carries a `corporation_id` column that references `corporations.corporation_id`. Similarly, `environmental_externality_id` columns in `responsible_investors`, `corporations`, `corporate_policies`, and `empirical_studies` reference `environmental_externalities.id`. The `corporate_policy_id` columns in `responsible_investors`, `corporations`, `environmental_externalities`, `corporate_policies`, and `empirical_studies` reference `corporate_policies.id`. The `responsible_investor_id` columns in `corporations`, `corporate_policies`, and `responsible_investment_funds` reference `responsible_investors.id`. The `empirical_study_id` column in `responsible_investment_funds` and the `responsible_investment_fund_id` column in `empirical_studies` form a bidirectional linkage between the fund and study entities. This dense interconnection means that every base table participates in multiple join paths, and the twenty-two views materialize these paths as queryable relations.

### View-Based Reconstruction of Domain Facts

Each view is a `SELECT` that joins two base tables on their shared foreign key, producing a denormalized fact table that answers a specific domain question. The naming convention `v_<entity_a>_<entity_b>` indicates the two entities involved.

The view `v_responsible_investor_corporation` joins `responsible_investors` to `corporations` on `responsible_investors.corporation_id = corporations.corporation_id`. It answers the question: which corporations does each responsible investor hold? Row 1 shows investor `Extended Review` linked to corporation `Extended Review` (id `bc908e7a-8fcd-11eb-924d-9cd76263cbd0`), revealing a self-referential naming pattern in the synthetic data.

### Synthesis

The schema encodes a tightly coupled domain where responsible investors, corporations, environmental externalities, and corporate policies form a four-way relational core. Each entity carries foreign keys pointing to the other three, creating a complete graph of inter-entity relationships. The two additional entities—responsible investment funds and empirical studies—extend this core by linking capital vehicles to corporations and investors, and by providing research evaluations that reference funds, externalities, and policies. The twenty-two views materialize every pairwise join among the six entities, ensuring that any domain question—whether about investor-corporation alignments, externality-policy mappings, or fund-study evaluations—can be answered through a single view lookup rather than a multi-table join. The design trades storage redundancy for query simplicity, a common pattern in analytical data warehouses where read performance outweighs write complexity. Representative values such as investor `Extended Review` (wealth `13.45`, motivation `pure_altruism`), corporation `Pilot Initiative A` (industry `clean`, pollution level `11.40`), externality `2` (externality_id `790470`, pollution size `0.02`, abatement cost `11.94`), policy `2` (policy_id `10207142`, type `production`, target reduction `7.90`), fund `Seasonal Programme` (total assets `21.70`, created `2025-01-01`), and study `100` (study_id `Karyawan`, theoretical model `true`, has recommendations `true`) ground the schema in concrete domain facts that the views reconstruct on demand.
