# Chapter 7: Temporal Anchoring of Descriptive Entities in Governance Frameworks

This chapter establishes the procedural and ontological requirements for recording assertions and quantitative data within the context of formal governance and compliance auditing. It details the necessary conditions for binding descriptive information content entities (ICE) to specific temporal processes, ensuring that all recorded data—whether qualitative claims or quantitative metrics—are rigorously anchored to observable events. The guidelines provided herein are mandatory for all compliance officers, data stewards, and members of the Board of Directors (BOD) involved in the maintenance of the central audit repository.

## 1. Claims Observed by Event

### 1.1 Definition and Scope

In the context of the governance framework, a "claim" is defined not merely as a statement of fact or opinion, but as a specific subclass of a Descriptive Information Content Entity (DescriptiveICE). As formalized by the axiom `claim_observed_by_event`, any entity designated as a Claim (Class X) must satisfy the condition of being observed at a specific temporal process, denoted by the BFO reference `bfo:0000015`. This ontological constraint ensures that no assertion exists in a vacuum; every claim must be traceable to a distinct occurrence, such as a meeting, a site inspection, or a system log generation event.

The verbalization of this axiom dictates that {X} is a descriptive information content entity. Consequently, the integrity of the audit trail relies on the immutable linkage between the claim itself and the event during which it was generated. For instance, if a member of the Audit Committee asserts during a convened session that "fiscal variance exceeds threshold," this assertion is not entered into the system as free text. Rather, it is instantiated as a specific Claim entity, ontologically typed and permanently bound to the "Audit Committee Meeting" event via the `observedAt` property.

### 1.2 Procedural Implementation

During the compilation of minutes and compliance reports, the Recording Secretary is responsible for identifying discrete assertions made by Members Present. These assertions must be logged in the central registry. The procedure requires that the claim be categorized (e.g., "RegulatoryAdherenceAssertion," "SafetyProtocolViolation") and that the timestamp and event ID of the proceeding be recorded.

Failure to anchor a claim to a valid `bfo:0000015` process results in a data integrity anomaly classified as an "Orphaned Record." Such records are inadmissible for regulatory reporting purposes. Therefore, all entries must reference a valid Call to Order, Inspection Start, or System Initialization trigger.

### 1.3 Evidence and Verification

It is incumbent upon the Compliance Officer to verify that the claims listed in the registry reflect the actual discourse held during the documented event. For example, the entry `CLAIM-2023-Q4-001` (SewerLineIntegrityAssertion) must be supported by the specific minutes from the Site Inspection held on October 12, 2023. If the minutes do not contain the verbal or written evidence of this assertion, the record must be purged or annotated as "Unsubstantiated."

## 2. Metrics Observed by Event

### 2.1 Definition and Scope

While claims provide qualitative context, governance frameworks rely heavily on quantitative evidence. A "metric," in this ontology, is also a Descriptive Information Content Entity (DescriptiveICE), but it is distinguished by its representational nature as a measurement value. Governed by the axiom `metric_observed_by_event`, a Metric (Class X) is similarly defined by its relationship to a temporal process (`bfo:0000015`).

The verbalization remains consistent: {X} is a descriptive information content entity. However, in practice, the instantiation of a Metric entity involves the capture of a data point (e.g., flow rate, voltage, attendance count) at a specific moment. The `observedAt` property links the value to the measurement event, preventing the ambiguity of "timeless" data. For example, a water pressure reading of 55 PSI is only valid if it is known *when* that reading was taken. The event provides the temporal context necessary for trend analysis and regulatory benchmarking.

### 2.2 Procedural Implementation

Technical staff and automated monitoring systems are the primary sources for Metric entities. When a sensor logs a value or an inspector records a measurement, the data ingestion pipeline must create an instance of the appropriate Metric Class. The system must validate that the incoming data stream is associated with an active "Observation Event."

The distinction between a raw number and a Metric entity is critical for the DescriptiveICE classification. The number "55" is a literal; the entity `EffluentPressurePSI` instantiated with the value "55" and anchored to `Event-882` is the DescriptiveICE. This abstraction allows the governance framework to query, audit, and report on the *metadata* of the measurement (who took it, when, under what protocol) rather than merely storing the numerical value.

### 2.3 Audit and Compliance

Regular audits of the `t_metric_observed_by_event` table are required to ensure data continuity. Gaps in the `id` sequence or missing temporal references indicate a failure in the data logging pipeline. For instance, if `METRIC-2023-10-102` (PumpStationFlowRateGPM) is present but lacks a corresponding event timestamp in the process logs, the metric cannot be used to demonstrate compliance with discharge permits. The governance framework mandates that all such anomalies be flagged in the quarterly "Compiled Releases and Authorizations" report.
