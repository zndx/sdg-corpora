# Chapter 7: Governance Ontology and Evidence Provenance

This chapter delineates the structural architecture of governance frameworks, specifically focusing on the formalization of audit processes, directives, and evidence chains within a semantic ontology. The scope encompasses the definition of core classes related to information content entities, the derivation of evidentiary support for compliance claims, and the cyclical relationship between authoritative directives and their subsequent attestations. The objective is to provide a rigorous, machine-readable schema that supports high-fidelity audit trails and automated compliance verification.

## 7.1 Audit Processes and Evidence Generation

In the context of governance frameworks, an audit is formally defined not merely as a review, but as a distinct processual entity that generates specific informational artifacts. Under the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO), an audit is a `bfo:0000015` (Process) that necessarily results in the production of evidence. This relationship is codified in the axiom stating that an audit class produces evidence which is a `cco:DescriptiveICE` (Descriptive Information Content Entity).

This axiom establishes the provenance chain for all compliance data. It asserts that for an audit to be valid, it must output descriptive entities that serve as the ground truth for subsequent governance activities. The structure implies that the audit process is the causal antecedent to the existence of the evidence record.

## 7.2 Descriptive Information Content Entities

The fundamental unit of data within any governance system is the Information Content Entity (ICE). Specifically, we utilize `cco:DescriptiveICE`, which denotes information that proposes or describes a state of affairs rather than prescribing one. This category encompasses logs, records, and generated reports that exist independently of any agent's belief or intention regarding their truth value, although they are often the substrate upon which beliefs are formed.

The axiom `evidence_basic` posits that any evidence utilized in the governance framework is a subclass of `cco:DescriptiveICE`. This classification is critical for distinguishing raw data (descriptions) from normative statements (directives) or subjective assessments (claims). By anchoring evidence in this class, the ontology ensures that all supporting material is treated as an objective, persistable entity within the information system.

## 7.3 Directive Governance and Applicability

Directives represent the normative layer of the governance framework. A `cco:DirectiveICE` is an information content entity that prescribes specific behaviors or states. The complexity of governance arises from the need to trace the lineage of these directives: they must be authored by a legitimate authority and must apply to specific target domains or classes.

The `directive_authored_and_applies` axiom formalizes this by defining a directive as an intersection of three conditions: it is a Directive, it is authored by some entity Y, and it applies to some entity Z. In this schema, the authorship slot (`y`) is constrained by a foreign key relationship to an attestation. This creates a validation loop ensuring that directives are not created in a vacuum; they must be grounded in a prior attestation of authority or validity.

The relational schema presented below captures this tripartite structure. The primary key is `id`, while `y` (the author) references an attestation record, and `z` (the target) specifies the scope of the directive.

## 7.4 Attestation and Supporting Evidence

While directives prescribe action, attestations provide the confirmation that such actions have occurred or that a state of compliance exists. An attestation is defined as a process (`bfo:0000015`) that possesses supporting evidence. This evidence, denoted as slot `y`, is logically linked to the directives defined in the previous section.

This linkage implies that an attestation validates a directive. The "supporting evidence" for the attestation is the directive itself, serving as the standard against which compliance is measured. This recursive structure—where directives authorize attestations, and attestations validate directives—forms the backbone of a robust, self-reinforcing governance cycle.

## 7.5 Claims and Evidence Support

In the final layer of the framework, we define claims. A claim is a subclass of `cco:DescriptiveICE` that asserts a proposition. Unlike raw evidence, which is merely descriptive, a claim implies a stance or a conclusion drawn by an agent. For a claim to be valid within the system, it must be supported by at least one piece of evidence produced by an audit process.

The `claim_min_one_evidence` axiom enforces a cardinality constraint: a claim must be supported by a minimum of one evidence item (`min 1`). The evidence referenced here (`y`) must be traceable back to an audit process (`t_audit_produces_evidence`). This ensures that all declarative statements of compliance or status are anchored to verifiable, audit-generated data artifacts.
